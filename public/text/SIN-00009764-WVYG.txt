Summary:

 Elliots Hill is a care home for up to 23 adults in Pembrokeshire. The service supports people with a
 range of needs, including older people, people with learning disabilities and mental health needs.
 People live in a homely rural environment and are supported by a staff team who know them well
 and provide person-centred care.

 People experience good well-being because people maintain important relationships and make
 choices about their daily lives. Staff understand people's individual preferences and routines, and
 people appear comfortable and relaxed.

 The care and support provided is good because people receive personalised care from staff who
 understand their needs, preferences and goals. Care planning is outcome focused, healthcare
 needs are supported and effective systems help ensure medication management and infection
 prevention arrangements remain safe.

 The environment is good because people live in a comfortable, personalised setting that supports
 their needs and well-being. The service provides suitable facilities, equipment and outdoor spaces.
 The provider continues to invest in environmental improvements which benefit people living at the
 service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good well-being because staff know them well and support them to achieve
 outcomes that are important to them. People are treated with dignity and respect and are supported
 to make choices about their daily lives. During the inspection, we observed many positive and
 natural interactions between people and staff. People appeared comfortable and relaxed in their
 home environment and sought out staff confidently for support and conversation. One person told
 us they liked the carers who support them, whilst another said staff were “kind”. We observed staff
 responding promptly to people's requests for support.

 People have control over many aspects of their daily lives. We observed people choosing how they
 spent their time and following routines that reflected their preferences. One person chose to have
 lunch earlier than other people, whilst another independently completed their daily exercise around
 the grounds. Personal plans contained detailed information about people's wishes, routines, life
 histories and desired outcomes, and staff demonstrated a good understanding of how to support
 people in line with these.

 People maintain relationships with family and those important to them and are supported to remain
 connected to their local communities. A relative spoke positively about the service and described
 staff as caring and welcoming. One relative told us the service felt “like a big family” and another
 said they were able to visit freely. During the inspection, people were attending a local day centre,
 which helps support their social well-being and maintain important connections. Another person
 planned a Halloween party with a staff member, including inviting people from other homes, which
 helped promote social connections and a strong sense of belonging.

 People are protected through effective safeguarding arrangements. We found the provider sought
 professional advice and reviewed care plans and risk assessments when concerns arose. Staff
 understood their responsibilities for protecting people from harm and knew how to raise concerns.
 Risk assessments provide staff with clear guidance about how to support people safely and are
 reviewed when people's needs change.

 People also benefit from living in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. The
 home provides comfortable communal areas, extensive outdoor space and opportunities for people
 to enjoy the environment around them. Ongoing improvements to facilities and the environment
 help ensure people continue to live in surroundings that support their well-being and quality of life.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support because staff understand their needs, preferences
and personal goals and provide support that helps them achieve positive outcomes. Staff know
people well and care records contain detailed information about people's histories, routines, health
needs and desired outcomes. Personal plans are person-centred and provide staff with clear
guidance about how people wish to receive support.

Care planning arrangements are regularly reviewed and updated when people's needs change.
Advice and recommendations from healthcare professionals are reflected within care plans and risk
assessments, helping to ensure staff have current information about how to meet people's needs.
Staff demonstrate a good understanding of people's preferences, risks and support requirements
and are able to explain how they help people achieve their personal outcomes.

People have access to healthcare services and specialist support when required. Records
evidence involvement from community mental health services, pharmacists and other specialist
professionals. Health appointments are attended and professional advice informs support relating
to nutrition, mobility, medication and emotional well-being. Staff are aware of their responsibilities in
supporting people to maintain their health and well-being. Risk assessments provide staff with
guidance about how to support people safely whilst meeting their assessed needs.

Medication management arrangements help ensure people receive their medicines safely and as
prescribed. The service uses an electronic medication management system supported by regular
audits, stock checks and competency assessments. Medication records are well maintained, and
managers provide competency reviews and monitoring. Good infection prevention and control
arrangements are also in place, and the home is clean and tidy.

Relatives and professionals speak positively about the care provided. One relative described the
service as “like a big family” and said staff “really care and make the residents feel special.”
Another relative told us they visit daily and said staff always contact them if any issues arise. A
healthcare professional described the service as “very supportive and professional” and stated staff
treated people with “compassion and kindness.”

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that supports their well-being outcomes because the home is
comfortable and welcoming. The provider continues to invest in the service and maintain facilities
that promote people's independence, safety and well-being.

People benefit from living in spacious surroundings with access to a range of communal and
outdoor areas. The home is set within extensive rural grounds which provide opportunities for
people to enjoy outdoor space, exercise and relaxation. During the inspection, we observed one
person independently completing their daily exercise around the grounds. Communal areas were
well presented and provided opportunities for people to spend time socialising or relaxing according
to their preferences.

People's bedrooms are personalised and reflect their individual preferences and identities. We saw
bedrooms decorated with personal belongings, photographs and items that were important to
people. One person proudly pointed out their bedroom and told us “I like my room.” These
arrangements help create a homely environment where people feel comfortable and supported.

People have access to facilities and equipment that help them remain safe and comfortable. We
reviewed a range of maintenance, servicing and health and safety records and found appropriate
arrangements were in place to monitor the environment. Regular environmental audits are
completed, and the provider has developed an environmental improvement plan to support ongoing
maintenance and refurbishment works. Recent investment includes improvements to buildings,
grounds and facilities used by people living at the service.

The home is clean and well maintained. We observed good standards of cleanliness throughout
communal areas, bathrooms and bedrooms. Appropriate infection prevention and control measures
are in place and recent monitoring provides positive feedback regarding cleanliness standards.
Some refurbishment works were ongoing at the time of the inspection and the provider had clear
plans in place to continue improving the environment. These arrangements help ensure people live
in surroundings that are safe, pleasant and meet their needs.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from good leadership and management because leaders are committed to providing
a positive service for people and demonstrate a clear commitment to continuous improvement.
Managers of the service maintain effective systems to monitor the quality and safety of the service
and are visible within the home, providing support and guidance to staff when required. These
arrangements help ensure people receive safe, consistent and person-centred care.

Leaders use electronic systems to support care planning, communication, quality assurance and
outcome monitoring across the service. We saw evidence of daily records being linked to people's
personal goals and reviewed reports which identified achievements, challenges and opportunities
to further develop support. These arrangements help staff access current information about
people's needs and ensure support remains responsive when circumstances change.

The provider maintains effective arrangements to monitor and improve the quality of the service.
Audits, quality reviews, Quality of Care Reports and Responsible Individual visits demonstrate good
oversight of the service. Leaders use feedback from people, relatives, staff and professionals to
identify areas for improvement and monitor performance. Leaders are visible within the service and
staff told us they are approachable and available to provide support and guidance when needed.
The service works openly with partner agencies and responds appropriately when concerns arise.
We found evidence of positive engagement with safeguarding services, commissioners, healthcare
professionals and regulators.

People are supported by a skilled and knowledgeable staff team. Staff complete an induction
programme and receive regular supervision, appraisal and ongoing training. Training records
demonstrated staff have access to a broad range of mandatory and specialist learning
opportunities relevant to the needs of people living at the service. Several staff described
opportunities to develop their skills and progress within the organisation. One member of staff told
us they “love working at Elliots Hill” and another described the “good development opportunities”
available to them.

Management continue to invest in the development of the service. We saw evidence of ongoing
environmental improvements and the recruitment of a new Activities and Enrichment Co-ordinator.
These improvements demonstrate a commitment to maintaining and improving people's
experiences and outcomes. Governance arrangements provide assurance that the service is
monitored effectively and that managers remain focused on the well-being of people living at the
service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
