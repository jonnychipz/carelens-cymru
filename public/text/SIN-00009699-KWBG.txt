Summary:

 Cottage View is a care home managed by Powys Health Authority.

 Well-being is rated as good because people receive a service that is person centred from a team of
 care workers who are motivated to offer people the best support they can.

 Care and support is good because of the knowledge care workers have together with helpful
 support plans and personal profiles meaning care staff know what and who is important to the
 individual.

 The environment is rated as good because it is clean and well maintained. There is ample space
 and natural light. Also people have personalised their rooms.

 Leadership and management is rated as good because the team is effectively led by an

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely and with some control over their lives. Care workers show people
 respect and have regard for their dignity. They understand the importance of supporting people to
 make choices where possible.

 The relationships people have with those caring for them is good with people describing the staff as
 “lovely” and “very kind”. Care workers feel they know people well, knowing who and what is
 important to them. The atmosphere in the service is calm with laughter and chatting heard during
 our inspection. Care workers engage with people in passing as well as during personal care and
 one to one time.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect because care workers have a good
 understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities and are confident the manager would take the
 steps needed to make sure people are safeguarded.

 There are some opportunities for people to do things that matter to them. Staffing levels mean care
 workers can engage with people either doing activities or spending time chatting, in addition to the
 time the activities worker spends with people. Visiting is flexible meaning people can see others
 who matter to them.

 People live in a service which enhances their well-being. The cleanliness as well as the space and
 natural light together with how people have personalised their rooms mean people live in a
 comfortable and safe environment.

 People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because premises and
 equipment are kept clean and hygienic. There are adequate supplies of personal protective
 equipment (PPE) available for staff and visitors. Food hygiene practices are good and both catering
 and housekeeping staff recognise their important roles in people’s care and the effective running of
 the service.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the care and support they need. Some people have made Cottage View their home
and others are there on a temporary basis. We saw interactions between people which were
friendly, supportive and respectful. People enjoy having a bit of banter and a joke with the staff and
staff talk about how they enjoy their job and the relationships they have with those they support.
Personal plans are informative and care workers find them helpful. The service has recently moved
over to a new electronic system and care workers feel this has been a big improvement. There are
personal plans for a range of areas including communication, mobility, nutrition and personal care.
Daily entries are mostly person centred and give a concise but informative description of the care
and support offered to people. Care workers feel they have the information they need from the care
records, communication book and handovers to provide good care to people or understand
people’s care needs.

There is good evidence that people can do things that matter to them. This includes group activities
such as quizzes, board games and art. Special days, such as Mother’s day, St Davids day and
Remembrance Day are recognised. There are also individual activities where care workers spend
time chatting with people. Some choose to spend their time alone in their rooms reading or
watching the television. The activities worker is well regarded by the team and keeps
comprehensive records of activities and each individual’s participation. Senior staff use the records
when reviewing personal plans. Most people feel the level of activities is about right for them.

People’s physical health needs are met. District nurses visit regularly and care workers know when
to report any concerns to them. Care workers know how to recognise signs of pressure damage
and they have the equipment they need to reduce the risk of pressure sores. People’s physical
observations are monitored regularly.

The senior carer is responsible for administering medication and they have received the
appropriate training and competency assessment. The senior carer completes the medication
rounds without interruption to reduce the risk of errors. Medication is stored in a locked room.
Controlled drugs are appropriately stored and a reconciliation showed the correct number of drugs
were available. A stock balance is also maintained for regular medication. The clinical room is
clean and free from clutter.

There is an understanding amongst the staff team of the importance of good nutrition. Most meals
are made using fresh ingredients and the catering staff are generally satisfied with the quality of
ingredients. People are encouraged to use the dining room and tables are attractively laid to
enhance people’s dining experience. There is a choice of main meal and people can have
alternatives if they want. Some food is available outside of mealtimes and staff understand the
need for people to remain hydrated to reduce the risk of infection. Drinks are offered and
encouraged throughout the day. However, portion sizes are considered to be small at times as they
are governed by the Health Board. Also, there are some restrictions on some food following a
directive from the Board, thereby limiting people’s choice. Care workers are responsible for serving

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that meets their needs. All accommodation is on one level making it
easy for people to mobilise through the service. Bedrooms have ensuite facilities and most have
been personalised with photographs, ornaments and soft furnishings. There are several lounge
areas for people to spend time with others if they choose.

Additional bathrooms and toilets mean people are able to access these facilities easily and in a
timely way, thereby helping to promote independence and dignity.

The service is as homely as possible with artwork, flowers displayed, with puzzles and games on
tables for people to spend time on. Some people particularly appreciate the large windows which
enable them to enjoy the views.

Standards of cleanliness are good. The housekeeping team take pride in their work recognising
their important contribution to people’s overall care and support. They have the equipment they
need and are satisfied with the quality of their products.

The service is in good decorative order and repairs are completed promptly.

There is some outside space for people to enjoy when the weather permits and people spend time
growing some fruit and vegetables as well as flowers.

Safety is a priority with visitors having to sign in to a visitors book meaning staff know who is in the
service at all times.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The Responsible Individual (RI) has good oversight of the service and visits regularly. The reports
written are concise and demonstrate the views of people are sought and a range of quality
indicators such as the environment, personal plans and staff satisfaction are considered.
The team are led by an experienced and well-respected manager who has confidence in their
deputy and team. Care worked feel able to raise any ideas or concerns and are confident of getting
a helpful and timely response. Care workers speak fondly about their colleagues, describing how
they work well as a team. One care worker told us “It’s a smashing team” and another said “It’s a
lovely atmosphere. The staff are just so kind”.


People are supported by knowledgeable and motivated staff who value the relationships they have
with the people they support. The provider makes sure there are enough staff on duty at all times to
make sure people’s needs are met in a person centred and unhurried way.

Care workers consider they have the training they need to safely and effectively carry out their
duties. Some care workers are completing additional training and some have been promoted during
their employment. Care workers are able to request additional training if they feel this is needed.
Training is made up of both online and face-to-face. Competency assessments are completed as
necessary.

Supervision is generally up to date and care workers get constructive feedback on their work to
help them in their professional development. Appraisals are up to date with objectives set and work
priorities considered.

People are safe and protected from abuse, harm and neglect. This is because all staff have
completed training in safeguarding and there is a comprehensive policy to be used as a resource.
Care workers know the actions they are required to take if they suspect an individual is at risk and
are confident their manager would take any concerns raised seriously and report them in the
correct way.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
