Summary:

 Peniel Green Care Home is a care home providing accommodation, personal care and nursing
 care for up to 34 people. The home is situated in Peniel Green, Swansea, with access to local
 amenities, community resources and opportunities for people to maintain connections with the local
 area. People also benefit from access to secure outdoor spaces and a range of activities and
 community engagement opportunities. Based on our findings, we have rated Well-being as Good,
 Care and Support as Good, Environment as Good, and Leadership and Management as Requires
 Improvement.

 During our inspection, we found people were supported by a caring and committed staff team who
 knew them well and promote their well-being and independence. People and relatives spoke
 positively about the home, describing it as welcoming and homely. Feedback demonstrates people
 are listened to and are able to influence aspects of daily life, including activities and food provision.
 We saw positive interactions between staff and people throughout the inspection and found people
 are supported to access healthcare services when required.

 The home is generally clean, comfortable and well maintained. However, our review of care
 records identified that care plans, risk assessments and daily recordings do not always fully reflect
 people's current needs or consistently evidence the care and support provided. Whilst we did not
 find evidence that people are receiving unsafe care, improvements are needed to strengthen care
 planning and recording systems. An Area for Improvement (AFI) was identified within Care and

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive outcomes because they are supported to exercise choice and control
 over their daily lives and maintain their independence. People appeared comfortable, relaxed and
 at ease in their surroundings. We observed respectful interactions between people and staff
 throughout the home. Staff took time to engage with people in a patient manner and demonstrated
 a good understanding of their preferences, and individual needs.

 People are treated with dignity and respect and are supported by staff who know them well. People
 and their relatives consistently provided positive feedback regarding the care and support they
 receive. One relative told us, "Staff are brilliant, everyone I have met has been lovely," whilst
 another described the home as "very homely." Feedback from relatives indicate they feel welcomed
 by the service and are confident staff treat people with kindness and consideration. Throughout the
 inspection we saw staff speaking respectfully with people and responding promptly when
 assistance or reassurance was required.

 People maintain relationships and participate in activities that are meaningful to them. The service
 provides opportunities for people to engage in activities both within the home and the local
 community. People told us they enjoy the range of events available and value opportunities to
 spend time with relatives, friends and other people living at the home. Relatives confirmed they
 could visit at times that suit them and described the home as welcoming and inclusive. We
 observed visitors spending time with people both indoors and in outdoor areas during the
 inspection.

 People's views are sought and acted upon, helping them influence the way the service is run. We
 found good examples of people being involved in decisions affecting their daily lives. People told us
 they had raised concerns about meal choices through residents' meetings and felt their views had
 been listened to and acted upon. Two people spoke enthusiastically about their campaign to
 improve the lunchtime meal and told us changes had been made following feedback from people.
 They explained that having successfully influenced lunch options, their next aim is to improve the
 choice available at teatime. This demonstrates people feel confident expressing their views and
 that the service responds to feedback provided. The example also showed people are actively
 involved in shaping their environment and experiences within the home.

 People benefit from living in a home where their well-being is promoted through positive
 relationships, meaningful engagement and opportunities to influence decisions. Feedback from
 people, relatives and our observations throughout the inspection provided assurance that people
 are listened to, treated with respect and supported to achieve positive outcomes. Overall, we found

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support from staff who know them well and understand their individual
needs, preferences and routines. Throughout the inspection, we observed positive interactions
between people and staff. Staff provided support in a kind, and respectful manner and took time to
respond to people's requests and preferences.

People appeared comfortable in the presence of staff and were observed receiving support in a
way that promoted their dignity and independence. Relatives consistently spoke positively about
the care provided. One relative told us, “They look after her very well,” whilst another said, “I can't
fault them.” These comments reflect the positive experiences shared by people and families during
the inspection.

People are supported to maintain their health, well-being and personal outcomes. Records
demonstrate involvement from a range of healthcare professionals, including GPs, community
nurses and specialist services. We saw evidence that referrals are made when required and that
professional guidance is incorporated into the support provided. Staff were able to explain how they
monitor people's health and recognise changes which may require additional support or medical
intervention. Relatives told us they are kept informed about significant changes in their family
member's health and are confident concerns will be acted upon appropriately. This provides
assurance that people receive support to access healthcare services and maintain their health and
well-being.

People benefit from care and support that is responsive to their needs and delivered by staff who
understand them as individuals. Staff we spoke with demonstrated a good knowledge of the people
they support and were able to describe individual preferences, and the support required to achieve
positive outcomes.

We reviewed a sample of care plans, risk assessments, medication records and daily records.
Whilst care plans generally reflect people's assessed needs and provide guidance for staff, we
identified areas where documentation requires strengthening. Care plans, risk assessments and
daily records do not always provide a clear and accurate reflection of people's current needs,
outcomes and the support provided. In some cases, information lacked sufficient detail, records
had not been updated promptly, or daily recordings do not consistently demonstrate how care and
support has been delivered. We also found some risk assessments do not fully reflect identified
risks or provide sufficiently detailed guidance to support consistent care delivery.

Overall, people receive care and support from a caring and knowledgeable staff team who
understand their needs and are committed to promoting positive outcomes. Our observations,
discussions with people and feedback from relatives provided assurance that people were
receiving the care they required. However, outcomes for people require improvement because care

relation to the provider's arrangements for maintaining accurate and up-to-date care plans, risk
assessments and care records.





       Environment                         Good

--- PAGE 8 ---
People live in an environment that promotes their safety, wellbeing and independence. We found
the home to be comfortable, welcoming and generally well maintained. People moved freely around
the service throughout the inspection and were observed using communal areas, spending time
with visitors and accessing outdoor spaces. Bedrooms are personalised and communal areas are
suitably furnished to support people's needs.

People benefit from premises and facilities that are suitable for the service provided. The home
offers a range of communal areas where people can socialise, participate in activities or spend time
quietly. People and visitors were observed using both indoor and outdoor spaces during the
inspection. Management had identified opportunities to further enhance the outdoor environment.
The garden room was temporarily being used for storage following a recent event and therefore
was not being used to its full potential at the time of our visit. The small lounge was also observed
to be underutilised at the time of the inspection. The provider acknowledged this and outlined plans
to make better use of the space to support people's social engagement and choice of communal
areas.

People are supported in an environment where risks to health and safety are identified and
managed. We reviewed maintenance and safety records and found appropriate arrangements are
in place to service and monitor equipment and premises. Records demonstrate that checks relating
to gas safety, electrical installations, portable electrical appliances, water safety, emergency
lighting, fire equipment and lifting equipment had been completed within required timescales. Fire
safety arrangements are well organised, hoist and sling checks were available, and substances
hazardous to health are stored securely. People benefit from arrangements that promote their
health and wellbeing through the safe preparation and handling of food. The service holds a Food
Standards Agency food hygiene rating of 5, indicating very good standards of food hygiene

People benefit from effective arrangements for the maintenance of the environment and equipment.
The maintenance person demonstrated a clear understanding of their role and responsibilities and
described effective systems for identifying, prioritising and responding to maintenance issues.
Essential replacement equipment, including spare mattresses, slings and cushions, are available to
ensure equipment can be replaced promptly when required.

Overall, we found the service to provide a safe, comfortable and suitable environment for people.
Whilst the inspection identified some minor issues, including clutter within one communal area that
reduced available space and created a potential trip hazard, these had not significantly impacted
on people's experiences of the service. We therefore found that people generally benefit from an
environment that supports their wellbeing and promotes their safety.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



People are supported by a management team that is committed to promoting their well-being and
delivering person-centred care. Staff spoke positively about the support they receive from the
manager and described them as approachable and available to provide advice and guidance. Staff
told us they receive regular supervision and appraisal and have opportunities to contribute to the
development of the service through meetings and day-to-day discussions. Throughout the
inspection, managers and staff demonstrated a commitment to supporting people and maintaining
a welcoming and homely environment.

People benefit from staff who are supported to develop the knowledge and skills required for their
role. We found arrangements are in place for staff induction, mandatory training, supervision and
competency assessments. Training compliance is generally good, and staff are knowledgeable
about the people they support. Staff described positive working relationships within the team and
told us they feel supported to carry out their responsibilities. These arrangements help promote
consistency of care and support for people living at the service.

People receive care and support from a service where effective leadership, governance and quality
assurance arrangements are in place to monitor quality, drive improvement and ensure compliance
with regulatory requirements. The provider has established systems to monitor the quality and
safety of the service, including audits and oversight visits completed by the Responsible Individual
(RI). However, we found governance and oversight arrangements are not always effective in
identifying, monitoring and addressing areas requiring improvement. This is evidenced by care
planning and risk assessment documentation needing strengthening, and shortfalls in the
monitoring of Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) renewals and limited evidence that quality
assurance actions were consistently followed through to completion.

During the inspection, we identified care planning and risk assessment records that do not always
fully reflect people's current needs and the support provided. These issues had not been identified
through existing auditing processes. We also found a number of staff DBS checks had expired and,
whilst risk assessments were implemented during the inspection whilst renewal applications were
progressed, this demonstrated weaknesses in governance arrangements for monitoring recruitment
records. Furthermore, we are not fully assured that actions arising from quality assurance activities
are consistently monitored and reviewed to ensure completion. We found RI quality assurance
reports are largely descriptive and do not consistently demonstrate evaluation of service quality or
the impact of improvement actions on outcomes for people.

Overall, we found a committed management team and a workforce that felt valued and supported.
However, improvements are required to strengthen governance and quality assurance

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
