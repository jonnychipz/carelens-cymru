Summary:

 The Old Manse is a care home service providing accommodation and support for up to four adults
 with learning disabilities, autism and associated needs.

 People experience good well-being outcomes because they are supported to make choices,
 develop independence, participate in meaningful activities and maintain relationships important to
 them. People are treated with dignity and respect and are supported to achieve personal goals
 which enhance their quality of life.

 People receive good care and support personalised to their individual needs, preferences and
 aspirations. Personal plans are detailed and person-centred, helping care staff provide consistent
 support which promotes independence, health and well-being. People are supported to access
 healthcare services, participate in community activities and achieve positive personal outcomes.

People benefit from a good environment which is comfortable, safe and personalised. The home
provides welcoming communal areas, well-maintained bedrooms and outdoor spaces which
support relaxation, recreation and social interaction. Facilities within the home, including sensory
resources, enhance people’s well-being and help meet individual needs.

People benefit from good leadership and management. Effective governance systems, quality

and focused on continuous improvement. Care staff feel supported by managers and receive
training and development opportunities which help them meet people’s needs effectively. Feedback
from people, relatives and care staff is sought and used to support the continued development of
the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good well-being outcomes because they have control over many aspects of
 their daily lives and are supported to achieve goals that are important to them. Positive
 relationships between people and care staff help create a welcoming and supportive atmosphere
 where people feel valued, respected and listened to. We found people are encouraged to make
 choices about their daily routines, activities and how they spend their time. One person told us they
 feel safe, enjoy living at the service and are able to do the things they enjoy. A relative stated their
 family member is supported to ‘live the best life that he can in his own way.’

 People are supported to pursue their interests, develop new skills and participate in meaningful
 activities within the home and wider community. Care staff work closely with people to identify
 goals and support them to achieve personal outcomes. Records demonstrate people regularly
 access community facilities, leisure activities, family visits and opportunities that promote
 independence and social inclusion. A relative told us their family member enjoys a range of
 activities and is “Always happy to go back home, which provides reassurance they are well
 supported.” Relatives consistently spoke positively about the care provided and described people
 as settled, happy and well supported within the service.

 People’s communication needs are understood and supported well. Information is available in
 accessible formats, and the service demonstrates a positive commitment to ensuring people
 receive information in ways they can understand. We saw examples of personalised approaches
 being developed to support people with specific communication needs. Individual communication
 methods are recognised and respected, which helps people to make informed choices and
 participate in decisions about their lives. The service is also working towards further promoting
 Welsh language and culture through bilingual resources and the increased use of Welsh
 throughout the home.

 People are supported to maintain their physical, emotional and mental well-being. Care staff ensure
 people attend routine health appointments and access specialist services where required. Positive
 risk management promotes independence whilst helping people remain safe. Individual risk
 assessments provide care staff with clear guidance on supporting people safely, enabling people to
 participate in activities and experiences that enhance their quality of life.

 People understand their rights and are supported to raise concerns if they wish to do so.
 Accessible information, house meetings and everyday discussions help ensure people’s voices are
 heard. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of safeguarding responsibilities and work to
 promote people’s safety and well-being.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive consistently good quality care and support because care staff have a clear
understanding of their individual needs, preferences and personal outcomes. The provider
completes detailed assessments and regularly reviews support arrangements to ensure care
remains appropriate and responsive to changing needs. Personal plans are strengths-based and
provide care staff with clear guidance on how to support people in a way that promotes choice,
independence and positive outcomes.

People are involved in planning and reviewing their care and support wherever possible. Personal
plans reflect people’s wishes, communication needs, routines, interests and aspirations. Regular
reviews help ensure information remains current and relevant. Care staff use this information to
provide personalised support that recognises people’s strengths and helps them achieve their
goals.

People benefit from care that is focused on promoting independence, confidence and meaningful
participation in everyday life. Care staff support people to develop life skills, maintain community
involvement and enjoy activities that matter to them. Records demonstrate people are encouraged
to work towards personal goals and are supported to celebrate their achievements. This contributes
positively to emotional well-being, self-esteem and social inclusion.

People’s health needs are managed effectively. Care staff work closely with healthcare
professionals to ensure people receive the support they need to maintain good health and well-
being. Health information is detailed and provides care staff with clear guidance regarding physical
health, mental health and specialist support requirements. People are supported to attend routine
health appointments, annual health checks and specialist reviews where necessary.

Medication is managed safely and people receive their medicines in accordance with their
assessed needs. Secure storage arrangements, regular audits and trained care staff support safe
medication practices. Effective oversight helps maintain consistency and promotes positive health
outcomes.

People are protected from harm through positive behaviour support approaches and proportionate
risk management. Care staff understand people’s needs and follow agreed strategies to support
them in the least restrictive way possible.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that supports their needs and contributes positively to their well-
being. The home is comfortable, welcoming and personalised, providing people with a place where
they can feel safe, secure and relaxed. Bedrooms reflect people’s individual preferences and
interests, helping to create a strong sense of ownership and belonging. Communal areas provide
opportunities for social interaction, activities and relaxation whilst also supporting people’s
independence. The atmosphere throughout the home is calm and homely, enabling people to
spend time in ways that suit their individual preferences and routines.

People benefit from a safe and well-maintained environment. The provider has effective systems in
place to monitor the premises and ensure facilities and equipment remain safe for people to use.
Regular health and safety checks, maintenance programmes and servicing arrangements help
ensure risks are identified and managed appropriately. Fire safety systems, emergency lighting,
water safety monitoring and environmental risk assessments are maintained to support people’s
safety and well-being. Emergency procedures are well established, and care staff have clear
guidance on supporting people safely in the event of an incident or emergency.

People have access to facilities and resources that enhance their quality of life. The service has
invested in spaces that support people’s individual sensory and therapeutic needs, providing
opportunities for relaxation and emotional well-being. The sensory room is a particular strength and
offers a dedicated space where people can access sensory equipment, calming resources and
activities that support their individual needs. This area has been thoughtfully developed and is used
to promote relaxation, engagement and emotional regulation.

Outdoor areas are attractive, accessible and used well. People have opportunities to spend time
outdoors, participate in recreational activities and enjoy social occasions throughout the year. The
garden provides a range of facilities including areas for relaxation, exercise and social interaction.
These spaces encourage people to spend time outside, remain active and enjoy meaningful
experiences within their home environment.

The provider demonstrates a commitment to maintaining a homely environment. Living spaces are
clean, tidy and suitably furnished, creating a pleasant atmosphere for people. Recent
improvements to communal areas and bathroom facilities demonstrate ongoing investment in the
environment. Photographs, personal belongings and decorative features help create surroundings
that reflect the interests of people who live there. Equipment and furnishings are suitable for
purpose and support people to remain comfortable and independent.

The environment promotes comfort, dignity and well-being. People benefit from surroundings that

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from a well-led service with strong governance and effective oversight which support
positive outcomes. The management team promotes a culture that is supportive, person-centred
and focused on continuous improvement. Care staff, relatives and other stakeholders speak
positively about leadership arrangements and describe managers as approachable, responsive and
committed to ensuring people receive high-quality care and support.

The management team has successfully developed a positive and stable culture within the service.
Care staff consistently described improvements in teamwork, morale and communication. Care
staff stated the service is ‘The best experience by all measurements’ and spoke positively about
the friendly atmosphere and sense of achievement gained from supporting people. Other care staff
described how colleagues work well together, support one another and create an environment
where they feel valued and respected. Care staff also told us management are helpful, supportive
and available when guidance is needed. These positive working relationships contribute to
consistency of care and support for people living at the service.

The provider has effective quality assurance systems that help maintain good standards across the
service. Regular audits, management reviews, quality of care reviews and responsible individual
visits provide oversight of key aspects of service delivery. Information gathered through these
processes is used to identify strengths, monitor performance and support ongoing improvement.
Governance processes demonstrate a clear focus on promoting people’s well-being, safety and
quality of life. Management maintain oversight of incidents, safeguarding matters, training, health
and safety, staffing and care practices, helping to ensure people receive consistent and effective
support.

People are supported by a committed and knowledgeable team of care staff. Care staff described
positive working relationships with managers and colleagues and said they feel supported within
their roles. New and established care staff spoke positively about their induction experiences and
said training provides them with the knowledge and skills required to support people effectively.
Care staff told us they enjoy coming to work and value the opportunity to make a positive difference
to people’s lives. Regular supervision sessions, probation reviews and annual appraisals provide
opportunities for care staff to reflect on their practice, discuss their well-being and identify
professional development goals. These arrangements help ensure care staff remain motivated,
accountable and focused on delivering high-quality care and support.

The provider places importance on workforce development and ensuring care staff have the
training and knowledge required to meet people’s needs. Managers maintain oversight of training
and support care staff to achieve professional qualifications and develop new skills. People,

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
