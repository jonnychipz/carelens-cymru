Summary:

 Elan Specialist Residential Care Service provides care and support for people with learning
 disabilities. The service offers accommodation for up to five people. Located in the village of
 Aberfan, the home benefits from close proximity to local amenities and community facilities, all of
 which are within walking distance.

 People experience good well-being outcomes. Staff have a thorough understanding of the people
 they support, including their preferences, needs, and what is important to them. They are
 knowledgeable about the most effective approaches to delivering personalised care and support.
 People are encouraged and empowered to make daily choices and decisions about their lives.

 Care and support is good. Care documentation is person-centred. Care documentation is reviewed
 regularly and updated as required to reflect changes in needs. Risks to people’s health and safety
 are appropriately assessed and managed. People have timely access to appropriate healthcare
 professionals and services to support their ongoing health and well-being.

 The service is well-led and has a positive culture of promoting the well-being of supported people
 and staff. There are governance and quality assurance systems in place helping the service

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are encouraged and supported to make choices about their daily lives, promoting
 independence, autonomy, and personal control. We saw people are able to personalise their
 bedrooms according to their individual tastes and preferences, creating environments that reflect
 their personalities and tastes. People are actively involved in planning their daily routines and are
 supported to make decisions about matters that are important to them, including meals, social
 activities, and holidays. This person-centred approach helps to ensure people’s preferences,
 wishes, and aspirations are recognised and respected.

 People are protected from harm, abuse, and neglect. Care staff are recruited in accordance with
 regulatory requirements, with all the required pre-employment checks undertaken. Care staff
 receive a comprehensive programme of training relevant to the needs of the people they support.
 Training includes key areas such as safeguarding training. Discussions we had with care staff
 confirm they understand their safeguarding responsibilities and are confident in responding to
 concerns appropriately.

 People live in an environment that promotes and supports their well-being. The home provides
 sufficient communal space for people to spend time socially or engage in activities of their choice,
 within areas that are clean, comfortable, and welcoming. People's bedrooms are personalised to
 reflect their individual preferences, interests, and identities, helping them to feel comfortable and at
 home within the service. We found the environment to be well maintained and appropriately
 furnished to meet people’s needs.

 People are treated with dignity, respect, and kindness. During the inspection, we observed
 numerous warm and respectful interactions between care staff and people using the service. Care
 staff demonstrated a good understanding of people’s needs, preferences, and communication
 styles, enabling them to provide care and support in a compassionate and person-centred manner.
 Care staff took time to listen to people, engage them in conversation, and respond appropriately to
 their wishes and preferences.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes. A review of a
sample of personal plans demonstrated they are person-centred and tailored to each person’s
specific needs. The plans include detailed personal profiles, outlining people’s life histories,
preferred routines, relationships, and activities that are important to them. Personal plans are
reviewed at least quarterly, or sooner where circumstances change, to ensure they remain
relevant, effective, and reflective of people’s current needs. Comprehensive pre-admission
assessments are undertaken to ensure the service can meet people’s assessed care and support
needs before they move into the home. Appropriate risk assessments are in place to promote
people’s safety and well-being. These are reviewed regularly to ensure they remain accurate, up to
date, and responsive.

People are supported to achieve and maintain the best possible health outcomes. They have timely
access to healthcare services when required, and records confirmed healthcare appointments and
correspondence are documented and retained within files. The service benefits from a stable team
with low staff turnover. As a result, care staff have developed strong relationships with the people
they support and have a good understanding of their individual needs, preferences, and usual
presentation. This enables care staff to identify any changes in a person’s physical or emotional
well-being promptly and seek advice, support, or intervention from relevant healthcare
professionals where necessary, helping to ensure people receive appropriate and timely care.

People are supported with their medication needs. We observed medication is stored securely and
administered in accordance with prescribed instructions. We identified some areas where
medication management arrangements could be strengthened. These included ensuring the
administration of ‘as required’ (PRN) medication is done in line with best practice guidance and
making some amendments to the service’s medication policy to ensure it fully reflects current
practice and best practice guidance. These matters were discussed with the management team
during the inspection. They provided assurance that the necessary actions would be taken to
address them and further strengthen medication management processes within the service.

People are protected from the risk of infection through effective infection prevention and control
practices. These arrangements are supported by a comprehensive infection control policy. The
service maintains an adequate supply of personal protective equipment (PPE), and care staff were
observed using PPE appropriately in line with current infection control procedures. During the
inspection, we observed high standards of cleanliness and hygiene throughout the home.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is well suited to their individual needs and promotes their
comfort, safety, and well-being. The home provides a range of communal spaces where people can
socialise, spend time with others, and participate in activities. These include a main lounge, a
smaller lounge area, and a kitchen/dining room. All communal areas are clean, comfortable, and
well decorated, creating a welcoming and homely atmosphere. People have their own bedrooms,
which are personalised to reflect their individual tastes and preferences, helping to promote a
sense of ownership and belonging. All bedrooms have ensuite bathing facilities. The home also
benefits from an enclosed garden to the rear of the property, which provides opportunities for
relaxation, social interaction, and outdoor activities. At the time of the inspection, the garden was
undergoing renovation works to further enhance the outdoor environment and improve the facilities
available to people living at the service.

The service demonstrates a commitment to maintaining the environment and its facilities to a good
standard. Appropriate arrangements are in place to ensure the premises remain safe, well
maintained, and suitable for the people living there. We reviewed a range of safety records and
found that up-to-date certification was in place for key services, including gas, electricity, and fire
safety systems. The provider has effective fire safety arrangements in place. An up-to-date fire risk
assessment was available, and regular fire drills are conducted to ensure both people and staff
understand the actions required in the event of an emergency. The kitchen has been awarded a
Food Hygiene Rating of 5 by the Food Standards Agency, indicating very good standards of
cleanliness and food hygiene. Staff follow cleaning schedules to ensure the home is kept clean,
tidy, and hygienic throughout. Laundry facilities are appropriate for the size of the service and
support the effective management of people’s personal clothing and household laundry.

There are appropriate security arrangements in place to help protect people living at the service.
The home is secure and measures are in place to prevent unauthorised access to the premises.
Visitors are required to make themselves known upon arrival, and staff ensure that appropriate
visitor signing-in and signing-out procedures are followed. The provider also has suitable systems
in place to protect confidential information. Personal records and sensitive documentation are
stored securely and are only accessible to authorised personnel.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported by a care staff team who have the appropriate skills, knowledge, and
experience to meet their needs effectively. We reviewed a sample of staff files and found all the
required pre-employment checks have been completed, helping to ensure those employed are
suitable to work with vulnerable people. Following appointment, staff complete a structured
induction programme to support them in developing the knowledge and competencies required for
their role. Care staff are subsequently registered with Social Care Wales, the regulator for the
social care workforce. Training records demonstrate care staff receive training relevant to the
needs of the people they support, enabling them to deliver safe and effective care. We found the
majority of care staff were up to date with their mandatory and service-specific training
requirements, providing assurance they have the necessary skills and knowledge to carry out their
roles competently.

Care staff receive appropriate levels of formal support and consistently report feeling well
supported by the management team. Feedback from care staff is positive, with one describing the
manager as “brilliant, easy to go to with problems, very approachable.” Another commented, “The
manager is great, I have no complaints, she listens and is very supportive.” Records reviewed
during the inspection demonstrate care staff receive regular supervision and an annual appraisal,
providing opportunities to reflect on their practice, discuss professional development, and identify
any additional support or training needs. In addition, staff attend regular team meetings where they
can discuss operational matters, share information, and consider the needs and outcomes of the
people they support. These arrangements help to promote effective communication, good practice,
and a positive working culture within the service.

Governance and quality assurance arrangements support the effective operation of the service.
The Responsible Individual (RI) visits the service regularly and demonstrates good oversight of
service provision. The RI also routinely engages with people using the service and staff to seek
their views and experiences, helping to ensure their feedback informs service development. The
manager undertakes a programme of monthly audits covering key areas such as health and safety,
the environment, medication management and staffing. These auditing processes help to identify
any issues promptly and ensure appropriate actions are taken to address them. A formal quality of
care review is completed every six months to evaluate the service’s performance and identify
opportunities for improvement. We reviewed the most recent quality of care report and found that it
provided a balanced overview of service strengths and areas requiring further development.
However, we identified these reports could be strengthened further through the inclusion of
analysis of audit findings and significant events occurring within the service. This would enhance
the service’s ability to demonstrate learning, identify trends, and drive continuous improvement. We
discussed this with the management team, who acknowledged the benefits of this approach and
provided assurance the matter would be addressed.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
