Summary:

 Bridge House Residential Home provides care and support to people with and/or without dementia
 needs. It is a caring, supportive and well-managed service where people experience positive
 outcomes and good quality care. People told us they feel safe, enjoy living at the service and value
 the support they receive. Staff are experienced, know people well and have a good understanding
 of each person’s needs and routines. The service works collaboratively with healthcare
 professionals to promote people's health and well-being.

 Managers have established effective governance arrangements and demonstrate a commitment to
 continuous improvement. The provider maintains effective systems to monitor and maintain the
 safety of the environment. We saw evidence of ongoing investment in the premises which ensures
 people live in a comfortable, safe and welcoming home. Service development would benefit from
 providing people with regular opportunities to share their views and contribute to decisions about
 how the service is run.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People receive good quality care and support that promotes their well-being and helps them
 achieve positive outcomes. Residents, relatives and visiting professionals spoke positively about
 their experience of using the service. People told us they feel safe, happy and settled. Staff are
 described as kind, caring and responsive. We saw warm and respectful interactions between staff
 and people living at the home. People are supported to make everyday choices about their
 routines, meals and activities. The provider is to strengthen formal consultation arrangements to
 provide opportunities for people to share their views and contribute to the development of the
 service. Residents told us, “Can’t fault it, find staff good;” “Lovely here food is great” and “Very
 good indeed.”


 The service provides a varied programme of engagement. People are encouraged to take part in
 activities that reflect their interests and preferences, and work towards their personal goals. We
 saw examples of people accessing the local community, maintaining relationships with family and
 friends, and achieving individual aspirations. The service provider recognises the importance of
 activities and is looking to increase opportunities for people to promote their wellbeing and
 independence.


 Staff understand safeguarding responsibilities and take appropriate action to report. Records show
 safeguarding referrals are made promptly and relevant agencies are notified. Healthcare
 professionals told us staff know people well, which enables them to recognise changes in a
 person’s health and seek timely medical advice. These arrangements help people remain safe and
 maintain good health.


 People live in accommodation that promotes their wellbeing, comfort and independence. The
 environment offers a choice of communal and private spaces, enabling people to spend time alone
 when they wish or socialise with others. Bedrooms are personalised and reflect people's individual
 tastes, preferences and interests. Pen pictures outside bedrooms, provide information about each
 person's life history and help to promote their identity and individuality. We saw ongoing
 refurbishment throughout the service, demonstrating the provider's commitment to maintaining and
 improving the quality of the living environment for people.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good care and support that is generally person-centred and responsive to their
needs. Personal plans contain information about people's health conditions, preferences, support
needs and desired outcomes. Staff know people well and have a good understanding of individual
routines, choices and support they require. This helps to ensure people receive consistent care
from staff who understand what matters to them.


The service regularly reviews personal plans to reflect changes in people’s needs, wishes and
circumstances. People, their relatives and representatives are encouraged to contribute to reviews
and to share their views about the care and support provided. Records show staff make timely
referrals to health and social care professionals when people’s needs change or concerns about
their health and well-being are identified. Visiting professionals spoke positively about the service
and told us leaders communicate effectively about changes in people’s health.


Effective arrangements are in place to safeguard people from harm and abuse. Suitable
assessments ensure people’s liberty is protected. The service assesses and manages risks to
support people’s safety while promoting independence. This enables leaders to monitor outcomes,
review the effectiveness of actions taken and identify opportunities to further improve people’s
safety and well-being. Staff receive safeguarding training and understand their responsibilities,
including how and when to make referrals. Records show safeguarding concerns are reported and
monitored to help ensure people remain safe and protected.


Medication is stored securely and managed safely. Staff complete medication training and
competency assessments to help ensure medicines are correctly administered to people. The
service carries out regular medication audits to monitor practice and maintain good standards.
Although, the provider could strengthen medication management by implementing clear escalation
procedures when prescribed medicines are unavailable. This would ensure people receive their
medication as prescribed and reduce the risk of delays.


The service maintains suitable infection prevention and control arrangements. Cleaning schedules,
audits and monitoring systems support oversight of hygiene standards across the service.
Managers review audit findings and take action to address issues and improve practice. These
arrangements help to promote a clean environment and reduce the risk of infection for people living
at the service.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a clean, comfortable and welcoming environment that promotes their well-being,
safety and independence. Bedrooms are personalised to reflect individual preferences and help
people feel at home. Communal areas are well maintained and provide suitable spaces for people
to socialise, participate in activities and spend time with family and friends. People can access
indoor and outdoor areas that support social inclusion, independence and quality of life. Accessible
garden spaces enable people to enjoy fresh air and outdoor activities when they choose.

There are effective arrangements in place to maintain a safe environment. Staff use equipment,
including sensor mats and mobility aids, to reduce risks for people who are at increased risk of
falls. Regular servicing, maintenance checks and audits help ensure equipment remains safe and
suitable for use. Environmental risk assessments are reviewed routinely, and fire safety systems
and planned maintenance arrangements support the protection of people from avoidable harm.

Health and safety monitoring systems demonstrate the provider takes appropriate action to meet
legislative requirements and recognise good practice. The service has achieved a Food Standards
Agency food hygiene rating of 4, indicating good standards of food safety. The provider continues
to invest in the environment to improve outcomes and experiences for people living at Bridge
House. Records and observations confirmed ongoing refurbishment works, including improvements
to bedrooms, bathrooms and communal areas. Plans are in place to upgrade the kitchen. During
the inspection, the Responsible Individual (RI) provided assurance that Care Inspectorate Wales
will be notified of the contingency arrangements while the work is undertaken.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Bridge House benefits from effective leadership and management arrangements that promote
positive outcomes for people. Managers foster an open and transparent culture where people, staff
and professionals feel valued and listened to. People told us they have confidence in the leadership
team. Staff consistently describe managers as approachable, supportive and responsive. They
spoke positively about teamwork, communication and the quality of care provided. Healthcare
professionals provided positive feedback about management oversight and partnership working.
Managers demonstrate a commitment to continuous improvement and investment in the service to
enhance people's experiences and outcomes. Staff comments reflected a positive workplace
culture. One staff member told us of “A good atmosphere” with colleagues “Who work well as a
team.” Another said that this is the “Best job ever. We all work together.”

The service has established governance and quality assurance systems that enable managers to
monitor service performance and drive improvement. Regular audits of accidents and incidents,
care documentation, medication, infection prevention and control, and environmental safety help
managers identify trends and areas for improvement. The RI maintains effective oversight of the
service and demonstrates awareness of areas that require further improvement via routine visits
and Quality of Care Reviews. Internal quality assurance processes have identified a lack of
opportunities for people to formally share their views. The RI has recognised the need to improve
how people's views are gathered, recorded and used to influence service delivery.

The service employs an experienced and stable staff team with the knowledge and skills required
to meet people's assessed needs. Many staff have worked at the service for several years,
promoting continuity of care and helping people develop positive and trusting relationships.
Training, supervision and appraisal records show high levels of regulatory compliance, supporting
staff to carry out their roles safely and effectively. Recruitment processes were generally robust,
and records confirmed appropriate pre-employment checks had been completed before staff
commenced work. This helps ensure staff are suitable to work with vulnerable adults and supports
the delivery of safe, person-centred care. Overall, leadership and management arrangements
promote a positive culture, effective oversight and continuous improvement.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
