Summary:

 Breakaway is a short break service in Kenfig Hill, providing respite care for up to six adults with
 learning disabilities and a range of additional needs, including autism, physical disabilities, mental
 health needs, acquired brain injuries and complex needs.

 People experience good well-being outcomes. They are supported to make choices about their
 daily lives, maintain and develop independence and participate in activities that are meaningful to
 them. Staff know people well, provide effective emotional support and work responsively with
 families and professionals to promote positive outcomes.

 The care and support provided is good. Robust assessment processes ensure staff have a good
 understanding of people's needs, preferences and risks before support is provided. Care staff use
 personalised approaches and specialist guidance to provide consistent care, promote positive
 experiences and support people's health and well-being.

The environment is good. People stay in a safe, accessible and suitable environment that supports

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People achieve positive well-being outcomes because staff provide care and support that promotes
 choice, independence and meaningful engagement. We heard from staff of how they provide active
 support and “not to take a person’s independence away and encourage them to do as much as
 they can”. Relatives describe Breakaway as a much-needed service, highlighting the importance of
 the respite it provides and the positive difference it makes to both people using the service and
 their families. One person who accesses short breaks told us “I love it. I count down the days to
 come”.

 People have opportunities to influence their daily experiences during short breaks, including
 choices about activities, meals and routines. Care staff understand people's preferred
 communication methods and use a range of approaches, including visual supports, prompt cards
 and technology, to ensure people can express their views and participate in decisions affecting
 them.

 People maintain and develop skills that are important to them. We saw examples of care staff
 supporting people to increase their independence and confidence. People access opportunities that
 are meaningful to them, continue attending services that are important in their lives and enjoy a
 range of community-based and in-house activities. The service also recognises that some people
 value space to relax and unwind following busy weeks attending day opportunities.

 People are supported to maintain relationships that are important to them and can keep in touch
 with relatives during their short breaks. Relatives spoke positively about the relationships they have
 with the service. During the inspection, we observed a coffee morning which brought families
 together to socialise, share their experiences and provide feedback to help shape the ongoing
 development of the service. People also benefit from opportunities to access short breaks
 alongside friends, helping them maintain important relationships and enjoy shared experiences.

 Care staff know people well and provide effective support with their physical, emotional and mental
 well-being. They recognise when people need reassurance and encouragement and respond in
 ways which reduce anxiety and promote positive experiences. We heard several examples of staff
 providing valuable support during periods of difficulty for people and their families, resulting in
 improved well-being outcomes.

 People are protected by staff who understand their safeguarding responsibilities and respond
 appropriately and promptly to any concerns about safety or well-being. Relatives told us they have
 confidence in the service and feel able to raise queries or concerns.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support because the provider completes thorough
assessments before offering a service. Care staff gather relevant information from a range of
sources, including referral documentation, care and support needs assessments, families, day
opportunities, case managers, health professionals and their own observations. Managers also
consider the compatibility of people accessing the service during the pre-admission process and
when planning respite stays. People have opportunities to visit the service before their stay.
Professionals told us the service takes time to learn about people's likes, dislikes and interests to
help them feel comfortable and reduce anxieties.

Care staff know the people they support well and provide consistent, responsive care. People's
care and support plans benefit from detailed supplementary assessments and specialist
information. Personal information, communication guidance and behavioural support strategies
help care staff provide consistent support and understand what matters to people. Managers
identify opportunities to strengthen arrangements further by reviewing care plans prior to each
respite stay, helping to ensure staff have the most up-to-date information about people's needs,
preferences and any changes in circumstances. One person told us, “The staff know me well and
they know what I like”. We observed a knowledgeable and committed staff team who demonstrate
a good understanding of people's individual needs, preferences and routines.

Medication is managed safely through organised systems, audits and staff competency checks.
Clear processes support safe administration and oversight. Care staff recognise changes in
people's health and wellbeing and take appropriate action when concerns arise. Effective
communication with relatives and liaison with health and social care professionals helps ensure
people receive timely interventions and appropriate support.

People’s risk of infection is minimised through effective infection prevention and control practices.
The home has achieved a Food Standards Agency rating of 5 (very good). We observed adequate
supplies of personal protective equipment (PPE) and suitable cleaning equipment throughout the
service. Staff receive appropriate training and maintain communal areas to a good standard of
cleanliness. As people access the service for short-term respite stays, staff ensure bedrooms and
associated equipment are cleaned and sanitised between admissions to promote a safe and
hygienic environment.

       Environment                         Good



Breakaway consists of a pair of semi-detached houses located within the local community, which
have been joined and extended over time to provide a safe, accessible, and homely environment.
Accommodation is available on both the ground and first floors. The ground floor includes a fully
adapted bedroom and bathroom suite to meet the needs of people with reduced mobility. A lift is
available to provide access between floors; however, at the time of the inspection, it had been out
of service for over six months. We found this does not impact people's ability to access respite
stays, as suitable ground-floor accommodation is available and staff ensure people's needs can
continue to be met safely.

People stay in an environment that is safe, suitable and supports their needs. There are spacious
communal areas that promote social interaction and enable care staff to provide support while
maintaining oversight across different areas of the home. A second lounge area provides people
with the option of spending time in a quieter space if they wish. Facilities are accessible and
support people with a range of mobility and support needs to move around the service safely and
comfortably. The service benefits from a large, accessible outdoor area that offers opportunities for
relaxation, recreation and social engagement. As a respite service, the environment is organised to
ensure people can settle in comfortably during their stay and access the facilities that best meet
their individual needs.

The environment is secure. The provider completes a range of environmental checks and
maintenance systems help identify and manage risks. We saw evidence of an ongoing programme
of servicing for facilities and utilities, with regular safety checks completed. Records showed routine
testing of equipment and utilities is undertaken. Care staff complete fire safety checks and fire drills
regularly, and personal emergency evacuation plans provide clear guidance on how people should
be supported safely in an emergency.

The home is clean and tidy, with adequate space and storage available. Whilst the environment is
safe and suitable for people using the service, some areas would benefit from refurbishment and
modernisation. We noted worn flooring in places, and the entrance area was relatively small,
leading into a long corridor with limited natural light. This resulted in a less welcoming first
impression of the service. The provider was aware of these areas for improvement and had
identified actions through its quality assurance processes to refresh and enhance the environment,
and creates a more welcoming atmosphere for people accessing respite care.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported by a motivated staff team who work within a positive and supportive culture.
Care staff told us they enjoy their roles, feel valued and take pride in making a difference to
people’s lives. One person told us, “this is the best job I’ve had – it’s like a family”. Staff morale is
good, and managers are highly regarded by the team, with the service’s manager being described
as “caring”, “supportive” and that you “couldn’t ask for better”.

Safe recruitment processes are in place, including appropriate pre-employment checks and Social
Care Wales registration requirements. The provider maintains staffing levels that enable people’s
needs to be met safely and consistently. Care staff are supported to develop the skills and
knowledge needed for their roles through effective induction, training, supervision and ongoing
development opportunities.

The provider has established governance and quality assurance systems that provide effective
oversight of the service. The manager has oversight of all aspects of care provided in the home.
The Responsible Individual (RI) visits the home on a quarterly basis and seeks feedback from
people using the service, their representatives and care staff. This feedback is considered
alongside a range of audits, including environmental and care practice audits, to monitor the quality
and safety of the service. The findings are brought together within a biannual Quality of Care
Report, which highlights areas of good practice and identifies opportunities for further development
and improvement. Team meetings take place regularly and provide opportunities for
communication, reflection and shared learning. Care staff receive regular supervision and
opportunities to discuss their practice, development and training needs.

Managers demonstrate a reflective approach and recognise areas where practice and systems can
be strengthened further. They have led the service through a period of significant workforce change
and have taken proactive steps to recruit and develop new staff while maintaining continuity of care
for people using the service. Policies and procedures are managed at an organisational level
across Bridgend County Borough Council services and provide care staff with clear guidance on
how to support people safely and respectfully. We found some policies are overdue for review,
although the provider has recognised this and taken steps to address it. A designated member of
staff has recently been appointed to lead the review of policies and procedures, and we saw
evidence of a clear action plan being developed to support this work.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
