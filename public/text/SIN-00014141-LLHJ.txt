Summary:

 Plas Eleri Nursing Home Denbigh Care Group Ltd is registered to provide support to a maximum of
 thirty four adults who require nursing care.

 Well-being is good because people are supported to maintain their independence, make choices
 about their lives and achieve outcomes that matter to them.

 Care and support is good as people are supported by a team of friendly and professional care staff
 who provide a consistently good standard of care. They enjoy the companionship of others and the
 home’s varied programme of activities.

 The environment is good as it is suitably maintained, with systems in place to promote safety and
 ongoing improvement.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have good control over their day-to-day lives. People told us their views are listened to and
 their preferences are respected and accommodated. Care documentation mainly reflects how
 people wish to be supported, including details of their preferred daily routines, such as when they
 like to get up and go to bed. People are offered choice and flexibility in relation to meals, including
 what they eat and when they prefer their main meal to be served. Activities are personalised to
 reflect people’s interests and life histories, which are explored as part of the admission process to
 ensure meaningful engagement.

 There are appropriate systems in place to safeguard people living in the home. Staff receive
 mandatory safeguarding training and are supported by robust policies and procedures, ensuring
 they understand how to recognise and report concerns. The processes for monitoring incidents and
 accidents is improving. Records demonstrate that any restrictions on people’s liberty are applied in
 their best interests, with appropriate consultation and agreement from individuals, their families and
 relevant safeguarding authorities. Risk assessments are comprehensive, individualised and
 regularly reviewed to ensure risks are minimised while supporting people to maintain as much
 independence as possible.

 People’s physical, mental and emotional well-being is supported by care staff, who seek
 professional advice and input when required. Nutritional needs are carefully monitored, with food
 and drink choices tailored to meet individual dietary requirements, preferences and promote overall
 wellbeing. People are offered opportunities to participate in activities throughout the day, and staff
 record levels of engagement and enjoyment to help inform future activity planning and ensure it
 reflects individual interests.

 The accommodation provides a warm and welcoming environment, with a choice of communal
 lounges where people can spend their time comfortably. There is an ongoing programme of
 redecoration and refurbishment to ensure the home remains well maintained and fit for purpose.
 People’s mobility needs are supported through the provision of appropriate aids, including grab
 rails and a passenger lift, enabling safe and independent movement around the home.

 This service is working towards providing the Welsh language active offer. There is a small number
 of care staff who can speak Welsh with people at the service who prefer to speak in Welsh.
 Bilingual signage is provided within the environment, and the Welsh culture is respected as an
 important part of people’s identity.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive care and support that is person-centred and delivered in line with their individual
needs and preferences. Personal plans are in place for each person and are developed and
reviewed with their involvement, or their representative where appropriate. Records are updated to
reflect any changes in people’s needs, helping to ensure care remains responsive. Information
about peoples’ life histories, family relationships, and personal interests is documented,
demonstrating how these aspects remain important in supporting their current wellbeing. Risk
assessments are in place to promote people’s safety while supporting their choice and control over
daily living. However, some improvements are required to ensure that information is consistently
and comprehensively documented across all personal plans.

Care and support are delivered with dignity and respect. People’s needs are met in a timely
manner, supported by appropriate staffing levels, which contribute to a calm and responsive
environment. People benefit from positive interactions with staff, and we observed these to be kind,
respectful, and empathetic. People we spoke with are complimentary about the staff, praising their
hard work and expressing no concerns regarding their care. Mealtimes are well managed. The
cook engages with people to ask for their preferences prior to meals, and we saw that individual
choices are respected and catered for.

People are supported to access health and wellbeing services from external professionals,
alongside the care provided within the home. We saw appropriate referrals are made to ensure
people receive the support they need. Involvement from external professionals is clearly recorded
within care records, and personal plans are updated to reflect any advice or guidance provided.
Peoples weight is monitored and prompt referrals are made to the dietician when required. We saw
one person had gained weight since moving into the home.

Medication is managed safely and stored securely within a designated medication room. The new
manager is currently implementing a medication audit to monitor safe practice, and records
demonstrate that people consistently receive their prescribed medication. The service has a clear
and up-to-date medication policy to support safe administration. Staff are required to complete
medication training and competency assessments before administering medicines, helping to
ensure safe and effective practice.

The home demonstrates a good standard of hygiene and infection prevention and control. Private
and communal areas were observed to be clean, tidy and well maintained throughout. Staff
complete mandatory training in infection prevention and control, supporting safe practices within
the service.

       Environment                         Good



People appeared relaxed and at ease in their surroundings. We observed people moving freely
between private and communal areas. People told us they are able to choose where they spend
their time and said they particularly enjoy accessing the outdoor space. Bedrooms can be
personalised with items that are meaningful to them, supporting a sense of comfort and familiarity.
Communal areas offer a variety of seating options to meet people’s physical needs. Lounges are
equipped with items to promote engagement, including games, puzzles and sensory objects.
Therapy dolls are also available, which we saw provided comfort to some individuals. The
accommodation is clean, comfortable and benefits from good quality décor and furnishings. The
manager informed us there is an ongoing programme of refurbishment. Improvements completed
to date include new flooring in the corridors and the relocation of the laundry to a larger, more
suitable room. There are also plans in place to move the clinic room to a larger space.

The home is safe and well maintained. External contractors attend the service to carry out required
checks on fire safety equipment, electrical systems and other essential equipment. Routine health
and safety checks are completed, including monitoring water temperatures and fridge and freezer
temperatures to ensure safe practice. Staff report maintenance issues through the organisation’s
maintenance system, and repairs are prioritised and completed accordingly. Certification confirmed
that gas and electrical systems have been inspected and are compliant within recommended
timescales. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are readily accessible to staff in the
event of an emergency. Records demonstrate that regular fire drills are carried out, and
documentation confirmed that fire safety equipment has been serviced within the last year.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Quality assurance processes are continuing to improve within the service. The manager is currently
developing a range of audits, including care plans, falls, and medication audits, to strengthen
oversight and support ongoing improvement. We observed, and people using the service
confirmed, they feel very comfortable expressing their views through both informal opportunities
and more structured methods. This includes options for individuals to share feedback
anonymously. The RI has completed a Quality of Care Review report, demonstrating how they
identify areas of good practice as well as aspects requiring further development. The RI maintains
regular contact with the manager and visits the service frequently, ensuring good oversight of day-
to-day operations. However, future reports would benefit from a more in-depth evaluation of the
service to further strengthen governance and drive improvement.

The service maintains appropriate staffing levels to ensure people’s needs are met and they are
supported to achieve their personal outcomes. Care staff told us they were enough staff on duty,
and they respond promptly to call bells and adopt a calm, unhurried approach to delivering care.
Many members of staff have worked at the home for a considerable period, which contributes to
continuity of care. Staff told us they feel valued and supported by the provider. New staff receive an
induction and are supported to register with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. We found
that staff access a wide range of training relevant to their roles, delivered through a combination of
online learning, face-to-face sessions, and ongoing continuous professional development.

Staff supervision takes place regularly, and management has a plan in place to ensure their annual
appraisals are completed. These provide opportunities to review performance, discuss learning
needs, and support professional development. Regular staff meetings are held, ensuring the team
is kept informed of important updates and provides an appropriate forum for raising any concerns
and discussing practice.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
