Summary:

 People who have made Plas y Dderwen their home receive an excellent standard of care.
 Everyone we spoke with and had feedback from, have high levels of confidence in the service and
 feel their decision to use it was the best one for the individuals involved.

 Wellbeing is rated as excellent because of the relationships people, and those important to them,
 have with the whole staff team. This, together with the opportunities to do things that are important
 to them and that they enjoy, mean they are treated with dignity and respect and their voices are
 heard.

 Care and support is excellent because the team is led by a highly effective manager who has set
 and embedded the values for the service. The team is well trained and motivated to offer people
 quality care in line with their individualised and person-centred plans.

The environment is excellent because the service is maintained to a very high standard with
options for people to spend time in a range of areas both inside and in the garden areas. Due
regard is shown to make sure the environment is safe and free of any hazards whilst remaining as
homely as possible.

Leadership and management is rated as excellent because people have high levels of confidence
in the service. The culture and values are practiced by a staff team who are committed to quality
and seek ways to enhance people’s lives further.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 The relationships people have with those caring for them is a special feature of this service.
 Interactions are warm, friendly and supportive. The values have been set by the manager who is
 very visible throughout the service. Relatives are very complimentary about the staff team and care
 workers are sensitive to the well-being of relatives offering them support and advice when
 necessary. One relative told us “The care x was given was outstanding” and another described the
 care as “Spot on”. One person said “It’s lovely here” and others spoke of how friendly and kind staff
 are, enjoying a bit of banter and good humour.

 People are able to communicate in both English and Welsh. Menus are available bilingually and
 Welsh is spoken freely throughout the service.

 People are protected from abuse and neglect because of the staff training, supervision and values
 which have been set for the service. Visitors are required to sign in to a visitor’s book and must be
 allowed access by a member of staff meaning staff know who is in the service at all times.

 People live healthily and receive the care and support they need from a team of experienced and
 motivated workers. The atmosphere in the service is calm and well organised. Care workers feel
 valued and supported by each other and describe the manager as “Approachable”, “Fair” and
 “Supportive”.

 There are many opportunities for people to engage in activities with the dedicated worker described
 as “Outstanding”. Care workers understand the importance of meaningful engagement and support
 the activities worker to make sure as many people as want to are able to participate.

 The provider prioritises people’s well-being by making sure the accommodation meets their needs
 and enhances their quality of life. People appreciate the environment which is clean, light, free of
 hazards and well maintained.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



Personal plans are detailed and care workers find them helpful. A comprehensive personal profile
helps inform and direct care, and some people have a cloud board in their rooms which contains
brief information about the individual and how care is to be offered. Personal plans cover a range of
needs including nutrition, mobility and mood. Records show a high level of engagement with care
workers. There is a ‘resident of the day’ when the individual’s personal plan is reviewed, there is a
deep clean of their room, and they are assisted to do something special for the day. The electronic
records system has alerts built in meaning if a task is not completed on time a senior member of
the team is made aware and can rectify this.

People are protected from harm and abuse. Care workers know their responsibilities in relation to
safeguarding and are confident about reporting any concern they may have. They are certain their
manager would take the actions needed. Staff have training in safeguarding and there is an up-to-
date policy for staff to refer to.

People’s medication is safely managed. A check of stock balances showed the correct number of
tablets were available. A nurse and senior care assistant is always on duty, meaning people’s need
for medication can readily be met, and some care workers have been trained in the administration
of medication as well as witnessing the administration of controlled drugs. Medication is stored in
locked cabinets inside locked rooms. Records show medication is administered as prescribed and
the reasons for any ‘as required’ medication is clearly recorded. There were no gaps or omissions
in the records.

The risk of infection is minimised because the provider promotes good hygiene practices. The
environment is clean and there is a housekeeper on duty every day. Regular audits are carried out
which show a high level of compliance with the standards set. There are adequate stocks of gloves
and aprons. Hand sanitiser is available and the provider is going to improve the signage for this.

There is an understanding of the importance of good nutrition. People are encouraged to use the
dining rooms which are lively and sociable times for people. Tables are nicely decorated with linen
tablecloths and napkins. Show plates are used in some parts of the service meaning care workers
take a sample meal to people to help them make an informed choice. Menus are also available in
both Welsh and English. The catering team take pride in their work and recognise their important
role in people’s care and support. They have a good knowledge of people’s likes and dislikes and
special events and special diets are catered for. Meals are fortified using butter and cream and
there is a focus on people remaining hydrated. They are generally satisfied with the quality of
ingredients they have. Meals are made using fresh ingredients and there is minimal use of
processed food. The lunchtime meal looked and smelt appetising and those individuals who require
assistance were helped by care workers in a relaxed and friendly way. People and their visitors are
highly complimentary about the food describing it as “Lovely” and “Fantastic”. One relative
commented on how much better an individual looked since being at the service and put this down,
in part, to the good food they were getting.

People’s physical health needs are met. The nurse on duty offers advice and support to all of the
care workers and contributes to the care of people receiving both residential and nursing care.
Care workers appreciated the knowledge and wisdom of the nurses. A district nurse visits as
necessary and care workers, where possible, offer assistance to make sure people can attend
routine or emergency medical appointments. Care workers know how to recognise if a person is at
risk of developing pressure sores and they have the equipment they need to reduce the risk of this.
A podiatrist and optician visit the service.

The importance of meaningful activities and engagement is understood. A well-regarded worker
spends time with people both individually and in groups. There is a programme of activities and
people are encouraged and assisted to take part. During the inspection some people were
engaged in a chair exercise game and later a large group played a game of bingo, with some
visitors joining in. Most people and care workers think the frequency of activities is about right but
one relative feels activities on the weekends would be beneficial. Recent recruitment means this
will be starting soon, Some people spend time away from the service doing things they have said
are important to them, this includes going shopping and to a local agricultural market. People from
the local area are invited in to entertain people and this includes singers, musicians and animal
therapy. Records show significant efforts are made to find out what and who is important to the
individual and this is clearly recorded. The impact and outcomes of activities are evaluated by the
staff team.

       Environment                              Excellent



The provider demonstrates a strong commitment to making sure the building and all equipment and
services are maintained to a good standard that meets people’s needs. Accommodation is all on
one level making it easier for people to mobilise throughout the service. The risk of trips and falls is
reduced because hallways and other areas are free of clutter.

The service is divided up into three separate units, or communities, with each having its own
lounge and dining areas. In addition, there are other areas for people to spend time either with
visitors and others or on their own. The main reception is welcoming with tea and coffee making
facilities for people and their visitors to use.

Standards of cleanliness throughout are of a very high standard. The housekeeping staff feel part
of the team and take pride in their work, recognising their important contribution to people’s overall
care. There are some effective processes in place to make sure all areas are kept clean with staff
having clear work schedules to follow as well as being responsive to any requests from people or
the care team.

The service is very well maintained with repairs being completed in a timely way from a responsive
maintenance team. There is an ongoing programme of refurbishment which includes redecoration
and the replacement of carpets.

Pictures and other artwork are on the walls throughout the service. These are mainly of the local
areas as well as photographs of outings and activities people have enjoyed. These help to start
conversations and can trigger certain memories. Up to date photo boards help people identify
different staff. Bedrooms are clean, comfortable, light and airy. Most have some ensuite facilities.
Many are personalised with photographs ornaments and soft furnishings.

There are some outside areas for people to spend time when the weather permits. The large
enclosed area with new furniture and hanging baskets help to enhance the area. New lights have
been bought which will enhance this further. A smaller area is currently being developed which has
a beach theme. Both of these areas are accessible to people whose mobility is reduced.

Effective governance arrangements ensure equipment and services are serviced and maintained
appropriately.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



There are some effective and robust governance arrangements in place to monitor quality. Reports
written by the Responsible Individual (RI) and managers are comprehensive and reflective,
showing the views of people, those important to them and staff. Care workers, the rest of the team
and people hold the manager in high regard, describing her as “Amazing”. She is considered to be
approachable and helpful.

A range of quality audits are completed including nutrition, infection control and medication. These
show a high level of compliance with the standards set by the provider. Senior staff carry out visits
at night and over the weekend to make sure standards are maintained at all times.

Residents and staff meetings take place, with people using the service having a voice. A “You said,
We did” board shows how ideas and comments are acted upon.

Staff are appointed following a safe recruitment process. Files are easy to navigate and contain the
information needed including references and safety vetting. There is a period of probation and a
comprehensive induction for staff. They are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce
regulator. Training is a priority and this is provided by a dedicated trainer as well as more informal
guidance and advice from experienced staff. Records show staff are trained in a range of areas
including food safety, fire safety and choking. People, visitors and other visiting professionals find
the staff team knowledgeable and skilled. Care workers have supervision where aspects of their
work and well-being are discussed. They feel they get balanced feedback on their work to help
them with their professional development. Some care workers are doing additional courses to help
them in their professional development.

The provider recognises the efforts made by staff. This includes gifts given to the employee of the
month. This is chosen by people, visitors and colleagues. Long service is recognised and efforts
are made to redeploy staff to other areas where possible if this is requested. Staff present as
motivated and dedicated to their work. Staff feel valued by their manager and the rest of the team.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
