Summary:


 Westhaven Nursing Home is situated in a residential area in Rhos-on- Sea. People can access
 local amenities and the sea front.


 The well-being for people in Westhaven is rated as good. This is because the home has worked
 hard to increase activities for people and has had expert advice to ensure appropriate activities for
 all residents. Menu choices have improved; people have varied meals.


 The care and support are rated as good. People benefit from a stable staff group who know their
 needs well. Care is planned according to people’s needs and preferences.


 The environment is rated good. The home has a continuing maintenance plan in place to refresh

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. People’s personal plans have been
 improved, we saw they are more detailed and person focused taking note of people’s preferences
 and objectives. Communication needs are documented and people are supported to access
 information in a method they can understand which fosters choice. A nurse consultant was
 employed to review activities for people in the home. A new well-being approach has been
 adopted, “ways of wellbeing,” to support people with cognitive impairments, the programme centres
 on simple, sensory and comfort measures to enable emotional connection and relaxation for those
 cared for in bed and unable to participate in structured activities. We saw a well-being policy is now
 in place to guide care staff towards good practice. People have more choice regarding meals,
 giving them variety and good nutrition. Menus are changed seasonally to add interest for people
 and offer appropriate foods. Special diets are catered for in the home. People can access snacks
 as they need them and are given enough drinks to ensure hydration. The quality audit resulted in
 good responses from people and families regarding care in the home. People said they feel safe in
 Westhaven. A family member said, “I feel confident in the care they have in Westhaven, and I trust
 staff to look after them.” We observed good instances of care, care staff know people well and can
 anticipate their needs. Call bells are answered in good time. We saw care staff have good rapport
 with people and treat them with dignity and respect.


 People are protected from abuse and neglect. The Statement of Purpose and resident information
 booklets detail how to raise concerns, the Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW), contact details are also
 in place. People who are unable to advocate on their own account can access an advocate to
 ensure their rights. Care staff receive safeguarding training and told us they are aware of who to
 contact should they be worried about anyone’s care.


 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. People can personalise
 their rooms and add items of importance to them. Rooms are clean and tidy and compliant with
 legislation. There is a large lounge for people to socialise in or receive visitors. There is a dining
 area for people to share in a dining experience.

       Care & Support                       Good





People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes.
Where able, we saw care is planned in consultation with people. The service has worked hard to
improve care records; personal plans are now easy to navigate and read, and are person centred.
Personal plans reflect people’s history and preferences and provide a good pen picture of the
person. Personal plans contain sufficient detail to guide care staff towards the care needed by the
person. Personal plans are regularly reviewed to ensure people receive appropriate care as their
condition changes. Plans reflect people’s objectives and specialist needs and how best to reassure
people with anxiety-based behaviour. People experience continuity in care from a stable staff group
who know their needs well. Families feel communication is good from the home regarding their
relative’s care, they commented in thank-you cards, “Thank you for the exceptional care my mother
received ““Thank you for keeping me always informed ““Thanks for the care, kindness and
compassion.” Care and support are provided in people’s language of choice where able. The
service is working towards providing the active offer of the Welsh language and actively try and
recruit Welsh speaking staff. Care staff refer people who need specialist health advice in a timely
manner. Risk assessments are in place to keep people as safe as practicably possible. Falls risks
are assessed and falls rates are audited and reported appropriately to authorities.

People are protected from harm and abuse. The service works closely with local authorities and are
transparent in their dealings. Senior staff notify Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) regarding any
incidents that prevent the smooth running of the service and people’s safety. Safeguarding
incidents are reported appropriately to ensure people’s safety and rights.

People’s medications are safely managed. We saw medications are safely stored. The service has
a good audit system in place to ensure good stocks of medicines for people and have a robust
count of the medications coming into and leaving the home. Care staff have regular updates of
medicines training and have competency testing to ensure good and safe practice.

       Environment                         Good





People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment. The
home presents as clean and tidy and is compliant to legislation. Walkways are clear of obstacles
and trip hazards. The home helps people achieve their wellbeing outcomes by identifying,
mitigating and reducing health and safety risks. There is an ongoing maintenance programme in
the home to refresh décor and upgrade furniture, some rooms still await upgrade, however the RI
assures us these will be prioritised. There are areas for people to socialise or rest, people are given
a choice of where to spend their day. There is a food hygiene rating of 5 (very good), in the home
which is the best rating available and shows the home is compliant to legislation. People can
access the equipment needed for their care; these are regularly checked and serviced to ensure
their safe use. The lift is regularly serviced to ensure safe access for people from top floors. The
front door is locked for people’s safety and there is a signing in and out book for fire evacuation
purposes to know who is in the building. People have personal emergency evacuation plans,
(PEEPS) for their safe evacuation in an emergency, these are kept in an accessible place for
emergency services. Fire alarms and equipment are regularly checked, and staff receive fire
evacuation training. There is some parking space to the front of the building and emergency
services can have access to the home.


We saw the home has up to date building and contents insurance in place. Utilities such as gas,
electricity, water are regularly tested by accredited people and are certified as safe for use.
Electrical equipment is tested annually to ensure they are safe to use. Staff record weekly checks
on hoist slings, beds, equipment and window locks to ensure they are well maintained.

       Leadership & Management               Good





People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance, and oversight to ensure smooth operations and high-
quality care. We saw visit and quality reports which are in line with legislation. Reports have
improved and contain more detail regarding the environment, people’s well-being, and any staffing
issues. The quality report contains good feedback from families regarding the standard of care in
the home and people and care staff views are also considered. The manager has been offered
supervision; this is not always with the appropriate person. The RI is required to provide
supervision for the manager or with a nurse in a similar role to ensure compliance with the nursing
governing body. The manager and senior staff can access training suitable for their role. The
complaints process is effective in the home and people are supported to raise concerns. Senior
staff audit the service to continually improve service delivery and share their findings with the RI
and care staff. The Statement of Purpose is regularly reviewed and available to people and
commissioners. Policies are appropriate and in date and are available for staff to guide their
practice.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, qualifications and skills to meet their
care needs. We saw a selection of staff files which demonstrate that staff have checks in place to
ensure they are appropriate to work with vulnerable adults. Recruitment and retention are good in
the home and rotas match the staff numbers promised in the Statement of Purpose. The training
matrix has improved; nurses can access special interest training as well as mandatory training.
Mandatory training is up to date for care staff. Care staff receive regular supervision in line with
legislation to support their well-being and practice. Care staff told us training has improved and they
feel well supported by senior staff.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
