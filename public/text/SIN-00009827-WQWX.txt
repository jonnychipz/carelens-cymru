Summary:

 Bay Tree House is in a quiet residential area of Wrexham and provides accommodation and care
 for adults with personal care needs and living with dementia.

 At this inspection, we found many people experience good well-being outcomes, supported by kind
 staff and a warm atmosphere. Staff interactions are friendly, meaningful, respectful and in line with
 people’s plans. Positive relationships between staff and people contribute to a relaxed, inclusive
 environment. We also found safe care practices, appropriate safeguarding processes and
 improving systems for medication management.

 People live in a comfortable, homely environment suited to their needs, with personalised rooms
 and communal spaces supporting well-being and social interaction. The provider has effective
 health and safety systems and is taking steps to make improvements they have identified across
 the home environment.

 Governance and oversight are effective, supported by a positive culture, strong senior leadership
 support for management and a committed staff team. A new manager started during the inspection
 period and is already embedding positive improvements across the service. Staff told us they enjoy
 working in the home and feel well supported by the provider.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People benefit from living in a welcoming and relaxed atmosphere, where care staff interact with
 them in a genuine and engaging way. We saw respectful, calm and well-paced supportive
 interactions and conversations between care staff and residents. These everyday interactions
 support people to maintain positive relationships that are important to them and feel part of the
 community. People told us care staff are friendly and approachable, reinforcing this positive culture.

 People are supported to feel safe in their home. Care staff demonstrate confidence in raising
 concerns, and safeguarding processes are used appropriately to protect people from harm. This
 helps create a secure environment where issues are identified and acted upon promptly. Systems
 for gathering people’s views are not yet fully effective, however. Although people are listened to
 through informal conversations and via resident meetings, structured approaches to capturing
 feedback, including for those with communication needs are being developed. A new manager has
 been appointed and will be taking this forward.

 People can take part in planned activities and events, including visits from entertainers, organised
 games, singing and dancing, as well as planned social occasions in the coming months. These
 opportunities support people’s emotional well-being, inclusion and a sense of connection with
 others. People are asked for feedback about the activities they would like to see. In response, the
 provider has begun sourcing mobile petting zoos and therapy animals to visit the home.

 People’s day-to-day experiences show that choice and control are not consistently promoted for
 people. While systems are being introduced to support more “in the moment” decisions, some
 people told us they are not always offered options at the time they need support. Some people
 described meals being brought to them without discussion, or at times that did not always reflect
 their preferences. We saw occasions where people were supported to eat in lounge areas using
 low tables, which affected their comfort and independence at that time. These experiences show
 further opportunities for the provider to embed person-centred approaches more consistently, so
 people are actively involved in decisions about their care and daily routines. The provider has
 introduced mealtime changes, including show plates, visual aids and suitable furniture, to support
 “in the moment” choices and dignified dining in lounge areas.

 The service environment supports people’s comfort and sense of belonging. Many people describe
 the home as welcoming and homely. Some aspects of the environment, including worn furnishings
 and décor, affect how people experience their surroundings. Although this does not detract from
 the overall warmth of the home, they may limit people’s pride in their living space. The provider is
 taking steps to improve the environment, and to further enhance people’s well-being by creating
 surroundings that better reflect a high-quality, personalised living environment.

       Care & Support                       Good



Many people receive appropriate and effective care from staff who know them well and treat them
with kindness. Care staff support people respectfully and work together to meet people’s needs.
Much of the care we saw was person-centred, with staff using their knowledge of people to
anticipate and respond to people’s needs. However, some interactions were more task-focused,
and care staff did not always recognise the smaller details that can affect people’s experience of
care. The provider is aware of this and of the importance of recognising and addressing these
details to make care entirely individualised and enhance people’s experience and well-being. The
provider has identified a stronger management presence will help care staff build confidence and to
recognise more opportunities to provide consistently person-centred care. A new manager is due to
start following our inspection visits and will oversee this work.

Care plans are personalised and help staff understand people’s needs, routines and preferences.
Care staff spoke confidently about people’s support needs, and we saw they were familiar with
people’s preferred routines. The provider told us the new manager will further develop care plans
by getting to know people and meeting with them, their representatives and relatives. This will help
ensure care plans include richer detail about people’s life stories, preferences and how they wish to
be supported. Risk assessments are individualised, provide clear guidance for safe care and are
reviewed regularly, and when people’s needs or risks change. Safeguarding concerns are
recognised, recorded and escalated appropriately. Incident records show staff respond promptly
and take suitable action to reduce risks to people.

Medication management arrangements are improving. Following the most recent external audit, the
provider has introduced regular audits, refresher training and competency checks for staff who
administer medicines. This supports safer practice and reduces risk. Staff we spoke with
understood medication processes.

Infection prevention and control arrangements are effective. The service maintains cleaning
routines, staff use appropriate protective equipment, and the provider has arranged deep cleaning
of flooring, soft furnishings, sofas and chairs. People told us their rooms are “nice and clean”, which
reflected what we saw. The service has a level 5 food hygiene rating. Some worn furnishings, wall
decorations and scuffed paintwork reduce how well some areas can be cleaned and affect the
appearance of the home. The provider is taking action to address this and has employed a
contractor to deep clean furniture, and decorating work is ongoing.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is comfortable and welcoming, and which supports their well-
being. People told us they feel comfortable living at the service, and we saw that bedrooms are
often personalised and contain personal possessions, photographs, books and furniture that hold
importance for people.

The service has suitable communal areas where many people spend time with others or take part
in activities and meet with visitors. There are two large lounges with comfortable seating, sofas and
family-style dining tables. A separate quieter lounge, also used as a library space, gives people
another place to relax and watch movies. A café-dining area has patio doors leading to an enclosed
courtyard at the centre of the service. The courtyard is decorated with artistic murals of plants and
bicycles, providing a safe and pleasant outdoor space and outlook from the dining area. Staff use
the kitchenette in the café-dining area to prepare drinks and refreshments for people between
meals. People can also access a lawned garden area from the quieter lounge. This is secure, with
fencing and a locked gate to help keep people safe. These spaces support social interaction,
choice and a positive living environment.

However, we saw parts of the environment are tired and require improvement. Decoration,
furnishings and some fixtures show signs of wear, which affects the overall quality and presentation
of the home. These issues do not relate to cleanliness but to upkeep and maintenance. Systems to
identify and address environmental issues have not always been consistently applied. This has
resulted in delays in addressing maintenance and refurbishment needs over time. However, we
saw these systems are in place now, with a maintenance team employed to handle day to day
repairs, some minor decorative work, and complete routine health and safety checks. Records in
the home show the new systems are working well to identify issues and prioritise them. The
provider has plans to refurbish the service, and maintenance staff have begun working through a
prioritised list of areas needing attention, for example corridors where wallpaper needs replacing.
The provider is aware how important it is that any improvements are made without causing too
much disruption to people’s lives.

Despite the decorative wear and tear issues, the environment retains a home-like and pleasant
feel, which is important to people’s well-being. People have access to the equipment they need,
which is serviced and maintained appropriately. We saw staff receive training in health and safety
matters and this is regularly updated, including ensuring fire marshals and first aid trained staff are
on shift.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People achieve good outcomes because the service has positive leadership, a supportive culture
and appropriate governance arrangements in place. The provider is working to strengthen
consistency and sustain improvement, so outcomes remain good for people. Senior leaders
provide effective oversight and have taken prompt action to address issues identified in recent
months. Records of their oversight show they have driven improvements and supported the service
to respond to concerns, safeguarding issues, and day to day operational issues. The home has
been without a permanent manager for some time. Interim leaders have provided good consistency
and guidance for staff, and the new permanent manager will strengthen day-to-day leadership
across the service. Staffing arrangements generally support the effective delivery of care. Rotas
reflect an appropriate skill mix and good staffing levels, and staff are deployed in a way that
enables people’s needs to be met. Records and oversight systems are still becoming embedded
following recent changes in management. We found some gaps in the consistency of audits and
monitoring activity. Some recruitment and training records also did not always clearly evidence that
all required checks had been completed, or that learning had been undertaken in a timely way. The
provider has recognised these areas and has begun strengthening governance systems. This
includes improving the frequency of audits, enhancing oversight of training and staff competencies,
and introducing clearer monitoring arrangements. These actions support greater accountability and
will help ensure systems remain consistent, effective, and focused on improving outcomes for
people over time.

The service has a positive and supportive culture. Staff are welcoming, open and confident in their
interactions with visitors and visiting professionals. They told us they feel able to raise concerns
and are supported to do so. This helps issues that may affect people’s well-being to be identified
and addressed early. Leaders, including interim leaders, have shown appropriate oversight of
safeguarding and medication management. When concerns have been identified, they have acted,
including escalating matters to external agencies and developing action plans.

Records show the provider follows safe recruitment processes. New staff complete induction and
shadowing following appropriate vetting to ensure they are suitable for the role. The provider
routinely checks Disclosure Barring Service (DBS) information for permanent staff, as well as
sponsorship and right to work in the UK. Staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce
regulator where required.

Staff receive training to meet people’s needs. This includes practical in-house training and online
learning. Training is refreshed regularly or when new needs are identified. Staff told us they feel
well supported by the provider and by the interim management arrangements. They were positive
about changes being made to develop the service and said they felt supported with their own
professional development too.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
