Summary:

 Tegfan is an adult care home that supports people with complex needs to live as independently
 and meaningfully as possible. Since the previous inspection, we found significant improvements
 across the service. Particularly in relation to people's well-being, opportunities for meaningful
 activities, communication with families and the overall culture of the service.

 Well-being is rated Good. People benefit from positive relationships with care staff who know them
 well and understand their individual needs and preferences. People are supported to access
 meaningful activities, maintain important relationships and participate in their local communities.
 Relatives and professionals consistently described improvements in people's quality of life,
 engagement and overall happiness.

 Care and Support is rated Good. People receive person-centred care from staff who understand
 their needs and work closely with families and external professionals. Personal plans are detailed,
 regularly reviewed and support people to achieve their personal outcomes. Whilst some
 improvements are required to ensure daily recordings consistently evidence care delivered and

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive outcomes and many enjoy increased opportunities to live fulfilling lives.
 Since the previous inspection, the service has worked hard to enhance people's access to
 meaningful activities, family relationships and community participation. We found people are
 supported to take part in activities that reflect their interests and preferences, these included,
 shopping trips; swimming; breakfast clubs; family visits; gardening; baking and recreational outings.
 Professionals and relatives consistently described improvements in people's quality of life,
 engagement and overall happiness.

 People have developed positive and trusting relationships with care staff. We observed people
 appearing relaxed and comfortable in the presence of staff, seeking them out for support and
 reassurance when needed. Care staff understand people's communication styles, routines and
 preferences well, which helps people feel secure and valued. One relative told us, "The quality of
 the care and support my son receives at Tegfan has been greatly improved... he is much happier
 and settled."

 People are encouraged to have choice and control over their daily lives. Personal plans clearly
 describe individual goals, aspirations and preferred routines, helping staff provide support in a
 person-centred way. We found examples of people being supported to make decisions about
 activities; meals; community access and maintaining important family relationships. Improvements
 in communication between the service and families has increased confidence and involvement in
 care planning and review processes.

 People benefit from a culture that focuses on meaningful occupation and reducing social isolation.
 Feedback from relatives and professionals highlighted improvements in community access and
 opportunities for engagement. While there are occasions where activity opportunities and outcomes
 could be evidenced more consistently within records, this does not detract from the positive
 experiences people are experiencing.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes as
care is designed in consultation with people, their relatives and their care team. People receive
personalised care and support from care staff who know them well and demonstrate a good
understanding of their individual needs. Personal plans are detailed, regularly reviewed and provide
person-centred guidance to support people safely and consistently. We found plans reflected
people's communication needs; health requirements; behavioural support strategies; routines and
aspirations. Records showed people and their relatives are involved in reviewing care and support
arrangements alongside relevant health and social care professionals. This helps ensure care
remains responsive to changing needs and reflects people's preferences and desired outcomes.

People experience continuity of care, with staff consulting relevant agencies and specialists as
required. Care staff and management work positively with external professionals to help people
achieve better outcomes. We saw evidence of effective partnership working with a variety of
professionals. As a result, people receive support that promotes independence, emotional well-
being and access to healthcare. Family members spoke positively about the service's involvement
in healthcare appointments and specialist interventions. This demonstrates effective partnership
working and supports people to access appropriate care and treatment at the right time.

Care is delivered in a way which promotes routines and structures in line with people's needs and
preferences. Many people benefit from structured routines and meaningful opportunities that
support their development and personal goals. We found people are supported to learn daily living
skills, maintain family connections and engage in activities that reflect their interests. A Relative
described the impact of these improvements, telling us, "His life has been transformed from how it
was in the three years leading up to this point.” This reflects positive outcomes for people through
care that promotes routine, independence, meaningful relationships and personal achievement.

People are supported by staff who understand their needs. This was evidenced through the
positive interactions we observed and care staff's knowledge of the people they support. Relatives
also provided positive feedback, with many telling us that staff at Tegfan understand the people
living there well. However, improvement is needed to ensure records consistently support care
delivery. We found some inconsistencies in the quality and detail of daily recordings. Records did
not always clearly evidence the outcomes achieved, the support provided or how care delivered
reflected individual personal plans. We also identified gaps between some daily recordings,
meaning the chronology of care and support provided was not always fully evidenced. However,
these issues did not significantly impact people's experiences, and care continued to reflect
assessed needs and personal preferences. The management team recognises that daily
recordings require further development and has already implemented additional oversight,
guidance and auditing arrangements to improve recording quality and consistency.

       Environment                         Good



The environment promotes people's well-being, independence and quality of life. People's views
and needs are carefully considered by the provider when designing and maintaining the building.
Individual rooms are designed to meet people's needs and are highly personalised, with people
actively supported to choose their room layout, décor, furniture and equipment. The service
celebrates people through photographs displayed throughout communal areas, reflecting their
interests, achievements and experiences. People benefit from an environment that is welcoming,
person-centred and supports their identity and sense of belonging.

People have access to a variety of communal and private spaces where they can spend time
alone, socialise or welcome visitors. Communal areas are designed to promote independence and
meaningful engagement. The recently developed sensory room provides a calming environment for
relaxation, whilst the dedicated activities room offers opportunities for people to participate in
activities of their choosing. Bathrooms, shower rooms and toilet facilities promote privacy, dignity,
safety and accessibility. Garden areas are safe, accessible and welcoming. The provider has plans
to further develop the outdoor space, creating additional opportunities for people to engage in
meaningful activities and spend time outdoors. This demonstrates the provider's commitment to
continually enhancing people's environment and overall well-being.

Tegfan has sufficient resources and effective arrangements to support people's well-being and
achieve their personal outcomes. Management have worked proactively to create a more person-
centred service. Improvements to activity provision, behavioural support, communication systems
and the environment demonstrate a commitment to continuous improvement. The service has
invested in communal spaces, activity areas and sensory environments to better meet people's
individual needs. Professionals recognised these developments, with one describing the service as
"a very homely, clean and inviting space to relax in and to live at." These improvements have
enhanced people's day-to-day experiences and created environments that support meaningful
engagement and positive outcomes.

The environment is safe, well maintained and supports people to live healthily and securely. Safety
remains a key consideration throughout the home. The design of the premises supports people to
safely navigate the environment and access the facilities they require. Staff understand people's
individual risks and apply agreed strategies consistently to support their safety and well-being.
Regular servicing, maintenance and prompt repairs help ensure the environment remains safe and
fit for purpose. Security arrangements are in place to protect people whilst maintaining their rights,
privacy and dignity. Since the previous inspection, the provider has taken steps to strengthen
environmental safety and security, including improvements to door security systems and monitoring
arrangements. Leaders and managers had identified this as an area for development and had
already implemented measures to reduce risk. While these actions have had a positive impact, we

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management arrangements are driving positive changes across the service and
promoting a culture focused on improving outcomes for people. Relatives, professionals and staff
consistently spoke positively about improvements in the leadership, communication and overall
quality of care provided. One professional told us, "There have been very positive changes to
management and staffing, and this reflects in the support that people are now receiving." Managers
have established a clear vision for the service and are working proactively to improve people's
experiences and opportunities. They have developed stronger relationships with families, staff and
partner agencies, helping to create a more collaborative approach to care and support. Relatives
and professionals described improved communication and greater involvement in decision-making,
whilst staff spoke positively about the support they receive from managers. This strengthened
partnership working is contributing to better outcomes for people and increased confidence in the
service.

People are supported by a skilled, competent and well-supported workforce. Staff receive regular
supervision, annual appraisals and attend team meetings, providing opportunities to reflect on
practice, identify learning needs and contribute to service development. Staff spoke positively about
the support they receive from managers, who are visible and approachable. One staff member told
us, "I feel supported in my role and can always go to the management team if I have any
concerns." The provider has taken positive action to address a previous concern regarding Social
Care Wales registration. We found staff who are required to be registered are now appropriately
registered, providing assurance that people are supported by a workforce that meets professional
requirements. Staff complete mandatory and specialist training relevant to the needs of people
living at the service, and training records showed staff are up to date with their training
requirements.

Leadership and management use quality assurance and governance systems to monitor the safety
and quality of the service and drive continuous improvement. They demonstrate a good
understanding of the service's strengths and the areas requiring further development and have
responded positively to feedback from people, relatives, staff and professionals. One professional
told us management are "committed to make improvements and happy to accept challenge and
suggestions from outside organisations." Whilst the service continues to experience some
challenges relating to workforce stability and recruitment, the provider recognises these issues and
have taken action to strengthen staffing arrangements, and support retention. Feedback from
professionals, staff and relatives highlighted the importance of maintaining recent improvements
and sustaining a stable workforce. Leadership and management acknowledged these concerns
and offered reassurance that they and have a continued focused on developing a resilient and
sustainable service. The progress achieved since the previous inspection, alongside leadership
and managements willingness to reflect on practice and implement change, demonstrates the

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
