Summary:

 Castle Court is a residential care home for older people located in the centre of Chepstow. We
 have awarded a rating of requires improvement for the well-being theme. Many people experience
 positive outcomes because they are supported by a stable and caring staff team who know them
 well. People, relatives and professionals spoke positively about the support provided, the homely
 environment and opportunities for social engagement. However, people are not always supported
 to identify and achieve their personal outcomes because information about their life experiences,
 aspirations and interests is not consistently reflected in care planning.

 We have awarded a rating of requires significant improvement for the care and support,
 environment, and leadership and management themes. People are at risk of inconsistent care
 because personal plans do not always provide reliable or up-to-date information. Medicines
 management arrangements are not sufficiently robust, environmental risks are not always identified
 and reduced, and governance systems have failed to identify and address significant concerns
 affecting people's care, well-being and safety.
 Outcomes for people require significant improvement. As a result, we have issued five Priority

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 Many people experience positive well-being outcomes because they are supported by a stable and
 caring staff team who know them well and treat them with dignity and respect. People told us they
 are happy living at the service and spoke positively about the support they receive. One person
 described staff as “Very nice” and another told us “They look after me well.” We observed warm
 and respectful interactions throughout the inspection and saw people appear relaxed and
 comfortable in the company of staff. Relatives and professionals also provided positive feedback
 about the caring culture within the home. A visiting professional told us, “The team know the
 residents very well, the residents appear happy and well cared for.”

 People are supported to maintain relationships with family, friends and others important to them.
 Relatives told us staff know people well and take time to build positive relationships. One relative
 described staff as “Dedicated, kind and hard working”.

 People live in accommodation that supports many aspects of their wellbeing. Bedrooms are
 personalised with photographs, ornaments and possessions that reflect people's identities and life
 experiences. Communal areas are comfortably furnished and provide opportunities for people to
 socialise and spend time together. One person told us they felt “Very lucky to be here” and another
 said “The bedrooms are lovely.”

 People have opportunities to take part in activities and social engagement. Activities include bingo,
 games, quizzes and exercise sessions. One person told us they enjoyed “Playing bingo, darts, pool
 and connect four” and another spoke positively about “Sitting together and getting on with other
 people.”

 However, people are not always supported to identify and achieve their personal wellbeing
 outcomes. People's life experiences, interests, aspirations and important relationships are not
 consistently reflected within care records. This means care and activities are not always planned in
 a way that reflects what matters most to people. People may not always have opportunities to
 share their views about how the service operates, and opportunities to influence service
 development require strengthening.

 Outcomes for people require improvement because people are not always supported to identify
 and achieve their wellbeing outcomes. Opportunities to promote choice, independence and
 meaningful engagement that reflects people's individual interests, strengths and aspirations require
 strengthening.

                                    Requires Significant
       Care & Support
                                       Improvement


People and their families provided largely positive feedback about the care they receive at Castle
Court. People told us staff are kind, caring and supportive. One person described staff as “Helpful”
and another said staff are “Friendly staff good at their job.” Relatives also spoke positively about
the consistency of the staff team. One relative told us, “There is not a big turnaround of staff. Most
staff are long term, so get to know the residents well.” We observed warm and respectful
interactions throughout the inspection.

Many people receive support from staff who know them well and understand their day-to-day
needs. People are supported to access healthcare services when required and professionals told
us staff generally communicated well and acted upon advice provided. The service has developed
positive working relationships with external professionals to support people's health and wellbeing.

However, people are at risk of not consistently receiving care and support that reflects their needs,
preferences and personal outcomes because personal plans contain limited, inconsistent and
sometimes unreliable information. Personal plans do not always provide staff with sufficient
guidance about people's current needs, aspirations and important relationships. Information
relating to people's life histories, strengths and wellbeing outcomes is not consistently recorded. A
visiting professional told us, “Care planning could be better and completed more swiftly; care
planning has been sparse." This reduces assurance that staff always have access to the
information they need to provide consistently person-centred care and support.

People are not always involved in planning and reviewing how their care and support needs will be
met. Records do not demonstrate that care planning is completed in partnership with people, their
representatives and relevant professionals. This reduces assurance that care and support
consistently reflect people's wishes, aspirations and personal outcomes.

People are at risk because medicines are not consistently managed safely. Medication systems are
not sufficiently robust to ensure medicines are always available, monitored and managed in
accordance with national guidance.

Outcomes for people require significant improvement because personal planning and medicines
management arrangements remain insufficient to ensure people consistently receive safe, person-
centred care and support. These issues were identified as areas for improvement previously and
have not been addressed effectively. We have therefore issued a Priority Action Notice. The
provider must take immediate action to address this issue.

                                    Requires Significant
       Environment
                                       Improvement


People live in accommodation that has many homely features and supports several aspects of their
wellbeing. Bedrooms are personalised with people's own furniture, photographs and possessions,
helping them maintain their identity and connections to what is important to them. Communal areas
provide opportunities for people to spend time together, socialise and participate in activities and
recreation.

The service is located close to local amenities and people benefit from living within their local
community. Relatives and professionals told us the service has a ”Friendly and welcoming
atmosphere”. One professional described it as a “Friendly environment within the home" and a
relative told us they liked “The homely feel of the place."

People also benefit from access to an attractive garden area which includes a variety of planting
and greenery. The outdoor space provides opportunities for people to spend time outdoors, enjoy
nature and socialise with others in a pleasant environment.

However, people's wellbeing outcomes are not consistently promoted because environmental risks
are not always identified, monitored and addressed effectively. We found hazards within the
environment that had the potential to impact on people's health, safety and wellbeing. Some of
these concerns reflected issues identified during previous inspections and remain unsatisfactorily
addressed. This reduces assurance that systems for monitoring and maintaining the environment
are effective in identifying and managing risks at the earliest opportunity.

People are at risk of harm because environmental risks are not consistently identified, monitored
and addressed. We have therefore re-issued a Priority Action Notice. This is still having an impact
on outcomes for people and placing them at risk. The provider must take immediate action to
address this issue. Where providers fail to take priority action, we will take enforcement action.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


People benefit from a stable staff team and generally positive working relationships within the
service. Staff told us they feel supported by colleagues and value the continuity within the
workforce. One member of staff told us, “I enjoy working here" and another said, “I work with
amazing people." Staff also described positive teamwork and a shared commitment to supporting
people living at the service.

Staff told us they feel supported in their roles and described positive working relationships within
the service. Staff spoke positively about the manager's open-door approach and said they felt able
to seek advice and support when needed. However, staff do not always receive supervision in a
timely manner. This reduces assurance that staff are consistently provided with opportunities to
reflect on their practice, discuss professional development needs and receive formal support in line
with the provider's arrangements and within the timescales expected within the regulations.

Governance and oversight arrangements are ineffective and place people at risk. The provider's
systems for monitoring, reviewing and improving the quality and safety of the service have not
identified or addressed significant concerns relating to care planning, medicines management and
environmental safety. Although audits and quality assurance activities are sometimes undertaken,
they have not provided sufficient scrutiny to identify the issues found during inspection. This
reduces assurance that risks are consistently identified, monitored and managed effectively.

Inspection findings identified concerns which had not been recognised through the provider's own
governance arrangements. Opportunities to gather, analyse and respond to feedback in a
meaningful way require strengthening. This limits the provider's ability to evaluate the quality of
care and support provided and demonstrate how learning is used to improve outcomes for people.

Outcomes for people require significant improvement because the provider's governance, quality
assurance and oversight arrangements are ineffective and have failed to identify and address
issues affecting people's care, wellbeing and safety. The Responsible Individual has not provided
effective oversight of the service, and improvements have not been made or sustained where
concerns have been identified. We have therefore issued Priority Action Notices and expect the
provider to take immediate action to improve outcomes for people and reduce the risks identified.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
