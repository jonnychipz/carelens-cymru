Summary:

 Thompson Court is a small residential home, situated in the Canton area of Cardiff. The service
 supports adults with mental health needs, learning disabilities and autism to build skills, confidence
 and independence. Support is tailored to each person and aims to help them achieve their goals
 and move towards more independent living where possible.

 Well-being is excellent because people experience exceptionally positive well-being outcomes.
 They are supported to have meaningful control over their lives, achieve ambitious personal goals,
 maintain important relationships and participate in opportunities that enrich their lives.

 Care and support is excellent because people receive highly personalised, strengths-based
 support. Care staff promote independence, well-being and choice, whilst providing outstanding
 advocacy, proactive healthcare support and effective risk management.

 Environment is good because people benefit from a safe, comfortable and homely environment that

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People achieve transformational outcomes because care staff work alongside them to build
 confidence, maximise independence and turn aspirations into meaningful achievements. Through
 highly personalised and creative support, people develop the skills, knowledge and self-belief
 needed to make informed decisions, express their views and take increasing control over their
 lives. As a result, people demonstrate significantly improved confidence, communication and self-
 determination. They achieve goals which matter to them and progress towards greater
 independence and less restrictive living arrangements.

 A notable strength of the service is its commitment to positive risk-taking. Care staff consistently
 balance safety with opportunity, enabling people to access experiences which enhance well-being
 and independence. People have travelled internationally, attended family events abroad and
 developed greater community participation. Care staff focus on managing risks positively rather
 than restricting opportunities, enabling people to experience lives comparable to their peers.
 Managers continually review restrictions and seek opportunities to increase independence and
 autonomy.

 People consistently experience exceptional outcomes through support which strengthens
 relationships, cultural identity and community participation. People maintain important family
 connections, achieve meaningful personal milestones and develop strong social networks. Through
 personalised support, people access employment, volunteering, education and community
 opportunities, enabling them to develop skills, achieve aspirations and lead fulfilling lives.

 The provider demonstrates strong recognition of culture, identity and diversity. Care staff support
 people to maintain family traditions, participate in culturally significant events and maintain
 connections which are important to their identity. This includes translation of information, use of
 interpreters and support to reconnect with cultural heritage.

 People’s physical, mental and emotional well-being is enhanced through proactive support. Care
 staff identify concerns early, secure timely professional input and ensure people access the
 services they need. This has led to improved health outcomes, increased independence and
 greater confidence in managing health and well-being. This includes improved understanding of
 health conditions, access to specialist assessments and better daily functioning.

 People tell us they are happy living at the service and value the support they receive. One person
 said, “The staff are kind and have helped me to become more independent”. They added, “Staff
 support me to visit my family” and “They treat me with dignity and respect.” Another person told us
 they can “Laugh and joke with staff and discuss things openly”.

 People have access to information about safeguarding, complaints and advocacy in formats which

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People achieve exceptional outcomes because they receive highly personalised care and support
designed around their strengths, aspirations and individual needs. Care staff know people
exceptionally well and work with families, healthcare professionals and other agencies to help
people achieve meaningful goals. Evidence consistently demonstrates people experience care,
which is compassionate, outcome-focused and centred on what matters to them. Care staff take
time to understand people’s wishes, aspirations, strengths and risks. Care planning is highly
personalised and focuses on helping people achieve outcomes which matter to them.

We found evidence of notably strong healthcare support. Care staff identified changes in one
person's physical health and sought specialist intervention promptly. They also recognised subtle
signs another person's mental health may deteriorate and worked with professionals to prevent
relapse. Staff demonstrated exceptional commitment helping one person access specialist
assessment and treatment. As a result, the person experienced positive changes in their
concentration, communication, and independence in day-to-day activities.

We found partnership working to be a considerable strength. As a result, people achieve outcomes
including improved health management, strengthened family relationships, progression towards
independence and successful access to specialist services. A professional involved in a recent
transition described the process as having been handled with “Outstanding professionalism and
genuine care”, adding “Communication was clear, timely and proactive”.

People benefit from an extremely strong safeguarding culture where safety, rights and
independence are equally valued. Care staff take proactive action to prevent abuse, exploitation
and harm whilst empowering people to make choices about their lives. People remain protected
whilst building confidence, independence and self-advocacy and continuing to access opportunities
which matter to them.

Robust medication management arrangements promote both safety and independence. Medicines
are stored securely, records are comprehensive and audit systems provide strong oversight. Care
staff work proactively with healthcare professionals to support positive health outcomes whilst
encouraging people to develop confidence and independence in managing their medication.
People are actively involved in decisions about their medicines.

People live in a clean, hygienic and well-maintained environment where infection risks are
effectively managed. Care staff understand their responsibilities and implement safe practices
which protect people's health and wellbeing. We observed a clean environment throughout the
inspection and saw staff using appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE). Hand hygiene
resources, cleaning products and PPE were readily available, supporting safe practice across the
service.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a home which is welcoming and comfortable. People are encouraged to make the
environment their own and many aspects of the home reflect the people who live there. Bedrooms
are personalised, communal areas are warm and homely, and people have access to spaces
where they can socialise, relax or spend time alone. Bathrooms are modern, clean and suitable for
people's needs. During our visit, we saw an attractively decorated lounge with photographs and
items to reflect the people living at the service. The hallway includes photographs of staff and
information promoting inclusion and community involvement.

People benefit from an environment which promotes independence and supports them to pursue
their interests. The kitchen is bright, spacious and set up to enable people to prepare meals
independently or with support when needed. Information relating to healthy eating and smoking
cessation is displayed to help people make informed lifestyle choices.

The garden provides an attractive outdoor space with seating, parasols and a smoking shelter. One
person has developed their own gardening area where they grow food. They have been supported
to help maintain and improve the outdoor environment through planting flowers and tidying
communal garden spaces.

People tell us they enjoy living in the home and value the atmosphere the environment helps
create. Feedback we received from a relative described the service as “A calm, peaceful
environment” and highlighted the living areas were “Well presented, clean and tidy.” Feedback from
professionals further demonstrates the positive impact of the environment on people's well-being. A
professional told us the service had created “A homely environment that feels safe.

The provider has systems in place to maintain the premises and ensure equipment is serviced
appropriately. Records showed regular testing of fire alarms, emergency lighting, fire-fighting
equipment, gas safety systems and portable electrical appliances. People are supported by robust
arrangements which help keep them safe in an emergency. Fire drills take place regularly and
records demonstrate learning is identified and acted upon. Emergency evacuation plans and a grab
bag containing essential contact information and equipment are available to support effective
responses should an incident occur.

The provider has secure access arrangements in place, including locked entrance doors and a
visitor signing-in system, which helps ensure only authorised individuals enter the home. These
arrangements allow people to welcome family, friends and professionals into their home while
reducing risks to their safety and security.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from a service which is exceptionally well led and managed. Leaders have created a
positive, inclusive and person-centred culture where people are respected, listened to and
supported to achieve meaningful outcomes. The provider's values are evident throughout the
service and are reflected in the experiences of people, staff and professionals. Leaders are visible,
supportive and committed to achieving positive outcomes.

Feedback from people, relatives, staff and professionals is actively encouraged and used to
improve the service. Staff consistently told us they feel valued, listened to and supported by
managers. One staff member described having "Managers who listen and support staff. Care staff
described managers as people who "Give us the tools we require and sort out any problems
straight away." Care staff are encouraged to take ownership of areas of practice, contribute to
decision-making and share ideas for improvement. This has led to developments in activity
provision, engagement monitoring and inclusion initiatives throughout the home.

The provider has developed an exceptionally stable and skilled workforce, with long-serving staff
who know people well, providing consistent, personalised support. Significant investment in
training, development and career progression ensures care staff have the skills and knowledge to
deliver safe and effective care. Care staff described learning opportunities as excellent. Mentoring,
leadership training and planning for future leaders help build a strong and sustainable workforce.
New staff complete a comprehensive induction programme combining structured learning, practical
experience, supervision and mentoring. Care staff told us they felt well supported and had sufficient
time to complete training and gain confidence before working independently.

There is a strong commitment to openness, transparency, learning and continuous improvement.
The responsible individual (RI) routinely reviews the quality of the service through a wide range of
audits, quality assurance systems, feedback processes and oversight arrangements. Governance
systems provide clear oversight of incidents, risks, safeguarding matters, training, service
performance and people's outcomes. These systems are used effectively to identify strengths,
address issues and drive improvements, providing assurance leaders maintain effective oversight
of safety, quality and people’s well-being.

The effectiveness of leadership is further demonstrated through external recognition. Two members
of staff were finalists in the Great British Care Awards, the service manager was shortlisted for the
Care Home Manager Award, and the service achieved the prestigious Care Home Team Award for
All Wales. These achievements provide independent recognition of the provider's commitment to
excellence and positive outcomes for people.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
