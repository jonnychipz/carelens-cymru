Summary:

 People experience excellent well-being outcomes through highly personalised support which
 promotes independence, emotional well-being, meaningful relationships and participation in
 community life. People develop life skills, access educational opportunities and engage in activities
 aligned with their interests and aspirations. Family members provided highly positive feedback
 regarding the quality of support and progress achieved. Care staff know people well and support
 them to lead fulfilling lives.

 Care and support people receive is excellent. Assessments, personal plans, behaviour support
 plans, risk assessments and health plans are comprehensive, regularly reviewed and focused on
 achieving positive outcomes. Care staff have clear guidance to provide consistent, person-centred
 support. Effective partnership working with health professionals, alongside regular health reviews
 and quarterly multi-disciplinary team meetings, ensures people's needs are monitored and
 responded to promptly. People are supported to increase independence, develop skills and achieve

People experience excellent environmental outcomes because they live in a safe, comfortable and

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being outcomes because they receive highly personalised
 support which enables them to live fulfilling lives, develop independence, maintain meaningful
 relationships and participate in opportunities aligned with their wishes, interests and aspirations.
 Care staff demonstrate a detailed understanding of individual needs, communication preferences
 and personal goals. Interactions observed during the inspection were consistently warm, respectful
 and responsive. People were treated with dignity and kindness, whilst choice and control were
 promoted throughout daily routines.

 People receive excellent support to develop independent living skills and confidence. People attend
 an organisational education centre where they gain qualifications and develop practical life skills,
 including cooking, cleaning and household management. Positive outcomes were evident. One
 person now prepares meals and washes dishes independently, whilst another has developed
 confidence to access cafés and restaurants within the community. Care staff encourage people to
 make decisions about meals, activities, personal goals and future aspirations, promoting autonomy
 and self-determination.

 Emotional well-being is strengthened through positive and consistent relationships with care staff
 who know people well and understand how to support them effectively. People appeared relaxed
 and comfortable within their home and confidently approached care staff for support and
 reassurance. Achievements are recognised and celebrated, helping people build confidence, self-
 esteem and a sense of accomplishment.

 People maintain strong and meaningful relationships with family members and friends. Records
 evidenced regular visits, overnight stays, holidays and community outings with relatives. Many
 family members provided exceptionally positive feedback regarding support. One relative told us,
 “The place has gone to another level since (Manager) has taken over. No concerns whatsoever.”
 Another commented, “Place is outstanding and it’s the best it’s been in the last 18 years.” These
 comments reflected confidence in the service and the positive impact support has on people's lives.

 People enjoy a wide range of meaningful activities personalised to their interests and aspirations,
 including swimming, cycling, horse riding, football, hiking, volunteering, holidays and community
 outings. Activities promote social inclusion, confidence, communication, independence, physical
 health and emotional well-being.

 People are safeguarded through established systems and care staff who understand their
 responsibilities. Monitoring and oversight help ensure risks are identified and managed

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive excellent care and support because documentation, assessments and planning
arrangements are highly personalised, regularly reviewed and focused on achieving meaningful
outcomes. Records provide care staff with clear guidance to ensure support is delivered
consistently and in line with people's individual needs, preferences and aspirations. Documentation
demonstrates a strong understanding of each person's strengths, abilities and support
requirements, enabling care staff to provide responsive and person-centred support.

The people's guide contains clear and accessible information about the service, helping people,
relatives and professionals understand the support available. Information is provided in suitable
formats to meet individual communication needs, supporting choice, participation and informed
decision-making. Care staff encourage people to contribute to discussions about their care and
future goals wherever possible.

Comprehensive assessments provide a detailed understanding of people's physical, emotional,
behavioural and social needs. Assessments identify both strengths and support requirements,
enabling care staff to promote independence whilst ensuring appropriate support is available.
Records are regularly reviewed and updated to reflect changing needs and personal achievements.

Personal plans are detailed, outcome-focused and clearly identify goals which are important to
people. Plans provide practical guidance for care staff and promote consistency in the delivery of
support. Reviews demonstrated positive progress across several areas, including independence,
communication, daily living skills and community involvement. Records clearly evidenced
achievements and highlighted how support strategies were adapted to help people continue
progressing towards their goals.

Risk assessments and behavioural plans are detailed, proportionate and regularly reviewed. Risks
are balanced against opportunities for choice, independence and personal development, ensuring
people are able to participate in activities and experiences which enhance their quality of life.

Health needs are well managed through detailed health plans which provide clear information
regarding medical conditions, treatment requirements and ongoing support needs. People are
supported to attend routine and specialist healthcare appointments, and care staff work
collaboratively with health professionals to ensure recommendations are implemented. Health
records are well maintained and provide clear oversight of ongoing health and well-being needs.

Quarterly multidisciplinary team meetings provide valuable opportunities to review progress,
evaluate outcomes and ensure support remains effective. Involvement from professionals, family
members and advocates contributes to informed decision-making and person-centred planning.

       Environment                              Excellent



People experience excellent outcomes because they live in an environment which is safe,
comfortable, highly personalised and designed to promote independence, well-being and inclusion.
The service is unique in its layout, comprising a mixture of self-contained flats and converted barn
accommodation. This provides people with opportunities to experience varying levels of
independence whilst benefiting from the support and security of a well-established service. The
environment has been thoughtfully developed to meet individual needs and support people to live
fulfilling lives.

The home is maintained to a very high standard and provides clean, spacious and well-presented
accommodation. Communal areas are welcoming and furnished to create a warm and homely
atmosphere. Many people have access to their own communal areas, enabling them to spend time
with friends, participate in activities or enjoy quiet time according to their personal preferences. This
supports choice, control and autonomy in daily living. Throughout the inspection, people appeared
relaxed and comfortable within their surroundings and moved confidently around the home.

People's bedrooms and living spaces are highly personalised and reflect their individual
personalities, interests and preferences. Bedrooms contained personal belongings, photographs,
decorations and furnishings chosen by the people living there. These highly personalised
environments promote a sense of ownership, identity and emotional security.

The design and layout of the service effectively promote independence. People are encouraged to
participate in daily living activities including cooking, household tasks and managing aspects of
their routines. The combination of flats and converted barns provides opportunities for people to
develop practical skills within environments which reflect increasing levels of independence.

People benefit from a range of facilities which enhance their quality of life. The service includes a
dedicated sensory room which provides a calm and relaxing environment for people who benefit
from sensory stimulation or require a quieter space. People also have access to an activity room
where they can participate in hobbies, creative activities and learning opportunities. These facilities
contribute positively to emotional well-being, communication, engagement and personal
development.

Health and safety arrangements are robust and contribute to a safe living environment. Routine
maintenance checks, health and safety audits, fire safety monitoring and servicing of equipment
help ensure the environment remains safe and suitable for people's needs.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from exceptionally strong leadership and management arrangements which create a
positive, aspirational and person-centred culture. The Responsible Individual (RI), manager and
leadership team demonstrate a clear commitment to delivering high-quality care and ensuring
people achieve the best possible outcomes. Managers have established high expectations across
the service and promote a culture where people are valued, respected and supported to reach their
full potential.

The manager is highly visible, approachable and well respected by people, relatives, care staff and
professionals. Throughout the inspection, there was clear evidence of a positive and supportive
culture where people’s well-being remained at the centre of decision making. Management has
driven significant improvements and created an environment where people thrive. Many family
members consistently shared very positive feedback about the service and the positive changes
they had experienced.

Governance and quality assurance systems are highly effective and provide strong oversight of the
service. Regular audits, quality reviews, observations and monitoring activities are used to evaluate
performance and drive continuous improvement. Actions identified through quality assurance
processes are completed promptly and result in meaningful improvements for people living in the
home. Managers actively seek feedback from people, relatives, care staff and professionals to
ensure the service continues to develop and evolve.

Care staff spoke extremely positively about the leadership team and the support they receive. One
member of care staff told us, “This is the best management team I have worked with. They
genuinely care about both the people we support and the staff.” Another stated, “The manager is
always available, approachable and willing to help. We feel valued and appreciated.” A further
member of care staff commented, “There has been a huge positive change within the home.
Morale is high, communication is excellent and everyone works together.” Feedback demonstrated
a highly motivated workforce with confidence in leadership and a strong commitment to delivering
excellent care.

Recruitment, induction and workforce development arrangements are robust and support the
delivery of high-quality care. Care staff receive regular supervision, annual appraisals and ongoing
opportunities for professional development. Training records demonstrated excellent compliance
across mandatory and specialist training.

An external professional provided exceptionally positive feedback regarding the quality of
leadership and partnership working within the service. They told us, ‘The management team are

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
