Summary:

 People achieve good well-being outcomes because they are treated as individuals and supported
 to live their lives as they wish. People engage in activities and are supported to maintain
 relationships with each other and with family and friends. People receive good care and support
 from care staff who understand their needs and treat them with dignity and respect. We found
 people to be well kempt.

 People live in a good environment which meets their needs. Conifers is well decorated throughout
 and has communal space which people can use if they so wish. The outside space is safe. People
 have their own bedrooms which they can furnish to their personal preference, which offer privacy
 and personal space when required. Improvement is required in regards testing of the water
 systems.

 Leadership and management at the service requires improvement. There are policies and

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. The provider undertakes detailed pre-
 admission assessments and receives information from health care professionals prior to people
 moving into the home. This ensures the service can meet their needs. People’s personal plans
 focus on their likes and dislikes, the support the individual needs, and the person’s personal
 outcomes and strengths. Care documentation also gives detailed background to any support
 needs. This information guides care staff on how to meet people’s needs. Where appropriate,
 people or their representatives have input into the review of their personal plans and how it is
 delivered, ensuring care is appropriate and relevant to the individual. People’s independence is
 encouraged, and they are offered choice wherever possible. We saw and were told people are
 offered choice in regards activities, bedtime routines, where to spend time, and eating and drinking.
 We saw people can stay in their rooms if they wish or use the communal spaces, this was
 confirmed by people we spoke with. People are treated with dignity and respect. Care staff are kind
 and patient with people, and we observed many warm interactions. A health care professional we
 spoke with confirmed people are happy with the service they receive, are well kempt and the
 provider raises issues appropriately.


 People told us they feel safe in the environment they live in, which was also confirmed by relatives
 we spoke with. The manager has an open-door policy, and we saw people approach the manager
 throughout the day for advice and support. People told us they feel confident to raise any concerns
 with the manager. The RI also visits the service regularly and spends time chatting to them, offering
 the opportunity to give any feedback they have. Care workers have undertaken training to meet the
 needs of the people they support, including safeguarding training, ensuring vulnerable people are
 protected from harm. People have risk assessments in place to help reduce risks and to ensure
 they are kept safe.


 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. Conifers promotes communal
 living and encourages people to form relationships with the people they live with via group activities
 and events such as celebrating peoples’ birthdays. Visitors to the service are welcomed and people
 are encouraged and supported to receive visits from their friends and family. Students from a local
 school visit fortnightly, reading, chatting and playing games with residents.

       Care & Support                       Good



People experience good care and support which ensures they meet their well-being outcomes.
Care is outcome and strength focused and delivered in a way which promotes the development of
routine and structures in line with people’s preferences. Personal plans are reviewed regularly.
Personal plans are strengths and outcomes based and give a clear picture of the person. Care staff
follow these to ensure good quality person-centred care and support is delivered. People’s
outcomes are clear, and they are supported by care staff to ensure their outcomes and goals are
met. One person told us they receive regular showers and staff support them with this, another told
us staff anticipate their needs and are “amazing”. Throughout the inspection, people’s requests for
support were met promptly with good quality care being delivered throughout the day. We observed
positive interactions between people and care staff, who clearly know the people they support, this
was confirmed by those we spoke with. People living at the Conifers are well kempt. We spoke with
people who told us there are no issues with the laundry and they get their clothes cleaned quickly.
People, and their relative’s confirmed people were supported and spoken to appropriately and
treated with dignity and respect. Relatives we spoke with told us staff know their relative well and
do not have to refer to notes when talking about them and staff contact them appropriately.

People’s medication is safely managed, and medication is stored securely. Only competent care
staff can administer medication. There is a medication policy in place which outlines how to
respond to medication errors. There are Medication Administration Record (MAR) charts in place
which contain all required information and are completed correctly to reflect when medication has
been administered. There are medication auditing processes in place.


People are protected from abuse and harm. Conifers have a safeguarding policy in place, and all
care staff receive training in safeguarding. The provider understands the legal requirements of
caring for vulnerable people and makes referrals to the local authority safeguarding team when
required. Managers monitor safeguarding referrals. Applications are made to the Deprivation of
Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) team and are renewed appropriately. This ensures that placements at
the Conifers are lawful where people lack the mental capacity to make decisions around their care
and accommodation needs.

       Environment                         Good



Conifers is a care home, located in a residential area in Ruabon, a few miles from Wrexham.

People live in a suitable environment. Conifers is set over two floors with a stair lift provided. The
home is warm, welcoming, and decorated nicely throughout, benefiting from communal spaces that
people can access as they wish. There is enough space for people to spend time alone, with other
residents or with visitors. There are bathrooms and toilets throughout the service which are clean
and in good working order and equipped with specialist equipment for those who may need to use
it. Welsh signage is provided. Externally there are tables and chairs people use. The area is
secure.

People have their own bedrooms which are clean, bright and benefit from en-suite facilities. People
are encouraged to personalise their rooms to make them as homely and comfortable as possible.
We saw that bedrooms have appropriate seating and a table for comfort, along with a call bell in
case they need help.

Conifers does not always provide a safe environment in which people can live. On attending the
home, we found the front door was locked, secure and our ID was checked. We were asked to sign
in so that visitors can be monitored for fire safety purposes. Managers undertake regular
environmental checks, however these are not recorded, though managers have told us this will be
undertaken in future. Appropriate servicing of appliances is not always in place to ensure risks to
people’s health and safety are rectified. Testing of systems such as gas, electrics, and fire safety,
are completed regularly, however we found testing and flushing of water systems is not always
undertaken as regularly as required. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the provider
to make improvements.

Investment in the Conifers is ongoing with a new fire alarm system being purchased and installed
recently, and new flooring in the corridor being laid. Painting of some bedrooms has been
undertaken and decoration of communal areas is ongoing.

Conifers is kept clean throughout, both the communal areas and people’s rooms. Infection
prevention and control procedures are conducted well. The Food Standards Agency have awarded
the service a level 5 rating, which is very good and the highest rating achievable.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



People are not always supported to achieve their personal outcomes because the service provider
does not have effective governance and oversight to ensure smooth operations. Conifers benefits
from a manager who oversees the day to day running of the service, however the manager is not
registered with the appropriate professional body, though this is now being undertaken. This is an
area for improvement, and we expect the provider to make improvements. The RI visits the service
in line with regulatory requirements and produces a report following each visit, however this report
does not show that stakeholders are consulted formally, that the RI monitors the performance of
the service or dip samples any records. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the
provider to make improvements. There are policies and procedures in place for the smooth running
of the service and to guide care staff on what is expected of them. All policies are detailed and
have recently been reviewed and updated where necessary. One member of staff told us that staff
work well as a team. Other staff, people living at the service, and visitors gave positive feedback
about the manager, saying they are approachable and take issues seriously. Systems in place to
monitor quality are not effective and require improvement. Managers audits of different areas of the
service are undertaken though are not formally recorded. An area the manager advised us would
be rectified. The quality-of-care review reports are completed every six months; however,
improvements are required to ensure the service provider has suitable arrangements in place to
assess, monitor and improve the quality and safety of the service as set out in the regulations. This
is an area for improvement, and we expect the provider to make improvements.


People are supported by care staff who are safely recruited, well trained and professionally
registered. Pre employment checks are conducted before a person begins working at the service,
which includes a disclosure and barring service (DBS) check and obtaining appropriate references.
This is to ensure potential staff are safe and appropriate to work with adults at risk. Care staff
complete an induction, and all staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator.
We reviewed training records and found care staff complete mandatory training in several areas
including Dementia care, moving and handling and medication. Care staff receive regular
supervision and appraisals. This helps identify any training needs, performance, and concerns to
support ongoing development and ensure people receive the support they require. Care staff are
employed in appropriate numbers; this was confirmed by records we saw, staffing numbers on the
days of inspection, and what stakeholders told us. Regular meetings are held with staff.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
