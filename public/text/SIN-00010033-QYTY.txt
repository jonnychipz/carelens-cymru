        Well-being                           Good




       Care & Support                       Good



       Environment                         Good



        Leadership & Management               Good





Summary:

 People who have made Dol y Felin their home receive a good standard of care. Everyone we
 spoke with has high levels of confidence in the service.

 People experience good wellbeing because of the relationships they have with the people
 important to them and the staff team. This, together with the range of opportunities to do things that
 matter to them mean their days are generally meaningful and they are treated with dignity and
 respect.

 Care and support is rated as good because the team is led by an experienced and dedicated
 manager who is supported by a deputy and team of staff who share the values of the service and
 strive to provide individualised and person-centred care and support. Detailed personal plans help
 to inform and direct care and the planned improvements will enhance this further.

The environment is rated as good because standards of cleanliness are high and whilst some
areas would benefit from updating it is mostly as homely and comfortable as possible.

Leadership and management is rated as good because the values set by the manager are
embedded into practice. The manager is very visible throughout the service and has a good
knowledge of the people cared for, their families and the staff team. Some improvements to the
internal auditing processes would enhance this further.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 The relationships people have with those caring for them is a special feature of this service.
 Relatives have very high levels of confidence in the staff team. One told us, “Staff are very very
 good… X is getting better care than I could give her” and another said, “The staff are very friendly.”
 People living at the service are just as positive with one telling us, “The staff are marvellous” and
 another described the staff as “Very friendly.”


 There are opportunities for people to do things that matter to them and they enjoy. This includes
 helping out in the garden and greenhouse as well as activities inside the service, including crafts,
 baking and quizzes. Residents of the local area come in to spend time with people including
 singers and musicians. There are photograph albums which capture how people engage with these
 activities. Most people think there is enough going on within the service but some people told us
 they would like to see more activities and opportunities for engagement taking place.


 There are opportunities for people to speak in Welsh if they choose. Personal plans contain
 information about the people’s language of choice and there are some Welsh speaking staff and
 others who are either learning or have some basic knowledge. Some people we spoke with
 appreciated the ability to speak in Welsh. Some information and signage is available bilingual

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. This is because there are good security
 measures to prevent unauthorised access to the home and its grounds; staff know their
 responsibilities in relation to safeguarding and they have completed training in safeguarding.
 People are treated with dignity and respect because workers understand they are working in the
 people’s home and the trust and responsibility placed on them. The recruitment processes are
 robust and safe meaning people appointed have the right skills, knowledge and aptitude for the job.
 Visitors are welcomed to the service and have a good rapport with the staff team.


 The environment contributes, to some degree to people’s well-being. Whilst parts of the service
 would benefit from some redecoration and updating, people describe the service as “Spotless.” The
 staff team have tried to make the service as homely as possible and are considering ways to
 enhance this further.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the care and support they need. The staff team are motivated, friendly and caring.
Care records are comprehensive and care workers find them helpful. Each person has an
informative personal profile which sets out what and who is important to them. Also other helpful
information including how people like to spend their time, what help they need and any
communication needs. Daily entries are informative and mostly person centred. Personal plans are
reviewed regularly and there is clear evidence people or their representatives have contributed to
their plans. We saw the efforts made by the staff team to celebrate people’s special events and a
relative told us “I can honestly say the staff always go above and beyond. X loves it here. She is
treated like a Queen.”.

There is an understanding of the importance of good nutrition. Most meals are made using fresh
ingredients, with little reliance on processed food. The catering team are satisfied with the quality of
ingredients they have and cupboards, fridges and freezers are well stocked. The catering team
take pride in their work and are responsible for serving people their meals meaning they have a
good knowledge of people’s likes dislikes and any special requirements. Special events are
celebrated and special diets catered for. The efforts made by the catering team are appreciated by
the rest of the team, people and their relatives. One person described the food they are served as
“Marvellous.”

Food is available outside of mealtimes and specialist advice is sought from both the dieticians and
speech & language therapists as necessary and their advice is followed by the team.

Physical health needs are met with people having dental and opticians’ appointments. The
manager told us they have a very good relationship with the local GP practice which is responsive
to any requests. A visiting health professional was very complimentary about the service,
describing it as “Very welcoming, very friendly.” Interventions they ask to be done are carried out by
care workers as requested. Some people have been assessed as being at risk of developing
pressure damage and are, therefore assisted to be repositioned on a regular basis. Care workers
are confident this is done but there are some gaps in the records. The manager agreed to discuss
this with the care workers to make sure the records are a full and accurate record of people’s care.

People are safe and protected from harm and abuse. Visitors are required to sign into a visitor’s
book meaning staff know who is in the service at all times. All staff spoken with know their
responsibilities to report any safeguarding concerns and there is an up-to-date safeguarding policy
for staff to refer to as well as regular safeguarding training. Referrals are made to the adult
protection team as needed. Whilst care workers have had no reason to raise any safeguarding
concerns, they are confident they know how to and are also confident the manager would deal with
any concerns correctly.

People’s medication is safely managed. A check of controlled drugs showed there are the correct
number of tablets available and accurate records are maintained. Some care workers have been
trained in the safe administration of medicines and there is always at least one member of staff on

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that meets their needs. There is a programme of ongoing
refurbishment and redecoration and whilst some areas are tired and dated, it is reasonably homely
and comfortable. People we spoke with and their relatives, are wholly satisfied with the
environment they live in.

Bedrooms are personalised with items of furniture, photographs, ornaments and soft furnishings.
None of the bedrooms have ensuite facilities but there are adequate numbers of bathrooms and
toilets, some of which have been updated.

Signs on bedrooms doors help with orientation and also contain some helpful information about the
individual including what and who is important to them.

There are communal lounges and a dining room for people to spend time with others. An additional
smaller lounge is a place where people can spend quieter time or meet with relatives.

Improvements have been made to the outside areas with further developments planned. A new
greenhouse is being used to grow vegetables and raised beds are planted up with colourful
flowers. The gardens are generally well maintained and accessible for people.

The kitchen has been awarded the maximum score of five by the Food Standards Agency. This
means the facilities in the kitchen were considered to be very good. Catering staff say they have
the equipment they need and everything is in good working order.

There are some effective processes in place to make sure equipment and services are maintained
and in good working order, including fire safety equipment, moving and handling equipment, beds
and water temperatures.

       Leadership & Management               Good



There are some effective and robust governance arrangements in place to monitor quality. The
Responsible Individual has good oversight of the service and visits regularly. The manager has
regular contact with them. The reports written are comprehensive and focus on quality and service
improvements. A range of audits are completed to further monitor quality but some greater rigor
would enhance the value of these further.

Staff are appointed following a safe recruitment process. Files contain the required information
including references and security checks. All staff have a period of induction before working
independently. There is some use of agency workers but these are staff who know the service and
work there regularly. There is a strong focus on training and records show training compliance is
largely up to date with some training planned. Care workers find the training helpful and they are
able to request additional training. Care workers are appropriately supervised and records show
supervision is largely up to date. Care workers consider they get balanced feedback on their work
to help them in their ongoing professional development.

The staff team work well together and respect and value everyone who works at the service. This
includes the housekeeping, catering, maintenance staff and volunteers who all contribute to
people’s care and support. Staff find the manager to be responsive and helpful and they are able to
talk about any ideas or concerns they have. We saw some friendly and supportive banter between
staff which shows a rapport has been built throughout the team.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
