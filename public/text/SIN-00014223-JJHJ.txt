Summary:

 Bod Hyfryd provides nursing care and dementia nursing care to a maximum of 40 people. It is
 located in Flint in Flintshire. People live in a generally clean environment; bedrooms are
 personalised and there is evidence of some activities taking place as well as links with the local
 community. People say staff are kind and compassionate. The provider has invested in the
 environment, and we found high training compliance and established systems for governance in
 place.

 However, we found well-being requires significant improvement as we found people cannot always
 exercise choice and control over their lives and do not always experience timely support when they
 need assistance. Care and support require significant improvement as people do not consistently
 receive care and support that meets their assessed needs.

 Environment requires significant improvement because although the premises is generally clean
 and well-maintained we found health and safety concerns were not always identified and

Findings:

                                    Requires Significant
        Well-being
                                        Improvement


 People do not consistently experience positive well-being outcomes because their opportunities to
 exercise choice and consistently and to take part in meaningful activities are limited, particularly if
 they can’t access communal areas or leave the building. Menus offer a range of meal choices and
 alternatives, and care records contain information about their life histories, preferences and
 important relationships. We saw evidence that outings, sensory activities, therapy dog visits and
 community projects have taken place. However, during inspection we observed most people
 remained in their bedrooms throughout the day and there is very little meaningful interaction taking
 place. One person told us they usually stayed in their room because “nobody is in the lounge
 anyway; I may as well be here”. People and relatives are not always informed activities are taking
 place.

 People are not always able to exercise choice and control over their daily lives. Care plans contain
 information about people's preferences, and risk assessments cover areas including mobility,
 nutrition, continence, oral health and evacuation planning. The provider is working towards the
 Welsh language active offer and has arrangements in place to identify and record people’s
 language preferences so these can be reflected in care and support. However, feedback from
 people indicated care is not always delivered in a way that promotes personal choice. One person
 told us, “I didn't really fancy one, but if I didn't go, I don't know when I would next have one”, when
 discussing having a shower. Another person said, “I've never had to ask they just say, ‘You're
 going for a shower today, come on’.” Records show routines are not always directed by people's
 preferences and choices.

 People do not always experience timely support when they need assistance. During inspection we
 found some people did not have access to their call bell. One person told us, “I shout if I need help
 and they do come quickly if I shout.” Another person said, “I say I want to go to the toilet; they say
 they'll get someone and then I think, where is everyone?” and reported they were still waiting for a
 cup of tea because they believed staff had forgotten them. Relatives also raised concerns
 regarding delays in responding to requests for assistance, including support with personal care,
 eating and drinking.

 People are not consistently supported to achieve positive well-being outcomes because staffing
 deployment and arrangements appear to be affecting their daily experiences. Family members,
 residents have raised concerns regarding staffing arrangements and the impact this has on
 people's opportunities for engagement, timely support and overall quality of life. As a result, people
 are not consistently experiencing the level of well-being, stimulation, choice and personalised
 support expected.

                                    Requires Significant
       Care & Support
                                       Improvement


People do not consistently receive care and support that meets their assessed needs and personal
outcomes. Care records contain detailed personal information, life histories, risk assessments and
specialist guidance relating to nutrition, mobility, continence, falls, oral health and skin integrity. We
saw evidence of multidisciplinary involvement, including dietetic, speech and language therapy, GP
and tissue viability input where required. We saw evidence of referrals to healthcare professionals,
management reviews of clinical concerns and implementation of pain assessment tools. Family
compliments described staff as kind, caring and respectful, particularly during end-of-life care. We
found repositioning requirements are met for those at risk of skin damage.

However, care records were not always accurate or reflective of people's current needs. We found
inconsistent dietary and weight monitoring information recorded across care plans and daily
records, and contradictory records regarding personal care. These inaccuracies had not been
identified through routine review processes. We found people assessed as requiring
encouragement, supervision and monitoring during meals were not provided with the level of
support required. Relative told us they often have to support their loved ones with eating their
meals due to low staffing levels. Outcomes for people requires significant improvement because
support is not always delivered in accordance with assessed needs and we have therefore issued a
priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address this issue.

People's medicines are not consistently managed safely. The service has implemented electronic
medication systems, PainChek assessments and medication audits, and staff have completed
medication training. However, outcomes for people require significant improvement because we
found medication competencies and training had not been retaken for staff involved in a significant
medication error. We also found required transdermal patch monitoring is not consistently
completed, and patch locations are not always recorded in accordance with the provider’s own
policy. We have therefore issued a priority action notice, and the provider must take immediate
action to address this issue.

The service has suitable arrangements in place to support infection prevention and control. Staff
training records demonstrated high compliance with infection prevention and control training and
the provider had detailed policies covering hand hygiene, personal protective equipment, cleaning
arrangements and infection control governance. We noted the home is generally clean and free
from unpleasant odours during our visit.

                                    Requires Significant
       Environment
                                       Improvement


The environment at the service provides mostly positive outcomes for people living there. The
home is generally clean, tidy and well-maintained. Bedrooms we visited are personalised with
photographs, flowers and personal possessions, helping people maintain a sense of identity and
familiarity. The premises are secure, with keypad entry systems and appropriate window restrictors
in place, and we saw people accessing the garden area. The provider is investing in the
environment and supplied evidence of a programme of refurbishment and improvement, including
replacement flooring, bathroom upgrades, windows and safety-related works. We also reviewed
evidence of statutory servicing and certification for equipment and systems, including gas safety,
water hygiene monitoring, Legionella testing, lift servicing and lifting equipment inspections.
Individual Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are in place and provided clear
information about people's mobility needs and evacuation requirements.

Despite these strengths, people are not consistently protected from avoidable risks within the
environment. During the inspection we observed trailing cables and extension leads in some
rooms, and found flammable emollient creams are not always stored safely and appropriately. We
found sensor equipment is not always positioned correctly for people identified as being at high risk
of falls, and several people did not have access to their call bell. Inspectors found bathrooms being
used to store equipment rather than being available for its intended purpose. Multiple fire doors
throughout the home were being held open using inappropriate objects including chairs, fans and
heaters. This practice was observed in several locations and appeared routine rather than isolated.
Although fire risk assessments and servicing records had been completed, inspection findings
demonstrated that risks within the environment were not always being identified and managed
effectively in practice. Outcomes for people require significant improvement as the provider has not
ensured the environment consistently supports people's safety and well-being and reduces the risk
of avoidable harm and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take
immediate action to address this issue.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


The provider has systems in place to support the operation of the service. Recruitment records
sampled contained appropriate pre-employment checks, including references, identity verification
and Disclosure and Barring Service checks. Training compliance across the workforce was high
and staff meetings, governance meetings and Responsible Individual visits are taking place. The
provider submitted evidence of regular audits, quality assurance activity, supervision arrangements,
management oversight and management complete daily walks around the home. Following
concerns identified during inspection, the provider responded promptly and provided a detailed
action plan, additional management support and enhanced oversight arrangements. We also saw
evidence of staff recognition schemes, including Golden Ticket awards and staff appreciation
initiatives.

However, we found governance arrangements are not effective in ensuring people consistently
receive safe, high-quality care and support. Inspection identified significant inaccuracies and
inconsistencies within care records, nutritional monitoring, medication management and health and
safety arrangements that had not been identified through audits or quality assurance processes.
For example, records contained conflicting dietary information, inaccurate IDDSI levels (thickness
of fluids and texture of food), omissions in transdermal patch monitoring and contradictory personal
care records. The provider's quality of care review and other governance documents recorded that
systems such as call bell monitoring, care plan reviews, resident of the day reviews and
environmental audits were in place. However, our findings did not consistently support the
assurances provided within these documents.

People, relatives and external agencies raised concerns regarding management visibility,
communication and responsiveness. Comments included, “There's always two or three people in
the office with the doors closed”, “Don't see them out in the home very often”, and “There have
been some occasions where I haven't been able to find someone to talk to.” Some relatives told us
they were worried about raising concerns because of the potential impact on their family member.
We also found evidence that previous external monitoring had identified concerns relating to
mealtime dignity, meaningful occupation, personal care recording, staff communication and staffing
pressures. Similar concerns remained evident during this inspection. This demonstrates that
governance systems had not consistently ensured improvements were embedded and sustained
over time.

The service has experienced a period of management instability. The registered manager and
deputy manager were absent at the time of inspection and interim management arrangements
were in place. The provider had introduced additional support from regional management and
quality teams and was actively recruiting to vacant posts. Staffing dependency tools, rotas,

respond to risks promptly or ensure people receive care and support that achieves positive

action to address this issue.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
