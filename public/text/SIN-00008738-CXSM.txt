Summary:

 Marine Court Psychiatric Unit provides support for people with functional mental health needs and
 can support up to 23 people at one time. It is in Colwyn Bay and located within walking distance to
 local amenities and local transport links. Having recently come under new ownership, we have
 found significant improvements across all inspection themes.


 Well-being is good because people are involved in decisions affecting their daily lives and the
 service promotes their well-being. Care and support is good because people are supported by a
 dedicated team of staff, who know people well and ensure their needs are met. Whilst still requiring
 work, the environment is good as we saw clear improvements and prioritisation and plans in place
 for the environment to reach the required standard. Leadership and management is good as quality
 assurance records and staff feedback demonstrated a service that is developing a positive culture,
 with systems in place to support improvement and achieve good outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to have choice and control over their daily lives and to take part in activities
 that are important to them. Feedback from people living at the service is positive. One person told
 us, “I like the staff, other residents, the food.” Another said, “Ever since the new owners have
 arrived, the mood has lifted a million percent. Everyone is so happy – we are cared for in a very
 good way.” People are consulted about their care and are given opportunities to decide how
 involved they wish to be in developing and reviewing their personal plans. Throughout the
 inspection, we observed staff and managers treating people with dignity and respect while
 promoting independence. Records demonstrate people are encouraged to pursue their interests
 and participate in a wide range of activities, including local walks, bowling trips, community groups,
 charity work, and visits to places such as Chester Christmas Market, Llangollen Aqueduct and local
 events.


 People are supported to maintain and improve their physical, mental and emotional well-being.
 Nursing staff are on site to provide ongoing support and ensure health needs are met. Records
 show that changes in people’s health are recognised and acted upon promptly, with concerns
 escalated appropriately. The service promotes positive risk-taking where suitable, supported by
 detailed risk assessments, regular reviews and effective communication with healthcare
 professionals. People told us they had experienced improvements in their well-being and quality of
 life, and we observed positive relationships between staff and people living at the service.

 The service supports people to maintain relationships with those who are important to them and to
 remain active within their communities. Evidence showed staff work hard to facilitate family contact,
 including supporting visits to relatives and welcoming family members into the home. People are
 informed about their rights and how to raise concerns through regular resident meetings.
 Independence is actively encouraged, including support with financial matters where Court of
 Protection arrangements are in place.

 People benefit from living in an environment where their views are now listened to and acted upon.
 Residents are regularly consulted about ongoing improvements to the home, and their preferences
 are taken into account when changes are planned. Where environmental work may cause anxiety,
 a gradual and individualised approach is adopted. One person said, “We’re going to have a choice
 of how things look - I’ve had my new window” and “They inform us of what is due to happen and
 check in with us.” Improvements to internet and Wi-Fi access have also been made at the service.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that is planned around their individual needs and reviewed
regularly. Personal plans are co-produced with people where they consent and are reviewed every
three months. Records showed plans reflect people’s wishes, aspirations, areas of risk and
specialist needs. Personal plans include information about people’s history, care and support
needs and preferences and outcomes they want to achieve. We found plans gave staff clear
guidance on how to support people and reflected their current health needs. Communication needs
are also considered as part of care planning.

People benefit from consistent care and support from staff who understand their needs. Continuity
of care is promoted through stable staffing levels and a management structure where staff are clear
about their roles and responsibilities. Daily handovers take place and provide incoming staff with a
reflection of the previous shift, helping them understand any issues requiring attention. Records
demonstrate positive working relationships with other professionals involved in people’s lives. We
saw evidence staff escalate concerns to relevant professionals when people’s needs change. Daily
notes completed by staff provide an overview of each shift, while clinical and nursing records give
more detailed information about people’s presentation and health needs. This supports the ongoing
monitoring of people’s care and well-being.

People are supported to stay safe and have their rights promoted. Safeguarding policies are in
place and staff understand their responsibilities. People are offered advocacy support when
required. The service has also introduced resident questionnaires to provide additional
opportunities for people to raise concerns. At the time of inspection, no one living at the service
was subject to a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) authorisation; however, the service
demonstrated awareness of its responsibilities in relation to DoLS. Risk assessments are clear, up
to date and aligned with people’s assessed needs. They provide staff with clear guidance and
demonstrate a proportionate approach to risk management.

People receive support with their medicines and health care needs in a structured and organised
way. We found medicines are administered as prescribed and records were completed accurately.
Regular medication audits and reviews take place to monitor safe practice. Medication sections
within personal plans clearly record relevant information. We also found infection prevention and
control arrangements are maintained during ongoing environmental works, with increased cleaning
introduced.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that promotes their privacy, independence and dignity. We found the
provider had made considerable progress with a programme of environmental improvements and
refurbishment works since last inspection. The service is working to make communal and private
areas welcoming, safe and accessible, with works prioritised according to urgency, level of risk and
impact on people. The quality of completed work so far is of a high standard. Environmental
changes are intended to improve people’s experience of living at the service and support their
dignity going forward. A number of formal project progress reports demonstrate ongoing oversight
by the provider and manager of both completed works and areas still requiring improvement.
Inspection evidence showed the provider has a clear focus on driving environmental improvements
and monitoring progress.


People and staff are beginning to experience the positive impact of the ongoing improvements to
the environment. One staff member said, “Now that renovations have started and we are starting to
see good progress we feel that we are starting to gain Marine Court back to its prime.” Another
described the refurbishment programme as “having a very positive impact” and noted that
“significant improvements and investment are being made throughout the home.” Although further
work is needed to fully address all premises issues, we found much improved oversight, good
progress against planned works and a reduced impact on people living at the service. The priority
action notice in place in relation to the premises will therefore be removed. However, outcomes for
people still require improvement because works are still needed to ensure the premises meets the
expected standards and we expect the provider to continue to make improvements.


People benefit from an environment where health and safety arrangements are monitored and
reviewed effectively. Appropriate health and safety checks are completed regularly, and facilities
and equipment are serviced as required. Since the last inspection, the service has introduced
thorough monthly health and safety audits which identify risks, record corrective actions and
provide clear escalation routes to the provider where required. Audit records showed issues
identified, actions taken, responsible persons and completion dates, supporting accountability and
oversight of environmental issues. The audits are based on the needs and risks of people living at
the service and are supported by daily, weekly and monthly checks. We found good oversight of
health and safety arrangements across the service. Risk assessments are in place for when
environmental works take place. The provider now has measures in place to ensure risks to
people’s health and safety are proactively identified and reduced as far as reasonably practicable.
The priority action notice in relation to health and safety has therefore been removed.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from strengthened leadership and governance arrangements that support both
the delivery of care and continued service improvement. We saw evidence of regular and
constructive engagement between the manager, responsible individual (RI) and directors. Meeting
records demonstrate effective oversight of key areas including environmental improvements,
maintenance, staffing, compliance, payroll and equipment. Directors described a planned
programme of refurbishment, with work prioritised according to risk and urgency. Initial
improvements focused on addressing roof, guttering and damp issues before progressing to wider
environmental and cosmetic enhancements.


The provider has established systems to monitor the quality, safety and effectiveness of the
service. The RI completes quarterly adequacy of resources reports, providing structured reviews of
staffing, finances, the environment, equipment, governance arrangements and clinical support.
These reports identify risks, record mitigation measures and evaluate the impact on people living at
the service. RI visits review people’s experiences, staffing levels, supervision, training, recruitment,
retention, care records, risk assessments, medicines management and environmental safety.
Reports highlighted both positive practice and areas requiring further development. We found
evidence of a proactive approach to quality assurance through RI visits, audits and a
comprehensive quality of care review. This review draws on multiple sources, including resident
and staff surveys, RI reports and documentation audits, and identifies clear actions for ongoing
improvement across all service areas. As a result of the progress made, the priority action notices
relating to the supervision of management of the service and requirements in relation to the
provision of care have been removed.


People are supported by a staff team that increasingly benefits from guidance, training and
supervision. Training compliance has improved since the previous inspection, and the manager
demonstrates a stronger oversight of learning and development needs. Staff have prioritised
completion of mandatory and essential training, while future training requirements are actively
monitored. Records confirmed all staff had received supervision since last inspection, with
arrangements in place for supervision to take place every three months going forward. Staff
described a positive culture of learning and teamwork.


There is evidence of a more positive culture throughout the service in general. Staff consistently
reported feeling supported by the manager and provider and spoke positively about teamwork,
relationships with residents and management support. One staff member told us, “It feels like there
is something amazing happening here now,” while another said, “We now feel supported and can

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
