Summary:

 People experience good well-being outcomes at Sycamore Lodge. There are regular activities
 which people can be involved in, and staff treat people with dignity and respect. People are
 supported to stay as healthy as possible, with systems in place to protect people in an environment
 that meets their needs.

 People receive good care and support from a care staff team who understand their needs and treat
 them with dignity and respect. Care plans need improvement as they do not always contain
 detailed, personalised information appropriate to the person. Medication processes within the
 service are undertaken well and people attend appointments with health professionals or health
 care professionals are contacted as required.

 People live in an environment that is good. Sycamore Lodge is decorated throughout and has
 ample communal space. The outside areas offer well laid out, safe and appropriate space which
 people use. People have their own bedrooms which they have furnished to their personal

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are protected from abuse and harm. People are supported to be safe and have risk
 assessments in place where needed. People we spoke with confirmed their relatives feel safe at
 Sycamore Lodge. Care staff are up to date in safeguarding training, and this was evidenced on the
 training matrix. Safeguarding records demonstrate that concerns are responded to promptly and
 managed in line with organisational and statutory guidance. The provider understands the legal
 requirements of caring for vulnerable people and makes referrals to the local authority safeguarding
 team when required.

 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. Prior to people moving into the home
 managers undertake pre-admission assessments which includes information from relevant
 professionals ensuring the service can meet their needs. People’s personal plans are outcome and
 strengths focused and show likes, dislikes, and the support the individual needs. This information
 guides care staff on how to meet needs. We saw people’s personal plans which had been reviewed
 in line with regulations. People’s independence is encouraged as they are offered choice wherever
 possible.

 We also saw and were told people are offered choice regarding activities, both group activities and
 individual, such as reading to people in their rooms and armchair exercises, with an activities co-
 ordinator employed to facilitate this. People are also offered choice in regards their routines such
 as eating and drinking and when they can get up and go to bed. We spoke with someone who told
 us, “Staff wash and shave me and clean my room and change my bedding regularly…I have seen
 health care professionals and I get three meals a day…The food is good.” We also witnessed
 activities being undertaken which people clearly enjoyed. People are treated with dignity and
 respect. Relatives, and residents spoken to confirmed this. Care staff are kind and patient with
 people, and we observed many warm interactions. One person said, “My relative gets the care and
 support the require, they have improved lately…Staff treat my relative with respect, they go in and
 read them a story…They are well kempt and sometimes they refuse food but staff keep trying and
 go back and encourage with different foods.” A person living at Sycamore Lodge told us, “Staff are
 good, I can have fun with the staff…There is plenty going on…I get the hairdresser and the
 chiropodist.” Friends and relatives can visit when they wish, thus helping to enhance wellbeing.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement



People experience good care and support. Care is outcomes and strengths focused and most
personal plans give a clear picture of the person. Care staff follow these to ensure person-centred
care and support is delivered. One person told us, “I get the support I need…Staff will do whatever
I need.” People’s outcomes are clear, and they are supported to ensure they are achieved.
However, we viewed one person’s personal plan, and though they were receiving appropriate care
and support from a staff team that clearly knew them well, the care plan did not reflect their acre
and support needs. Outcomes for people require improvement as the persons support needs and
how they were to be supported had not been updated in their care plan and we expect the provider
to make improvements.

People’s requests for support were met promptly and care staff respond to call bells appropriately.
We saw good quality care being delivered and positive interactions between people and care staff
throughout the day. Care staff clearly know the people they support. This was also confirmed by
people we spoke with. People living at Sycamore Lodge appear well kempt with people confirming
our observations on the day. People, and their relatives confirmed that people were supported and
spoken to appropriately and treated with dignity and respect. We were told, “If I want anything, staff
are here quickly, I don’t have to wait hours.” Another person commented, “I am treated with
respect…I can have a laugh and a joke with staff…They are very good with me, I am very pleased.”


Medication processes at the services are safe, with medication stored securely. People receive
their medication as prescribed, in accordance with national guidelines and the service provider’s
medication policy.

People are referred for appropriate appointments at the right time and recommendations for care
and treatment by other professionals are conducted as directed. We saw personal plans, which
showed numerous and detailed records of liaison with health care professionals. Care records are
updated with any changes in health conditions and there are clear protocols to follow to maintain
people’s health and wellbeing. A relative told us, “The staff contact us in a timely fashion about any
issues, they have contacted us at 5 a.m. about our relative’s medical deterioration.”

       Environment                         Good



Sycamore Lodge is a care home located in a residential area in Hawarden and is set over two
floors, with a stair lift provided.

People live in a service which is suitable and meets their needs. The home is warm, welcoming,
well decorated throughout and benefits from communal space people can access whenever they
choose. People can spend time on their own or with visitors and other residents in a property which
has enough space to facilitate this. There are communal bathrooms and toilets which are clean and
in good working order. Externally the grounds at the rear are spacious, safe, and secure, with
seats, raised beds and a grassed area which people can enjoy.

People have their own bedrooms which are clean and have en-suite facilities. People can
personalise their rooms to make them as homely and comfortable as possible, if they wish. We saw
a sample of bedrooms that have appropriate seating, along with a call bell in case they need help.
Bedrooms we viewed were clean and personalised to the individual’s taste. A person we spoke
with confirmed that their bedroom and bedding are kept clean and tidy.

Sycamore Lodge provides a safe environment in which people can live. We had to ring to gain
access to the home and our identity was checked upon entry. The RI as part of their regular
checks, reviews the environment to ensure any works required is undertaken promptly. Appropriate
servicing and testing of systems such as gas, electrics, fire safety, and water are undertaken
regularly. Investment to both the interior and exterior of Sycamore Lodge is ongoing with the
painting of certain areas of the home and new chairs having been purchased. We also saw a
schedule of ongoing works.

Sycamore Lodge is kept clean throughout, both the communal areas and people’s rooms. Laundry
facilities are adequate for the size of the home. The Food Standards Agency have awarded the
service a level 5 rating, which is very good and the highest rating achievable.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People live at a service which is organised and has good leadership oversight. The provider has
established a stable team of care staff, with some staff members having been at the service for 10
years or more. Care Staff have daily support from the manager. One member of staff told us the
staff team is really good, and everyone gets along. Another carer told us, “I have worked here for
several years, and I love my job…It’s like a second family here…Managers are fine, approachable
and any issues get sorted out.” There are regular meetings between the manager and the staff
team to keep them informed. Care staff and people at the service gave positive feedback about the
manager, saying they are “Supportive and approachable.” Another member of the care staff team
spoke positively about the manager stating, “Any concerns are listened to.”

There are effective systems in place to monitor quality. This is conducted through managers
comprehensive audits of all areas of the service which are undertaken regularly. The RI visits the
service on a regular basis and ensures to gain feedback from people and their representatives
through the service’s own quality assurance questionnaire. The RI creates reports following visits to
evidence what areas they have looked at and what actions are needed ensuring continuous
improvement. There are policies and procedures in place to guide staff in how to support people
effectively and safely, and these are reviewed and updated as required. People and care staff can
be assured they have access to relevant information.


The provider has robust selection and vetting processes in place. Care staff are subject to pre-
employment checks, including a Disclosure and Barring Service check and all staff are registered
with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. We reviewed staff files and found they are in good
order with all the required documents in place. Care staff are employed in appropriate numbers;
this was confirmed by records we saw and staffing numbers on the day of inspection.
Training for all care staff is a priority and there is a training programme offering training on a wide
range of topics. New staff are supported via an induction programme with ongoing training for care
staff. Care staff feel the training they receive is informative and of good quality. Care staff
supervision takes place quarterly and on a one-to-one basis, with annual reviews also being
completed.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.

The table(s) below show the area(s) for priority action and/or those for improvement we have
identified.
