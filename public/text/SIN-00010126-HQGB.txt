Summary:

 Llantrisant Care Home provides care and support to adults requiring residential or nursing care, set
 in the countryside near Llantrisant.


 We have not considered the inspection themes in full and have focused on specific areas of the
 service. Well-being is good because people receive care and support which promotes their rights,
 independence and overall wellbeing. Care and support is good because people receive safe,
 person-centred care from care staff who are kind, respectful and responsive to their needs. The
 environment is good because the provider takes appropriate action to manage identified
 environmental risks while maintaining a suitable home for people. Leadership and management is
 good because effective governance, oversight and quality assurance arrangements support
 positive outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Our findings for this theme are based on the specific areas reviewed during this inspection. Well-
 being is good because people receive care and support which meets their individual needs, their
 rights are promoted, and they are treated with dignity and respect. People receive care and support
 which is appropriate to their assessed needs. Care records reflect people's health needs, mobility
 requirements and identified risks, helping care staff provide consistent support. They are supported
 to maintain their independence where possible, with personal plans recognising abilities, strengths
 and preferences. People are supported to make choices about their daily lives, including their
 routines, visitors and personal preferences.


 People are treated with dignity and respect. During our inspection, we observed patient, kind and
 supportive interactions from care  staff. We saw people appear clean, comfortable and well
 presented. Positive relationships between care staff and people help support emotional wellbeing,
 with people appearing settled and comfortable in their surroundings.


 People are supported to maintain relationships which are important to them. Visiting arrangements
 are unrestricted, enabling family and friends to remain actively involved in their loved one’s lives.
 We received consistently positive feedback about the care provided and the experience of living at
 the home.


 People's rights are promoted through care planning processes, mental capacity assessments and
 Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards arrangements. Records demonstrate consideration of people's
 liberty and the least restrictive approach to care. Physical health needs are monitored, and people
 have access to healthcare professionals when required.


 The environment is comfortable, personalised and suitable for the needs of people living at the
 home. Bedrooms and communal areas support comfort, familiarity and opportunities for social
 interaction, helping promote people's wellbeing.

       Care & Support                       Good



Our findings for this theme are based on the specific areas reviewed during this inspection. Care
and support is good because people receive care which meets their needs, promotes their safety
and wellbeing, and is delivered by care staff who are kind, respectful and responsive. Personal
plans are generally reflective of people's assessed needs, identified risks and legal protections.
The atmosphere throughout the home is calm and relaxed. We observed consistently positive
interactions between care staff and people, with support provided in a respectful, patient and
unhurried manner. Mealtime experiences are positive, and people receive support which promotes
dignity and choice.


People and their relatives speak positively about the care provided. People told us "the best thing I
ever did was to come here" and "staff are fantastic... I can get up when I want and go to bed when I
want." Relatives describe care staff as caring, professional and responsive to individual needs.
Comments include "they're understanding...they treat my loved one with kindness, dignity and
respect", "they are very happy with the care…at the home and constantly say how friendly the staff
are," and "residents are always treated with dignity and always look well cared for." Feedback also
describes the service as welcoming and homely.


Systems are  in  place  to  help  protect people from harm. Safeguarding arrangements are
established and  referrals are made when concerns  arise. Appropriate actions are taken  to
investigate  concerns and  reduce  risks. Care  staff  demonstrate a good  understanding  of
safeguarding responsibilities, reporting procedures and whistleblowing arrangements. Management
demonstrates the service learns from incidents and takes action to improve practice where
required.

       Environment                         Good



Our findings for this theme are based on the specific areas reviewed during this inspection. The
environment is good because the home provides a safe and suitable environment for people, and
the provider takes appropriate action to address identified environmental issues.


The provider is open and transparent regarding environmental matters affecting the home and
provides evidence of engagement with relevant agencies. Interim measures are in place to manage
identified risks while longer-term solutions are progressed. Fire safety arrangements are monitored
and managed. The  provider has considered  the  security  of  the environment. Discussions
demonstrate a balanced approach to keeping people safe while maintaining a welcoming and
accessible setting for relatives, visitors and professionals. Overall, the provider demonstrates a
commitment to managing environmental risks and maintaining a safe environment for people living
at the home. The environment remains suitable for people living at the home, with people provided
with a home which is comfortable and meets their needs.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Our findings for this theme are based on the specific areas reviewed during this inspection.
Leadership and management is good because effective governance arrangements help promote
people's safety and wellbeing, support care staff to carry out their roles, and enable the provider to
identify and respond to issues when they arise.


The provider has systems in place to monitor the quality and safety of the service. The Responsible
Individual undertakes regular visits in line with Regulation, completing detailed quality of care
reviews. Management systems demonstrate oversight  of  incidents, complaints, safeguarding
matters and concerns  relating  to  staff  conduct. Records show concerns  are  investigated
appropriately, and the provider demonstrates a willingness to seek independent oversight where
required.  The  provider  demonstrates  openness  and  transparency when  concerns  arise.
Safeguarding referrals and regulatory notifications are submitted appropriately, and records show
investigations are undertaken with actions implemented to help reduce risks and improve practice.
Policies and procedures, including safeguarding, medicines management and whistleblowing, are
comprehensive, accurate and  reflect  current  legislation and guidance. Recruitment  records
demonstrate safe recruitment practices, helping to ensure suitable people are employed to work at
the service. Training records show care staff complete mandatory and role-specific training relevant
to the needs of people living at the home. Staffing arrangements support the delivery of care. Rotas
demonstrate planned staffing levels are generally maintained in line with the Statement of Purpose.


We received feedback from a significant proportion of care staff working at the service. Most
feedback is positive regarding the quality of care provided, management support and the working
environment. Care staff frequently tell us people receive good care and describe teams as
committed to supporting people's wellbeing. Comments include "the residents always come first",
"management are approachable and supportive when needed" and "we work well together as a
team and genuinely care about the residents." Many care staff also describe the home as a positive
place to work and would recommend it to others. The overall feedback provides assurance that
people are cared for by a workforce committed to achieving positive outcomes and continually
improving the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
