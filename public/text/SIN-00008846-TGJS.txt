Summary


Children are settled and content in their comfortable, homely home. They have positive,
trusting relationships with trained, consistent care staff and are achieving positive well-
being outcomes.

A rights-based approach is evident at the service and children are respected and listened
to.

Documentation and guidance for care staff is detailed and care staff know children well and
can anticipate their needs. Care staff’s value base is one of empathy and empowerment for
children to achieve to their potential and to be part of the community.

There is a positive culture at the service and care staff feel supported and valued.

The home is in a good state of repair; it is homely, child-friendly and has the space and
facilities children need.

Sound quality assurance systems are enabling the service to improve and develop and
demonstrate the responsible individual (RI) is fulfilling their regulatory role.

Well-being
Children express themselves and are listened to. Each week children are consulted in a
session called “My Voice”, where their views are elicited and recorded. These sessions are
used to explore topics such as the décor of their rooms, the outcomes they wish to achieve
and their feelings about care staff and being safe. Children make daily choices about their
clothes, food and activities and they communicate their wishes informally. Care staff are
responsive but ensure suitable boundaries are maintained. Children are consulted as part
of the service’s quality assurance processes and they can influence the service they
receive.

Care staff support children to achieve good health and education outcomes. There is a
focus on healthy eating and exercise at a sensitive pace. Some children are eating healthily
and are readily taking exercise, while others are gradually engaging in creative approaches
to new foods and are taking up opportunities to go outdoors for walks. Care staff know how
best to support children’s emotional stability and how to respond if they do become anxious.
Children are attending school regularly and are making good progress. They benefit from
the partnership work between the home and their schools in addressing their complex
needs.

Care staff ensure children are safeguarded. Children are supported by suitably trained and
knowledgeable care staff and a manager who ensures safeguarding is a regular topic for
discussion at various staff forums. Some children have advocates and care staff support
children to see their families and people who are important to them regularly. Applications
for deprivation of liberty orders are in place for some children and others have their own fob
access to the front door.

Children like their comfortable, homely home. Following a difficult transition for some
children, they are settling well and now say: “Ty Galan is my home”. The home is colourful,
child-friendly and well-furnished and offers the space needed for the children to feel
comfortable and calm. It is safe and secure and provides facilities for children to develop
independence skills.

Care and Support
Children are assured care staff read and understand their regularly reviewed personal plans
so they can safely care for them and support them to achieve their well-being outcomes.
Children’s personal plans contain detailed guidance for care staff about their daily routines,
likes/dislikes, health and the support they need when they are anxious and unsettled. Risk
assessments contain comprehensive information and guidance which care staff follow to
keep children safe. Care staff we spoke to showed they were familiar with how best to
support and respond to children’s needs. We saw clear evidence of children achieving their
personal outcomes such as staying in a hotel, visiting a theme park and going bowling
together.

 Motivated, committed care staff are meeting children’s communication needs which
enables children to build positive, trusting relationships with them. Care staff talk about
children with affection and warmth and they use a range of communication tools to promote
children’s engagement and understanding, which ensures they are able to make
contributions and choices. They are familiar with the meaning of children’s verbal
responses and respond appropriately to ensure children receive the right care at the time
they need it. Children are relaxed around care staff and humour is used to good effect.

Care staff support children to be healthy as they can be and to engage in education.
Children are registered with primary health services and are prepared and supported by
care staff to attend appointments with these and more specialist health professionals. There
is evidence of joint work with the service’s clinical staff which results in successful health
outcomes. Some children’s diets are healthy and they are supported to achieve a more
healthy weight while creative partnership work with schools is supporting the introduction of
different foods to other children’s self-limiting diets. Children are engaging in exercise at a
pace which suits their needs; they are doing things like swimming, trampolining and walking
and they also enjoy other fun activities such as bowling. Some children initially struggled
with the transition to school, however, following patient support and clear boundaries they
are now attending regularly. Children are making good progress and there are positive
relationships between the schools and the home.

Suitable safeguarding practices means children are safe and protected. Trained care staff
have access to a suitable safeguarding policy, they know their safeguarding duties and
safeguarding is a regular topic at team meetings and supervisions. Incident reports show
children are supported in the least restrictive manner; debriefs are held with care staff when
needed and children are offered sensitively timed, informal debriefs. Important information
about expressing a concern or making a complaint is shared with children in suitable
formats.

Children safely receive their medication. Medication administration charts show care staff
are following the service provider’s medication policy and they are recording the reasons for
administering any PRN (as needed) medication. Care staff use their knowledge of children
to help them tolerate the application of topical medication.

 Environment
The environment is suitable and is meeting children’s needs. The home is well furnished,
comfortable and homely. Colourful pictures reflecting children’s interests are displayed
around it along with pictures of children enjoying activities which promotes a sense of
belonging. Children’s bedrooms are clean and decorated according to children’s choices
and personal interests. The provision of two living rooms means children have the space
they need to self- regulate.

The kitchen contains suitable facilities and is clean and well-stocked. Some children plan
their menu each week which is displayed on the wall and both children are taking part in
preparing food. Children are helping with laundry tasks and some manage the washing of
their clothes independently. They maintain good personal hygiene in a modern, clean
bathroom which contains their labelled basket of toiletries and colour coded towels.

The service provider ensures the home is as safe and secure as possible. We were asked
to show our identification and sign in on arrival and the visitors’ book showed people are
regularly signing in and out as required. Care staff undertake regular health and safety and
fire safety checks and both children and care staff participate in regular fire drills. Cleaning
materials and medication are stored in locked cupboards; medication is clearly dated on
opening and there are daily temperature recordings of the medicine cabinet. The repair log
shows repairs are attended to in a timely manner and the home is in a good state of repair.

The garden contains a small, paved area and has sufficient space for children to play ball
games and to sit outside. Steps lead to an upper platform where there is a swing chair and
plant pots and children are encouraged to take part in seasonal gardening activities to
enhance the outdoor area. Children can access the safe rear garden and some have a fob
for the locked front door, however, they tend to rely on care staff to open it.

Leadership and Management
The service provider gathers a range of information to ensure they are able to meet
children’s needs. Referral documents, local authority care plans and contributions from
other professionals and stake holders are used to inform the service provider’s admission
processes and decisions for children to come and live at the home. Impact assessments
are carried out and partnership work with other agencies supports children’s settling in
period at the home. Provider assessments are completed and are regularly updated in
relation to children’s changing needs.

The service provider ensures children and their representatives have the information they
need about the service. The statement of purpose is regularly updated; it includes recent
changes in legislation and it reflects the service we saw at inspection. Suitably formatted
guides to the service are shared with children which include information about how to make
a complaint or communicate something worrying them.

There are a sufficient number of trained, experienced care staff to meet children’s needs
and keep them safe. Aside from two new starters, children are cared for by a consistent
team of care staff, some of whom are qualified while others are completing the required
qualification for their role; all are registered with Social Care Wales. Rotas show there are
enough care staff each day with no use of agency staff.

Care staff are supported to fulfil their role.  All care staff are trained in the core areas of
safeguarding, behaviour management, first aid and fire safety and they also undertake
training on a range of topics relevant to their role. They receive regular supervision which
has a balanced focus on their well-being and work. Regular team meetings provide
opportunities to discuss children’s needs and to consider developments in practice. Care
staff told us they were a “close, supportive team”. Feedback questionnaires from care staff
indicate a positive team culture of motivated care staff, with statements such as:
“Even after tough days I look forward to returning to Ty Galan and embracing the amazing
opportunity to support and be a part of the young people's lives that I get to call work.”

“The manager of the home is what makes the house work well. She goes above and
beyond for all her staff team and young people”.

There are clear quality assurance systems which ensure improvements are made to the
service children receive. The RI regularly visits the home to undertake a range of checks
and they consult children, care staff and other stakeholders. The service provider carries
out a range of audits and the manager responds promptly to the identified shortfalls in both
the audits and regulatory visits. The quality of care reports present a detailed, rounded
picture of the service, its achievements and challenges and areas for development. The RI
has oversight of these reports and makes a considered view on its progress.
