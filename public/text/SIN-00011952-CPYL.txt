Summary:

 People living at Towy cottage experience excellent well-being outcomes. People are supported to
 achieve their aspirations through meaningful education, community involvement, positive
 relationships and a wide range of activities. Their communication needs are met effectively, family
 relationships are promoted and maintained, and safeguarding arrangements help people feel safe
 and supported. The service is also working towards the Welsh Active Offer, helping people engage
 with Welsh language and culture.

 People achieve good outcomes from the care and support they receive. Thorough assessments,
 detailed personal plans, health plans and behaviour support plans provide care staff with
 information to meet people's needs consistently. People receive support to access healthcare
 services, and daily records evidence the support provided and outcomes achieved. Medication
 systems are organised and managed safely.

People live in a good quality environment which supports their well-being. The home is
comfortable, welcoming and personalised, with bedrooms reflecting people's interests and
preferences. Communal areas support social interaction and leisure, whilst outdoor spaces provide
opportunities for recreation and gardening activities. The kitchen is clean, well-organised and has
achieved a Food Standards Agency food hygiene rating of five.

People benefit from good leadership and management arrangements. The manager and
Responsible Individual (RI) provide effective oversight through quality assurance, monitoring and
governance processes. A stable staff team promotes continuity and consistency for people. Safe
recruitment practices are followed, and care staff receive regular supervision and training. Positive
feedback from families and care staff reflects a service with a supportive and person-centred
culture.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being outcomes because care staff provide highly effective
 support which promotes independence, choice and achievement. People are treated with dignity
 and respect and are actively involved in decisions about their daily lives. Care staff know people
 exceptionally well and use this knowledge to build confidence, encourage personal development
 and support people to achieve their aspirations.

 People are extensively supported to maintain and improve their physical, emotional and mental
 well-being. Care staff ensure people access routine and specialist health services, annual health
 checks and professional support when required. People benefit from a wide range of meaningful
 activities which promote healthy lifestyles, social engagement and positive emotional well-being.
 These include football, gardening, arts and crafts, community events and recreational activities.
 Planned holidays and trips away provide enriching experiences, broaden social opportunities and
 enhance confidence and independence.

 People benefit from excellent educational opportunities through attendance at the provider’s
 specialist college. Educational programmes are tailored to people’s interests, strengths and
 aspirations and include activities such as pottery, weaving, woodworking, creative arts and
 horticultural and growing activities. Care staff work closely with college staff to ensure a consistent
 approach between education and residential support, helping people achieve positive outcomes
 and develop practical skills which enhance everyday life. This highly effective approach helps
 people build confidence, fulfil their potential and experience a strong sense of achievement.

 People are exceptionally well supported to maintain relationships with family members and others
 who are important to them. Communication between the service and families is extensive and
 highly valued. Families consistently praised the quality of support provided. One family member
 told us the service is doing an “Amazing job”, whilst another described the progress made by their
 relative as “Incredible”. Families also described communication from care staff and managers as
 “Excellent” and “Brilliant”. These strong partnerships enhance people’s emotional well-being and
 provide reassurance and continuity.

 People make a valuable contribution to their communities and are supported to engage
 meaningfully in society. They participate in local clubs, community groups, Mencap activities and
 social events which promote inclusion, friendships and a sense of belonging. People also
 contribute to life within the service through regular meetings, activity planning and involvement in
 environmental projects.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support because systems and processes are organised and
provide care staff with relevant information to meet people's needs consistently. Assessments
completed before people move into the service are thorough and consider care and support needs,
known risks, compatibility with others and desired outcomes. These are reviewed regularly and
provide a clear rationale for how the service can meet people's needs.

Personal plans are detailed, strengths-based and person-centred. They provide care staff with
clear information about people's daily routines, preferences, health needs and support
requirements. Plans are written from the person's perspective and contain practical guidance which
promotes consistency of care. We found plans include information about likes and dislikes,
personal care needs, health requirements and routines important to people. Systems are in place
to ensure care staff read and understand plans and any updates made.

Health plans contain information about people's health needs, prescribed medication, healthcare
professionals involved in their care and arrangements for accessing health appointments. This
information helps care staff understand the support people require and promotes a consistent
approach to meeting health needs. Records demonstrated people regularly access healthcare
services and receive support to attend routine and specialist appointments. Information relating to
health conditions and ongoing treatment is recorded clearly, helping care staff provide consistent
support.

Behaviour support plans provide useful guidance about people's individual needs, triggers and
preferred approaches. Care staff have access to clear information about how best to support
people during periods of anxiety or distress. Plans promote positive behaviour support and focus
on reducing the likelihood of incidents occurring. Risk assessments complement these plans and
identify potential risks alongside measures required to keep people safe while supporting their
independence.

Daily records provide a good overview of people's experiences and the support provided. Entries
evidence participation in activities, attendance at appointments, progress towards goals and any
significant events which occur. Records are detailed and help demonstrate how people spend their
time and the outcomes they achieve. Incident reporting systems are good and records identify
actions taken to keep people safe.

Medication systems are safe and well organised. Medication is stored appropriately and records
are completed consistently. Clear protocols are in place where required, including arrangements for
prescribed and non-prescribed medication. Regular audits, stock checks and monitoring processes
help ensure medication is managed safely and people receive medicines as intended. Overall,
people benefit from care and support which is well planned, consistent and responsive to their

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment which is comfortable, welcoming and suitable for their needs. The
home provides a domestic setting where people can personalise their bedrooms with items
reflecting their interests, hobbies and personalities. Bedrooms contain personal belongings,
photographs and memorabilia, helping create familiar surroundings and promoting a sense of
ownership and individuality.

Communal areas are well furnished and support both social interaction and relaxation. People have
access to spaces where they can spend time together, participate in activities or relax
independently. The living room provides a comfortable area for leisure and socialising, while
displays throughout the home celebrate people’s experiences, achievements and interests.
Accessible information and activity planners help people remain informed and involved in daily life.

The kitchen is clean, well-organised and stocked with a variety of food options to meet people’s
needs and preferences. The service has achieved a Food Standards Agency food hygiene rating of
five, demonstrating high standards of food safety and hygiene. Adjacent to the kitchen, the dining
area provides a welcoming space for meals and social interaction and contains accessible
information, activity planners and resources which support people’s involvement in daily life and
menu choices.

People benefit from an environment which encourages participation and independence. Information
boards and easy-read resources help people understand what is happening within the service and
contribute to decisions affecting daily life. Photographs displayed throughout the home reflect
activities, holidays and achievements, creating a positive and person-centred atmosphere.

Outdoor areas contribute positively to people’s well-being. The garden provides opportunities for
recreation, relaxation and meaningful occupation. People are involved in gardening activities and
growing herbs, vegetables and other plants. This supports engagement in purposeful activities and
provides opportunities to enjoy outdoor space. Seating areas and recreational facilities further
enhance opportunities for social interaction and leisure.

The provider has arrangements in place to maintain the environment and promote people's safety.
Regular health and safety checks, environmental audits and maintenance processes help ensure
the premises remain suitable for the people living there. Records evidenced routine monitoring of
fire safety systems, water temperatures and other key health and safety measures. Maintenance
issues are monitored through established systems, supporting the ongoing upkeep of the home.

People benefit from clear emergency arrangements. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans

positive daily experiences for people living at the service.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from a service which is well managed and provides effective oversight of care and
support. The manager and RI have established governance arrangements which support the safe
and smooth operation of the service. The manager maintains a visible presence within the home
and has developed positive relationships with people, families and care staff. Feedback gathered
during the inspection demonstrated confidence in the management of the service and the quality of
support people receive.

Quality assurance systems help the manager and RI monitor service delivery and identify
opportunities for improvement. Regular audits, health and safety checks, team meetings and
reviews of care and support contribute to ongoing monitoring of the service. Quality of Care
Reviews demonstrate consultation with people, families and care staff and identify actions to
support service development. These reviews highlight positive outcomes for people, including
increased independence, community engagement and access to healthcare services. The RI
undertakes regular visits to the service and maintains oversight of care, safeguarding, staffing and
environmental matters.

People benefit from a stable and consistent staff team. Staffing arrangements provide continuity of
support and help people develop trusting relationships with care staff. The service does not rely on
agency care staff and has maintained a consistent workforce. Rotas are organised effectively and
ensure people receive support from care staff who know them well and understand their needs.

Safe recruitment practices help ensure people are supported by suitable care staff. Staff files
contained the required recruitment information, including proof of identity, references, employment
history and Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks. Records demonstrated care staff are
registered with Social Care Wales or are progressing towards registration requirements. These
arrangements help promote people's safety and well-being.

Care staff receive regular supervision and have access to training relevant to their roles. Training
records demonstrated nearly all care staff have completed mandatory training, including
safeguarding, first aid and mental capacity training. Supervision sessions are completed regularly
and include discussions about well-being, safeguarding, service developments, performance and
learning needs. This supports care staff to remain knowledgeable and confident in their roles.

Care staff spoke positively about working at the service and the support provided by the manager.
One care staff member told us, “It’s a good team to work for” and said they felt supported within
their role. Another care staff member stated, “The manager is excellent, she is very supportive.”
Additional feedback described the home as “A safe, homely environment” and highlighted

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
