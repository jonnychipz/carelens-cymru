        Well-being                           Good

       Environment                         Good



        Leadership & Management               Good


Summary:

 Hafan Deg is a residential care home on the outskirts of Lampeter. Well-being is rated as good
 because people receive a service that is highly person centred, that enables them to achieve their
 health and well-being outcomes. People have developed extremely positive relationships with the
 familiar and caring staff.

 Care and support is rated as good because people receive care and support from a team of
 passionate and friendly care workers. Care workers are guided by accurate and individualised
 personal plans that enable them to successfully meet people’s needs.

 The environment is rated as good because people are very comfortable and relaxed throughout the
 home. Improvements have been made to the home and the provider intends to continue these to
 enhance people’s well-being.

 Leadership and management is rated as good because the provider has good oversight of the
 service. Strong leadership ensures people receive high-quality care from a team of dedicated and
 skilled care workers who receive effective support from their managers.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are treated with dignity and respect by the whole staff team. They are supported to identify
 their health and well-being outcomes, while also being encouraged to use and build on existing
 strengths. A visiting representative told us, “Overall it’s a good home and the staff are great and
 make this place so good”.

 People have as much control over their day-to-day lives as possible. They are actively supported to
 be involved in decisions that affect them, ensuring that their voices are heard. This approach helps
 people to take charge of their lives with confidence and assurance.

 There is a strong sense of community within the home. Positive relationships between people living
 at the service are evident and contribute to a family atmosphere. We observed people engaging
 naturally with one another, sharing conversations and offering support. This demonstrated people
 are comfortable in each other's company and promote one another's well-being, creating a genuine
 sense of belonging.

 People enjoy taking part in a wide variety of planned activities and there is unrestricted visiting for
 family and friends. People are supported to maintain and sustain existing relationships with family,
 friends and important people in their lives as far as possible. This is facilitated by staff who respect
 these relationships and understand the importance they hold in people’s lives.

 Welsh language and culture is embedded in the service, people converse with each other and the
 staff team in English or Welsh as they choose. Signage around the building is both accessible and
 bilingual to assist people further with their communication needs.

 People are safeguarded from abuse and neglect. People are provided with a secure environment
 where they feel safe. The service encourages feedback from people and their representatives to
 ensure every voice is heard. There are effective support systems to ensure any risks are promptly
 identified and addressed.

 People live in accommodation that supports them to achieve positive outcomes. The new design,
 layout and decoration of the setting and quality of the furnishings and fixtures meets people’s
 needs.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are very happy with the care and support they receive from the team of understanding and
familiar care workers. We observed many warm, fun and friendly interactions throughout the
inspection. A person who lives at the service told us, “The staff here are first class”.

People experience very good quality care because there is a detailed process before the manager
agrees to provide the service. The effective assessments check if the provider can fulfil their needs
and personal outcomes alongside people already receiving the service.

People are involved in their personal plans. Their care and support is co-produced between them,
their representatives, relevant professionals and care staff. Personal plans are strengths based,
highly individualised and outline how staff should support people to achieve their well-being
outcomes. People’s plans include their preferences, routines and beliefs. A care worker told us, “It’s
nice to be able to make a real difference to someone’s life”.

People's well-being and safety is prioritised by the service through identifying and managing
potential risks. People’s right to make their own choices and take informed personal risks is
promoted by the positive culture in the service. This is clearly reflected in people’s individual plans.

The service works creatively to ensure people are offered choices in enjoyable and stimulating
activities. People engage in meaningful pastimes, such as gardening, cooking, exercise classes,
shopping, visiting entertainers and access their local community. Photographs of people taking part
in these activities are recorded in appealing activity books and shared with family and friends.

People experience care and support that is dignified and respectful. People have positive
interactions with staff, friends, family, and each other. People are supported to attend and
participate in activities related to health promotion, staff are aware of their role in this and the
current needs of each individual. A support worker said, “It’s nice to have a job where you can
spend quality time with people”.

People receive their medication as prescribed in accordance with national guidelines and the
service provider’s medication policy.

People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because premises and
equipment are kept clean and hygienic. Food hygiene practices are good. There is an effective
approach to managing and preventing the risk of infection, with clear roles and responsibilities.

       Environment                         Good



The building is homely and very well maintained. The provider has upgraded communal rooms, the
majority of bedrooms, bathrooms and improved the overall décor, with further plans to replace the
heating system. People's views and needs are considered when maintaining the building. Individual
rooms are highly personalised; people can decorate them with items of their own furniture,
ornaments and equipment.

People value the ability to access different communal and private spaces in which to spend time
alone, socialise or entertain visitors. Communal spaces are bright and welcoming. They meet the
needs of people, promoting independence and providing opportunities for private meetings,
activities, and recreation.

Outdoor areas are safe, accessible and extremely welcoming, allowing people to spend time
outside independently. We were told about people enjoying them to grow their own food, socialise,
relax and exercise in the warmer weather.

Safety is a key consideration at the service, people can easily navigate the home and access
necessary equipment. The service manages risks to people, staff are familiar with the strategies
and apply them consistently.

Regular servicing, maintenance and immediate repairs to equipment ensure the safety and well-
being of people using the service.

Security arrangements are in place to protect people without compromising their rights, privacy,
and dignity. This includes protecting personal property and providing appropriate access to and
from the premises.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider oversees the quality of the service through their detailed and effective auditing
processes. Information gathered from both internal and external sources guides quality review
reports and improvement plans

The Responsible Individual (RI) is well known and visits the service regularly to talk with people,
their representatives and staff. Feedback from everyone involved is actively encouraged by them to
support assessing the quality of the service and to consider any improvements. People know how
they can provide feedback about their experiences of the service.

The manager is well supported by a deputy and a team of senior care workers. They have worked
together effectively to create a friendly and caring culture, that enables people to live as well as
possible. Care workers are positive about the relationships they have with people, one told us, “I
love working with the residents and every day is different”. People, their representatives and care
workers have confidence in the manager and deputy, they can openly discuss any concerns they
have with them.

Care workers have regular one-on-one supervision sessions with their manager at least quarterly
and an annual review to provide feedback and identify any training needs. Care workers told us the
manager and deputy are accessible and extremely supportive. They told us, “The open-door policy
here is brilliant, [manager] and [deputy] are always available and so supportive” and “The
managers are easy to talk to, and they know I’m coming from the resident’s point of view”.

The service provider has strict selection and vetting processes for hiring staff to ensure they are
qualified and trustworthy. Care workers undergo routine and regular checks to ensure they remain
suitably fit to work in the service and are appropriately registered with professional bodies. The
service has an effective induction that prepares new staff for the role. Care workers are positive
about the opportunities they receive.

Care workers demonstrate a positive approach to protecting people and keeping them safe. Staff
have good communication with their managers and are confident to report concerns if needed.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
