Summary:

 College Fields provides high-quality, person-centred care and support to men aged 18 and over
 with mental health needs, including those with forensic histories.

 The well-being outcomes for people are excellent, with highly personalised support enabling
 individuals to build on their strengths, maintain and develop their independence and achieve
 positive outcomes.

 The care and support people receive is excellent. People receive personalised support that is
 tailored to their needs through effective care planning, risk management and professional
 involvement. This helps people develop skills, pursue interests and make informed choices safely.

 The environment is good. People live in a homely, personalised and well-maintained environment
 that supports comfort, dignity and well-being. The provider undertakes regular maintenance and
 repairs, and ensures the home is safe.

 The leadership and management of the service is excellent. The provider has good oversight of the
 service. The management promotes an exceptionally strong, positive culture where people,
 relatives, care staff and professionals feel listened to and valued.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People at College Fields experience excellent well-being and quality of life through highly
 personalised support that promotes independence, choice and inclusion. Care staff empower
 people to build on their strengths, develop skills, pursue their interests and participate in meaningful
 activities within the service and wider community. A visiting professional told us, “I am 100%
 confident in their ability to construct and deliver meaningful support and care plans that are
 beneficial to our service users in their journey towards community rehabilitation”.

 People are actively involved in decisions about their care and daily lives. Their individual needs,
 preferences, culture, identity and relationships are consistently respected and reflected in person-
 centred planning and support. One person said “I feel able to voice my opinion. There is flexibility to
 be able to still lead my own life with support in areas needed”.

 People benefit from a strong, open and inclusive culture where they are treated with dignity and
 respect. Care staff know people well, promote trust and positive relationships, and consistently
 support individual identity, diversity and well-being. Feedback from people and relatives is
 exceptionally positive, with one relative commenting: “We are so grateful to College Fields for their
 understanding, attention to detail, high standards of behaviour and respect for those in the house
 and others. They are always ready to go the extra mile.”

 People are protected from harm, as far as is possible, through proactive risk management that
 promotes safety, independence and choice. They are supported to make informed decisions and
 take positive risks, helping to build confidence and maintain control over their lives. People’s right
 to make their own choices and take informed personal risks is promoted by the positive culture in
 the service. Care staff and management provide clear information and encouragement to people.
 This approach is reflected in person-centred plans and risk assessments. Another visiting
 professional told us the service provides a “Holistic approach to care of patients with complex
 mental health difficulties and risk issues. The right balance of support and positive risk
 management.”

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive excellent care because the manager completes thorough assessments to ensure
the service can meet their needs and personal outcomes. Compatibility with others living at the
service, accommodation suitability and information from professionals are carefully considered.
People’s needs and preferences inform admission decisions, ensuring the service is well placed to
provide effective, personalised support. One professional stated, “I am 100% confident in their
ability to construct and deliver meaningful support and care plans that are beneficial to our service
users in their journey towards community rehabilitation”.

People are encouraged to set and achieve their own goals, with progress regularly reviewed and
support tailored to maximise outcomes. The service goes above and beyond to help people
achieve their goals, which includes achieve greater independence, develop life skills, access
meaningful opportunities and progress towards more independent living. Care staff told us, “Our
staff team working together to promote the gentlemen to move on to a less supportive role, so they
gain confidence and are happy to move on”.

People receive highly effective care and support and are involved in their own care planning. Care
plans viewed are comprehensive, reviewed regularly and provide clear guidance about
communication, health needs, risks and what matters to people. Feedback from people and
professionals evidenced positive health and well-being outcomes, particularly for those with
complex needs. Recommendations made by health professionals for care and treatment, are
carried out as directed. A relative said “The wide experience of those running College Fields makes
a huge difference to our son's care as they really understand his needs and help us better
understand them too.”.

People are protected from harm and abuse. We found appropriate systems in place to protect
people whilst ensuring their rights and freedoms are respected. Care staff receive appropriate
training, understand their responsibilities and are supported by robust policies, risk assessments,
audits and specialist input when required. Management actively review incidents and embed
learning to drive continuous improvement. People live in personalised environments they regard as
their home, where their well-being, rights and safety are consistently promoted.

People receive their medication as prescribed and in accordance with the service provider’s
medication policy. Medication management systems are safe, records are complete, storage
arrangements are robust and the overall administration of medication is effective. Care staff are
trained in medication administration, and routine audits ensures practices remain safe and
effective. People are supported to become more independent with medication and move toward
self-medicating when suitable.

       Environment                         Good



People benefit from a warm, comfortable and welcoming environment at College Fields. The
service has good transport links to the city and local areas. The home is nicely decorated and
suitably furnished. People personalise their bedrooms as they wish, and all have ensuite facilities.
The home is secure to prevent unauthorised access and protect people. Visitors make themselves
known on arrival and care staff ensure they sign in and out of the premises. People told us they are
happy, with their accommodation with relatives saying they are always made to feel welcome when
visiting. A visiting professional said, “College Fields always appears to provide a friendly
environment”, with another saying “The home is welcoming and pleasant to attend. The
management and the staff are approachable and enjoyable to talk to.”.

People have access to comfortable communal areas for socialising and private spaces when they
wish to spend time alone. Communal areas are well-furnished and cozy. There is an enclosed
garden and patio area to the rear of the home. The home environment promotes well-being,
independence and daily living skills, with people encouraged to take responsibility for household
tasks and their personal spaces. With support from care staff people develop daily living skills
through tasks such as laundry and meal preparation. Relatives value the environment, with one
commenting, “The homely atmosphere is also a major factor” in supporting their relative’s
independence, and another saying, “The house is spotless and welcoming.”.

The service is maintained to good standard, and the provider has procedures in place to identify
and mitigate risks to health and safety. We saw there is routine servicing of utilities such as
electricity and gas. There is an up-to-date fire risk assessment and fire safety features are regularly
checked and serviced by suitably qualified people. There is a plentiful supply of cleaning products,
which are stored in accordance with Control of Substances Hazardous to Health (COSHH)
recommendations. Infection control policies and procedures are in place, with sufficient supplies of
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for care staff to use. We saw evidence of a comprehensive
programme of environmental, health and safety and infection control audits which help identify and
address issues promptly.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



There is a positive and empowering culture at College Fields. Management promote an
exceptionally strong, positive culture where people, relatives, care staff and professionals feel
listened to and valued. It is clear the management team know people and their families well, are
conscientious and well organised. Feedback was highly positive, with one relative stating, “Julie
[manager] runs a great team. They are all very kind and supportive of our son as well as taking
time to discuss with us,” and another commenting, “We are so grateful to College Fields for their
understanding, attention to detail, high standards of behaviour and respect for those in the house
and others. They are always ready to go the extra mile.”

The registered manager is supported by a deputy manager and Responsible Individual, who
promote a positive and proactive culture across the service. Care staff described the manager as
“compassionate” and “extremely supportive”. Feedback from care staff was extremely positive, with
one commenting, “In my experience, the staff team all appear happy, and I think it's due to
supporting each other and feeling supported by management”, while another said management are
“always open to feedback and trying new things to improve the service”.

People achieve positive outcomes through support from a skilled and experienced team of care
staff who know them well and understand their individual needs. Safe staffing levels, robust
recruitment processes and ongoing training ensure people receive consistent, high-quality care.
Care staff say the training they receive is comprehensive and helps them deliver care and support
that is safe and effective. A visiting professional described the service as having an “Experienced
staff team. Expertise in managing complex patients.”

Effective governance arrangements support the quality and safety of the service. A comprehensive
programme of audits, regular visits from the Responsible Individual and ongoing stakeholder
feedback inform quality assurance and service improvement. Audit findings are reviewed through
action planning and monitoring processes, ensuring strengths and areas for development are
identified and addressed. Policies and procedures are regularly reviewed to reflect current
legislation and best practice.

Leadership and management arrangements are exceptionally effective. Strong governance, high
staff engagement, robust oversight and a clear commitment to person-centred practice result in a
service that consistently delivers excellent outcomes. The provider demonstrates a sustained ability
to maintain high standards whilst continually adapting and developing support to meet the people's
changing needs and aspirations.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
