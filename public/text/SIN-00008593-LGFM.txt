Summary

Ty Canol is a children’s home providing care and support for young people.

At the previous inspection, significant shortfalls were identified, and we issued seven
Priority Action Notices and two Areas for Improvement in relation to the care and support of
young people, the environment and Leadership and Management of the service. At this
inspection, we found the service provider has taken action to address the regulatory
breaches to ensure good quality care and support for young people is being provided.

The service closed for a period of review and now provides young people with a full
complement of permanent care staff who have received adequate training. Care staff told
us there has been significant change within the leadership and management and it is a
positive place to work.

Young people are beginning to form positive relationships with care staff and although they
do not always actively engage, care staff encourage young people to reach their potential
and demonstrate a desire to improve the well-being of young people

Well-being


Young people are provided with a children’s guide to the service to ensure they can access
the right information when they need it. The guide provides important information around
how they will spend their time at Ty Canol, advocacy, and information regarding the
complaints process.

The service promotes the voice of young people and provides regular opportunities to
express their views through individual discussion and house meetings. Key worker sessions
are completed monthly although the content still requires improvement. Young people are
provided with monthly questionnaires to share their views and engage in monthly house
meetings. When young people make requests about their care they are provided with an
outcome. Family visits are encouraged and supported.

Young people are not developing and learning to their potential and do not routinely attend
education. Records show, care staff encourage young people to attend school, create
healthy sleep routines and plan fun and engaging activities they will enjoy but there is little
engagement from young people. Care staff, external professionals and family members
work together to support young people’s engagement. Young people are registered with
universal health services and the service supports young people to access further specialist
services when appropriate. Young people have a user-friendly health plan to help them
understand their health needs.

A healthy lifestyle is promoted by care staff and a varied and balanced menu is offered to
young people. Care staff complete two monthly menus to promote choice and autonomy,
but records show young people often decline these options and will choose unhealthy
options more frequently.

The service ensures young people are safe from risk of harm. Care staff have received
safeguarding training and are confident in following suitable safeguarding processes.
Safeguarding referrals are submitted within the required time frame and a chronology of the
incident is recorded. Incidents reports are detailed and provide important information
including the managers review. Physical intervention is rarely needed, however,
appropriately used when required. After serious incidents, young people are offered a
debrief, however records show young people do not always engage.

Care and Support


Provider assessments assess the needs of young people and provide assurances to
commissioners and external professionals care staff can meet those assessed care and
support needs. Provider assessments are routinely reviewed and updated; however,
improvement is needed to ensure the service provides detail on how young people will be
supported whilst living at Ty Canol. Impact risk assessments are completed to ensure all
young people can live together safely.

Young people have personal plans which identify their needs and focus on their well-being
outcomes to ensure care staff can provide the right care and support as early as possible.
Care staff record young people’s voice and wishes, although engagement is limited. The
service has created outcome status logs to motivate young people on their
accomplishments and to record why and how outcomes may have changed. Care staff told
us they would like young people to engage more. Monthly evaluations capture consultation
with young people and are focused on their personal well-being outcomes. Care should be
taken to ensure records represent the training care staff have received.

Behaviour support plans are detailed and describe how care staff should support young
people to reduce risky or challenging behaviours. Important information including triggers
and preventative strategies ensure care staff follow safe practices and young people are
treated with dignity and their individual circumstances are considered. Risk assessments
are in place which include known risk behaviours and are reviewed and updated with new
information.

Daily recordings are focused on young people’s well-being outcomes and care staff
evidence the care and support provided and any progress made. As engagement from
young people can be limited, daily recordings should ensure care staff capture all
interaction and safety checks they have carried out. We saw care staff engaging positively
with young people and encouraging them to have breakfast and get ready for school.

Young people are supported to take prescribed medication which best supports their
physical and mental health. Medication records detail the prescribed medication and if
young people decline. The medication cabinet is secure, and the temperature is recorded
daily.

All non-compliance in relation to the care and support of young people has been met.

 Environment


Young people live in a home conducive of a family home. Since the last previous
inspection, the service has ensured the home is clean, well maintained and decorated to a
good standard. Young people have space for socialising and privacy both within the home
and the external grounds.

Young people are encouraged to personalise their bedrooms to create a sense of
belonging. Young people have photos of their family around the home, and there is a wide
range of DVDs, games, and toys within the living room to support the young people to play,
learn and relax.

The service protects young people from risks by completing necessary fire and health and
safety checks. Effective health and safety manager checks, and recordings provide
assurances to young people that when any issues arise, maintenance work is completed
quickly, and their environment is safe from risk. Visitors are required to sign in and provide
identification when entering Ty Canol.

Non-compliance in relation to the environment has been met.

Leadership and Management


The service being provided to young people reflects the statement of purpose, and the
provider has taken action to address the shortfalls in relation to the leadership and
management of the service.

The service has recently created a new management structure to ensure suitable oversight
and support for young people as the team, work across two homes within a minutes’ walk
from each other. The manager is supported by a deputy and a full complement of care staff.
There is no longer a requirement to use agency staff and care staff rota’s show required
staffing ratios are met.

Young people are supported by suitably trained and supervised care staff to ensure they
receive their assessed care and support needs. Training records show nearly all care staff
have completed mandatory training in line with the statement of purpose however care staff
told us some training such as Positive Behaviour Management (PBM) could be improved.
Care staff receive monthly supervision to ensure they can reflect on practise and provide
good quality care and support to young people. Supervision records demonstrated
purposeful dialogue, praise, and areas to action. Care staff told us the management team
are very supportive and they are confident in working with young people.

The service provider has completed safe recruitment checks to provide assurances to
young people they are cared for by suitable care staff. Recruitment and personnel files
demonstrate regulatory checks including, the verification of references, identification, and
checks to the Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) are completed.

The Responsible Individual (RI) has suitable arrangements in place to assess and improve
the quality of the service for young people. Records of regulatory visits demonstrate
consultation, through informal discussion and questionnaires, checks on the environment
and a review of documentation. Improvements and recommendations are recorded within
regulatory visits and the Quality-of-care report. Care staff told us the RI visits frequently and
is approachable. Regular manager audits are completed on medication charts, training
records, and health and safety to provider further scrutiny and review.

All non-compliance in relationship to the leadership and management of this service has
been met.
