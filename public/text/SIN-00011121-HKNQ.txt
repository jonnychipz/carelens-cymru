Summary:

 People living at Cherry Tree Care Home receive good care and support from trained staff who treat
 them with kindness, dignity and respect. We saw warm, friendly interactions and people being
 offered choices throughout the day. People can take part in social and recreational activities, and
 residents and relatives speak positively about the care provided. They describe staff as caring and
 management as responsive and approachable.

 Since the last inspection, the service provider has made sustained improvements to staffing,
 supervision, training, medication management and the culture within the home. Staff told us they
 feel supported by managers and listened to when they raise concerns.

 The home is welcoming and comfortable, but some maintenance and decoration issues still need
 attention to ensure the environment consistently meets people’s needs. Managers and the provider
 are continuing to address oversight issues identified at the last inspection; however, records do not
 consistently evidence how improvement actions are identified, assigned, completed, evaluated or
 reviewed for effectiveness. The service has improved since the last inspection, and we have
 reflected this in ratings awarded. However, further improvement is needed to show consistently
 positive outcomes for people from a good environment, governance, and oversight.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People experience care that promotes dignity, choice and well-being. During our visit we saw care
 staff interacting warmly and respectfully with residents. Care staff offer people choice throughout
 the day, using their knowledge of individuals to support decision-making where people have
 difficulty communicating their wishes directly. People told us staff are kind, responsive and provide
 support when needed. Relatives describe care staff as caring and compassionate and speak
 positively about the support provided during periods of change, grief and transition into residential
 care.

 Residents are supported to maintain interests and engage in activities. Records showed a varied
 programme of opportunities including music, exercise, games, reminiscence activities, social
 events and one to one activities. We saw people taking part in bingo, chair-based exercises, music
 and social interactions. Care staff supported participation in different ways according to individual
 needs and abilities. The manager told us that exercise and movement activities have contributed
 positively to people's mobility and confidence.

 People benefit from monitoring and support to maintain their health. Monthly weight monitoring and
 analysis take place, and healthcare professionals are involved when required. We saw evidence of
 district nurse involvement and ongoing monitoring of health needs. Care staff demonstrated their
 understanding of safeguarding responsibilities and were able to explain how concerns would be
 reported. They told us they feel listened to and confident in raising issues with the manager as they
 arise.

 Although there have been improvements since the last inspection and outcomes for people are
 positive overall, opportunities remain to further improve how personal histories, preferences and life
 experiences are recorded and used within the service to inform person-centred care delivery. The
 provider supports the Active Offer of Welsh by making information about the service available in
 Welsh and by recognising Welsh culture through celebrating national festivals, sporting events and
 holidays. However, community inclusion and meeting people’s spiritual or cultural preferences, and
 environmental improvements are still required. These issues have the potential to affect people's
 experience of living in the home if not addressed fully. The provider continues to work on
 developing these areas.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that is tailored to their individual needs. The care plans we
reviewed covered key areas of support and were underpinned by individual risk assessments. Care
staff know people’s plans and can access them electronically using handheld devices, on which
they record what support they provide. Records we saw show people receive care and support in
line with their plans and care staff support them in ways that reflect their individual preferences and
routines. Daily care records also demonstrate that care is provided consistently and that staff
respond appropriately when people's needs change. Family members are involved in planning and
reviewing care and support where appropriate.

People's nutritional and healthcare needs are monitored. Weight monitoring systems are in place,
and records demonstrate regular review and analysis to ensure people remain healthy. Staff work
with healthcare professionals, including district nurses and general practitioners, to support
people's health needs. Mealtime observations showed a relaxed atmosphere where people could
eat at their own pace and choose where they wished to dine. Alternative meal choices were
available and meals were attractively presented.

Medication management arrangements are good and keep people safe. Regular audits are
completed, and staff competency assessments are undertaken to ensure their practice remains
safe. The manager ensures learning is taken from external audits of medication management and if
medication related incidents occur. We saw records of detailed routine auditing and monitoring
processes, supported by staff training and oversight from leadership in the service. Infection
prevention and control processes have improved, with personal protective equipment readily
available and in use. The home was being inspected by environmental health on the day we visited
and was awarded a good food hygiene rating.

Some aspects of care planning require further development. We found examples of incomplete
biographies, inconsistencies within care records and care plans that would benefit from more
detailed information about people's preferences, routines and support strategies. End-of-life
planning was not consistently recorded. The manager and leaders in the service are aware and
working to improve this. This is not currently affecting the quality of care and support people
receive?

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is secure, comfortable, welcoming and decorated in a homely
and pleasant style. The home is split in two on the ground floor and connected on the first floor by a
communal area that provides dining and seating space and can be used to meet with visitors if
people prefer not to use their rooms. People personalise their rooms with photographs, ornaments
and soft furnishings that reflect their individual preferences. The main communal areas are on the
ground floor, and we saw they are clean, comfortable, and contain plenty of seating and space for
people to socialise and relax. The lounge and dining room layouts on both sides encourage social
interaction while also providing quieter areas for privacy. People also have access to shared
outdoor spaces which are secure and accessible from main living areas on both sides of the home.

Systems are in place to support people's safety and sense of security. Access to the building is
controlled through secure arrangements and environmental risk management systems were in
place. Visitors must sign a logbook as they enter and leave the premises; this is essential for fire
safety and tracks who is in the building. We saw servicing and maintenance arrangements are in
place for key equipment and facilities in the home, including fire safety systems, hoists, lifts and call
bell systems. Fire safety arrangements are improving, although additional staff training and
continued monitoring were identified following a recent drill. The manager is taking action to
address this.

The provider has made good progress in relation to deep cleaning of communal areas since the
last inspection. We saw evidence of deep cleaning in several areas of the home and saw systems
for identifying and recording and actioning of repairs and maintenance issues. Some environmental
issues remain since the last inspection. Issues across the home include worn flooring in some
communal areas and decorative repairs and refurbishment required for some bedrooms. While
leaders are aware of these matters and have plans to address them, further progress is required to
ensure the environment consistently supports people's well-being, comfort and safety. At the last
inspection in in December 2025, the rating awarded for environment was ‘Requires improvement’.
Having considered the progress made to date, the improvements still required, and the provider’s
plans to address them, on balance we consider outcomes for people in relation to the environment
are improved and the environment is now rated as ‘Good’. We expect the provider to have
sufficiently addressed the remaining improvements at the next inspection.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service has experienced positive changes in leadership and management since previous
inspections. Staff, relatives and external professionals describe a more positive culture within the
home. Staff told us they feel supported, listened to and valued by the manager and provider. We
reviewed records of regular staff meetings, improved supervision arrangements and a strong focus
on communication, teamwork and improving outcomes for people.

The provider has strengthened recruitment, supervision and training arrangements for staff. Staff
files demonstrate safe recruitment practices, and supervision records show staff receive regular
support from the manager, including consideration of their wellbeing. Staffing levels have stabilised,
and agency use is much reduced. The atmosphere throughout the home is calm and relaxed, and
staff work at the pace of the people they support. People’s needs are met in a timely manner by
good numbers of regular staff, including when agency staff are used, who are familiar to people.
This supports consistency and continuity of care for people.

The provider has implemented a range of regular audits covering areas such as medication
management, infection prevention and control, accidents and incidents, safeguarding, weight
monitoring and health and safety. Oversight visits from leaders take place regularly and reports
identify areas requiring improvement. This demonstrates a commitment to monitoring the quality of
the service.

At the last inspection in December 2025 Leadership and Management was rated as ‘Requires
improvement’ because of issues with quality assurance systems oversight of the RI (responsible
person in charge of this service). We raised these issues as areas for improvement at the last
inspection. Despite progress made since the last inspection, we saw governance and oversight
arrangements require further strengthening. Oversight reports identify concerns and revisit previous
issues, but do not consistently demonstrate clear action planning, completion of actions, evaluation
of effectiveness or sustained improvement. A quality of care review has not yet been completed in
line with the regulations and there was no registered RI in post at the time of our inspection visit.
While there is evidence the prospective RI is actively overseeing the service and driving
improvements, regulatory requirements relating to oversight, quality assurance and evidencing
outcomes are not fully met still. These matters do not appear to be having an impact on people,
and actions taken to date by the provider and prospective RI are reducing risks to their wellbeing.
However, further improvement is needed to ensure governance systems consistently support high-
quality care and positive outcomes. Therefore, on balance, and having considered all the
information and evidence gathered at this inspection, we have decided that leadership and
management are now rated as ‘Good’. We expect the provider to have addressed these issues at
the next inspection

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
