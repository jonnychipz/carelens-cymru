Summary:

 Trevelyan is a mental health service that provides support to adults who have a functional mental
 illness requiring personal care and support. It is in the rural village of Bronwydd, three miles from
 the market town of Carmarthen.

 The well-being outcomes of people is “Excellent” as people receive exemplary care and support to
 achieve self-directed outcomes relating to their physical, mental and emotional health.

 The care and support people receive is “Excellent” as highly skilled care staff understand and
 support people as individuals consistently considering their wishes, aspirations, risks and specialist
 needs.

 The environment of which people live is “Excellent” as people benefit from living in a home that
 meets their individual needs and places them at the heart of the service.

 The leadership and management of the service is “Excellent” as leaders promote a strong positive
 culture that is supportive, inclusive and respectful.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People receive extensive care and support to live well and achieve self-directed outcomes. Care
 staff provide exemplary support that helps people maintain and improve their physical, mental and
 emotional health. Care staff treat people with dignity and respect. They actively support people to
 identify their well-being outcomes and encourage them to use and build on their strengths.
 Managers have developed and continue to promote a positive reablement and “can-do” culture
 across the service. Care staff help people overcome barriers by building trust and working in
 partnership with them and external professionals.

 People access highly tailored care and support that meets their individual needs. People play an
 active and integral role in care planning and review. They lead the development of their outcome-
 based personal plans. Care staff empower people to thrive by supporting them to maintain, develop
 and explore their interests, strengths and skills. This includes volunteering, work-based activities
 and involvement in charitable events. People told us, and records confirmed, they enjoyed
 holidays, meals out, music concerts, walking rescue dogs, playing pool and learning to play musical
 instruments. People enjoy individual activities and spend time together, which gives the service a
 strong community spirit.

 Staff always prioritise people’s well-being and safety by identifying and managing risks effectively.
 The service promotes a strengths-based approach and supports positive risk taking. Care staff
 encourage people to develop the skills they need to move towards more independent living. Care
 staff discuss identified risks openly with people and maintain excellent communication with external
 professionals, which supports people to progress. The provider has comprehensive personal and
 environmental risk assessments in place. The manager and Responsible Individual (RI) complete
 detailed audits. Care staff understand safeguarding and risk management processes and access
 support from internal and external professionals when needed.

 People live in accommodation that significantly enhances their quality of life. They personalise their
 accommodation, take ownership of it and describe it as home. The provider maintains the service
 extremely well, creating a strong community ethos that promotes independence and prepares
 people to move on. Managers oversee maintenance and health and safety procedures effectively.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive exceptional care and support because care staff consistently consider their wishes,
aspirations, risks and specialist needs. The provider maintains strong, established links with local
mental health, health and social care services, which helps people achieve excellent outcomes.
Visiting professionals gave exceptionally positive feedback about the service. The provider works
closely with professionals involved in people’s care to gather detailed information and plan effective
support.

We found personal plans were person-centred and strengths-based, and they place people at the
centre of decisions about their care and support. The plans identified risks clearly and explained
how staff should manage them through comprehensive risk management plans. Care staff keep
people safe through an exceptionally strong approach to safeguarding. People take an active role
in personal planning and review. They meet care staff regularly and informally to discuss their
personal plans and the outcomes they want to achieve. The service successfully prepares and
supports people to move towards more independent living. One person now manages a tenancy
successfully, and another person was working towards this at the time of inspection.

Highly skilled care staff support people and understand them well as individuals. The provider
recruits professional, experienced care staff who demonstrate strong values. Care staff encourage
people, remain positive and make sure people lead decisions about their care and support. The
provider gives care staff high-quality training and support based on the needs of people using the
service. Regular, open communication supports trusted working relationships between people and
care staff. Detailed care planning documentation gives care staff the information they need to
provide highly effective support.

Care staff manage people’s medication safely through high-quality management systems. Care
staff store medication correctly to maintain its efficacy and safety. The provider keeps the
medication room well organised and has fitted an air-conditioning unit to control the temperature
adequately. Care staff who administer medication understand and follow the comprehensive
corporate medication policy. Medication administration records were accurate and in good order.
Senior care staff complete regular medication audits and check care staff competencies.

       Environment                              Excellent



People benefit from living in a home that meets their individual needs and places them at the heart
of the service. Trevelyan provides a warm, homely and positive reablement environment where
people feel relaxed and valued. People told us they enjoyed living at the home. Their comments
included, “It’s lovely here, I feel very happy” and “I enjoy living here”. Professionals who know the
service well told us the service continues to make a significant difference to people’s lives.

People use high-quality indoor and outdoor communal spaces freely. The kitchen diner and
communal lounge are well used, nicely decorated and reflect people’s interests, including personal
music memorabilia. People personalise their bedrooms and the external studio apartment, and the
provider maintains these areas to a very high standard. People were proud to show us these areas
of the home. We saw strong evidence that the provider continues to invest in the environment,
including recent upgrades to two-bathroom areas and plans to replace communal carpets.

People enjoy a mature and well-maintained rear garden, which includes a relaxation area, small
lawn and patio area used for barbecues and parties in warmer weather. The garden also includes a
small storage shed and smoking shed. Staff encourage and support people to take part  in
maintaining the indoor and outdoor areas of the home. The front of the property provides a large
parking area and easy access to a bus route into Carmarthen.

The provider supports people’s care and support needs through highly effective health and safety
and risk management systems. Care staff maintain the building to a high standard and complete
relevant maintenance and health and safety checks. The provider keeps all required certificates in
place. Fire safety arrangements are to a very high standard and supported by stringent fire risk
assessments. Care staff complete regular fire evacuation procedures, and the provider plans to
replace several fire doors.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leaders promote a strong positive culture that is supportive, inclusive and respectful. We saw
leaders act as visible role models. They set the strategic direction for the service and support the
pace of change. Care staff promote a strong “can-do” culture by encouraging and supporting
people to achieve their goals and aspirations. The service places a clear focus on supporting
people to move towards independent living.

People, care staff and visiting professionals hold the manager in high regard. The manager is
hands-on, visible and accessible to people, staff and visiting professionals. Professionals who know
the service well told us, “It’s an excellent service, it’s the first place I look for any vacancies” and
“Communication is always very good”. The RI visits the service regularly and is well known to care
staff, people and their representatives. Care staff gave excellent feedback about the manager and
RI. Their comments include, “They are so supportive” and “They are always there when we need
them”.


Leaders involve commissioners, professionals, staff and people in extensive quality assurance
processes. They use feedback to drive continuous improvement. Governance arrangements are
robust and well embedded, including high-quality quarterly and six-monthly reports. These reports
evidence the range of outcomes people achieve. The management team completes a broad range
of detailed audits, identifies emerging issues early and addresses them promptly.

People achieve excellent outcomes because the provider ensures the service has enough skilled,
knowledgeable and well-supported care staff. We found a consistent and experienced care staff
team, with low turnover and no use of agency workers. Care staff receive extensive training that
reflects the needs of people living at the service. The provider also supports care staff through
highly effective, individualised supervision processes. Supervision focuses on care staff wellbeing
and on how care staff support people’s wellbeing. Communication is regular and effective through
daily handovers, house meetings and staff team meetings. The manager continues to prioritise
whole staff development and supports care staff to maintain and develop their knowledge and
skills.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
