Summary:

 Llanrhaeadr Hall is a care home for older people, some of whom have dementia care needs. The
 service is provided in a beautiful, characterful listed building with spacious grounds.
 People’s wellbeing is excellent because they are treated with dignity and respect, supported to
 make choices, and enjoy a wide range of tailored, meaningful activities.
 Care and support is good as people are consistently treated with kindness, empathy and
 compassion. Comprehensive assessments and care planning ensure people receive support in
 their preferred way. We saw examples of excellent care, including respect for people’s differences
 and language, and support to do what matters to them. Staff regularly go above and beyond. The
 manager is reviewing care planning and reviews to strengthen outcome-focused practice.
 The environment is good. The home and grounds provide ample space, giving people choice about
 how and where they spend their time. The environment is safe, well maintained, and continually
 reviewed for improvement.
 Leadership and management is good because the RI (responsible individual for the service) has
 established systems in place that provide good oversight of the whole operation. Effective oversight
 identifies strengths and areas for development. Recruitment includes robust vetting, and staff
 retention is excellent because employees feel supported and valued. We also saw strong examples
 of reflective learning and the sharing of good practice with other providers.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. Choices and preferences, interest and
 hobbies are thoroughly discussed as part of the assessment prior to and during the first week of
 admission into the home. Care documentation consistently illustrates freedom of choice; some
 people have opted out of the two hourly night checks, routines are flexible so people can carry out
 daily activities, such as waking and sleeping time, mealtimes and support with personal care. One
 person shared how they opted out of activities on the day we visited, preferring the peace and quiet
 of the entrance lounge. People are supported by positive risk management; one person has a
 manageable easy pour kettle and a fridge in their room; people are encouraged to use outside
 gardens and courtyard and take walks up the long drive. A well-designed activities programme is
 tailored to suit individual’s interests; one person enjoys having Shakespeare poetry read to them,
 another plays the piano and enjoys Welsh songs. People partake in Tai Chi and other exercise
 suited to their physical abilities. Activities are creative and person centred. Everyone shared how
 excellent the service is and how well they feel because of it.

 Residents’ meetings provide an opportunity for staff to seek preferences on things such as the
 seasonal menu, recently requesting more salads than hot food. Everyone we spoke with shared
 how much they enjoy the variety, portions and taste of the food provided. People like that they can
 talk in their first language of Welsh as several staff speak the language; additionally, signage and
 documents are bilingual.

 People told us they feel safe and that they can speak with the manager and staff about their
 worries and concerns. We observed relatives and staff taking advantage of the open-door policy,
 speaking with the management team freely. Any decision to place restrictions on a person’s liberty
 are a multi-disciplinary matter, approved by the relevant authority. A visiting professional gave
 credit to the service for proactively seeking to minimise the use of sedatives for people with
 dementia.

 People are supported to maintain and develop their friendships and family relationships. A couple
 living in the home but in different units are supported to spend their days together. Another person
 recalled how the service had allowed their husband to stay over on special occasions, provided
 free lunches when he visited and sandwiches to take away for their evening meal. The home’s
 layout with various lounges, dining rooms and seating areas, allows people to sit with those they
 get on with best.

       Care & Support                       Good





People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes
because care is designed in consultation with people, considering their wishes, aspirations, risks,
and specialist needs. The manager is reviewing current care documentation to ensure people’s
outcomes are explicit and at the centre of the care. People experience continuity of care, as most
staff have worked at the home for years. Residents and relatives, and a visiting professional
consistently praised the care, food, activities, environment and staff. We saw numerous cards,
emails, texts and letters from relatives paying tribute to the staff for amazing care. They praise the
range of activities. There are framers living in the home and staff have arranged the local, annual
‘tractor run’ to stop at the home. Many events are arranged to which family are invited.

One visitor said, ‘everyone is welcoming, nothing too much trouble. It’s always spotless. Food is
great. Mum is so well looked after. She has her own room upstairs like an apartment. Staff are
aways popping in to see her. Its home from home’.

Another similarly praised the cleanliness. ‘His room is always clean and the bedding is changed
daily. He has showers regularly and always in clean clothes. He loves the food here. X used to stay
in his room mostly but now goes into lounge for activities’. Another relative said ‘we are happier
than we thought we’d ever be’. Everyone consistently agreed, ‘there are always plenty of staff
around’. When asked what is most impressive about the home, one of the two activities organisers
said ‘There’s a lot of love here. It is a close and cohesive community’.

Records evidence health and wellbeing is monitored closely. The visiting health professional
shared how vigilant the service is, quick to seek guidance when needed. The service arranged for
bereavement counselling for one person. One staff gave an example of how slight nuances in body
language suggested a person was less comfortable with them being a male carer, even though no
objection was verbally raised, and now only female care staff attend them. Staff are attentive and
know the residents well.

People’s medication is safely managed. Medication arrangements are frequently audited, for
completion of records, security of the medication and storage temperatures. Only trained and
competent staff administer medication.

       Environment                         Good





People live in an environment that helps them achieve their wellbeing outcomes safely. There are
risk assessments in place to keep people safe in the home, and evacuation plans for each person
in case of an emergency. Fire safety services come into the home to carry out checks on
equipment, alarms, lighting and door closers. The manager routinely conducts similar checks.
Other checks are carried out to ensure water, gas and electrical safety. Areas identified for
maintenance and renewal at the home are logged and swiftly resolved by the service’s own
maintenance person. Buildings audits are routinely completed, and a building development plan is
in place to ensure continued improvement. The Food Standards Agency have awarded the service
level 5 which means it is very good. Infection control measures are audited by the local health
board and we saw minor recommendations were swiftly addressed.

The design and layout promote privacy and dignity; there are a variety of places to spend time and
people who need assistance with their meals are supported discreetly. Staff encourage people with
dementia to come to the ‘café’ for their meals and make dining a pleasant, social experience.
Throughout, there is a homely and welcoming environment, domestic in appearance ad well lit.
Bedrooms are personalised with people’s own photos, paintings and things of interest to them.
Some people have SMART televisions and access the home’s Wi-Fi. One bedroom is laid out to
provide a level of independence, where the resident can make their own drinks and keep their
snacks. All rooms have adequate furniture for their needs and the gardens are maintained. We
suggested more discreet storage of personal products and better storage of mobility aids, and the
manager set out to resolve this on the day of the visit.

       Leadership & Management               Good





People are supported well to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
governance arrangements in place and good oversight of the service. The manager and RI
complete a range of quality and compliance checks to ensure effective, quality provision and
practice. Care records, policies, practices, safety compliance and the environment are all checked
on a routine basis. The RI visits the home every three months to complete checks and speak with
people, visitors and staff about the quality of the service. This information is evaluated and forms
part of the biannual quality of care review reports. The RI knows what is working well and where
improvements need to be made. Staff know how to raise concerns, and all said they are happy to
speak openly with the manager about such matters. We saw lots of complements and expressions
of thanks for care provided to relatives.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. Staffing numbers consistently allow for unrushed, person-centred
care. We spoke to many relatives and people using the service and they all complimented staffing
levels. Additionally, they each praised staff for their compassion, sense of humour and
cheerfulness, many describing a sense of family and home.

Records evidence staff are safely vetted prior to employment and then inducted so they become
familiar with the service, it’s aims and its processes, practices and policies. There is a range of
mandatory training in relevant areas so staff are equipped with the right knowledge. There are
systems in place to ensure each staff then receives one on one support every three months. A
number of staff have been promoted from carer to senior carer showing personal development in
the team is facilitated and encouraged.

There is good continuity of staff which is testament to management approach. Staff shared how
they feel well supported and appreciated and valued by the management team and the residents.
They enjoy working here and want to stay. Staff said the best thing here is the genuine care and
warmth from staff to residents. ‘It’s a lovely place to work’.

The home enjoys a good reputation and allows other care home providers to visit to see what they
do. The management team encourage reflective practice learning from each other and other homes
in the organisation. It is evident the service provider is keen to look for ways of improving and also
sharing good practice.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
