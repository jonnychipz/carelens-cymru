Summary:

 Briarswood is a small, friendly, family run home on the outskirts of Llanelli, providing support to
 people with learning disabilities.

 People have good wellbeing outcomes because they have choice and control over their day to day
 lives and are involved in decisions that affect them. They are treated with dignity, kindness and
 compassion from care staff who know them well. People are supported to do the things that matter
 to them.

 Care and support are good and personal plans are detailed, up to date and inform care staff on
 how best to support people. Care is provided by a small, stable team that provide consistency and
 have a good understanding of people’s needs.

 The environment is comfortable and homely, and people are supported to remain active within their
 community. We identified that some actions are required to optimise fire safety as advised by the
 Fire Service. This is an area for improvement and we expect the provider to address this as a
 priority.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to achieve positive well-being outcomes and live in a home where they are
 valued, respected and treated with dignity. We observed warm, caring and meaningful interactions
 between staff and people living at the home. People appeared relaxed, comfortable and at ease in
 the presence of staff, demonstrating positive and trusting relationships.

 People are encouraged and supported to make choices about their day-to-day lives. Records
 demonstrated that personal preferences and individual wishes are recognised and respected. Care
 documentation included accessible information, photographs and pictorial formats to support
 understanding and participation in decisions. We saw people choosing what they wanted to eat for
 breakfast and lunch and they are empowered to prepare their own meals.

 People are supported to maintain good health and access the relevant professionals when
 required. Care records evidenced involvement from health and social care professionals, and
 discussions with staff demonstrated a good understanding of people's individual needs.

 People are encouraged to remain active and participate in activities meaningful to them. Individuals
 access community resources, enjoy hobbies and maintain relationships with family and friends, if
 appropriate. Professionals told us people are happy living at the service and considered it their
 home. One person spoke positively about attending activities in the community and maintaining
 important social connections.

 People are protected from abuse and neglect. Safeguarding arrangements are in place and staff
 have an understanding of their responsibilities for keeping people safe. Risk assessments are
 comprehensive and reflect people's individual circumstances whilst promoting independence and
 positive risk-taking.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive personalised care and support that reflects their assessed needs and desired
outcomes. We reviewed care records and found detailed, person-centred plans containing
comprehensive information about people's histories, preferences, health conditions, support needs
and personal aspirations. Documentation is written respectfully and in a format accessible to the
individuals concerned. Records demonstrating how people achieve their personal outcomes could
be further strengthened by including more detailed evaluative information.

Risk assessments are detailed, regularly reviewed and provide clear guidance for staff. Specialist
plans are in place where required, including behaviour support and management of certain health
conditions. Staff demonstrate a clear understanding of people's needs and how support should be
delivered safely and consistently.

People experience continuity of care through a stable staff team that know them well. Discussions
with a professional confirmed that people have positive relationships with staff and benefit from
consistent support. Staff work effectively with external professionals to ensure people's health and
well-being needs are met.

Medication arrangements are generally safe. Medicines are stored securely, temperature checks
are completed, staff have received relevant training and medication administration records (MAR)
sampled were appropriately completed. Controlled drug arrangements are in place and stock
balances corresponded with records. Although the manager undertakes regular medication checks,
these are currently not formally recorded. Formal medication audits would strengthen oversight
arrangements.

Infection prevention and control measures are effective and appropriate. The environment is clean,
tidy and well stocked with personal protective equipment. Staff have completed infection prevention
and control training and appropriate hygiene practices were evident throughout the inspection.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a comfortable, homely environment that supports their well-being. Bedrooms are
personalised with photographs, personal possessions and items of significance to individuals.
People have access to communal areas and safe outdoor spaces which they enjoy using. One
person is supported to pursue their interest in gardening and enjoys maintaining plants within the
grounds.

The home is clean, tidy and well maintained. Appropriate arrangements are in place for the storage
of hazardous substances and equipment. Safety measures such as window restrictors, fire safety
systems and secure storage facilities were evident.

We reviewed maintenance and servicing arrangements and found evidence of ongoing checks,
including fire safety monitoring. The fire service has recently completed a safety check and have
recommended some minor adjustments. The provider has begun this work however some actions
are outstanding. We have identified this as an area for improvement and expect this to be
addressed as a priority.

Appropriate facilities are available to meet the needs of people living at the service. People are
supported within an environment that promotes independence and enables access to communal
and outdoor areas. The premises provide a homely and familiar setting which contributes positively
to people's sense of home and belonging

Some areas of the premises would benefit from refurbishment, including flooring in communal
areas and carpet in one bedroom. Whilst these issues were not having a significant impact on
people currently living at the home, elements of décor and furnishings are showing some signs of
wear.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The proprietor is the manager and although is not registered as the Responsible Individual, is the
appointed person who takes on this role. There is good oversight of the service and the manager is
actively involved and present at the service most days. A small but dedicated team work closely
together to ensure smooth running of the service. The manager covers a high number of shifts to
ensure people continue to receive care and support from staff they know well. The provider is
required to register the service to meet legal and regulatory requirements. We saw positive
relationships between management, staff and people receiving care.

There is a stable and suitably trained staff team. Staff receive induction, supervision, appraisal and
ongoing training to support them in their roles. Staff are registered with Social Care Wales and
training records demonstrated that mandatory training requirements are being met.

The service works with professionals and external agencies to support good outcomes for people.
One professional told us, “From day one they have been extremely accommodating. They have
supported them and ensured that every activity during their break has been focused on them. They
have supported us in emergencies and staff have been professional and supportive through out.
The management have got back to us straight away if we have had to contact them and always
accommodated where they have been able too”.


Policies and procedures are available and a sample have been reviewed. These were
comprehensive and relevant and are evaluated by the manager regularly. Systems for recording
incidents, safeguarding matters and notifications are in place. Feedback mechanisms and
satisfaction surveys are used to gather views about the quality of the service.

Staff described receiving good support from management and confirmed they had access to
training and supervision. Staffing levels were suitable to meet the needs of people currently living at
the home.

Whilst quality assurance arrangements are in place, we found some audits were being undertaken
informally but not consistently documented. Formal recording of governance activity would
strengthen provider oversight and provide clearer evidence of quality monitoring.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
