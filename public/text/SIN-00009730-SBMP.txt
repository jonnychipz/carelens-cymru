Wellbeing during the inspection was excellent. People experience warm, respectful, person-centred
care that promotes dignity, independence, and choice. Staff build meaningful relationships, adapt
communication, and provide varied activities that support emotional well-being. Residents feel
involved, informed, and connected, with strong support for relationships and community
engagement.

Care and support was excellent during the inspection. The service delivers timely, person-centred
care with strong multidisciplinary collaboration. Staff know individuals well, escalate concerns
promptly, and maintain accurate records. Clear pre-admission processes, regular reviews, adaptive
communication, and responsive support ensure needs are consistently met and well-being is
prioritised.

Environment was good in the most recent inspection. The service maintains strong health and
safety arrangements, with regular audits and ongoing investment in improvements. Bedrooms and
communal areas are upgraded, personalised, and well maintained. Robust fire safety measures,
effective maintenance systems, and accessible facilities support a safe, comfortable living
environment.

Leadership and management was excellent. The service benefits from strong, visible leadership
with robust governance, regular audits, and effective supervision. Staff receive ongoing
development and feel well supported. Leaders actively seek feedback, drive continuous
improvement, and maintain a positive, open culture that promotes high-quality, person-centred
care.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People consistently experience warm, respectful, and person-centred care. Staff respond quickly to
 expressed needs, approach people at eye level, and hold natural, friendly conversations that show
 genuine interest in each person’s life. Staff encourage independence by offering meaningful
 choices, supporting positive risk-taking, and involving people in activities such as dancing, crafts,
 and eating independently. This reflects a strong “doing with, not for” culture that promotes dignity,
 autonomy, and confidence.

 Choice is embedded throughout daily routines. People receive meal alternatives, and picture
 menus help individuals understand options. An up-to-date activities board clearly displays the day’s
 programme. Regular residents’ meetings allow people to influence menus, discuss safety, revisit
 their rights, and choose whether to hold meetings without staff present. These arrangements
 strengthen voice, involvement, and access to information.

 People benefit from a varied mix of planned and spontaneous occupation, including quizzes,
 entertainers, crafts, dancing, and social conversation. Photographs of events and the presence of a
 well-liked resident cat offer emotional comfort and companionship. Staff adapt communication
 methods sensitively, using Welsh language, visual prompts, or photographs to enhance
 understanding.

 Although communal lounges were not in use during the visit, they remain available as additional
 social spaces, providing a quieter space if required.

 Staff support people to maintain meaningful relationships. Relatives receive a warm welcome, and
 staff help people connect through visits, letters, phone calls, and video calls. New residents report
 feeling welcomed and supported to settle in. The service promotes community connections and
 continues to seek solutions to transport limitations to maximise engagement.

 Overall, people experience supportive relationships, meaningful occupation, and a culture that
 values their voice, choice, and control. These factors contribute strongly to their overall well-being.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



The service works proactively with health and social care professionals to meet people’s assessed
needs. Staff make timely referrals to audiology, physiotherapy, frailty, falls, mental health, and
dementia-mapping services. A GP visits weekly, and staff share written updates beforehand to
support effective ward rounds. Staff escalate concerns promptly and seek specialist guidance
where required.

The deputy manager leads the weekly ward round and documents each person’s needs and
outcomes. The GP reviews this information, and staff update care records immediately. Care staff
add relevant observations between reviews, ensuring professionals always receive accurate,
current information.

The pre-admission process is clear and well organised. Staff review care plans in advance and
encourage prospective residents and families to visit informally. At admission, staff gather essential
information including mobility assessments, GP summaries, and advance care plans. Staff discuss
individual preferences and invite people to share a meal to experience the home’s routines. The
provider considers clinical information, dependency levels, staffing capacity, and time required for
support to determine whether they can meet a person’s needs.

Staff review care plans monthly or sooner if needs change. They hold conversations with
individuals about what matters to them and use tools such as This Is Me to identify personal goals,
including improving mobility or reducing loneliness. Staff encourage people who prefer time alone
to take part in activities, while respecting their choices. Staff maintain open communication with
families and involve them appropriately in decision-making.

Staff adapt communication sensitively, using whiteboards, picture cards, talking books, and
alternative formats. Staff request detailed hospital discharge information to ensure equipment and
support are in place. They respond quickly to distressed behaviour, work with families to identify
triggers, and provide one-to-one support when necessary. Mental health teams deliver additional
training to increase confidence and competence.

People consistently receive compassionate, timely, person-centred care. Staff demonstrate strong
knowledge of people’s histories and needs. Professionals confirm that staff escalate concerns
quickly and communicate well. Robust governance and regular audits help staff identify risks early
and act appropriately.

       Environment                         Good



Health and safety arrangements are robust. All essential safety certifications were found to be up to
date, and the service maintains a comprehensive compliance matrix that clearly tracks when
external inspections and testing are due.

Monthly monitoring covers environmental risks, mattresses, wheelchairs, pressure equipment, pest
control, maintenance tasks, and evacuation arrangements. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans
are in place for all residents and updated monthly.

The provider continues to invest in improvements. Bathrooms have been renewed, corridors
repainted in resident-chosen colours, and carpets replaced in communal areas and some
bedrooms. Five bedrooms are scheduled to receive en-suite facilities during 2026. A number of
improvements have recently been completed such as, an accessible shower room, bathroom
panelling to improve infection control, and upgraded toilets to higher models to aid mobility.

The service has installed new windows, replaced part of the roof, replastered areas, installed new
lighting, and added extractor fans. A quiet room with a piano and another with a smart-speaker
device offer spaces for relaxation. When rooms become vacant, maintenance staff repaint and
replace carpets unless deep cleaning is appropriate. Cleaners complete thorough deep cleans after
room changes.

A three-person maintenance team completes most internal work. Staff report issues through a
logbook, which managers review weekly. Managers complete routine walk-arounds, and
housekeeping staff record environmental concerns through daily cleaning sheets.

Fire safety arrangements are robust. The fire service completes annual inspections, and an
external company maintains the fire alarm system. The provider recently installed a new alarm
system that meets updated regulations, and no concerns were raised at the last inspection.
The service promotes personalisation. During inspection a “loft-style” bedroom for one resident was
observed. Other personalisation includes; chosen colours, fire-retardant curtains, and preferred
bedding. The provider funds additional items when needed to support comfort and well-being.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



The service demonstrates strong, consistent leadership. Managers complete regular audits across
all practice areas, providing effective oversight and identifying trends, risks, and required actions.
Leaders meet with people at least monthly to gather feedback, and a clear governance schedule
ensures essential monitoring occurs reliably.

Supervision arrangements are well established. Staff receive regular supervision, supported by
appraisals and development plans. Managers observe practice directly and deliver targeted
workshops to strengthen skills. When issues arise, leaders provide immediate learning sessions to
prevent further concern. Staff report that this creates an open, supportive learning culture. The
provider promotes professional development through access to accredited training, leadership
programmes, and Health and Social Care qualifications.

Leadership is visible and engage a wide range of individuals in the quality assurance processes.
Valuing their feedback to drive continuous, highly effective and sustainable improvements. This
inclusive approach ensures dynamic improvement plans that align with best practices in social
care, significantly enhancing outcomes for people.

The Responsible Individual (RI) visits frequently, meets with people and families, and reviews
service data. RI audits cover a broad range of governance areas. Managers also conduct
comprehensive audits in infection control, health and safety, staffing, care planning, and regulatory
compliance.

Managers’ report regular supervision with the RI and maintain weekly leadership meetings.
Decisions made by the RI are people centred and adapt according to the needs of the people they
support. Staff describe high levels of managerial support and strong team cohesion. Team
meetings focus on responsibilities, shared learning, and improvement. People and relatives give
positive feedback about leadership, and the service has recorded no complaints since 2024.

Overall, the service benefits from strong leadership, effective governance, and a supportive staff
culture, with clear priorities for ongoing development.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
