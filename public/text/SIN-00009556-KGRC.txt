Summary:

 Well-being is good because people receive care from a committed staff team who know them well
 and support them to live active and meaningful lives. People are treated with dignity and respect
 and are supported to make choices about their daily lives. Care staff respect people’s preferences,
 communicating in a patient, professional and kind manner.


 Care and support is good. People have access to healthcare services, meaningful activities and
 opportunities to participate in their local community. Relatives and staff spoke positively about the
 quality of support provided. Comprehensive assessments and care plans, which are reviewed
 regularly, provide care staff with clear guidance to support people safely and effectively.


 The environment is good. It is clean, spacious and designed to meet the sensory needs of people
 living at the service. Bedrooms are personalised and people have access to well-maintained indoor
 and outdoor spaces which supports their wellbeing, privacy and independence. Robust systems
 are in place to monitor health and safety.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Well-being is good because people are supported to live healthy and fulfilling lives, exercise choice
 and maintain positive relationships. Care staff know people well and support them in a way which
 promotes dignity, respect and independence. We observed warm and positive interactions. Care
 staff communicate effectively with people, including those who use non-verbal forms of
 communication, and respond appropriately to individual preferences and wishes. People appear
 relaxed, comfortable and confident in their surroundings, demonstrating they feel secure in their
 home environment.


 People are supported to make meaningful choices about how they spend their time and are
 supported to achieve their personal goals. Care staff encourage people to take part in activities
 which interest them and help them maintain strong links with their local community. People
 undertake a variety of activities appropriate to their abilities, ranging from activities in the
 community to low arousal hobbies and interests at home. Activity plans are personalised and reflect
 people’s interests. Relatives told us people go out regularly and enjoy meaningful experiences.


 People are safeguarded from abuse and neglect. Care staff receive safeguarding training and are
 aware of the procedures for reporting concerns. A safeguarding policy is in place and aligned with
 current statutory guidance, supporting care staff to respond appropriately when issues arise. Risks
 to people’s health and safety are assessed and managed effectively, helping to promote people’s
 well-being. Policies and procedures for complaints and whistleblowing are accessible. Risk
 assessments highlighting areas of concern are completed and regularly reviewed.


 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. Care staff create a calm,
 structured and low-arousal environment which supports people’s emotional well-being. They
 understand the impact sensory experiences can have on individuals and adapt support accordingly.
 Bedrooms are personalised and reflect people's interests and identities. One person proudly
 showed us their room containing photographs, personal possessions and memorabilia linked to
 interests important to them. Another person told us, “I love it here" and described the service as
 “like home." Relatives spoke positively about the care and support provided, the commitment of
 staff and the opportunities available to people living at the service.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care and support is good because people receive personalised care and support which reflects
their assessed needs, preferences and desired outcomes. The service completes detailed
assessments prior to people moving in. A planned transition process enables staff to get to know
people, understand their needs and preferences, and determine whether the service can provide
appropriate support. The provider considers people's language preferences and can access Welsh
language resources when required.


Care staff know people very well and demonstrate a strong understanding of their communication
methods, sensory preferences, daily routines and behavioural support needs. Care staff use this
knowledge to provide consistent, person-centred support which reflects individual wishes and
promotes wellbeing. Personal plans are detailed, regularly reviewed and supported by
comprehensive risk assessments. The plans provide clear guidance about how care staff should
support people safely while promoting independence, choice and control.


People are protected from abuse and neglect. Staff understand their safeguarding responsibilities
and can describe how they would report concerns. Systems are in place to record and monitor
safeguarding matters. The service has a Positive Behaviour Management (PBM) lead who provides
oversight, guidance and specialist support to care staff. This helps ensure behaviour support
approaches remain proactive, person-centred and focused on improving quality of life.


People receive their medication as prescribed and care staff assist them in accordance with
national guidance and service policy. Care staff complete appropriate training and competency
assessments before administering medication. People have timely access to healthcare services
as required, ensuring their physical and emotional wellbeing is monitored and supported.
Medication storage and administration is well-managed.


The service promotes good infection prevention and control practices. The service is clean and well
maintained. Sufficient personal protective equipment is available, and staff understand infection
control procedures. Processes are in place to monitor hygiene standards and cleaning activities.
Care staff follow cleaning schedules and ensure communal areas, kitchens and other facilities
remain clean and well presented. Kitchen facilities support safe food preparation and storage
practices. This includes designated space for people with allergies. Laundry facilities meet the
needs of the service, and staff store cleaning products securely in accordance with Control of
Substances Hazardous to Health (COSHH) requirements.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is good because people live at a service which is safe, comfortable and suitable
for their needs. The service has been adapted to support adults with autism and provides a low-
arousal environment to help reduce anxiety and promote well-being. The service consists of a main
house, and two annexes on site. These annexes enable people to live on their own and provide
private and personalised homes which reflect individual preferences, interests and lifestyles. This
means people can have control over their daily routines, make choices about how they spend their
time and enjoy a greater degree of independence. All areas of the service are personalised with
people’s belongings, furnishings and decorations, creating familiar and comfortable living spaces
which support their sense of identity and belonging.


The service provides access to shared facilities, including a communal kitchen, lounge, dining room
and garden. These areas encourage people to socialise, build and maintain relationships and take
part in activities with others. People choose when they wish to spend time in communal areas and
when they prefer the privacy of their own space. A family tree display incorporating photographs of
people and staff promotes inclusion and belonging within the service. The environment is designed
to meet the needs of people. Calm surroundings, predictable layouts and comfortable living spaces
help reduce unnecessary stress and support emotional well-being. The environment is welcoming,
homely and purposeful. We found the service clean, tidy and well maintained throughout.


The provider demonstrates a strong commitment to maintaining the premises and equipment to a
high standard. Effective systems ensure the environment remains safe, suitable and fit for purpose.
The provider arranges routine servicing and safety checks for utilities, including gas and electrical
installations, through appropriately qualified contractors. Staff promptly report maintenance issues,
and the provider takes action to address repairs in a timely manner. The service maintains robust
health and safety arrangements. Staff complete regular environmental checks and identify potential
risks promptly. Records confirm staff regularly test fire alarm systems and maintain an up-to-date
fire risk assessment. Each person has a Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan (PEEP) which
clearly outlines the support they require in an emergency. Staff participate in regular fire drills and
training, ensuring they understand their responsibilities and can respond effectively if an
emergency occurs.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management is good because robust governance and quality assurance processes
help ensure people receive safe and high-quality care. Management have a clear understanding of
the service's performance, recognising both areas of strength and opportunities for further
development, demonstrating an ongoing commitment to improvement. The Responsible Individual
(RI) is regularly involved in the service and maintains a strong presence. They have developed
positive relationships with people and staff, fostering an open, transparent and supportive culture
where feedback, concerns and ideas for improvement are encouraged and acted upon.


Systems are in place to evaluate and improve the quality and safety of the service. Regular
auditing, quality assurance activities, and feedback from people, staff, and professionals provide
management with oversight of service performance. Outcomes from these processes are used to
identify areas requiring development and to support continuous improvement. The RI produces a
quality of care report every six months, demonstrating ongoing oversight and identifying areas to
develop and improve the service.


People benefit from care provided by a skilled and committed workforce. Staff speak positively
about their roles and describe a supportive working environment where they feel listened to and
valued. They report managers are approachable, responsive and readily available to provide
advice and guidance. Staff demonstrate a good understanding of people's individual needs and are
focused on delivering care which reflects personal preferences and promotes positive outcomes.
Feedback consistently indicates the manager is supportive, accessible and dedicated to promoting
staff well-being.


The provider ensures staff have access to both mandatory and role-specific training to equip them
for their responsibilities. Management maintain oversight of training compliance and provide regular
supervision, giving staff opportunities to reflect on their practice, receive feedback, and discuss
professional development. This supports a competent workforce and helps ensure people receive
high-quality care and support. Recruitment processes support people's safety. Records show the
provider completes all required pre-employment and regulatory checks before staff begin work.
This helps ensure the service employs suitable staff with the appropriate skills, experience, and
character to work with vulnerable people.

 Areas identified for improvement
 Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
 immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
 action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


 Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
 people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
 Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
 improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
 and outcomes for people have improved.


 CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
