Summary:

 Priory House Care Home provides care and support for up to 29 people. The home is situated
 within Prestatyn town and close the local shops and amenities.

 People’s well-being requires improvement as they are not consistently living in accommodation that
 promotes or supports the achievement of their personal well-being outcomes. Shortcomings in the
 quality and maintenance of the living environment, are having a negative impact on people’s
 comfort, dignity and overall quality of life. People do always have a choice.

 Care and support require improvement due to inadequate arrangements for the safe storage of
 medication and oversight of weight loss and falls management. However, people benefit from a
 committed and dedicated staff team who know them well and provide appropriate support, helping
 individuals to maintain their well-being outcomes as far as possible.

The environment requires significant improvement. The premises are not being maintained to a
suitable standard and do not currently support the delivery of safe, dignified care. Poor upkeep,
outstanding repairs and maintenance issues present risks to people’s health, safety and well-being
and require urgent attention and investment from the provider.

Leadership and management also require significant improvement. The Responsible Individual has
not ensured effective governance, oversight or organisational arrangements to support the safe
and effective operation of the service or the delivery of consistently high-quality care

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 Personal care is delivered with sensitivity and respect, by good numbers of care staff. Daily care
 records show staff support people mostly in line with their assessed needs and personal plans.
 Records also showed when people declined aspects of care, this was documented appropriately.
 People appeared well cared for, wearing clean, appropriate clothing and presenting with a good
 standard of personal hygiene. Relatives spoken with consistently described people as being well
 presented and confirmed they were satisfied with the care their family members received. People
 living in the home told us staff responded when they used their call bells and said they felt
 supported when assistance was needed.

 The systems in place to help keep people safe require improvement. The RI monitors the quality of
 the service, however, has not identified the issues found at this inspection which is placing people,
 staff and visitors at risk of harm. There is no oversight of falls management, weight loss and the
 safe storage of medications need attention. People do not always have the correct equipment
 before they move into the home which has placed them at risk of harm. Staff are recruited safely
 and have received training in safeguarding and there is safeguarding policy in place. There is
 enough staff to meet people's needs, provide supervision and support and keep them safe.

 People do not always have control over their day to day lives. People told us they felt cared for and
 supported by staff and described staff as helpful. People said they were able to make choices
 about their daily routines, including deciding when to get up, go to bed and what clothes to wear.
 We observed positive interactions between people living at the service and saw people spending
 time together in communal areas. However, people's opportunities to engage in meaningful
 activities and maintain their well-being are inconsistent. Whilst some people told us they enjoyed
 organised activities and entertainment such as bingo, exercise sessions, arts and crafts, musical
 entertainment, hairdressing and nail care. Others said they felt bored between scheduled events
 and had little to occupy their time. There are not enough bathing facilities for people to choose
 from. Some people told us they were not given a choice in regard to their breakfast and meals.

 People live in a home which is not being well maintained or safe. Equipment is regularly serviced to
 ensure it is safe for use. Some areas of the home are in need of refurbishment and cleaning. Some
 curtains and blinds were broken. Storage of equipment and other items have been placed in
 communal areas therefore people cannot freely use these areas as described in the statement of
 purpose. Infection prevention control practices require improvement. The provider does not have a
 robust environment audit in place to ensure the home is maintained to an acceptable standard. The
 home had received a food hygiene rating of 1 (major improvement was necessary). Since the
 inspection, this area has been reviewed following remedial work undertaken by the provider and

       Care & Support
                                       Improvement


People are assessed before moving into the home. Information is obtained from health
professionals, people and their families to help determine whether the service can meet their
needs. Personal plans are developed using information gathered from a range of sources, including
people, relatives and professionals. We saw evidence management regularly reviewed care
records to help ensure they remained up to date. Personal plans contained detailed, person-
centred information to guide staff in meeting people's assessed needs. Daily notes are completed
consistently and demonstrated care is delivered in line with people's personal plans. Regular
reviews help ensure records remain accurate and reflect people's changing needs. Staff spoken
with knew people well and were able to describe how they supported individuals to achieve positive
outcomes.

However, despite these arrangements, we identified an instance where one person did not have
the correct equipment in place to support their safety. The management team took immediate
action once this was brought to their attention. Furthermore, the provider's quality assurance
systems had not consistently identified the issues found during this inspection relating to falls
management and the oversight of weight loss. This indicates that, while care planning and record-
keeping arrangements were generally effective, governance and auditing processes require
strengthening to ensure risks are identified and addressed proactively, and that people consistently
receive safe and effective care. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have
therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address this
issue.

People's nutritional needs are not consistently supported to the standard expected and require
improvement. Feedback regarding the quality of food was generally positive, with people describing
meals as enjoyable and stating they looked forward to them. However, we observed limited
availability of fresh fruit, salad items and vegetables within the home. In addition, there was little
evidence that fortified food options were routinely available for individuals requiring enhanced
nutritional support. Arrangements for monitoring and responding to weight loss require
strengthening. More robust oversight is needed to ensure changes in people's weight are identified
promptly and appropriate professional advice is sought without delay where required.

The service manages and administers medicines safely, but the storage requires improvement.
People consistently receive their prescribed medicines. Staff complete appropriate medicines
management training prior to administering medication, and competency assessments are
undertaken to ensure safe practice. However, improvements are required in relation to the storage
of medicines within people's rooms. We found that when medication storage temperatures
exceeded the recommended range, appropriate action was not always taken.

                                    Requires Significant
       Environment
                                       Improvement


People's needs are not always met because some areas of the environment and maintenance
arrangements require improvement. While the home appeared clean on initial inspection and many
bedrooms are personalised and homely, further observations identified areas requiring repair, deep
cleaning and better organisation. One bedroom required a deep clean. The carpet contained food
debris, there was a noticeable urine odour within the room, and the overbed table used for meals
had an accumulation of dried food spillages. Throughout the home, skirting boards were chipped
and damaged and some window furnishings were not maintained appropriately, with curtains in
several bedrooms not fully attached. We also observed broken blinds and a damaged window in a
communal lounge area which allowed a noticeable draught into the room. The large ground floor
lounge was being used as a storage area for chairs, curtains, a stained mattress, moving and
handling equipment and hairdressing equipment. The room remained accessible and unsecured
despite containing equipment and items which could pose a risk to people. The storage of these
items significantly reduced the availability of communal space that could otherwise support
people's well-being, social interaction and use of the garden. There is one usable shower in the
home, and this is shared with the staff and visitor’s toilet. This is placing people’s health and well-
being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take
immediate action to address this issue.

The provider does not consistently identify, assess, and manage health and safety risks within the
service. Systems are in place to monitor access to the home, with visitors required to sign in and
out. Records confirmed that routine fire safety checks had been completed and that required
servicing, maintenance, and safety checks for gas and electrical systems were up to date. Each
person had an individual Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan to support their safe evacuation in
the event of an emergency.

However, we identified weaknesses in infection prevention and control and environmental safety
arrangements. Several clinical waste bins were filled beyond the recommended capacity, and staff
were not consistently disposing of personal protective equipment waste appropriately in line with
infection prevention and control measures. In addition, management were unaware of the
requirements relating to the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations (COSHH) and
had not ensured appropriate oversight of hazardous substances and the required documentation.
There was no formal programme of health and safety checks to ensure equipment and the
environment remained safe and fit for use. This included the absence of routine checks and
monitoring of beds, call bells, sensor mats, wheelchairs and other equipment used to support
people’s care and wellbeing. The home had also received a Food Hygiene Rating of 1, (major
improvement was necessary) following a food hygiene inspection in January 2026. Although this
has since been reviewed and a new rating of 4 awarded, it is concerning that standards were
allowed to deteriorate unchecked. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we

this issue.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


Governance and oversight arrangements are not effective. The service has been without a
registered manager since April 2023. An interim manager is currently in post and is working
towards the required qualification. However, leadership and management arrangements have not
ensured effective oversight of the quality and safety of the service. While the RI visit reports
evidence engagement with people living at the home and staff, along with reviews of selected
records and documentation, they failed to identify a number of significant concerns found during
this inspection. Furthermore, there was limited evidence that these visits were resulting in
meaningful actions, measurable improvements, or enhanced outcomes for people using the
service.

The provider has not demonstrated effective management systems to monitor the quality and
safety of the service. Risks relating to nutrition, weight loss, falls, safeguarding, infection prevention
and control, equipment safety, and environmental health and safety are not consistently identified,
monitored, analysed, or addressed. In addition, there was no comprehensive system of routine
health and safety audits to monitor the condition and safety of equipment and the environment. As
a result, people cannot be assured they are consistently receiving a service that promotes their
wellbeing, choice, independence, and safety. The provider has completed a Quality of Care
Review, this was largely descriptive in nature and did not provide robust evaluation of service
performance. The review did not demonstrate effective analysis of quality assurance information,
audits, incidents, accidents, trends, patterns, stakeholder feedback, or outcomes for people.
Consequently, it failed to identify a number of the shortfalls found during this inspection and did not
provide sufficient assurance that governance processes were effective in driving continuous
improvement.

In addition, the service provider has failed to publish their annual report within the timescales
required by the Regulations. This represents a breach of regulatory requirements and limits
transparency regarding the quality of the service. As a result of these concerns, and the impact on
people's health, safety and wellbeing, a Priority Action Notice has been issued. The provider must
take immediate action to establish effective governance, quality assurance and oversight
arrangements to ensure people receive safe, high-quality care and support.

We found staff receive support from the provider through supervision, staff meetings, and training.
Safe recruitment and vetting processes and ongoing professional registration checks are in place.
A majority of staff have completed their mandatory training, but improvements are required in some
areas such as COSHH training. Records also show staff have received regular supervision,
providing opportunities for support and performance monitoring.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
