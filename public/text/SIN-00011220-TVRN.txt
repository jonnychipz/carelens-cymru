Summary:

 20 Frances Avenue is a small home that supports young adults with mental health needs, who
 aspire to live independently.

 Well-being is excellent because everyone is treated with dignity and respect and are actively and
 creatively supported to achieve their wellbeing outcomes. They have as much control over their
 lives as possible and experience a sense of calm, freedom, stability and security.

 Care and support is excellent because people experience very high quality care and support; the
 manager completes in-depth, effective assessments of each person’s needs. People build very
 strong relationships with the manager and staff, and compatibility is carefully considered so they
 get along with each other. People have significant involvement in their assessments, care plans
 and reviews. They achieve their outcomes and some have gone on to live independently.

 The environment is good because it meets people’s needs. Large bedrooms and quality fixtures
 and fittings support people’s wellbeing; they feel they have the space and privacy they need to

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. Everyone is treated with dignity and
 respect and are actively supported to achieve their wellbeing outcomes. They have as much control
 over their lives as possible, partaking in activities they have chosen and at their own pace. Care is
 highly person centred as people are actively involved in decisions that affect them, coproducing
 their plans and ensuring their voices are respected. Information in plans is highly tailored to
 people’s individual preferences, likes and dislikes and their aspirations. They receive support for
 their health needs, both from external health care and from the services own health practitioners
 who have the expertise required. People shared how happy they are to live here, to have their own
 space in which their privacy is respected, and the pace of life is calm.

 People are safe and protected from abuse because they know how to raise concerns. Their rights
 are upheld; being supported to access the right health care, welfare benefits, finding homes and
 using the community. People are free to come and go as they please and are kept safe with
 appropriate security of the building at night. They describe a consistent culture of openness without
 judgement and feel they can speak with staff freely. Positive risk taking is fully supported so people
 learn to budget, shop and cook, enhancing their independence skills safely and with guidance.

 Safe and healthy relationships are cultivated. Opportunities to socialise in the community are taken
 up, such as going to local cafes, gyms and clubs; home stays with family are encouraged where
 appropriate. People enjoy their gaming with friends and have access to IT and their phones to
 contact friends and family. Each person has a twelve-week assessment period on admission into
 the home to ensure it is the right place for them; strong friendships with each other are made more
 possible because compatibility is key factor.

 The accommodation supports people’s wellbeing and outcomes. We spoke to three people who
 expressed great satisfaction with their bedrooms; they are large and provide them with comfortable,
 private space to sleep, watch television or follow other hobbies and interests, and shower in their
 large en-suite facilities. There is a choice of communal rooms to lounge in and free access to the
 kitchen, where people can store food they have shopped for and cook their own meals. Promoting
 independence is a key aim of the service.

       Care & Support                           Excellent




People receive excellent support and achieve their personal outcomes. Care is designed with the
person receiving it, so it is personalised and planned around their wishes, needs, strengths and
aspirations. We learned how two people have moved on to independent living since the last
inspection. We spoke with one of those people who shared their highly positive experience living in
the home. They said, ‘it gave me time to breathe and bounce back’ and ‘support was always at my
pace – no pressure. It was nice there, a good place to live’. Another person said ‘staff are great. I
am accountable but have my freedom. I feel good because I’m doing well’. Progress towards
outcomes is measured frequently throughout the year when new goals are also considered. Step
by step plans are put in place, breaking big outcomes into incremental goals to make them more
achievable. We saw evidence of people achieving their outcomes such as attending college,
learning to drive, learning to manage their own medication, and keeping fit. The service is part of a
larger organisation which employs its own health professionals in psychology, psychiatry,
occupational therapy and nursing, enabling early intervention.

In addition to the service’s culture of openness, enabling honest conversations, people are also
protected by safeguarding policies and procedures. All staff are trained in safeguarding, so they
know what they need to do if they have a concern. People have access to advocacy services if they
need someone else to speak on their behalf. Everyone is supported to access facilities in the
community that promote their wellbeing, including leisure facilities and health appointments, and in
house expertise. Oral care is promoted; staff are trained and each person has a mouth care plan;
they are supported to attend dental and GP appointments.

People’s medication is safely managed. It is safely and securely stored, recorded and
administered. Staff are trained to administer medication and their competency is checked. Most
people living in the service are supported to be independent with their medication, within risk
management strategies, which include breaking the activity down into steps and monitoring
incremental progress towards complete independence. Every person has a locked cabinet in their
room in which medication can be stored, and the checks and arrangements in place help ensure
nothing is missed. The manager completes a monthly medication audit to check medication
processes are compliant.

       Environment                         Good



The home provides an environment conducive to supporting people’s outcomes. Bedrooms are
spacious so people feel they can spend a lot of time there. Each person has a comfortable bed,
quality furniture for storing their clothes, shelving and space for gaming gear and a television. We
saw a sofa in one bedroom and armchairs in others. Each room has a large ensuite including a wet
room. People shared how pleased they are with their room; they feel the home offers freedom,
stability and security and the environment is a contributing factor. One person said, ‘I love
everything about my room’. He compared his room to having ‘my own flat’ and expressed how it
made him feel independent. There are two large, comfortable, well lit lounges and other communal
spaces, including a games room. People help themselves in the equipped kitchen, where there is
ample space for meal preparation and dining. The laundry facilities allow people to take care of
their own laundry, with support if required. Established routines ensure bedrooms are cleaned
weekly and laundry is taken care of. People let staff know when they are going out but are free to
do so whenever they wish. The garden is maintained and there is seating and shelter for those who
smoke.

The provider employs a maintenance person to oversee homes in the organisation. There is
currently no separate log of repairs and maintenance work for Francis Avenue, but the manager
assured us they can raise any issues for repair, and they are dealt with promptly. We found the
home to be a positive, bright environment. It is beautifully furnished with an ornate wooden
staircase and quality furniture throughout. Some minor paintwork and floor scratches had already
been identified by the manager and are being addressed. Adhesive privacy film on some bedroom
windows blocks the view. We discussed other measures that can be taken to afford privacy without
blocking a view and the manager is looking at this.

Records show compliance with safety checks in relation to water, electricity, gas, fire, and
equipment. Routine inhouse checks are completed to ensure fire safety and a record is kept. The
home holds the highest score of level 5 from the Food Standards Agency for food safety practices.
Infection control audits completed.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the provider has effective governance
arrangements in place. They have good oversight of the service including the staff and people
living here. Records evidence the RI visits the home at least every three months, to speak with staff
and people using the service, check records, policies and processes and ensure compliance. They
complete audits to ensure compliance, and check audits carried out by the manager such as
medication audits, infection prevention checks and care record audits. Staff told us they know the
RI well and have confidence in their willingness to respond to queries and keep staff updated.
People told us they also find the RI very approachable, and that they listen and understand their
challenges and outcomes. The RI uses a range of evidence to gain feedback such as one-to-one
meetings with people using the service and the staff, as well as surveys. Feedback informs the bi-
annual review of the quality of care provided. We reviewed associated reports which are
comprehensive, evaluative and show year on year progress on desired improvements and
developments. We saw actions from previous reviews, RI visits, and audits are consistently
reviewed to assess progress and identify any further priorities.

Records show staff are safely recruited using established procedures for vetting and assessing
suitability, including checking references and criminal records data. We spoke with staff who
confirm they feel supported. They can meet with management every three months for one to one
support, during which they discuss work practices, and future career goals. Staff can access the
service’s employment assistance programme, including health and well-being advice. Staff told us
how much they enjoy working in Francis Avenue; ‘I really like getting up in the morning and coming
to work’. They feel well trained and records show a good range of mandatory training and other
training relevant to individuals they support. The service uses What’s the Message training to
understand positive behaviour support and how behaviour is a form of communication. The service
is proactive in seeking ways to develop and has invested in technology that will reduce a reliance
on paper and make records more accessible and easier to keep.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
