Summary:

 Hengoed Court Care Home is a large nursing care home situated in Winch Wen, Swansea. The
 home benefits from extensive grounds, scenic views over Swansea Bay and access to its own
 eight-acre nature park. The service provides nursing, complex nursing, palliative, end-of-life and
 respite care for up to 114 adults aged 55 years and over.

 Well-being is excellent because people are supported to live the lives they want to live. We saw
 clear evidence of improved outcomes, meaningful experiences and strong relationships. People
 told us they feel safe and listened to, while professionals recognised the service's innovative and
 person-centred approach.

 Care and support is good because people receive care that is responsive to their individual needs.
 Staff know people well, monitor their health closely and work effectively with families and
 professionals when concerns arise. Robust systems help ensure people are safe and receive the

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. We saw numerous examples of people
 achieving positive outcomes through personalised support, positive risk management and effective
 partnership working with healthcare professionals. This enables people to make informed
 decisions, manage complex health needs and pursue goals that are important to them. One person
 experienced a significant improvement in their comfort and quality of life because of staff's
 determination and coordinated support. Another person told us, “There’s no way I can express how
 much better I am.” A dedicated activities team enhances people's physical and emotional wellbeing
 through a large range of meaningful opportunities, including creative activities, music, walks, animal
 therapy and community events. People's spiritual needs and beliefs are respected and valued, with
 support to maintain meaningful spiritual connections and practise their faith where this is important
 to them. Professionals consistently recognised the service's strengths, with one commenting, “They
 are innovative in their holistic approach to care.”

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. People told us they feel safe and that staff
 listen to them. Relatives describe being actively involved in their loved one's care, commenting,
 “We keep an eye on the care, we are involved, anything we want we ask. Everything always gets
 followed through.” This reflects a culture of openness and responsiveness, where people and their
 families are encouraged to share feedback. Robust safeguarding, whistleblowing and complaints
 procedures support people's safety and wellbeing. Complaints are taken seriously, investigated
 appropriately and used as opportunities for reflection and learning.

 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. A stable and compassionate staff
 team enables people to develop trusting relationships with those supporting them. Staff told us
 many team members have worked at the service for a long time and “know the residents really
 well.” One person described staff as “really helpful, supportive and kind.” During the inspection, we
 observed warm and respectful interactions throughout the home, reflecting positive and trusting
 relationships.

 Staff support meaningful relationships and experiences, including celebrating important life events,
 hosting events and fetes at the home, inviting the wider community to join, and enabling people to
 attend Swansea City football matches using the home's season tickets. This helps people maintain
 meaningful connections with the people and interests that matter to them and strengthens links with
 the wider community. The environment reflects the people who live there, with personalised
 displays celebrating their lives and experiences.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes.
Relatives spoke positively about the care provided, with one describing it as “superb.” People told
us staff closely monitor their health and wellbeing, with one person stating, “they keep a close eye.”
We saw evidence of strong clinical oversight, including regular monitoring and recording of
important health information, regular GP visits, prompt escalation of concerns and effective
communication with families and involved professionals, helping to ensure deteriorating health
needs and emerging risks are identified and managed appropriately. One professional told us,
“The staff are always very helpful and are always very aware of patients and their health concerns
when discussing clinical problems’. Personal plans are detailed and regularly reviewed to reflect
people's changing needs and preferences. They are written in language that is person-centred and
promotes dignity, particularly in relation to communication and end-of-life planning. Staff ensure
people's wishes and dignity remain central to the support they receive at the end of life. People can
receive support in Welsh from some Welsh-speaking staff. Some documents are readily available
in Welsh, and others can be translated on request.

People are protected from harm and abuse. Staff demonstrated a clear understanding of
safeguarding responsibilities and how to report concerns. Safeguarding training compliance was
high and audits provided effective oversight of practice. Risk assessment arrangements were
robust and comprehensive, with personalised assessments providing clear guidance for staff on
how to support people safely. Records showed assessments were reviewed promptly following
incidents, changes in health needs, professional advice and people's choices. This responsive
approach helped ensure risks were identified and managed effectively while reflecting individuals'
needs and circumstances. One member of staff told us, “Any concerns are responded to promptly.”

People’s medication is safely managed. Medication is stored securely. People consistently receive
their prescribed medicines, and medication administration is supported by an electronic system
which provides clear oversight of medicines due and administered. Medication management
arrangements are well organised, with regular audits and spot checks. A notable strength is the
dedicated pharmacy technician role, who provides additional oversight of ordering, stock control,
record keeping and prescription changes. Controlled drugs checked during the inspection
corresponded with recorded balances and we found clear procedures for the receipt, return and
disposal of medicines.

People’s risk of infection is minimised. Infection prevention and control training compliance is high
and designated infection control and wound care champions promote good practice. We observed
staff using PPE appropriately and found supplies were readily available throughout the home.

       Environment                              Excellent



People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment to help
them achieve their wellbeing outcomes while identifying, mitigating and reducing health and safety
risks. The home provides an extensive range of communal and private spaces, enabling people to
choose how and where they spend their time, whether socialising with others or enjoying quieter
surroundings. Bedrooms are highly personalised and reflect people's identities and life
experiences, creating a strong sense of ownership. The environment is bright, welcoming and
thoughtfully designed, with colour contrasts supporting orientation and independence. Furniture
and facilities are maintained to a high standard, and there is clear evidence of continuous
investment in the environment, including a new kitchen, and a programme of flooring replacement.
During lunch, we observed an inclusive atmosphere, with staff from across the home working
together to create a positive social occasion rather than simply a mealtime.

Garden and outdoor spaces are a particular strength of the service and make a significant
contribution to people's wellbeing. People have access to attractive and well-maintained outdoor
areas that encourage connection with nature. Secure courtyards, seating areas and decking
spaces with scenic views provide opportunities for relaxation and recreation, while wheelchair-
accessible pathways enable people to safely access the home's farm and lake areas. The provider
has created additional facilities, including a café and ice cream parlour, which offer welcoming
spaces for people to spend quality time with family and friends and maintain important
relationships. The home's continued success in the Swansea in Bloom competition reflects the care
and investment dedicated to creating an environment that people can enjoy and take pleasure in.
One relative told us, "The flowers here, they make my day."

Robust systems are in place to promote safety and security, including an InterCall system
throughout the home, keypad and monitored access arrangements where necessary, and CCTV
surveillance, which is operated in line with regulatory requirements and people's rights. We viewed
comprehensive records which demonstrate strong oversight of health and safety, including current
gas and electrical certification and fire safety checks. Regular property audits and dedicated
maintenance and grounds teams support the home's high standards and contribute to an
environment that is both safe and attractive. The kitchen has the highest rating of 5 (very good) and
discussions with kitchen staff demonstrated a strong understanding of Speech and Language
Therapy (SALT) guidance and individual dietary requirements, ensuring people are offered
meaningful choice and meals that meet their preferences and assessed needs.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent




People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements to ensure smooth operations and high-quality care. Strong
governance arrangements underpin the service. Managers complete a comprehensive programme
of audits covering key areas, including medication, safeguarding, complaints, falls, wound care,
infection control and health and safety. Findings are incorporated into weekly reports submitted to
the directors, providing effective oversight of service performance and enabling leaders to identify
trends and take timely action where required. The responsible individual and directors maintain a
visible presence within the home, and, during the inspection, we observed they were well known to
people and staff. Feedback from people, relatives and staff is routinely gathered through
questionnaires, meetings and day-to-day discussions, ensuring leaders remain informed about
people's experiences and the quality of the service provided.

Innovation, investment and partnership working are clear strengths of the service. The service
works collaboratively with healthcare professionals to develop and introduce innovative approaches
to care. This includes current involvement in projects linked to advance care planning and
investment in specialist clinical equipment to enhance assessment and decision-making within the
home. Investment decisions are clearly linked to improving outcomes for people, with new
equipment and technology helping staff identify health concerns earlier and potentially supporting
reducing avoidable hospital admissions. The service has been recognised for its contribution to the
development of the I Stumble falls management process, with learning from the home helping to
influence practice more widely.

Staff wellbeing is prioritised, fostering a positive and inclusive culture. We heard examples of
managers responding sensitively to individual needs and making reasonable adjustments to
support staff wellbeing. A range of initiatives promote recognition and emotional wellbeing,
including employee awards and access to specialist counselling and training relating to grief. Staff
consistently spoke positively about the management team and comments included “As a practising
nurse I feel well supported and able to deliver the best care that the residents deserve.” and “We
all work as one team. The management support is unbelievable”. This positive culture helps retain
skilled staff, promotes wellbeing and enables staff to provide high-quality care for people living at
the home.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, skills and qualifications to meet
people's care and support needs. Safe recruitment processes help ensure suitable staff are
employed. Training compliance is high, providing assurance staff have the knowledge and
competence required for their roles. Investment in training and professional development helps
ensure staff have the skills and confidence to deliver high-quality, person-centred care.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
