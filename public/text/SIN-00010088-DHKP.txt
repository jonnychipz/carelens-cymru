Summary:
 Gardens Care Home is situated in Neath within walking distance of community facilities such as
 cafes, restaurants and parks. The service provides person-centred care and support to people.

 Well-being is good and people are actively involved in decisions about their lives. They are
 supported to maintain their health and well-being and participate in activities that are important to
 them.

 Care and support is good. There are thorough personal plans in place which are reviewed regularly
 with people and reflect their individual needs, preferences and aspirations.

 The environment is good as it is welcoming and homely and supports people's wellbeing.
 Bedrooms are personalised and communal areas are well maintained and inviting. Environmental
 improvements had been completed since the previous inspection, including redecoration, new
 flooring and replacement furnishings.

 Leadership and management of the service is good. The manager and responsible individual (RI)
 provide effective oversight and have implemented robust governance systems that drive
 continuous improvement. Care staff consistently described the management team as approachable

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. Care and support are delivered in a
 way that reflects individual preferences, enabling independence, choice and positive well-being
 outcomes. People can spend their time as they wish doing things that interest them and matter to
 them. Those spoken with told us they like where they live and are happy with the support from the
 care staff. People are actively involved in decisions about their day-to-day lives, including meal
 choices, activities and how they spend their time. We saw people accessing the community
 regularly and engaging in activities that reflect their interests and personal goals. One person
 spoken with told us “I am very happy here, everything is going good”.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. Systems are in place to safeguard people
 and protect them from harm. Risk assessments are detailed, regularly reviewed and linked to
 people's assessed needs, enabling care staff to support people safely while promoting
 independence. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of safeguarding procedures and are
 confident that any concerns would be responded to promptly by management. We spoke with a
 visiting professional who told us the service communicates effectively and keeps them well
 informed about people’s care and support needs. Records show people receive ongoing support to
 maintain good health, access input from healthcare professionals and monitor nutritional needs

 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. People are encouraged to
 maintain meaningful relationships with family members, professionals and their wider community.
 People are supported to access community facilities and activities that reflect their interests. We
 saw respectful interactions between care staff and saw people are listened to and treated with
 dignity and respect. Efforts are made to communicate with people in their preferred language. We
 saw materials available for people in their preferred language and support to access community
 activities promoting different language and culture. Bilingual signage and Welsh language
 initiatives, such as Welsh word of the week, help to promote the Welsh active offer.

 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. The environment is
 comfortable, homely and well maintained, with notable improvements made since the previous
 inspection. Recent redecorating, replacement furnishings, new flooring and other environmental
 enhancements demonstrate the provider's commitment to continuous improvement.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive person-centred care that is planned, delivered and reviewed in a way that promotes
positive outcomes. Personal plans are comprehensive, regularly reviewed and provide care staff
with clear guidance on how to meet peoples’ assessed needs. Records seen show involvement
from people, their representatives and professionals, ensuring care remains responsive to
changing circumstances. Detailed information about people's histories, preferences,
communication needs and aspirations enable care staff to provide consistent and tailored support.
Records evidence effective partnership working with external professionals to support people's
wellbeing, such as health appointments and specialist input. The quality of records and reflective
monthly reviews show a service focused on supporting people to achieve their desired outcomes.

People are protected from harm and abuse, and systems are in place to promote people's safety
whilst supporting them to live as independently as possible. Risk assessments are detailed,
regularly reviewed and proportionate to individual needs. Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards
authorisations are in place where required and appropriately reflected within care planning
documentation. Care staff spoken with demonstrate a clear understanding of safeguarding
responsibilities and reporting processes, with one staff member stating, "If I have a concern, I can
always go to the manager and I know it will be dealt with."

There are systems in place to ensure the safe management of medication. People are supported to
receive their medicines safely and as prescribed. Medication is stored securely, temperature
monitoring records are maintained, and monthly audits promptly identify and address any issues.
Medication administration records seen are completed appropriately and (when required)
medication guidance is available when required. Weekly stock checks and clear arrangements for
ordering and returning medicines strengthen oversight processes. Care staff complete medication
training and competency assessments, helping to ensure medicines are administered safely

People’s risk of infection is minimised as the service is clean, tidy and well-presented. The
environment is free from clutter, cleaning equipment and hazardous substances are appropriately
managed, and regular environmental audits provided ongoing oversight. Care staff receive infection
prevention and control training, with additional audits completed to monitor standards and identify
improvements. Equipment and facilities are subject to routine checks and maintenance schedules,
supporting a safe environment for people living at the service. Daily records and observations
demonstrated that people's personal care and health needs are managed appropriately, reducing
the risk of infection and promoting their overall health.

       Environment                         Good



People have access to suitable, well-maintained facilities and equipment that support their well-
being and reduces risks to their safety. Gardens Care Home provides a comfortable, homely and
person-centred environment that supports people to achieve positive well-being outcomes. The
environment is clean, tidy and clutter-free, creating a pleasant living environment where people can
move around safely and comfortably. Bedrooms are personalised to reflect people's tastes,
interests and preferences, helping to promote a sense of ownership and belonging. Communal
areas are welcoming, appropriately furnished and designed to encourage engagement and
socialisation. There is an outdoor space people can access as they wish, and we saw people
making the use of this area. Since the previous inspection, the provider has continued to invest in
the environment, with improvements including redecoration of hallways, new flooring, replacement
furnishings and planned enhancements to outdoor spaces. We saw the paving stones at the front
of the property are slightly uneven and could pose a trip hazard. We have discussed this with the
provider who assured us they would rectify it to reduce the hazard.

Effective arrangements are in place to maintain the environment and ensure people's safety. Fire
drills are completed supporting preparedness in the event of an emergency. Robust servicing and
maintenance arrangements help ensure the building remains safe, well maintained and complaint.
Environmental audits are completed monthly and form part of a wider quality assurance system,
enabling issues to be identified and addressed promptly. Staff and management demonstrate
awareness of ongoing maintenance requirements and are actively progressing improvements
where required. The continued investment in the home, combined with regular health and safety
checks and responsive management oversight, provides assurance that people benefit from an
environment that is safe, well maintained and supports their quality of life.


Maintenance records demonstrate a proactive approach to identifying and addressing issues
before they impact people. The environment is visibly well cared for, and care staff understand their
responsibilities in maintaining a safe environment. The service has a dedicated maintenance
person who is available to ensure repairs and improvements are completed promptly.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service provider has effective organisational arrangements, governance and oversight to
ensure smooth operations and person-centred care. There are established systems in place to
monitor quality, identify areas for development and respond proactively to issues. Governance
processes are comprehensive and include regular audits of medication, the environment, infection
prevention and control, and records relating to staffing. Action plans are developed as a result to
monitor progress and promote continuous improvement. Records are organised, accessible and
demonstrate clear management oversight. The RI has a visible presence within the service, visiting
regularly and providing ongoing support to the manager and staff team. They regularly obtain
feedback from people, their relatives and care staff about the service. This feedback is used to
make continued improvements to the service. This approach creates a culture of accountability and
continuous development, resulting in sustained improvements in the quality of care and the
environment.

People are supported by care staff with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. There is effective recruitment, training and workforce
development arrangements in place. Staff files are organised and contain the information and
background checks required to demonstrate safe recruitment practices. Supervision and appraisal
records show care staff receive regular support, and opportunities for professional development in
line with regulatory expectations. Training compliance is monitored, and care staff have access to
both mandatory and service-specific learning opportunities. Care staff demonstrate a good
understanding of people's individual needs and spoke knowledgeably about how they support
people to achieve positive outcomes.

Care staff described the management team as approachable and supportive. One person told us,
“If we've got problems we can always go to the manager and they will always sort it out for us,"
whilst another said, “If I need help, the manager and RI will support me." This positive culture
contributes significantly to the consistency and quality of care people receive. Care staff spoken
with are passionate about their roles and the importance of achieving positive outcomes for people.
One staff member told us, “I love my job, I love to see people happy” whilst another stated, "We
really care about people, and supporting them to do the things they enjoy doing."

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
