Summary:

 Hullah Farm provides care and support for up to 10 adults with learning disabilities, autism and
 complex support needs. People live in a range of accommodation including cottages, apartments
 and farmhouse accommodation. Well-being is excellent because we found people experience
 exceptionally positive outcomes and benefit from highly personalised care and support. Strong
 communication approaches help people make choices and exercise control over their daily lives.
 People are supported to participate in meaningful activities, maintain important relationships and
 engage in their local communities. The environment is good because the premises is well
 maintained and supports people to achieve their well-being outcomes.

 Care planning and positive behaviour support (PBS) are excellent. Documentation is detailed,
 person-centred and focused on improving quality of life and helping people achieve their personal
 outcomes. The service works in partnership with families and professionals and receives
 consistently positive feedback about the care and support provided. Leadership and management
 arrangements are also excellent. Governance systems support a culture of learning, reflection and
 continuous improvement, with a strong focus on achieving positive outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience exceptionally positive well-being outcomes because they are supported in
 highly personalised ways that maximise choice, control and independence. People confidently
 make decisions about their daily lives and receive support that adapts to their preferences. Staff
 use an impressive range of communication approaches, including communication books, visual
 timetables, communication dictionaries, activity strips and augmentative and alternative
 communication (AAC) systems. These approaches are embedded in practice and enable people to
 participate meaningfully in decisions affecting them and increase their independence. Activities are
 highly individualised and reflect people's interests, health needs, family relationships and
 aspirations. People are supported to plan how they spend their time. Staff respond flexibly when
 people change their minds about planned activities, demonstrating a culture that consistently
 promotes rights, autonomy and personal control.

 People achieve significantly improved health and well-being outcomes because staff know them
 exceptionally well and work proactively with a range of professionals. Records demonstrate
 substantial progress for several people since moving to the service. We saw improved sleep,
 increased dietary variety and increased community participation. Other records evidence
 improvements in communication, emotional regulation, personal care, independence and
 engagement in meaningful activities. PBS approaches, structured routines, visual supports and
 specialist input help people experience greater stability and quality of life. Professional feedback is
 particularly positive. A professional described seeing "really positive changes" in a person's life.

 People maintain important relationships and participate in meaningful activities that enrich their
 lives. Community engagement is extensive and tailored to people's interests, with records
 evidencing visits to beaches, parks, shops, garden centres and local attractions. Family
 involvement is actively promoted through regular communication, monthly updates and planned
 visits. Feedback from relatives is exceptionally positive and consistently highlights improvements in
 quality of life. One relative describes the service as "by far the most suitable and competent
 placement" they have experienced. Another states their family member is "in good hands". People
 are also supported to pursue new opportunities and achievements.

 People live in accommodation that promotes their well-being and supports positive outcomes. The
 service demonstrates a strong commitment to adapting environments in response to changing
 needs and wishes.

       Care & Support                           Excellent




People receive exceptionally personalised care and support that is designed around their individual
needs, strengths and aspirations. Care planning documentation is detailed, person-centred and
regularly reviewed through daily meetings, care plan reviews, PBS reviews and multi-disciplinary
discussions. We saw excellent examples of communication support, including communication
strategy files, communication dictionaries, visual timetables, activity strips, social stories and AAC
systems. Staff are provided with detailed guidance on how to support people effectively. Personal
plans aim to ensure staff understand “who people are, how people experience the world and what
matters to them”. A revised care planning system places people at the centre of their support and
includes statements like, ‘quality of life is measured by engagement and well-being, not task
completion.” Evidence shows people achieve positive outcomes including increased community
access, improved communication, greater engagement, improved emotional regulation and
increased independence.

People are protected from harm and abuse. The service has clear systems in place to understand
people’s behaviour, reduce risks and keep people safe. Staff review incidents in detail to identify
patterns, possible causes and whether the support provided is working. Behaviour support plans
explain how staff should support people before, during and after incidents. Staff use reassurance,
redirection, visual aids and calming approaches before considering restrictions. This helps reduce
risks and supports people’s quality of life. The service regularly reviews restrictive practices,
safeguarding concerns and the use of medicines given when needed (PRN). When concerns are
identified, staff review behaviour support plans and incident information to consider what could be
done differently. Records following incidents show that staff reflect on what happened, learn from
incidents and consider how future support could be improved.

Medication management arrangements are comprehensive and support people to receive their
medicines safely. Medication administration records are completed appropriately, and medication
files contain detailed guidance regarding administration, PRN use and monitoring requirements.
Weekly medication audits, stock checks, daily counts and regular spot checks provide oversight of
medication systems. Medication protocols provide clear guidance for staff and systems are in place
to escalate and review any concerns regarding medication administration appropriately.

People's risk of infection is minimised through established infection prevention and control (IPC)
systems. Regular IPC, cleanliness and health and safety audits are completed across all areas of
the service. Audit records demonstrate environments are generally clean and well maintained, with
actions identified where improvements are required.

       Environment                         Good



People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes and reflects their individual
needs, preferences and lifestyles. Accommodation is clean, comfortable and personalised.
Bedrooms and living areas contain personal items, preferred furnishings and décor which reflect
people's interests and identities. The provider adapts environments in response to people's wishes
and changing needs. Robust furniture and protective fixtures are used where required, enabling
people to continue accessing valued possessions and activities safely.

The environment supports people to live safely whilst promoting independence and quality of life.
Comprehensive Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are in place for all people and are
supported by personalised emergency grab bags containing essential information and equipment.
Accommodation is adapted to meet people's sensory, behavioural and communication needs. For
example, door frames, doors and other fixtures have been strengthened where required and
changes are introduced gradually to reduce anxiety and disruption. Staff and managers
demonstrate a good understanding of balancing environmental maintenance with the needs of
people who may find building works distressing.

The provider has systems in place to monitor the condition and safety of the environment. Weekly
health and safety inspections, maintenance logs, cleanliness audits and environmental checks
identify areas requiring attention and track progress of repairs. We saw evidence of ongoing
maintenance and improvement works across the service. Maintenance records identify issues and
demonstrate these are reviewed and monitored. We also saw evidence of significant repairs and
environmental improvements completed across apartments, cottages and communal areas.
Accommodation is clean, personalised and designed around individual needs, preferences and
lifestyles. Bedrooms and personal spaces clearly reflect people's identities and interests and help
create environments that feel like home. We saw evidence of sensory adaptations; communication
supports and plans for additional sensory spaces.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from a service with highly developed governance systems that focus on improving
outcomes and supporting continuous improvement. Daily meetings provide structured oversight of
people's well-being, incidents, activities, medication, safeguarding, staffing and environmental
matters. Management review incident reports, daily notes, activities, medication checks and
resident welfare every day, supported by weekly governance processes including safeguarding
trackers, medication audits, supervision monitoring, risk register reviews and continuous
improvement reviews. The service also uses an electronic planner system to monitor actions
relating to communication, support strategies, care reviews and recommendations. The
Responsible Individual (RI) and managers also review incidents and information about the quality
of service in a reflective way.

The service demonstrates an open and learning culture. Leaders routinely review incidents,
safeguarding concerns, restrictive practices, medication management and quality assurance
information to identify trends and improve practice. Incident analysis is particularly detailed; reviews
identify patterns and consider what could have been done differently. We saw examples where
concerns had led to changes in procedures, increased checks and further guidance for staff.
Policies have been reviewed and simplified to make them easier for staff to use. Staff meetings
take place regularly and discuss important matters, including safeguarding, medication, training
and improvements to the service,

People are supported by staff who have opportunities to develop their skills and knowledge. The
service has a competency framework which links progression to demonstrated skills and
knowledge rather than length of service. Staff knowledge is supported through resident-specific
training materials, communication resources, PBS questionnaires, supervision discussions and
competency assessments. Evidence shows a significant proportion of staff have high levels of
compliance with mandatory and specialist training. Staff feedback regarding leadership and support
is exceptionally positive. One staff member states, "The management team are one of the service's
greatest strengths", whilst another describes a "wonderful service where everyone cares deeply
about the residents". Staff consistently report feeling valued, included and supported and describe
opportunities for development and progression. One member of staff stated the improvement in
residents' quality of life is "the most important thing for those who work there".

There is strong evidence that leadership and management arrangements have a positive impact on
people's lives. The service has developed innovative approaches including communication strategy
files, Goodness of Fit reviews, competency frameworks, revised care planning systems, AAC
implementation and specialist communication resources. RI reports, care reviews and quality
statements consistently identify positive outcomes for people, including improvements in

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
