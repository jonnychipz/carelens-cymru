Summary:

 Milford House is a residential care home in Milford Haven. Well-being is rated as excellent because
 people receive a highly person-centred service, that enables them to achieve their health and well-
 being outcomes. People have developed extremely positive relationships with the skilled and
 passionate staff.

 Care and support is rated as excellent because people receive care and support from a team of
 well-informed and friendly care workers. Care workers are guided by accurate and outcome
 focused personal plans that enable them to successfully meet people’s needs.

 The environment needs improvement. The provider has resolved the health and safety issues
 identified previously; upgrades have been made to the home and the provider intends to continue
 these to further enhance people’s well-being.

 Leadership and management is rated as good because the provider has good oversight of the
 service. The leadership ensure people receive high quality care from a team of dedicated and
 motivated care workers who receive effective support from their managers.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People are treated sensitively, with dignity and respect. They are actively supported to identify their
 specific health and well-being outcomes, while also being encouraged to use and build on their
 strengths. This has enabled many people who have used the service to move on to live more
 independent lives.

 People are part of a strong culture of openness without judgement. People’s emotional and
 psychological well-being is actively supported and enhanced by the knowledgeable and
 experienced team of care workers. Examples of some of the outstanding outcomes people have
 achieved are recorded by them in their own words, in a ‘Magic Moments’ document.

 People are enabled to have as much control over their lives as possible. They are supported to be
 fully involved in decisions that affect them, ensuring that their voices are always heard. People are
 supported to live well and achieve individualised self-directed outcomes. The service consistently
 helps people maintain and improve upon their physical, mental and emotional health.

 People are actively supported to maintain and sustain existing relationships with family, friends and
 important people in their lives as far as possible. This is facilitated by the creative staff team, who
 respect these relationships and the importance they hold in people’s lives. People who use the
 service told us they value this support greatly.

 People are safeguarded from abuse and neglect at the service. People are provided with a secure
 environment where they feel safe. The service encourages feedback from people to ensure every
 voice is heard. There are highly effective support systems to ensure any risks are promptly
 identified and addressed, while allowing people to make informed decisions and to live as
 independently as possible.

 The provider is completing work to ensure people live in accommodation that supports them to
 achieve positive outcomes. The design, layout and decoration of the setting and quality of the
 furnishings and fixtures is improving to better meet people’s needs.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People are very positive about the care and support they receive from the team of professional and
understanding care workers. There is an enabling culture at the service and this ‘can do’ approach
has supported many people to move-on to achieve their goals of living more independently.

People experience excellent quality care because there is a detailed process before the manager
agrees to provide the service. The effective assessments check if the provider can fulfil their needs
and personal outcomes alongside people already receiving the service.

People are at the heart of the service and are encouraged to be fully involved in their personal
plans. They lead on setting their own goals and their support is co-produced between them,
relevant professionals and care staff. Personal plans are highly individualised and outline how staff
should support people to achieve and exceed their health and well-being outcomes.

People's well-being and safety is prioritised by the service by identifying and managing potential
risks. People’s right to make their own choices and take informed personal risks is promoted by the
positive culture in the service. This is reflected in people’s individual plans.

The service works creatively with the staff team to ensure people engage in meaningful past times.
Activities of daily living, socialisation and community engagement are key components in the
success of people becoming more confident and developing their independence.

People experience care and support that is dignified and respectful. People have positive
interactions with staff, friends, family and each other. People are actively supported to attend and
participate in activities related to health promotion, such as GP appointments; staff are aware of
their role in this and the current needs of each individual.

People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because premises and
equipment are kept clean and hygienic. Food hygiene practices are good. There is an effective
approach to managing and preventing the risk of infection, with clear roles and responsibilities.

       Environment               Requires Improvement



We have closed the Priority Action Notices issued to the provider in February 2026 because of the
improvements made to the environment. Risks previously identified with the environment have
been addressed and the provider is in the process of improving the overall décor and appearance
of the building. Whilst these matters did not present an immediate risk to people's wellbeing they
have been identified as an Area for Improvement.

The provider has consulted with people and considers their needs and choices regarding the
environment. Facilities in the service are now safe and meet people's requirements. Living spaces
are functional, homely clean, with better attention to decoration and quality of furniture.

Outdoor spaces have been improved and there are plans to further improve the gardens for people
to use. We saw people enjoying sitting out and relaxing in the patio area during the inspection.

The improvements to the environment mean people are now protected as much as possible from
the risk of infection. The premises and equipment are kept clean and communal areas look inviting,
with further plans to replace flooring in these areas.

People are relaxed and comfortable in their home, Support workers are also positive about the
improvements made to the environment and the impact this has had on people’s well-being.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider oversees the quality of care through detailed and effective audit processes.
Information gathered from both internal and external audits guides quality review reports and
improvement plans.

The Responsible Individual (RI) is well known and visits the service regularly to talk with people
and staff. Feedback from everyone involved is encouraged by them to support assessing the
quality of the service and to consider improvements. People know how they can provide feedback
about their experiences and the provider is focusing on improving how they capture positive
outcomes.

The manager leads by example and has a ‘hands-on’ approach. They are well supported by a team
of senior care workers. They have worked together effectively to create a professional and positive
culture, that enables people to live as well as possible. People and care workers have confidence
in the manager and can discuss any concerns they have with them.

Care workers have regular one-on-one supervision sessions with their manager at least quarterly
and an annual review to provide feedback and identify any training needs. Care workers told us the
manager is accessible and extremely supportive.

The service provider has strict selection and vetting processes for hiring staff to ensure they are
qualified and trustworthy. Care workers undergo routine and regular checks to ensure they remain
suitably fit to work in the service and are appropriately registered with professional bodies. The
service has an effective induction that prepares new staff for the role. Care workers are positive
about the development opportunities they receive.

Care workers, demonstrate a positive approach to protecting people and keeping them safe,
without restricting their liberties. Staff have good communication with each other and their
managers; they are confident to report concerns if needed.

Overall, people benefit from effective leadership and management that promotes a positive culture.
Leaders have effective oversight of the service and a dedicated staff team help ensure people
experience high quality care and support within a well-managed home.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
