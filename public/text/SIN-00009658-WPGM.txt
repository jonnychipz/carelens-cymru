Summary:

 The Forge Care Centre provides high standards of care and support, including nursing care.
 People at The Forge receive exceptionally detailed, personalised and effective care and support,
 promoting positive outcomes while responding to complex needs.
 People reported feeling safe, loved and valued. Relatives describe the home as welcoming and
 supportive, and evidence demonstrates people’s physical, emotional and social wellbeing is
 promoted to a very high standard.
 The service shows strength in assessment, care planning, dementia care, clinical oversight and
 multidisciplinary working. Support is tailored around individuals, ensuring care is responsive,
 flexible and driven by each person's needs and preferences.
 The environment is safe, clean and meets the needs of people living at the home.
 The Forge Care Centre is well-led, with a highly supportive culture and an experienced workforce
 committed to achieving positive outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People living at The Forge Care Centre experience exceptionally positive wellbeing outcomes
 supported by a compassionate, skilled and highly person-centred workforce. There is strong
 evidence people are treated with dignity, respect and kindness and supported to maintain their
 identity, relationships and health. Feedback from people and relatives was overwhelmingly positive
 demonstrating confidence in the service. One relative described the care as “Second to none” and
 reflected that staff had become “family” for their loved one, illustrating the strength of relationships
 developed within the home.

 People are supported to exercise choice and control wherever possible. Staff have excellent
 knowledge of individual preferences and adapt care accordingly. For example, relatives described
 how meals are tailored to individual needs, including providing finger foods and favourite snacks.
 One relative stated, “Mum always enjoys a jam sandwich” and confirmed staff ensured she never
 went hungry or dehydrated. Another commented that staff were “Constantly going around with
 fortified food and drinks.”

 Health outcomes for people are particularly strong. Relatives consistently reported confidence in
 the home’s ability to respond to changing needs, with one stating, “Health needs are always
 addressed quickly enough.” There is effective medication management, excellent skin integrity
 monitoring and high-quality end-of-life care. Relatives described being fully involved in advanced
 care planning and supported throughout difficult periods.

 The inspection observed numerous examples of warm, meaningful interactions. Staff demonstrated
 a sophisticated understanding of dementia care, responding sensitively to distress and adapting
 approaches to suit each individual. One relative explained, “They’re all amazing,” describing how
 staff recognised signs of distress and remained with their loved one until calm. We observed
 affectionate and respectful interactions, including a clinical lead sitting with a resident, holding his
 hand, resulting in a visibly positive response. While there is no requirement at present to provide a
 service through the Welsh language, the provider is mindful of the Welsh Active Offer.

 Activity provision is being adjusted to meet the aspirations of all people. Whilst entertainers and
 group activities are available and staff routinely engaged people in singing and dancing, evidence
 showed limited opportunities for more targeted meaningful occupation, particularly for people
 spending significant periods in bed. One relative commented, “The only thing is activities,” and
 expressed a desire for more trips out. The service is addressing this through appointment of two
 additional coordinators who have plans to arrange more access to the community. Despite this,
 people express they are happy and content, and overall outcomes are positive.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People have full information about the service and are part of the care planning. Pre-admission
assessments are particularly impressive. A mental health professional described the clinical lead as
“Very thorough” and someone who “Will go above and beyond” to spend time assessing people’s
needs and meeting their families. The professional highlighted the service's success in supporting
people with complex dementia needs who might otherwise struggle to access appropriate care and
support, many having spent a long time in hospital.

Care planning is a significant strength. We found highly detailed plans providing staff with practical
guidance on meeting both emotional and physical needs. Records include rich information about
personal preferences, communication styles and behavioural presentations. Examples include
detailed instructions such as reminding staff how best to reassure individuals when distressed.
Plans addressed medical needs comprehensively, covering areas such as wound management,
mobility, catheter care, and diabetes management.

The care is person centred and of a high standard. During inspection visits we saw staff
demonstrate a sophisticated understanding of dementia and mental health needs, providing
compassionate support to people who might present with distress or behaviours which challenge.
Relatives and people told us there are highly effective approaches. One person told us they see
others experiencing distress, but staff respond quickly and positively to give reassurance, physical
comfort and dedicated attention, which is highly effective. People or their relatives describe the
care as “Exceptional,” “Highly person centred,” “Calm,” “Supportive,” and “Caring.” While we are
confident all care is being delivered, daily records need minor adjustments to ensure all care
delivered is consistently captured, such as oral care.

Healthcare support is robust and proactive. Records show good access to GP and we saw one
visiting to support people as part of their routine clinical reviews. People also have access to other
professionals such as, tissue viability nurses, wheelchair services and dentists. Relatives praised
the management of skin integrity, one stating, “Any marks, they're on it, they take a photo, use
spray and sort it out.” The service has achieved particularly strong outcomes, including no home-
acquired pressure damage despite supporting people with significant health needs, demonstrating
the robust approach to prevention.

The medication system is an area of significant strength. Following previous challenges, the service
introduced more robust processes which led to improvements in oversight and reduction of errors.
Stock control, competency checks and medication audits are exceptional.

       Environment                         Good



The environment at The Forge Care Centre is safe, welcoming and generally well maintained. The
home provides comfortable living spaces which supports people's wellbeing, dignity and
independence. The home's location on a busy road creates practical challenge for people to
access the community, but opportunities to access outdoor space is provided in a garden area with
trees and other plants. We saw this was used to good effect and enjoyed by all in warmer weather.
There is sufficient shade to safeguard people from the sun.


The service successfully creates a homely atmosphere. Smaller living areas and
compartmentalised lounges provide a more intimate environment and helps create a ‘small care
home feel.’ Each area has access to lounge facilities and kitchenettes which support social
interaction. The service operates safe areas which are accessible using electronic access; this
keeps people safe, especially those living with dementia who may not be at risk if they left the
home independently.


Bedrooms can be highly personalised according to individual wishes. We saw examples reflecting
people's identities and interests, helping residents maintain a sense of self and familiarity.
Communal areas are comfortable and welcoming, and the service is actively enhancing dementia-
friendly provision through additional sensory and engagement resources.


Standards of cleanliness are consistently good and there is sufficient equipment available. We
found the home to smell fresh and observed domestic staff actively cleaning the premises
throughout the inspection. Appropriate specialist equipment is readily available, including profiling
beds, hoists and slings. There are effective systems for equipment servicing and environmental
maintenance. Managers display good awareness of compliance requirements and take action
when maintenance issues arise.


Health and safety arrangements are generally robust. Relevant doors are kept secure, hazardous
substances appropriately stored and there is effective oversight through maintenance schedules
and external inspections. We identified some minor maintenance matters which were immediately
addressed and rectified. The home achieves high food hygiene standards and demonstrates a
proactive approach to fire and environmental safety. The provider is considering the future of the
environment as this year’s heatwaves have presented with challenges to maintain cooler
environmental temperatures while external temperatures are higher than usual.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The Forge Care Centre has strong leadership, positive organisational culture and stable
management arrangements which support good outcomes for people. Feedback regarding the
leadership team was overwhelmingly positive. One relative commented staff are “All brilliant, from
the top down.” Staff consistently described leaders as supportive and approachable. Examples
included staff stating that management are “Visible, supportive and actively involved in the day-to-
day running of the service” and maintain an “Open door policy.” Many staff referred to the home's
family atmosphere and one employee remarked, “It doesn't feel like going to work, it also feels like
home.” We saw the positive culture being fostered during the two-day inspection visits.


Governance and monitoring systems are generally effective. The responsible individual (RI) visits
the service and has good oversight through management. They often visit the service weekly or
more frequently, which is in excess of expectations. The RI produces required reports, but some
reports are not always available in a timely manner. There is no impact on the service or people
living there. The provider carefully considers the development of the service, and this is planned
only with full consultation of people, staff and management.


Daily monitoring of the service is supported through auditing systems. These are mostly
comprehensive, but the provider is considering how these can be streamlined as there is a mixture
of electronic and paper-based evidence which could increase risks of matters being missed.
Communication across the home is mostly robust, with good handover of information between
shifts and departments, ensuring a consistent and effective approach to care and support.


Staffing arrangements are particularly strong. The service benefits from an experienced and stable
workforce, with many employees having worked at the home for more than a decade. Staff and
relatives report there are sufficient staff available and agency usage is minimal. The management
keep staffing levels under review and make adjustments when required.


Recruitment and induction arrangements are comprehensive with evidence of professional
development opportunities. Training is an area of significant strength. Staff described being
supported to learn and develop, with specialised programmes in dementia care, falls management
and prevention, and behavioural support. The service's investment in dementia-focused training is
supporting exceptional outcomes for people.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
