Summary:

 Orchard House, situated in Barry, offers residential accommodation for up to 40 individuals. This
 inspection was unannounced. Feedback from people living and visiting the service consistently
 indicate very high levels of satisfaction and overall happiness with the service. Staff are praised for
 their attentiveness, compassion, and respect, and are consistently recognised for going above and
 beyond in delivering person-centred care.

 People live in accommodation that is suitably aligned with their assessed needs. The premises are
 maintained to a good standard of cleanliness, supported by robust infection prevention and control
 procedures. The environment is fit for its intended purpose, offering a range of communal areas
 that facilitate both social interaction and privacy, with required refurbishment works undertaken on
 an ongoing basis.

 Management demonstrate good oversight of the service through regular audits and up-to-date
 policies. The Responsible Individual (RI) is available at the service on a daily basis, supporting
 effective monitoring and governance. Recruitment processes are robust and compliant, ensuring

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People are protected from harm and abuse. The home environment is safe and promotes the
 wellbeing of individuals by minimising risks of harm. Robust and well-organized systems ensure the
 premises are cleaned and maintained to a high standard. All services and equipment are regularly
 tested and appropriately serviced. Staff adhere to infection prevention and control procedures,
 contributing to a hygienic and secure setting. Care staff are recruited safely as pre-employment
 checks are completed correctly. There is a system to renew Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS)
 certificates when required. Care staff, where required are registered with Social Care Wales, the
 workforce regulator. Documentation and medication are stored securely.

 People experience positive and respectful relationships with care staff. Throughout the inspection
 visit, staff were observed interacting with people in a kind and friendly manner. Care staff
 responded promptly to people’s needs, offering reassurance and support as required. People are
 extremely well-presented and appeared content and happy, either engaging in activities or enjoying
 quiet time alone. Feedback from people living at the home was extremely positive. People
 expressed that they feel happy residing there and described the staff as exceptionally kind and
 attentive. Comments include, “I really couldn’t ask for more,” and “staff are amazing here, I have no
 worries.” Feedback from provider audits and questionnaires consistently evidence that families rate
 the service as “outstanding.”

 People receive timely care, supported by staffing levels that are consistently maintained at a good
 standard. This allows care staff to perform their roles effectively while building positive relationships
 with people and their families. Personal care plans are in place to support care staff in delivering
 appropriate care and are regularly reviewed to ensure they remain up to date and responsive to
 individuals’ needs. Individuals have suitable access to healthcare services when needed, and
 medications are administered in accordance with prescribed guidance. The Responsible Individual
 (RI) provides strong oversight of the service, carrying out quality assurance monitoring in line with
 regulatory requirement and also available at the home on a daily basis.

 People appear happy and actively involved in activities they enjoy. There is a clear and consistent
 focus on maintaining family relationships. One person told us, “My family are welcomed whenever
 they want to visit; staff always make us feel special.” People spoke positively about the regular
 programme of activities and visiting entertainers. During our visit, we observed that people have
 access to personal belongings that reflect their interests and hobbies, and are also encouraged to
 try new activities, such as flower arranging taking place during the visit. People said they are able
 to choose where they spend their time and who they spend it with. The environment and excellent
 garden areas were also seen to have a positive impact on overall well-being.

 No formal complaints have been received. However, the service has received a substantial number

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People told us they feel safe and secure living in the home, and we observed this through people’s
body language and interactions, including where individuals were not always able to express this
for themselves. Documentation confirmed that staff have completed safeguarding training. A
safeguarding policy is in place, clearly outlining staff responsibilities in protecting adults from harm,
abuse and neglect. We saw people comfortable and at ease in the presence of care staff. People
told us, and we observed, that staff engaged are warm kind and respectful, demonstrating an
excellent understanding of individual needs and how best to respond. We observed that care staff
showed sensitivity and provided appropriate support when individuals experienced difficulties with
decision-making or communication. We saw care staff offering reassurance and encouragement
while effectively anticipating their needs, particularly for those who were unable to express this
themselves.

People can be assured they have autonomy over their lives and are supported to make informed
decisions about their daily routines. Personal preferences are acknowledged and respected, with
people encouraged to take part in activities that are meaningful to them. The service benefits from
a dedicated activities coordinator who provides a structured programme five days a week. This
includes celebrating special occasions, fostering links with local schools and community groups,
and arranging visits from external entertainers. In addition, care staff support more informal, ad hoc
activities during each shift to ensure people remain engaged throughout the day. Regular outings
are organised enabling residents to access local amenities and enjoy trips to places such as the
seaside and community coffee mornings. During our visit, we observed people taking great
enjoyment in spending time with the visiting dogs, which attend the home daily. There was a clear
sense of delight and frequent laughter evident throughout the day. Feedback from residents/visitors
was consistently positive. One individual shared, “I cannot say anything other than the home
exceeds in everything, moving here was such a shock at first, but really I’ve settled and its
fabulous.” We observed the lunchtime meal and saw that people were thoroughly enjoying the food
served. The chef also informed us that fresh produce is delivered to the home every day.

People have access to other services to support their ongoing health and well-being. Care
documentation evidenced timely referrals to relevant healthcare professionals, including opticians,
dentists and GPs. Personal plans contain the required information and accurately reflect the daily
needs, preferences and wishes of individuals living at the home. We observed that care workers
are highly attentive, anticipate individuals’ needs and provide excellent responsive support.
Personal plans are reviewed and updated as required. When individuals experience difficulties with
decision-making, the service demonstrates good practice by ensuring appropriate applications are
submitted to the relevant authority, supporting the inclusion of the person’s wishes in decision-
making processes. Medication management systems within the home are robust, with clear
procedures in place for storage and administration. These processes are well organised and
consistently maintained to ensure accuracy and safety.

       Environment                         Good



People are supported in a clean and extremely homely environment. Bedrooms are personalised
with items of the individual’s choice, including their own belongings. The home provides several
small communal areas, as well as a pleasant dining space where we observed people enjoying
activities together. During our visit, we found the home to be consistently clean and fresh
throughout. Visitors were warmly welcomed, and people we spoke to highlighted how important
these visits are for maintaining connections with their relatives. The gardens are beautifully
presented and well maintained. People told us they enjoy spending time outdoors, particularly in
warmer weather, and we saw that the garden is easily accessible for everyone. We saw people
taking the opportunity to spend time outside during our visit and enjoying drinks and chatter
throughout the day. People spoke positively and excitedly about a recent garden party, which was
enjoyed by all. We observed people are safe, happy and well supported.

There are effective systems in place for monitoring and maintaining both the environment and
equipment. Maintenance staff ensures that all facilities and equipment are regularly inspected,
serviced, and tested. External contractors are engaged to undertake specialist checks, including
gas electrical safety and lift safety. Repairs are generally addressed promptly, and a planned
redecoration programme supports a good standard of presentation within the home. However, at
this time certain areas of the home would benefit from refurbishment to further improve the
environment; the provider has confirmed that these enhancements are currently underway. The
Food Standards Agency has awarded a rating of three* (satisfactory) for its catering provision, the
provider told us the required actions have been met.

Effective systems are in place to promote public safety and minimise the risk of cross-infection.
Staff have access to appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) and demonstrate a clear
understanding of its correct use and disposal. All staff receive training in infection prevention and
control and recognise its importance in safeguarding people. Robust oversight arrangements are
established, ensuring staff consistently follow current infection control guidance. People are further
protected through effective security measures, including keypad entry systems and controlled
access for visitors. A visitors’ book is maintained in line with fire safety procedures, and identity
checks are undertaken for all visitors prior to entry.

The service provides suitable accommodation for people living in, working in, and visiting the home.
Management demonstrates a clear commitment to continuous development and improvement for
the benefit of those residing there. People experience an extremely strong sense of belonging,
supported by the provider’s efforts to create a homely and welcoming environment that meets
individual needs. The home is overall well presented, and both residents their families and visitors
appreciate its traditional style, which contributes positively to overall well-being.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported by care staff who are appropriately trained and recruited safely. Care staff
are extremely happy working at the service and told us they feel valued and supported by both the
manager and RI. We looked at a selection of staff recruitment documentation and found they
contained all the required information including identification and full employment history. We saw
evidence that pre-employment checks including DBS certificates and references are sought prior to
employment commencing to ensure that all staff are suitably qualified and fit to work with
vulnerable people. Individuals using the service and their relatives consistently praised the quality
of care, with one stating “I can’t fault it here, the care staff are kind and helpful.”

People benefit from strong leadership and effective management within the service. Orchard House
is a registered service with robust oversight by the Responsible Individual (RI) and supported by a
manager who is registered with Social Care Wales. The RI undertakes maintains an active, visible
presence, engaging with both individuals receiving care and staff in an approachable manner.
Clear and comprehensive policies and procedures support the day-to-day running of the service
and provide staff with well-defined guidance on their roles and responsibilities. The manager has a
thorough understanding of their legal duties in safeguarding vulnerable individuals and makes
timely referrals to the local authority when required. Since the last inspection the manager has
received some formal supervision with the RI, we discussed this needs to be strengthened further
to meet regulatory requirements. We were told this would be addressed immediately.

The provider supports staff development at all levels. Care staff complete a comprehensive
induction programme, which includes opportunities to shadow more experienced colleagues. Their
competency is assessed before they begin working independently. Training is provided in line with
the responsibilities of their roles. One staff member commented, “We are an excellent team here
now, there is really good staff morale. We all work together to ensure residents’ needs are met.”
Regular supervision sessions take place between staff and management; several care staff told us
they feel extremely well supported. Staff turnover remains low, which many attribute to the positive
working environment. Several care staff told us they have worked at the home for many years and
“wouldn’t consider working anywhere else.”

Quality assurance processes are carried out in accordance with regulatory requirements, reflecting
the provider’s commitment to delivering high-quality care and implementing improvements where
needed. Individuals are provided with detailed information about the service, including guidance on
how to raise concerns or make complaints should they be dissatisfied. The home’s Statement of
Purpose (SoP) is regularly reviewed and updated as necessary. This document is essential as it
outlines the scope of care provision specifying who can receive care, as well as how, where, and
when that care is delivered. The management team demonstrate strong and effective leadership,
placing people at the centre of a well-organised and smoothly operated service that is valued and
enjoyed. The home performs to an excellent standard, truly excelling in providing a “home from

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
