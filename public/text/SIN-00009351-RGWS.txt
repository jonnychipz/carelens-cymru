Blaen-Y-Pant House is situated in a residential area of Newport, close to the community of Bettws.
The home is easily accessible for relatives, friends and professionals and provides accommodation
and care for older people.

Well-being is good. People told us they feel safe, valued and well supported by staff who know
them well. We observed warm and respectful interactions, opportunities for social engagement and
support that promotes people's independence, dignity and personal preferences.

Care and Support is good. People receive personalised care from staff who understand their needs
and preferences. Personal plans are detailed and reviewed regularly, helping staff provide
consistent support. People and relatives spoke positively about the care provided, and we saw
evidence of effective partnership working with healthcare professionals to promote people's health
and well-being.

The Environment is good. People live in surroundings that are safe, comfortable and well
maintained. Bedrooms are personalised and communal areas provide welcoming spaces for
relaxation and social interaction.

Leadership and Management is good. Leaders have effective systems in place to monitor the
quality of the service and drive improvement. Staff described managers as approachable,
supportive and visible. We found a positive culture within the home, with managers and staff
committed to achieving good outcomes for people and responding appropriately when
improvements are identified.

Findings:


        Well-being                           Good




 People experience positive outcomes and are supported to live fulfilling lives within a welcoming
 and inclusive environment. Staff know people well and help them maintain their individuality,
 interests and relationships, where possible. People are encouraged to make choices about their
 daily lives and receive support that promotes their independence and dignity.


 People spoke positively about living at the service. They told us staff are kind, caring and take time
 to build meaningful relationships with them. One person told us staff keep them company, spend
 time talking with them and support them to take part in activities they enjoy. They said they have
 never felt lonely since moving to the service and have developed friendships with other people
 living there. People described feeling safe and comfortable in their surroundings and told us they
 are treated with respect.


 We observed warm and positive interactions throughout the inspection. Staff spoke to people in a
 calm and friendly manner and responded promptly when people required assistance or
 reassurance. People appeared relaxed in the presence of staff and were comfortable seeking
 support when needed. We saw staff taking time to engage people in conversation and activities,
 encouraging participation and responding sensitively to individual preferences and communication
 needs. We also observed an external activities provider engaging with a group of people during our
 visits, and people expressed enthusiasm for the activities on offer.


 People have opportunities to take part in activities that are meaningful to them. Staff told us they
 tailor activities around people's interests and life experiences, wherever possible. We heard
 examples of people enjoying music, dancing, social events and spending time with others. We
 observed the garden being used effectively during the warm weather, with appropriate measures in
 place to protect people from the sun and help keep them safe. People are also supported to
 maintain aspects of their identity and personal preferences. For example, one person told us how
 important it is to have their hair done and explained the significance of their hairbands. They told us
 staff understand this preference and ensure their hairbands are available and used daily. Relatives
 we spoke with confirmed their loved ones are supported to maintain their appearance and personal
 style.


 People spoke positively about the food provided. They told us there was a good choice of meals
 available and alternative options were offered if they changed their mind. People with dietary

Overall, people experience positive relationships, meaningful engagement and personalised

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support from staff who know them well and understand their
individual needs. Care staff develop positive relationships with people and work alongside
healthcare professionals to promote their health, safety and well-being.


We reviewed a sample of personal plans and found they contained information about people's
backgrounds, preferences and daily routines. Personal plans provided staff with guidance on how
to support people safely whilst promoting choice, independence and dignity. Records showed plans
are reviewed regularly and updated when people's circumstances change. Plans were written in a
person-centred way, reflecting people's individual histories, preferences and outcomes. The service
actively encourages families and representatives to be involved in reviews where appropriate,
helping to ensure support remains relevant and responsive to people's needs. We also saw
evidence of involvement from healthcare professionals, including GPs, district nurses and specialist
services, when additional support or advice was required.


People and relatives spoke positively about the care provided. They described staff as caring,
attentive and responsive to people's individual needs. During the inspection, we observed staff
delivering care in a respectful and compassionate manner, taking account of people's preferences,
routines and wishes. Staff demonstrated a good knowledge of the people they supported and were
able to explain how they adapted care to meet changing needs and personal choices. We saw
people being supported at their own pace and encouraged to remain as independent as possible.


The service has arrangements in place to support people's health and protect them from harm.
Staff demonstrated a good understanding of safeguarding procedures and were able to explain
how they would identify, respond to and report concerns. Records showed safeguarding referrals
are made where required and the service works effectively with external agencies to manage risks
and promote people's safety. Accident and incident records were maintained, and there was
evidence of learning and review following events to support continuous improvement.


The management and storage of medication was an area where we identified a need for
improvement. During the inspection, we found the temperature of an overflow medication storage
room had exceeded recommended limits. The provider responded promptly by seeking advice from
the supplying pharmacy, increasing temperature monitoring arrangements and relocating
medication storage to a more suitable environment. Management provided assurances that
additional measures have been implemented to reduce the risk of reoccurrence.


Overall, people receive personalised care and support that promotes their well-being and helps

ensure needs are understood and met. Positive relationships, person-centred planning and

       Environment                         Good




People live in an environment that is comfortable, safe and suitable for their needs. The home
provides a welcoming atmosphere and supports people to spend time independently or socially,
according to their wishes. The environment promotes people's well-being and enables them to
maintain their independence whilst receiving the support they need.


During the inspection, we found communal areas were clean, tidy and well maintained. Bedrooms
were personalised with photographs, ornaments and personal possessions, helping people
maintain a sense of identity and belonging. People had access to a variety of communal spaces
where they could relax, socialise, meet visitors or participate in activities. The layout of the home
supported people to move around safely and independently wherever possible.


People told us they were happy with their surroundings and felt comfortable living at the service.
The atmosphere within the home was calm, relaxed and welcoming. We saw people choosing
where they wished to spend their time throughout the day and moving freely between communal
areas. People appeared familiar with their surroundings and comfortable accessing different parts
of the home.


The provider maintains appropriate arrangements to promote people's safety. Records
demonstrated regular checks and servicing are completed in key areas, including fire safety
systems, water safety and electrical installations. We saw evidence that equipment is serviced and
repaired when required to help ensure it remains safe and suitable for use. Staff demonstrated an
awareness of health and safety responsibilities and understood the processes for reporting
environmental concerns.


The service has a small outdoor area that is well maintained and easily accessible. This space
provides opportunities for people to spend time outdoors, socialise with relatives and enjoy fresh air
when they choose. During the inspection, we observed people making use of outdoor areas during
periods of warm weather. Appropriate measures were in place to promote people's comfort and
safety whilst outdoors.


The provider continues to invest in the environment and has undertaken significant refurbishment
and improvement work. Records showed ongoing investment in flooring, fire safety arrangements
and wider building improvements. The Responsible Individual reports and quality assurance

reviews demonstrated that management regularly considers environmental issues and identifies
opportunities to further improve the setting. This shows a commitment to maintaining an

During the inspection, we identified an issue relating to the temperature of an overflow medication

Overall, the environment supports positive outcomes for people and meets their day-to-day needs.

invest in maintaining and improving the quality of the environment.

       Leadership & Management               Good




The service benefits from effective leadership and management arrangements that support positive
outcomes for people. Leaders have a good understanding of the service and have established
systems to review quality, monitor performance and identify areas for improvement. There is a
positive culture within the home, with a clear focus on supporting people to achieve the best
possible outcomes.


Staff spoke positively about the management team and consistently described managers as
approachable, supportive and visible within the service. Staff told us they feel able to raise
concerns, seek advice and discuss ideas openly. Many spoke positively about the culture within the
home and described feeling valued, respected and supported in their roles. We found staff worked
well together and demonstrated a shared commitment to providing good quality care. Staff
consistently told us they have sufficient time to engage in meaningful conversations and activities
with people, helping to promote positive relationships and person-centred care rather than focusing
solely on tasks.


The provider has systems in place to monitor and improve the quality of the service. We reviewed
Responsible Individual reports, quality of care reviews and other governance records. These
demonstrated the provider regularly evaluates service performance, identifies areas for
development and takes action to address issues identified. We found evidence of self-evaluation,
consultation with people using the service and ongoing oversight of key aspects of service delivery.
Quality assurance processes support continuous improvement and help ensure the service
remains responsive to people's needs.


The management team demonstrates a willingness to learn and improve practice. We found
leaders were open and transparent throughout the inspection process and responsive to feedback.
For example, following the identification of elevated temperatures within an overflow medication
room, management took prompt action to reduce risk and strengthen monitoring arrangements.
This included seeking advice from the supplying pharmacy, reviewing storage arrangements and
introducing additional safeguards. This demonstrated a proactive approach to addressing concerns
and improving practice.


Training, supervision and staff support arrangements are in place. Staff told us they receive regular
supervision and have access to training opportunities relevant to their roles. Staff described
managers as supportive of their professional development and reported they felt confident seeking
                                Page 11 of 14

demonstrated knowledge of safeguarding and reporting procedures.


Professionals and stakeholders who provided feedback spoke positively about the service and the

to support people's well-being and promote positive outcomes.


Overall, leadership and management arrangements are effective and promote a positive culture.
Managers support staff well, respond appropriately to issues when they arise and demonstrate a
commitment to maintaining and improving outcomes for people living at the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
