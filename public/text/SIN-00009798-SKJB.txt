Summary:


 Magnolia House provides residential care to older people. It is based close to local amenities such
 as a local supermarket and public houses. There is a leisure centre and country park nearby.

 People are supported by care staff who know them well. Many people are supported to achieve
 good wellbeing outcomes, with access to choices about their meals, activities and where they
 spend their time. Whilst we found personal plans lacked clear detail for care staff to follow, they
 know people well and overall, a good standard of care and support is provided. Required
 improvements to personal plans will help strengthen and maintain this standard and ensure people
 receive the right care at the right time.

 The home is clean, homely and welcoming, with access to facilities and specialist equipment to
 support people. People have comfortable bedrooms which they can personalise to suit their

The oversight of the service is inconsistent, and we found several areas which require improvement

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 Many people experience positive outcomes and are supported to maintain choice and control over
 aspects of their daily lives. During the inspection, people were observed making choices about
 meals, and care staff routinely offer drinks throughout the day. Meals are home cooked with fresh
 ingredients, and people told us they enjoy the food. Menus provide options and there is evidence
 that suggestions made through resident meetings are incorporated into meal planning,
 demonstrating that people have opportunities to influence the service they receive. Feedback from
 residents and relatives is largely positive, with several people describing satisfaction with the care
 and support they receive.


 People personalise their bedrooms with photographs, ornaments and personal belongings, helping
 to promote comfort, familiarity and a sense of identity. The environment includes dementia-friendly
 features, such as colour-contrasting grab rails which support orientation and independence.
 Accessible outdoor spaces, including a patio and garden area, provide opportunities for people to
 spend time outside and maintain connections with their surroundings. Relatives have been involved
 in recent improvements to the patio area.

 People can join in with a range of activities, including bingo, films, entertainers and community-
 based opportunities. A new activities coordinator has been recruited, and they will continue to
 progress the activities offer. The service also promotes social and community connections through
 links with local faith groups. Care staff support people to maintain relationships with family and
 friends. People can be supported by care staff who speak Welsh, and all care staff have access to
 Welsh phrase books to assist them to communicate in basic Welsh with people.

 The service demonstrates awareness of the importance of safeguarding and promoting people's
 rights. Applications for deprivation of liberty safeguards are in place where appropriate. This means
 that people who lack capacity to consent to their care and support needs, have a representative to
 ensure their voice is heard and their rights are upheld. Relatives told us they are confident to raise
 any concerns about care arrangements.

 Care staff know people well, and we saw numerous occasions in which care staff responded
 promptly and with kindness and respect when people were anxious or upset.

       Care & Support                       Good





People receive care from staff who know them well and demonstrate a caring approach.
Throughout the inspection, staff interacted warmly with residents and provided reassurance when
individuals required support. The manager knows people well and could tell us about their histories,
preferences and current needs. There are monitoring systems in place for areas such as nutrition,
hydration and repositioning, and records demonstrate these are completed regularly. However,
personal plans do not consistently contain sufficient person-centred information to ensure care staff
can fully understand individuals' wishes, life history, emotional wellbeing needs and preferred
approaches to support. While staff demonstrate knowledge of people in practice, the written
information does not always provide adequate guidance, particularly for individuals who are unable
to communicate their needs or preferences.

People’s personal plans lack clearly defined outcomes and goals, making it difficult to evaluate
whether people are achieving the outcomes that matter to them. Daily records are often task-
focused and contain limited information about people's experiences, wellbeing or responses to
care. Moving and handling documentation requires strengthening, as plans do not consistently
provide clear step-by-step guidance to support staff in delivering care safely and consistently.
Whilst there is a risk to people due to the quality and consistency within personal plans, we found
this was not affecting the care and support people receive at present. However, these shortfalls
create a risk that important information is not clearly communicated across the staff team, and we
have highlighted an area for improvement. We expect the provider to take action.

Care staff ensure people receive health care support they need and referrals are made to
healthcare professionals as required. Records show involvement from a range of healthcare
professionals including general practitioners, district nurses, and hospital services. People are
referred for medication reviews as required to meet their changing needs.

Senior staff ensure medication management is safe and well organised. They check medication
administration records prior to administering medicines and seek consent appropriately, including
establishing whether individuals require "as required" pain relief medicines. Secure storage
arrangements are in place, medication rooms remain locked when not in use, and environmental
temperatures are monitored consistently. Regular audits provide an additional level of oversight.

Infection prevention and control arrangements are robust. The home is clean and well maintained,
appropriate supplies of personal protective equipment are available, and audits relating to infection
prevention are completed regularly.

       Environment                         Good





People live in an environment that is safe, comfortable and well maintained. The home presents as
clean, welcoming and homely, with evidence of ongoing investment and improvement by the
provider. Residents have access to communal areas, personalised bedrooms and outdoor spaces
that support wellbeing and social engagement.

People who are able, can move around the home freely with aids such as colour contrasting grab
rails and mobility equipment. We saw people enjoying the outdoor patio area to socialise with each
other and visitors. During the afternoon the dining room was accessed for receiving visitors and for
engaging in art activities.

The provider demonstrates a commitment to maintaining the physical environment and ensuring
equipment remains safe and suitable. Safety measures, including window restrictors, radiator
covers, secured furniture and evacuation equipment, are in place throughout the service.
Hazardous substances are stored securely, and records show that monitoring and servicing
arrangements are completed for key health and safety systems. This includes fire safety systems,
emergency lighting, electrical installations, water temperature testing and legionella controls. The
building is secure, with a visitor signing in book. We were required to identify ourselves before we
could enter the building.

Fire safety arrangements are well embedded. Regular alarm testing and fire drills take place, and
management provides evidence that actions identified through external fire safety reviews are
addressed promptly. Documentation demonstrates ongoing monitoring of environmental safety,
helping to support the protection of people living at the home.

The service provider is committed to investing in the service and continuous improvement of the
environment. Some rooms have been recently refurbished and there are plans for development of
additional wet room facilities.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement




Care staff are well supported by the service provider with continuous training provision which is
relevant to the needs of the people they support. Training records show staff complete mandatory
learning and additional workshops are available in areas such as medication management,
diabetes care, mental capacity and electronic care systems. Care staff receive regular supervision
every three months and an annual appraisal to consider their learning and development needs.
Care staff are subject to regular disclosure and barring service checks and are required to register
with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator.


The manager and responsible individual (RI) have ensured there are governance arrangements in
place, including audits covering medication, infection prevention and control, health and safety and
operational performance. RI visits take place regularly and records demonstrate engagement with
residents, visitors and staff. The provider also seeks feedback through quality questionnaires.
These are analysed for any recurring patterns or themes. Quality questionnaires reviewed were
consistently positive about the service provided. The manager also maintains systems for
responding to complaints and concerns raised by relatives.

We found governance systems are not consistently effective in identifying and addressing issues to
ensure they do not impact people. Concerns identified during the inspection around access to call
bells have not been recognised through existing audit processes. We found issues relating to call
bell accessibility and monitoring have been raised previously through complaints and professional
concerns, yet governance systems do not provide sufficient evidence that these matters have been
comprehensively investigated, monitored or resolved. This raises questions regarding the
effectiveness of oversight and quality assurance arrangements.

The service provider did not provide all documentation requested during the inspection within
agreed timescales, creating difficulties in evidencing compliance. There is no business continuity
plan in place to consider how the service would continue to operate effectively in the event of an
emergency. We found some policies, including the safeguarding and moving and handling policy
require strengthening. Quality of care reviews sought the views of people and relatives. However,
they did not demonstrate analysis of incidents, accidents, safeguarding issues or complaints to
identify whether there are any patterns or trends. The service provider has failed to submit an
annual return. It is a requirement for a provider to submit an annual return within the timescales set
out by the regulation. Whilst many people achieve good outcomes, there is evidence of poor
outcomes for some people. The service requires improvement because of the gaps identified in the
oversight of the service and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must
take immediate action to address this issue.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
