Summary

--- PAGE 4 ---
Well-being

People can do things that matter to them and that they enjoy. Care workers build
relationships with people by spending time with them, seeking their views and preferences
on an ongoing basis. Staff cater for people’s preferences, are able to anticipate their needs,
and interprete non-verbal communication as required. People can choose what to do and
where to spend their time. Visits from relatives and friends take place, and for those who
require it, visits to family members at their home, are organised. The service strives to
ensure people have as much choice and control over their daily lives as possible.

People are supported to remain as independent and as healthy as possible. Stimulating
activities and good relationships with care staff help to support people’s emotional health.
Appointments with health and social care professionals are arranged for regular checks or if
individual needs change.

People are protected from abuse and neglect as care staff know what to look out for and
how to raise concerns if they suspect someone’s well-being is compromised. Care staff are
trained in safeguarding and have clear policies and procedures to guide them. Well-
established protocols protect people from having their freedom restricted unnecessarily,
and detailed risk management plans help to keep people safe and as independent as
possible.

The accommodation provides people with a spacious, comfortable environment. The
service is clean, warm and welcoming. Bedrooms reflect individuality and clearly represent
individual peferences. The service is set in a quiet and tranquil rural area with access to
safe outside spaces. Equipment is in place to support people to enjoy their time spent
outdoors. There is a small maintenance team employed who undertake regular
maintenance checks.

--- PAGE 5 ---
Care and Support


People appear content. Staff are kind and caring and provide individual support detailed in
personal plans. Staff demonstrate a clear understanding of individual needs. There is
choice including about activities, menu options and daily routines. Care staff know people
well and how they like their support to be provided and strive to deliver support to people in
the way which matters most to them. Personal plans are comprehensive and detailed and
guide care workers about the support required by the individual. The outcomes people
wish to achieve are recorded. Personal plans are updated when changes are identified.
Although informal reviews of personal plans take place regularly and are documented, the
service needs to clearly reflect formal reviews have taken place. Feedback from people
using the service, where possible, and their representatives should be recorded as part of
the review process. This would ensure the service has a clearer audit trail of identified
changes and decision-making. Feedback from peoples’ relatives is positive. One person
told us “The service is first class”. Another stated “X loves it there; they go above and
beyond”.

People receive support as identified in their personal plans. Daily care recordings
completed by care workers give a detailed overview about the support provided. People are
encouraged to be as independent and healthy as possible. People have access to arrange
of activities which helps to enhance their feelings of well-being. The service regularly
engages with other health and social care professionals as required. The service takes a
pro-active approach in order to ensure people remain as healthy as possible.

Staff ensure people are as safe as possible by adhering to clear policies and procedures
and take swift action when needed. Records show management deals promptly and
appropriately with incidents affecting people’s well-being. Detailed and comprehensive risk
assessments are compiled and adhered to. Steps to ensure any restrictions placed on a
person’s liberty are only in their best interests are taken. Written information about people’s
capacity to make decisions, decisions made in their best interests and deprivation of liberty
safeguards applications are stored appropriately.

Medication systems are in place and have been recently enhanced. This demonstrates the
service places high regard to ensuring the ongoing improvement and development of
medication is in place. Medication is securely stored and is only accessible to staff who
have the training and knowledge to administer medication. Medication administration
records (MAR) are available at the service and staff routinely sign when medication is
given. All MAR charts contain a photograph of the people living at the service in order to
reduce the likelihood of medication being given to the wrong person. Written
comprehensive monthly audits of medication which consider the effectiveness of the current
arrangements are not available. Completion of regular medication audits would assist the
provider to identify and make further changes if required. There is a medication policy

 Environment


The environment helps to support people to achieve their personal outcomes. The service
is located over two sites close to one another. Both sites are welcoming, and entrances are
secure. Our identity and reason for our visit was checked prior to admission. People live in
spacious accommodation and are supported to spend time communally and privately.
People’s bedrooms reflect their taste and interests. The service is set in a quiet, peaceful,
rural location with access to pleasant outside spaces.

Arrangements are in place to ensure risks to people’s health and safety are identified and
dealt with. Checks are conducted which identify and address issues. The service has
access to a small team of maintenance staff. There are arrangements in place for ensuring
maintenance issues are reported and dealt with. The recommendations identified following
a recent Fire Officer’s report have been completed. Individuals living at the service have
appropriate personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEP) in place and these have been
reviewed and updated. Copies of PEEPs are easily accessible in a separate file should
these be required in the event of an emergency. There is appropriate oversight regarding
electricity safety checks and portable application testing (PAT). Both sites have had new
gas boilers installed within the last year and servicing of these were not due at the time of
the inspection. An up-to-date fire risk assessment is in place, and there is documentation
which confirms fire drills are carried out at appropriate intervals.

Leadership and Management

People are provided with accurate information about the service. There is a statement of
purpose (SOP) which describes how the service is provided. This helps to support people
to choose a service which is able to meet their needs. The statement of purpose reflects the
service being provided.

The provider has systems in place which help identify areas where the service needs to
develop and improve. The manager completes a range of information regarding quality
assurance which demonstrate their ongoing oversight of the daily management of the
service. Regular team meetings take place and feedback from these meetings help the
service to identify areas where they need to further develop. The most recent quality of care
review report was provided to us at the time of the inspection. The provider considers how
well the service is able to meet people’s well-being outcomes. The provider visits regularly
to oversee progress and developments and to complete checks. Reports completed on a
three-monthly basis reflect what is working well and identifies areas for further
development.

People are supported by staff who are trained, valued and developed. The provider
conducts the necessary checks when recruiting staff. New staff receive an induction in line
with the requirements of the social care workforce regulator Social Care Wales (SCW).
Most staff are fully registered with SCW as members of the social care workforce. Some
newly recruited staff members are working towards their registration. Staff receive an
extensive training programme relevant to their roles and refresher training is available.
Individualised supervision is provided to staff at regular intervals in line with regulation, and
annual appraisals take place. Staff are complementary about working at the service, are
happy in their roles and feel appropriately supported. One care worker told us “I love my
job”. Another stated “I feel valued, the manager is approachable and listens”.

The service provider ensures the leadership and management arrangements in place,
supports the smooth and effective operation of the service.The manager is experienced and
has worked at the service for some years. They know people well and demonstrate
commitment to providing a good quality service. They ensure effective day-to-day
management and oversight of the service takes place.
