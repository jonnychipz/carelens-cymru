Summary:



 People living in Queen Elizabeth Court benefit from being cared for by a consistent care staff group
 who know their needs well. People have a voice and can influence their care. The rating for well-
 being is “Excellent”. This is because people are supported to meet their aspirations and objectives
 to a high degree. Activities available for people are, with little exception, creative, proven and
 praise-worthy. People praised the activities available for them, we saw consistent planning
 regarding varied activities to encourage memory, interest and socialisation. People are happy with
 their bedrooms and facilities in the home and feel they have choices regarding décor. People feel
 safe in the home and care staff are trained regarding safeguarding and protecting people from
 harm and neglect.

Care staff treat people with respect and dignity. People said they have good care and support. Any
new symptoms are identified quickly and medical assistance sought. People’s personal plans
centre around their individual needs and are kept updated to ensure they continue to meet
changing needs. Medicine administration and storage practices are sound and reflect good
practice. The home presents as clean and tidy and infection control practices are compliant with
best practice guidance. The rating for care and support is “Good” as care is provided in a way
which supports people’s needs and preferences.


People live in an environment which is well maintained and comfortable. Plans are in place to
continually upgrade and refresh the environment. The home is spacious with good facilities. There
are several communal spaces, areas promoting calm and others for socialisation. The rating for the
environment is “Good” as systems are in place to ensure people’s safety and maintenance plans
are in place to ensure a service which is continually evolving to meet people’s objectives.


People benefit from living in a home which is well managed and senior staff have good oversight of
the service provided. The Responsible Individual (RI) meets the needs of the regulations by visiting
the home regularly and providing reports assessing the quality of care given to people. Care staff
receive training and supervision to support good practice. Continuity of care is provided by a stable
care staff group who are safely recruited. Providers invest in the home to ensure good standards of
care. The rating for leadership and management is “Good” as standards are continually monitored
to ensure good care.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent





 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. People are very happy in the home; we
 had sterling feedback from the people spoken with. One person said, “My health and well-being
 have improved since being here”, and “I wish I’d moved here years ago”. People’s personal plans
 are person centred and prioritise their objectives which are supported by positive risk management.
 A person said, “I can still be independent.” They explained how the service supports them to go out
 and about and attend local and far-afield meetings and events. The activities in the home are, with
  little exception, meaningful and well organised. We saw preparation for a Christmas fair, with stalls
 and games and Christmas décor in the reception and throughout the home. Family members told
 us they were excited to attend the event with their relative. Neighbours and local school children
 were also invited. Families said they are always welcome in the home and grandchildren are,
 “Made a fuss of”. We saw people dressed in 1920’s attire, watching a black and white silent film
 whilst an entertainer played the piano, people said they enjoyed it. A person showed the weekly
 newsletter they all receive showing the activities and events available in the week, they said
 communication in the home is, “Excellent”. The manager told us activities staff in the home had
 been short-listed for an award for their work. The home has started a “wishing well” project where
 people can write their dearest held wishes and objectives. Two people who missed walking in and
 climbing the mountains were taken to Snowdon and treated to a train ride up the mountain which
 they thoroughly enjoyed. Overall, we saw a superb commitment to providing activities for people
 which helps them achieve their personal aspirations and outcomes.



 People are protected from abuse and neglect. People are aware of how to raise concerns should
 they need to and are assisted with communication needs. People’s first language choices are
 recorded in their personal plans, and the service is working towards an ‘Active Offer’ of the Welsh
 language by including the preference for Welsh speakers in their recruitment adverts. Any
 communication difficulties are recorded in personal plans and strategies for assistance are
 recorded. Care staff told us they are aware of how to raise concerns should they be worried about
 a person’s care.


 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. People praised the
 environment and their bedrooms, one person said, “This IS my home”, another person said, “I am
 at home.” People said they are comfortable, a person commented, “My bedroom contains all I
 need.” A family member commented on the cleanliness and homeliness of the home describing it
 as, “So welcoming”. The home is spacious and there are areas for people to socialise in or quieter
 areas for relaxing. People said they have daily choices as to where they wish to spend their day.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive care and support which enables them to achieve their personal outcomes. Care is
planned in a person-centred way and records their wishes, aspirations, risks and specialist needs.
Personal plans are tailored to people's needs and are frequently reviewed to account for any
changes and to keep plans current. Care staff have electronic devices to input into care records as
they go along ensuring care records are correct. Personal plans include people’s preferred routines
and choices ensuring they have a voice in their care. People’s history and people and dates of
importance are also included in their records to enable their personal objectives. We saw evidence
of good health monitoring, people said care staff pick up when they are unwell and source medical
attention in a timely way. A person said care staff anticipate their needs and bring medicines as
prescribed to alleviate symptoms. We noted good rapport between people and care staff. People
said care staff are, “Respectful”, and “Kind”. Each unit in the home has its own care staff and
people experience continuity in care from care staff who know their needs well. Communication
needs are recorded, and people receive support to make their needs known. People are assessed
before receiving the service to ensure the home can meet their needs. A family commented they
were pleased with the transition from the person’s own home to living in Queen Elizabeth Court,
they said, “It was such an easy transition.”


People are protected from harm and abuse. Care staff receive training and refresher training
regarding safeguarding and are aware of local reporting processes to keep people safe. Care
markers such as falls rates are audited to look for any patterns and address any issues. Senior
staff report incidents and accidents appropriately to Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) and local
authorities.


People’s medications are safely managed. There are good storage and administration practices in
the home. Medications are stored in specific rooms which are temperature checked to keep them in
an optimal way. There are good ordering systems in the home to ensure people have good stocks
of their prescribed medications. Nurses have medicine refresher training to ensure good knowledge
and practice. Medicine stocks are counted and recorded and there are good auditing systems for
receiving medications into the home and destroying medication which is no longer required.

       Environment                         Good





People live in a home which cares for their health and safety. The home presents as clean and tidy
and has spacious lounges and dining areas. Walkways and fire exits are kept clear of obstacles
and are spacious so that people can walk around safely. People can choose to spend time in their
bedroom or in the lounge areas to socialise, engage in activities, eat meals in and have visitors.
The home is spacious and has good sized bedrooms which are maintained to a good standard for
people’s comfort and enjoyment. An over-flow car park is planned to accommodate visitors and
care staff. People can have a say in the décor of their room and can personalise them. There are
appropriate facilities in the home and people are happy with the laundry service provided. The
hygiene rating for the home is five, this is the highest rating possible. People can access the
equipment needed for their safe care; this is well maintained and serviced to ensure their safety.




Health and safety checks and assessments are in place and up to date to enable people’s safety.
Utility checks and certificates are in place for water, gas and electricity. Weekly fire alarm checks
are conducted, and care staff are trained regarding evacuating people in the event of a fire. People
have personalised Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs), to ensure their safe
evacuation in the event of an emergency. There is a maintenance person employed to ensure the
upkeep and maintenance of the home. Housekeeping staff are employed to ensure the home is
clean and has good infection control. Entrances to the home are locked and people sign in and out
for accountability and a record in the event of a fire or emergency of any extra people within the
home. There are car parking areas to the front of the home with sufficient space for ambulances if
required.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The provider has good governance arrangements in place to ensure the smooth running of the
service. The RI visits regularly and considers the views of people, family and care staff regarding
the service. The RI provides a quality report about the home in line with the requirements of the
regulation, the RI considers people’s well-being, the care and support provided, environmental
needs and maintenance and managerial issues such as staffing requirements, training and
supervision. Senior care staff feel supported by the RI and can access training required for their
roles. Service audits conducted by senior care staff are discussed with the RI and any actions to be
taken to maintain good care standards. People have information regarding the service prior to
using the service, this information is frequently reviewed to ensure its accuracy.

Care staff are safely recruited. We saw from staff records that appropriate employment checks are
in place and in date to ensure they are appropriate to work with vulnerable adults. Checks are
made to ensure care staff are qualified and registered with Social Care Wales and the Nursing and
Midwifery Council. Staff receive regular training and supervision suitable for their role. Work is
ongoing to ensure good morale among care staff groups and to ensure good communication and
that they can access the support they require. A nurse said they are supported to give people good
care and can have a voice in the running of the service, they said, “I sleep easy at night knowing
we are enabled to give people the care they need,” The service retains good numbers of care staff
to ensure continuity of care for people.


The provider invests in the home; the building is maintained to a good standard to provide dignified
surroundings for people. People are offered meals made with fresh ingredients and can access
snacks and drinks as required. Care staff are safely recruited, and investment is made in
appropriate training. Equipment is replaced as required as are soft furnishings, towels and bedding.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
