Summary:

 Crick Care Home is situated in a rural location on the outskirts of Caldicot in Monmouthshire. The
 service provides accommodation, support and nursing care for up to 49 people.

 At this inspection, we found people's wellbeing is good. People and their relatives spoke positively
 about the service, and our observations confirmed that staff treat people with dignity, respect and
 kindness.

 People receive good care and support, with personal plans and risk management arrangements in
 place to promote their health, safety and wellbeing. The management team recognises
 opportunities to further strengthen care planning to ensure it consistently reflects people's individual
 wishes, aspirations and personal outcomes.

 People live in a good environment that is clean, comfortable and well maintained. They can move
 around safely and have access to the equipment they need to promote their independence, comfort
 and wellbeing. The provider continues to invest in the environment to enhance people's quality of
 life.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Feedback from people living at the service and their representatives was overwhelmingly positive.
 People are supported to make as many choices about their daily lives, including food, clothing and
 routines as possible. We observed staff offering choices and responding to people's preferences in
 a respectful and person-centred manner. Opportunities exist for the service to further strengthen
 how choices and people's views are recorded and evidenced within daily records and care planning
 documentation. The management team recognises the value of ensuring people have a stronger
 voice in shaping the service and is committed to developing a wider range of methods for gathering
 feedback and involving people.


 Changes in the activities team has impacted on the current range and quality of activities,
 engagement and stimulation available to people. Activity assistants are developing their skills to
 ensure meaningful opportunities are available and people achieve positive well-being outcomes.
 People benefit from positive relationships with family and friends, and relatives told us they receive
 regular updates regarding their loved one's well-being. Visitors are welcomed, helping people
 maintain important connections and contributing to their sense of belonging and emotional well-
 being.

 People are encouraged and supported to achieve and maintain the best possible health outcomes.
 Effective clinical oversight and monitoring help ensure people's physical health needs are identified
 and responded to promptly. Staff know people well and demonstrate a proactive approach to
 seeking medical advice and intervention when required. Medicine management processes ensure
 people receive their prescribed medication safely and reliably. The management team has
 identified opportunities to further strengthen staff confidence and consistency when supporting
 individuals who experience periods of heightened anxiety or distress to enhance outcomes for
 everyone living in the home.

 There are effective systems in place to help protect people from harm. People are supported by
 staff who have been recruited safely. Safeguarding arrangements are well established, supported
 by clear policies and regular staff training. Accidents and incidents are monitored appropriately,
 with action taken to reduce the risk of recurrence. The service is also introducing additional
 monitoring of call bell response times to further strengthen assurance that people receive timely
 support when required.

 People live in an environment that promotes their well-being and comfort. Bedrooms reflect
 people's individual preferences providing a sense of belonging. Outdoor space is accessible and
 include plans to develop a mini allotment where people can grow produce. Planned flooring

       Care & Support                       Good



Feedback from people receiving a service, as well as their relatives and representatives, is
consistently positive about the care and support they receive. Staff provide support in a caring,
compassionate and person-centred way, demonstrating kindness and respect in their day-to-day
interactions. We observe warm and meaningful engagement, particularly during mobility support,
where staff offer reassurance, encouragement and praise to promote people's comfort, confidence
and well-being. The management team promotes a culture of continuous improvement and is
focused on building on these strengths to ensure every interaction consistently promotes dignity,
respect and well-being.


An assessment is completed prior to admission to ensure the service can meet peoples identified
needs. This assessment informs the development of personal plans that set out how people wish to
be supported and identify the assistance they require. Plans provide clear guidance for staff and
are regularly reviewed to reflect people's changing needs. The service effectively captures and
responds to people's clinical needs; however, there is an opportunity to further enhance person-
centred practice by including more detailed information about people's life histories, interests,
preferences and what matters most to them. Greater emphasis on personal outcomes and clearer
evidence of people's involvement, or that of their representatives, in care planning and reviews
would further strengthen the personalised approach. The management team recognises these
development areas and is committed to building on existing strengths to ensure care planning
consistently reflects people's individual wishes, aspirations and well-being outcomes. We expect
action to be taken to ensure a consistently person-centred focused is achieved.

Risk assessments are detailed and person-centred, providing clear guidance to support people
safely and promote their well-being. Effective systems are in place to ensure people's rights and
best interests are upheld. Mental Capacity Act assessments and Best Interest processes are in
place where required. Systems will be further developed to reflect current changes associated with
deprivation of liberty safeguards and legislative changes.

Appropriate referrals to health and social care professionals are made with recommendations acted
upon by the service. People’s health is closely monitored to enable the service to act responsively
to any change in their needs. There are effective medication management systems in place. We
saw medication is securely stored and it can only be accessed by authorised personnel. Medication
administration record (MAR) charts are filled in correctly confirming people receive their medication
at the right time. We saw evidence of internal checks and actions taken in response to issues
identified through external monitoring. The management team took action to strengthen monitoring
and auditing processes, supporting the consistent delivery of care in line with people's assessed
needs and specified timeframes for example with pressure relief.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a safe, clean and well-maintained environment at Crick Care Home. Access to the
home is controlled through a keycode entry system, which helps ensure people’s safety while
maintaining their freedom to move around the service. Effective infection prevention and control
measures are in place. Dedicated domestic staff maintain a good standard of cleanliness
throughout the home, and all areas observed during the inspection were clean, tidy and odour free.
Staff receive infection control training and demonstrated safe, calm and confident use of equipment
when supporting people. Food hygiene standards are maintained to a high level, as evidenced by
the service achieving a Food Standards Agency rating of 5.

People have access to comfortable and personalised accommodation. Bedrooms are of an
appropriate size and reflect people’s individual preferences, with personal belongings and items of
significance helping to create a homely atmosphere. There are sufficient bathroom and toilet
facilities to meet people’s needs. The large communal area has been informally zoned through the
arrangement of furniture to create separate spaces for social interaction and relaxation.
Management is considering further enhancements to provide a dedicated calm and quiet area for
people who prefer a lower-stimulation environment. People also have access to a garden with
seating and shaded areas, and plans are in place to develop a mini allotment to support meaningful
occupation and provide opportunities for people to grow their own produce.

There are maintenance systems with clear lines of communication, so any repairs are logged and
completed in good time. We were told about a programme of upgrades including the replacement
of flooring and redecoration. Comprehensive systems are in place to monitor the condition of the
premises and equipment, with regular health and safety checks undertaken. Essential servicing
and maintenance of utilities, including gas and electrical systems, are completed in a timely
manner, and specialist equipment such as hoists and slings are routinely inspected to ensure they
remain safe and fit for purpose. Fire safety arrangements are embedded, with regular checks
completed and clear personal emergency evacuation plans in place for individuals. These
measures help ensure people live in a safe, secure and well-maintained environment that supports
positive outcomes and enhances their quality of life.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from established governance arrangements that support the delivery of safe
care and positive outcomes for people. Quality assurance systems are in place, including
Responsible Individual (RI) oversight visits and Quality of Care reviews. Operational audits and
monitoring systems provide management with oversight of service delivery and demonstrate a
commitment to continuous improvement. Going forward audit systems will include evidence of
actions taken to address any deficits identified.


The service benefits from strong clinical oversight and effective leadership, with the service
manager and clinical manager providing clear direction and support to a skilled team of nurses and
healthcare assistants who work collaboratively to meet people's needs. Leaders are visible,
approachable and well regarded by staff, promoting a positive and inclusive culture within the
service. Management work closely with health professionals to support positive outcomes for
people and responds constructively to external feedback, demonstrating a clear commitment to
continuous improvement and the ongoing development of service quality.

People are supported by a stable staff team who are committed to their roles. Safe recruitment
practices are well established and help ensure suitable staff are employed. All staff must provide
appropriate identification documents as well as complete a Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS)
check, provide satisfactory references and full employment history. New staff complete an induction
programme that supports them to develop the skills and knowledge required for their roles. Going
forward the management team will incorporate the All-Wales Induction Framework within workforce
development arrangements. This will support staff to develop the knowledge and competence
required to meet professional standards and progress towards recognised health and social care
qualifications.

Staff compliance with core e-learning training was generally good. Gaps in staff’s moving and
handling, and fire safety training were identified, management recognised the significance of this
and have started to address the deficits. Management have also experienced difficulties in
maintaining consistent staff supervision and appraisal arrangements. This has previously been
identified through internal monitoring and external reviews. Some improvements have been made
since our inspection visit, but further work is required to ensure all staff receive regular supervision,
appraisal and opportunities for professional development, we expect action to be taken.

Sufficient staff with the inclusion of agency staff were available to meet people’s needs during our
inspection. The provider does not currently operate a formal dependency tool or system to
demonstrate how staffing arrangements are adjusted in response to changing needs. This is
particularly important given feedback from staff regarding the complexity of people's needs,

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
