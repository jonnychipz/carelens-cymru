Summary:

 Glascoed Residential Care Home Limited provides care without nursing to adults requiring personal
 care.

 The service demonstrates strengths in staff compassion and people report feeling happy and well
 treated. However, the inspection identified some significant shortfalls. Wellbeing requires
 improvement because opportunities for people to engage in meaningful activities is limited.

 Care and support require significant improvement because care planning documentation is
 infrequently reviewed, compromising accuracy of direction to staff, and important health
 communication is not available.

 The environment requires significant improvement. Redecoration, renewal and repair is required
 and equipment is not safely stored to prevent obstruction and risks of trips and falls.

 Leadership and management requires significant improvement. There is currently no manager. We
 identified shortfalls and evidence of poor outcomes. There is no review of the quality of care, and
 no evidence that ongoing competency of staff practice is being assessed. These issues limit

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People have some choice and control within the home supported by sensitive staff responses, but
 they shared that sometimes there is little to do. They have their own books, puzzles and watch
 television but meaningful activities are not provided as described in the Statement of Purpose.
 Apart from occasional activities such as exercise with a ball and Bingo, occasional visits from a
 musical entertainer and summer evenings in the garden, there was little evidence of regularly
 planned, interest-based engagement. People only go for trips out if supported by their family to do
 so, limiting opportunities for them to maintain meaningful connections with their community. No
 activity planning or records were available, limiting assurance that people’s wellbeing and
 independence are being actively promoted. Outcomes require improvement because people need
 meaningful, interesting activities to promote their wellbeing, and we expect the provider to make
 improvements.

 People praised staff for their kindness and described a positive emotional experience of living at
 the home. They said they are treated well. Staff interactions observed were compassionate. They
 demonstrated respectful support that promoted privacy and dignity, particularly with personal care,
 and this supports protection from neglect and abuse. However, we identified risks within the
 environment that could impact safety, including mobility aids obstructing pathways to the dining
 table and an absence of adequate hand washing equipment in the communal bathroom. These
 issues highlight the need for more consistent monitoring to protect people from harm. There are no
 routine arrangements to support residents to share their views collectively, as residents’ meetings
 do not take place. Surveys are used to gather feedback, with staff support where needed. Using an
 independent advocacy service would make this process more transparent.

 People appeared settled and emotionally comfortable within the home. They are supported to
 express their identities in their dress, their religion and sports teams they support. One person
 reported enjoying a positive social atmosphere with staff and other residents. They said, “Everyone
 is so friendly here. Nothing is too much trouble. I’m doing what I’d be doing at home, but I have a
 laugh here.” Visitors are welcomed including local clergy, who called to offer communion on the day
 of our inspection.

 Many bedrooms are personalised with photographs and personal belongings, supporting identity
 and familiarity. External garden areas are well maintained and accessible, providing lots of space to
 sit out. However, the internal environment requires significant improvement. Several bedrooms and
 communal areas are dated and in need of repair and decoration. One room had inadequate
 sanitary arrangements and a décor the person found ‘depressing’. Once alerted to the resident’s
 view of their room, the RI (responsible individual) immediately offered an alternative with a
 functional ensuite.

                                    Requires Significant
       Care & Support
                                       Improvement




People receive the care and support they need to help achieve their recorded personal outcomes,
but records are not always accurate and could be misleading. Outcomes are generic and repetitive,
such as “maintain health and wellbeing,” and do not reflect individual aspirations or self-directed
goals. Pre-admission assessments are present in care files, providing a basic understanding of
individual needs prior to moving into the service. Risk assessments are in place. However, care
plans are not reviewed in line with requirements. One had not been reviewed since August 2024,
and another plan, dated August 2025, contained outdated information. Missing documentation such
as district nurse’s notes and instructions, reduce assurance that health needs are being
consistently monitored and reviewed.

The presence of kind, caring staff helps ensure people are protected from harm and abuse. Staff
interactions observed demonstrated respectful, discreet practice, supporting dignity and protection
from harm. People said they feel cared for and they can speak with visitors and staff if they have
concerns. Staff are trained in safeguarding and know what to do if they have concerns. However,
incomplete, inaccurate care records in some files and the lack of updated health information in one
person’s care plan, reduces assurance that risks are consistently reviewed and managed. These
omissions put people at risk of harm.

There are arrangements in place to help ensure medication is safely managed. A pharmacy-led
medication audit identified recommendations, which were actioned. A staff-led medication audit
was completed in March 2026, demonstrating monitoring arrangements. Staff are trained to
administer medication, and records are kept. We found two unexplained gaps in the medication
administration charts and found frequent competency checks of staff practice are not currently
carried out.

Infection prevention practices require improvement. In their own audit, the RI identified some
mattresses were marked and some pillows lacked protective covers, but no subsequent action was
noted. On the day of the visit, no handwash soap was available in the ground floor communal
bathroom, and fabric towels were used instead of paper towels, increasing infection risk.

Outcomes for people require significant improvement because people’s wellbeing is at risk and we
have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address
this issue..

                                    Requires Significant
       Environment
                                       Improvement


People live in an environment with well-maintained equipment and safety systems, but
improvements are required to the aesthetics of the home and storage of aids, to enhance people’s
wellbeing. Records evidence safety systems and equipment are appropriately tested and serviced,
including fire alarms, lifts, and electrical and gas checks. External garden areas are well
maintained. However, internal communal and private areas require significant improvement.
Walking aids stored in the living room created clutter and trip hazards. The living room is dated and
in need of redecoration. Some bedrooms require repair, with broken drawers, missing handles,
damaged doors and missing or faulty window coverings. A robust plan, including proposed dates
for completion of actions such as repairing, redecorating and renewing parts of the environment is
lacking. Outcomes for people require significant improvement because the condition of internal
communal and private spaces does not consistently support people’s comfort, dignity or
independence. The provider must take immediate action to address these issue.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


Some staff have worked for the provider for many years and they spoke positively about
leadership. One member of staff said: “It’s been fantastic working here. I couldn’t ask for a better
boss and staff. I feel really valued.” The RI is present daily so knows the people who live and work
here very well.

There are arrangements in place to help ensure the provider has oversight, but these need
significant improvement if they are to positively affect the quality of the service. We found policies
have been reviewed. A schedule of audits is in place and records show these have been
completed. Despite these regular audits, the RI has not identified key risks and shortfalls, including
incomplete and inaccurate care documentation, incomplete evidence of safe staff recruitment, lack
of staff training in moving and handling, improvements needed in the environment, inadequate
infection prevention controls and continued competency checks with practices such as medication
administration.

Surveys of people and relative’s views on the service are not being completed as required;
Relatives’ surveys were last completed in February 2025. Quality of Care Review reports have not
been completed, and this was identified as an area for improvement at the last inspection.

People at Glascoed are supported by staff who have received training in most areas necessary to
meet their needs. We saw records and certificates evidencing knowledge in a range of relevant
areas, although no record or certificates for moving and handing training. Competency checks are
not carried out to ensure continued accuracy of knowledge and safe practice, particularly important
in relation to safeguarding and medication administration. Personnel files could not evidence safe
recruitment and vetting procedures. There were insufficient or no references in some files. The
provider has accepted DBS (Disclosure and Barring Service) certificates requested by staff’s
previous employers instead of carrying out their own checks. The files held no information
regarding staff registration with Social Care Wales. The RI was unable to provide evidence of their
own up to date Disclosure and Barring Service (criminal records) check.

The registered manager has been absent for six months, and CIW was not notified, which
represents a significant governance failure. The inspection has identified shortfalls in the leadership
and management of the service. Outcomes for people require significant improvement because the
RI has not demonstrated sufficient oversight of service compliance in several areas and has no
manager to support them. This puts people at risk of poor outcomes, and we have therefore issued
a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address these issues.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
