Summary:


 Fairfield House is located in Llandaff, Cardiff, and provides residential care and support for adults
 with a learning disability and/or autistic people who may present with behaviours that challenge
 services, as well as those with complex needs.

 Well-being is excellent because people experience exceptional, life-enhancing outcomes. They are
 empowered to make meaningful choices, achieve ambitious goals and maximise their
 independence. As a result, they enjoy improved well-being, strong relationships, greater community
 inclusion and enriched, self-directed lives.

 Care and support is good because people receive safe, personalised support which helps them
 stay healthy, maintain their independence and achieve what matters to them. Care is regularly
 reviewed and adapted to meet changing needs, and effective health, safeguarding, medication and

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience exceptional and life-enhancing well-being outcomes through support expertly
 tailored to their strengths, aspirations and potential. Care staff have an outstanding understanding
 of what matters to people and consistently empower them to lead meaningful, ambitious and self-
 directed lives. As a result, people achieve outcomes which extend far beyond their care and
 support needs, developing confidence, independence, valued relationships and active roles within
 their communities.

 We found compelling evidence of a highly aspirational and strengths-based culture. People are
 supported to recognise their abilities, pursue their ambitions and maximise their potential. People,
 including those with complex needs and experiences of restrictive environments, have overcome
 barriers and achieved outcomes previously unattainable. This has led to transformative and
 sustained improvements in well-being, confidence and quality of life. Professionals highly commend
 the provider's approach, noting “The organisation supports individuals to develop their potential by
 ensuring any outcomes are service user specific.”

 People are at the centre of decision-making, with care staff demonstrating an exceptional
 understanding of individual communication needs, preferences and wishes. Highly personalised
 communication approaches enable people to express their views, make informed choices and
 exercise maximum control and independence in their daily lives. Through tailored communication
 tools and support, people have a strong voice in decisions affecting them.

 We saw excellent progress towards meaningful personal goals. One person, following a lengthy
 hospital stay, rebuilt their confidence and independence through positive risk-taking and now
 accesses community facilities independently. Professionals report a significant improvement in their
 confidence, self-esteem and quality of life, while reviews evidence increased confidence, stronger
 communication and greater participation in everyday life.

 People tell us they feel safe. One person said “The staff treat me as they should. I feel safe here”.
 Keeping people safe is central to the service and is reflected in staff's everyday practice
 Knowledgeable staff, accessible information, advocacy and personalised communication methods
 ensure people can confidently raise concerns.

 People are supported to build and sustain meaningful relationships, strengthen community
 connections and live enriched, fulfilling lives. Care staff promote inclusion, belonging and
 opportunity, enabling people to overcome barriers, grow in confidence and participate fully in
 activities which reflect their interests and aspirations. Family relationships are actively nurtured,
 helping people maintain important connections and enjoy meaningful time with loved ones,

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support. Detailed assessments help ensure care staff have a
good understanding of people's strengths, health needs, communication styles, risks and desired
outcomes before support is provided. The provider works closely with families, advocates and
healthcare professionals to ensure care reflects what is important to each person and promotes
positive outcomes.

People are involved in planning and reviewing their care wherever possible. Personal plans provide
care staff with clear guidance about how people wish to be supported, including their routines,
preferences, communication needs and goals. Reviews help ensure support remains relevant as
needs change. We found examples of care being adapted in response to changes in people's
circumstances and achievements.

People benefit from support which encourages them to develop skills, increase independence and
participate in daily life. During our inspection, we observed people taking part in household
activities, preparing food and making choices about how they spent their time. Care staff were
patient, encouraging and focused on promoting independence, rather than completing tasks for
people. Interactions were warm, respectful and person-centred, helping people feel valued and
involved.

People receive support which promotes their health and well-being through effective partnership
working with healthcare professionals. These include GPs, psychiatrists, psychologists and
specialist services. This ensures people receive timely advice, treatment and support. We saw
evidence of positive health outcomes, including a significant reduction in one person's seizure
activity through specialist guidance, regular reviews and staff training.

People are protected through effective safeguarding arrangements. Care staff demonstrate a good
understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities and were able to explain how they would
respond to concerns. Risk assessments are detailed and regularly reviewed, helping people remain
safe whilst promoting independence and choice.

People receive their medication safely from trained and competent care staff. Robust medication
systems support safe administration, storage and recording practices. Care staff complete
medication training, competency assessments and refresher training to ensure they remain
knowledgeable and skilled. Where people have specialist health needs, such as epilepsy,
additional training and guidance are provided to ensure medication is administered safely and
effectively. Medication arrangements are reviewed regularly to help ensure they continue to meet
people's needs.

People are protected from the risk of infection through effective infection prevention and control
arrangements. Staff receive infection control training and were observed using appropriate
personal protective equipment. Cleaning schedules, environmental checks and health and safety
monitoring help maintain a safe environment.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a spacious and welcoming home which provides a comfortable environment and a
range of facilities to support their well-being, independence and quality of life. Bedrooms are
personalised to reflect people's tastes and preferences, helping them to feel at home and maintain
their individuality. People share communal bathrooms which are modern and adapted to meet
individual needs. The home offers several communal areas which provide people with choice about
how they spend their time. A large lounge provides a comfortable setting where people can relax,
watch television and spend time together. There is also a smaller lounge equipped with games,
craft materials and other resources to support people's interests and encourage meaningful
activities. A bright conservatory-dining area overlooks the garden and provides an attractive space
for people to enjoy meals and social interaction with others. The layout of the home also includes
quieter areas where people can spend time alone or meet with visitors and professionals in private.

People have access to a secure and enclosed garden which offers opportunities to spend time
outdoors in a safe environment. The garden includes lawned areas, plants and seating areas,
enabling people to relax, socialise and enjoy outdoor activities throughout the year.

During the inspection we identified some areas of the environment which would benefit from
improvement, including wear and tear to décor, flooring and furnishings, together with a small
number of maintenance issues. The provider had already identified these areas and was able to
demonstrate a comprehensive programme of refurbishment and improvement works. We saw
evidence of ongoing investment in the home, including plans for redecoration and upgrades
designed to further enhance the quality of the living environment. Management demonstrates a
proactive approach to identifying priorities, reducing risks and monitoring progress. We are assured
improvements are being progressed in a planned and timely way.

Arrangements to support health and safety are well established. Access to the home is controlled
through secure entrance doors and visitor sign-in procedures, helping to promote people's safety
and security.

The provider undertakes regular monitoring and testing of the environment, including fire safety
checks, fire drills, emergency lighting tests, legionella monitoring, water temperature checks,
electrical testing and gas safety inspections. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are
available for individuals, and first aid equipment is subject to regular checks. These arrangements
help ensure people live in an environment where risks are identified and managed appropriately.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from a well organised, effectively managed service which focuses on achieving
positive outcomes. Care staff, professionals and managers consistently describe a service which is
person-centred, supportive and committed to continuous improvement.

People are supported by leaders who know them well and maintain effective oversight of their care
and support needs. Managers regularly review people's health, risks, goals and future plans,
working with professionals and responding to changing needs to help ensure positive outcomes.
Professionals speak positively about the leadership of the service. One professional described the
Registered Manager as “a very proactive, hands on manager who understands the needs of the
people supported.”

Care staff tell us they feel well supported by managers and have access to regular supervision,
appraisals, team meetings and informal support. One member of staff said, “The support from
management is very good,” while another told us, “Management are great and really supportive.”
Care staff describe opportunities to progress within the organisation, with one deputy manager
explaining they had progressed from a support worker role into management.

People are supported by staff with the skills, knowledge and training needed to meet their needs.
Training is tailored to the people living at the service, including autism, epilepsy, positive behaviour
support, safeguarding, trauma-informed practice and medication management. Care staff speak
positively about the training provided, with one member of staff saying, “The training is very good
here, trauma training has helped me gain a better understanding of people's experiences and
needs.”

People, families, staff and professionals have opportunities to share their views about the service.
Feedback is gathered through reviews, meetings, surveys and visits from senior leaders.
Information is provided in accessible formats to help people participate as fully as possible.

Safe recruitment processes help ensure suitable staff are employed. We found evidence of
appropriate pre-employment checks, including references, Disclosure and Barring Service checks,
right to work documentation and verification of qualifications.

The provider has effective systems in place to monitor the quality of the service and identify areas
for improvement. These include visits by the Responsible Individual, quality assurance audits,
health and safety checks, medication audits and reviews of the quality of care provided. Where
issues are identified, action plans are developed to address them and improve outcomes for
people. We found evidence learning from audits is used to strengthen practice and support service

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
