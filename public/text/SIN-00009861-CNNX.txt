Summary:

 Ty Mawr is located in Neath and provides personal care and support to people. It is close to a
 number of community facilities which can be easily accessed from the service. During this
 inspection, we found significant improvements have been made since the previous inspection in
 March 2026. Earlier concerns relating to care planning, leadership, care staff support, training
 compliance, medication management and governance have largely been addressed. The service
 has been rated good in all four themes.

 People participate in meaningful activities, access the community and appear relaxed within a
 personalised and homely environment. Relatives provided positive feedback, describing care staff
 as caring, communicative and person centred. Menus offered choice and information was
 displayed in accessible formats. Personal plans have improved considerably and are now detailed,
 regularly reviewed and supported by appropriate risk assessments. Daily records are detailed and
 informative.

 Leadership and management have strengthened since the last inspection. Supervisions, team
 meetings and training compliance have improved, and care staff reported higher morale, better

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Well-being is rated as good and people live healthily and safely with control over their lives. People
 at Ty Mawr experience positive well-being outcomes and are supported by a staff team that
 understands their individual preferences, aspirations and support needs. Since the previous
 inspection, significant improvements have been made to the quality of personal plans, daily
 recording systems and opportunities for meaningful occupation. People are actively involved in
 decisions about their day-to-day lives, including meal choices, activities and how they spend their
 time. We saw people accessing the community regularly and engaging in activities that reflect their
 interests and personal goals. Personal plans are detailed, reviewed frequently and provide care
 staff with clear guidance to promote independence, choice and positive risk-taking. Records show
 people receive ongoing support to maintain good health, access healthcare professionals and
 monitor nutritional needs. Relatives spoken with felt their loved ones are happy, well cared for and
 supported to do things that matter to them.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. Arrangements are in place to safeguard
 people and promote their safety. Improvements identified during the previous inspection have been
 effectively addressed, including increased staff training compliance, stronger management
 oversight and improved recording systems. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of
 safeguarding responsibilities and are confident in recognising, reporting and responding to
 concerns. Personal plans are supported by comprehensive and person-centred risk assessments
 which help care staff manage risks while supporting people to live fulfilling lives. The service has
 established a stronger culture of accountability and oversight, helping to ensure risks are identified
 promptly and people's safety remains a priority.

 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. People are encouraged to
 maintain meaningful relationships with family members, professionals and their wider community.
 Feedback from relatives is positive, with one family member describing care staff as “marvellous”
 and praising the communication and support provided. People are supported to access community
 facilities and activities that reflect their interests. We saw respectful interactions between care staff
 and saw people are listened to and treated with dignity and respect.

 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. The environment is clean,
 comfortable and personalised. Bedrooms reflect individual tastes and preferences, communal
 areas are welcoming and people have unrestricted access to outdoor space. The environment is
 well maintained, free from clutter and supports people to move around safely and comfortably.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality of care and support to achieve their personal outcomes. People benefit
from person-centred care that is planned, reviewed and delivered in a way that reflects their
individual strengths, preferences and aspirations. Significant improvements have been made since
the previous inspection, resulting in more effective and consistent support for people. Personal
plans held on the electronic care system are detailed, regularly reviewed and provide care staff with
clear guidance on how best to support people to achieve their personal outcomes. Associated risk
assessments are comprehensive and align with identified needs. Daily records now provide a
meaningful account of people's day to day life and demonstrate how care staff support people to
access healthcare, achieve personal goals and maintain their well-being. A relative told us, "He's
happy there. They communicate well, and they always run things by us.”

People are protected from harm and abuse. People's safety and rights are promoted through
effective safeguarding arrangements and a workforce that understands its responsibilities. Care
staff spoken with demonstrate confidence in recognising, responding to and reporting safeguarding
concerns and are knowledgeable about the procedures they would follow should concerns arise.
Improvements in governance, staff supervision and team communication have strengthened
oversight of people's welfare and safety. Care records show risks are assessed, regularly reviewed
and managed proportionately, enabling people to live as independently as possible whilst
remaining safe. Care staff describe a positive culture where concerns are taken seriously, and they
feel confident safeguarding concerns would be acted upon appropriately.

Medication systems have improved considerably, and people are supported to receive their
medicines safely and as prescribed. The service has responded positively to previous inspection
findings, with medication now stored securely and robust auditing systems in place to provide
effective oversight. Medication administration records are completed accurately, and temperature
monitoring is recorded consistently. PRN protocols are in place, and the service will be reviewing a
small number of specialist guidelines with relevant healthcare professionals. Care records contain
detailed medication information, ensuring care staff have access to guidance necessary to support
people safely.

People live in a clean, organised and well-maintained environment with effective infection
prevention and control measures in place. Staff training compliance in infection control is improved,
strengthening their knowledge and practice. Equipment and facilities are subject to routine checks
and maintenance schedules, supporting a safe environment for people living in the home. Daily
records and observations demonstrated that people's personal care and health needs are
managed appropriately, reducing the risk of infection and promoting their overall health.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a comfortable, homely and personalised environment that supports their individual
well-being and promotes positive daily experiences. People have access to communal living
spaces, personalised bedrooms and outdoor areas that can be used whenever they choose.
Throughout the inspection, the home was observed to be clean, welcoming and free from clutter,
enabling people to move safely and independently around their home. Bedrooms reflect people's
individual personalities, interests and preferences, providing privacy, familiarity and comfort.
Information is displayed in accessible formats, including pictorial menu choices, helping people
make informed decisions about their daily lives. People appear relaxed within their surroundings
and were seen using different areas of the home according to their preferences. Environmental
improvements are underway, including planned bathroom refurbishment and ongoing decoration,
demonstrating a commitment to ensuring people continue to benefit from an environment that
supports their comfort, independence and quality of life.

People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment to help
them achieve their well-being outcomes while identifying, mitigating and reducing health and safety
risks. The provider has effective arrangements in place to ensure the premises, facilities and
equipment are maintained to a good standard and remain safe for people living at the service. A
programme of routine and annual checks supports the identification and management of
environmental risks. We saw evidence of regular monitoring of fire safety systems, including weekly
alarm tests, monthly fire safety checks, fire drills and servicing of fire alarms, emergency lighting
and fire extinguishers. Water safety monitoring, legionella testing, gas safety certification and
electrical inspections have been completed within appropriate timescales. Monthly maintenance
audits cover a wide range of areas, including emergency lighting, water temperatures, carbon
monoxide alarms, fire doors, extractors, Personal Protective Equipment and cleaning products.

Maintenance records demonstrate a proactive approach to identifying and addressing issues
before they impact people. The home is visibly well cared for, and care staff understand their
responsibilities in maintaining a safe environment. The service has a dedicated maintenance team
who are available to ensure repairs and improvements are completed promptly.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance and oversight to ensure smooth operations and high-
quality care. Leadership arrangements have become more stable since the previous inspection,
resulting in improved oversight of the service. We saw an improvement in management oversight
which has driven improvements across all areas of the service, particularly regarding personal
planning, staff support, quality assurance and day-to-day operations. Previous areas of non-
compliance relating to leadership, governance and care planning have been addressed and robust
monitoring systems are now in place. Quality assurance processes are now effective and
demonstrate a clear focus on improving people's experiences and outcomes.

Quality of care reviews are completed by the Responsible Individual within required timescales and
are increasingly focused on evaluating people's lived experiences and identifying opportunities for
further enhancement. Relatives report a high level of confidence in the service and its leadership.
One relative told us, "They communicate well, and they always update us," while another stated, "I
wouldn't change anything; they really look after him." The service's ability to implement significant
improvements within a relatively short period demonstrates strong organisational capacity and a
clear commitment to delivering high-quality care.

People are supported by a staff team with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. Care staff are provided with the support, guidance and
development opportunities necessary to perform their roles effectively and deliver person-centred
care. Training compliance has improved significantly across mandatory and service specific
subjects, ensuring care staff have the knowledge and competence required to meet people's
needs. Supervision and team meetings take place regularly and care staff told us they feel valued
and listened to in their roles. We saw robust recruitment and background checks, ensuring care
staff are safely and appropriately recruited to the service.

Care staff at the service feel supported and are motivated to ensure people achieve positive
outcomes. They spoke positively about the changes introduced by leaders and the impact these
have had on both morale and outcomes for people. One staff member told us, "It's a different place
to work than it was" while another commented, "We're working more as a team, we have support,
suggestions are being taken on board and we're going in the right direction." Care staff told us
there are now increased opportunities for communication, regular team meetings and improved
access to training and management support. Those spoken with have a strong understanding of
people's needs and are passionate about supporting people to lead fulfilling lives.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
