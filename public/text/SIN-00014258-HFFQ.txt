Summary:

 Bevan College is a care home service for up to 32 people with additional learning needs, providing
 accommodation, care, education and developmental opportunities. The service supports people to
 develop independence, pursue personal goals and prepare for adulthood within a person-centred
 environment.

 People are supported to experience good well-being outcomes through positive relationships with
 care staff and access to meaningful educational, social and recreational opportunities. People are
 encouraged to share their views, participate in decision-making and develop confidence,
 independence and skills that support their personal aspirations.

 People receive good care and support informed by assessment and care planning arrangements
 which consider their individual needs, preferences and goals. Care staff have access to information
 and guidance to support consistent care delivery, whilst risk management and incident

People benefit from a good environment that is welcoming, accessible and designed to meet their
needs. Personalised living spaces, specialist facilities, outdoor areas and opportunities for learning

safety arrangements help ensure the environment remains safe and suitable.

People are supported by good leadership and management arrangements that provide oversight of
service delivery and support continuous improvement. Quality assurance processes, workforce
development opportunities and recruitment practices contribute to a positive culture focused on
delivering safe, consistent care and positive outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People achieve positive well-being outcomes because they receive support promoting
 independence, confidence and personal development. Feedback gathered during the inspection
 demonstrate people feel safe, enjoy their time at the service and develop positive relationships with
 care staff. People spoke positively about attending college, pursuing personal interests and
 participating in a range of recreational and social activities.

 There are effective opportunities for people to influence decisions affecting their daily lives. Regular
 student meetings, key worker sessions and visual activity planners support participation and ensure
 views are listened to and acted upon. Records demonstrate requests relating to activities, food
 choices and aspects of the environment had been considered and responded to. A varied
 programme of meaningful activities, including hydrotherapy, yoga, cinema visits, sports, cooking
 and creative projects, which help promote social inclusion, confidence and well-being.

 People are supported to develop skills and work towards their personal aspirations. Examples seen
 during the inspection demonstrate progress towards greater independence and preparation for
 adulthood, including opportunities to develop independent living skills and support with future
 education and transition planning. Family members consistently described positive outcomes and
 enhanced confidence. One family member told us, “I can’t fault them, (Person) has come on leaps
 and bounds”. Another stated their family member was “More confident since being there” and
 participates in activities they would not ordinarily undertake at home. A further family member
 described their relative as having “Flourished” and becoming increasingly confident and socially
 independent.

 Communication needs are recognised and supported through a range of approaches, including
 visual resources. Accessible information is available to support understanding of safeguarding,
 advocacy and how to raise concerns. Safeguarding information was clearly displayed throughout
 the service and people told us they feel safe. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of their
 safeguarding responsibilities and support people to express their views and make choices.

 The service actively supports people’s health, emotional well-being and important relationships.
 People access activities supporting physical and emotional health, whilst specialist support is
 available where required. Care staff respond appropriately to changing health needs and ensured
 family members remained involved when significant health concerns arose. Family members spoke
 positively about communication with the service and the support provided, demonstrating
 confidence in the care delivered and the outcomes achieved.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support based on detailed assessments completed before admission to
the service. Records demonstrate an informed assessment process, including informal visits,
formal assessments and overnight stays. Assessment documentation considered a wide range of
needs, including health, communication, education, independence, relationships and personal care
requirements. This ensures admissions are suitable and care staff have a clear understanding of
the support required.

Personal plans viewed provided relevant information about people’s needs, preferences, routines
and support requirements. Plans were written in a person-centred format and contained information
about communication, health needs, family relationships, daily routines and personal aspirations.
Records demonstrate efforts to involve people in planning and reviewing their care, with signed
agreements evident within records sampled. Care plans also identified areas where independence
can be maintained and developed.

Records reviewed showed people’s specialist needs are considered. Information was available
regarding communication methods, mobility requirements, eating and drinking needs,
physiotherapy guidance and health conditions. Additional specialist documents, including speech
and language therapy reports, eating and drinking plans, physiotherapy guidance and epilepsy
information, were available to support care staff in meeting assessed needs. Daily records
demonstrate people participate in education, activities and routines in line with their assessed
needs and preferences.

Daily recordings provide a useful overview of care and support delivered. Records sampled
demonstrate attendance at college, participation in activities, support with personal care, nutritional
intake and general well-being. Entries showed people were engaging in meaningful experiences,
maintaining routines and receiving support appropriate to their assessed needs.

Incident records reviewed demonstrate appropriate reporting and oversight arrangements.
Documentation included details of incidents, actions taken by care staff and involvement of relevant
professionals where required. Records show timely responses were implemented to address
emerging concerns and support people’s safety and well-being.

Medication was managed safely, with medicines stored securely, including suitable arrangements
for controlled drugs. Medication counts, MAR records and temperature monitoring were completed
routinely, and staff demonstrated a good understanding of medication administration and
responding to errors.

The guide to the service is presented in an accessible format and provides people with relevant
information about the service. It includes guidance on advocacy, complaints procedures, the

       Environment                         Good



The environment meets the needs of people using the service and supports their well-being
outcomes. The service provides a range of communal and private spaces, including spacious living
and dining areas, sensory rooms, a creativity room and accessible outdoor areas. Bedrooms are
personalised with people’s belongings, photographs and items of personal importance, creating a
homely atmosphere. An independence flat is available to support people in developing skills
associated with greater independence and preparing for adulthood.

The environment supports a variety of social, educational and recreational opportunities.
Communal areas are welcoming, spacious and suitably furnished, providing opportunities for
people to spend time with friends and care staff. Displays throughout the service celebrate
achievements, activities and aspirations. Information boards provide accessible information
regarding safeguarding, student voice and upcoming activities. The creativity room offers
opportunities for arts, crafts and computer-based activities, whilst dedicated sensory rooms provide
quieter spaces for relaxation, sensory engagement and therapeutic activities.

Suitable facilities and equipment are available to support people’s assessed needs. Bedrooms
contain specialist equipment where required and bathrooms include accessible facilities to promote
dignity, privacy and independence. We observed hoists, bathing equipment and mobility aids
available for those requiring additional support. Equipment servicing records viewed demonstrate
arrangements are in place to maintain equipment and support its continued suitability for use.

The dining and kitchen facilities support people’s daily living experiences. Dining areas provide
sufficient space for people to eat together and participate in household routines. Menus offer a
variety of meal choices and consideration is given to individual dietary requirements, including
medical, cultural and lifestyle preferences. The kitchen achieved a Food Standards Agency food
hygiene rating of 5 (the highest rating) and was observed to be clean, organised and appropriately
stocked.

Outdoor areas support leisure, gardening and social activities. Raised planters, flowers and
vegetable beds enable people to participate in gardening activities should they wish. We saw
evidence of people growing vegetables, which had subsequently been used in activities within the
service. Seating areas provide additional opportunities for people to spend time outdoors during
favourable weather.

A range of health and safety arrangements are in place. Fire safety documentation, maintenance
records, equipment servicing certificates and routine safety checks were available for review.
Maintenance issues are identified through suitable reporting systems and monitored through

       Leadership & Management               Good





People benefit from a service with established leadership and management arrangements which
support the delivery of care and positive outcomes. Quality assurance systems provide oversight of
care and support, safeguarding, health and safety, medication management and staffing.
Information gathered through Responsible Individual visits and Quality of Care Reviews is used to
monitor the quality of the service and inform development. Quality of Care Reviews identify
strengths, areas for development and actions to support improvement. They demonstrate
consideration of key areas of service provision, including well-being, care and support, the
environment and leadership arrangements. Governance systems strengthen oversight of service
delivery, helping management monitor performance and maintain standards.

The service promotes opportunities for people to share their views and contribute to service
development. Feedback systems support management to understand people's experiences and
consider areas for further development. No complaints have been received since the previous
inspection, indicating satisfaction with the service provided.

Care staff speak positively about the leadership and support available within the service. Care staff
describe managers as approachable and responsive, with one member of care staff stating,
“(Manager) is very proactive with support.” Care staff explain managers are available to provide
guidance and assistance when needed. They describe a positive team culture where colleagues
work together to meet people's needs. One member of care staff describes the service as a “Good
and healthy environment” whilst another says they are “Proud of this place.” Care staff also explain
managers provide practical support and maintain a visible presence within the service. This
contributes to a supportive culture and promotes consistency in the care provided to people.

People are supported by care staff with the skills, knowledge and experience to meet their needs.
Care staff are registered with Social Care Wales and the majority hold, or are working towards,
relevant qualifications. Training opportunities are aligned with the needs of people using the
service, including specialist training where required. Supervision arrangements provide care staff
with opportunities to discuss people's needs, safeguarding responsibilities, well-being and
professional development. Regular team meetings support communication, reflection on practice
and shared understanding of how best to support people.

Recruitment arrangements support the employment of suitable care staff. Pre-employment checks
include verification of identity, employment history, references, qualifications, Social Care Wales
registration and Disclosure and Barring Service checks. These arrangements help ensure people
receive support from care staff who are appropriately vetted and suitable for their role, promoting

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
