Summary:

 Colwyn Bay Old Convent Nursing home is located in a quiet residential area near to the centre of
 Colwyn Bay.

 People living in the service receive good care and support from care staff who know them well.
 Care staff treat people with dignity and respect. People and their relatives speak positively about
 care staff and we saw warm and respectful interactions during the inspection. Care records reflect
 people’s needs and preferences. Care staff seek advice from health professionals when people’s
 needs change.

 The home is clean, comfortable and pleasantly decorated. There is appropriate equipment,
 communal and private spaces to meet people’s needs. We saw people personalise their rooms
 with photographs, ornaments and items important to them. The responsible individual (RI) and
 manager have effective systems to monitor the quality and safety of the service. They seek
 feedback from people, relatives and care staff and act on this to make improvements.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are well supported to make choices about their daily lives and follow routines that are
 important to them. We saw people choosing where to spend their time, where to eat and whether to
 take part in activities. Records show people can eat in communal areas or in their bedrooms, and
 we saw people being supported to eat their lunch where they wished. Care records and daily notes
 show care staff understand people’s preferences and support them in line with these. People
 describe the home as welcoming and told us they enjoy living there.

 Care staff support people to maintain social connections and take part in activities. Visitors are
 welcomed to the service, and we saw care staff were kind and approachable and had good
 professional relationships with relatives as well as residents. Activity records show people enjoy
 bingo, crafts, visits from entertainers and one-to-one activities. Records show care staff adapt
 activities for people who prefer quieter engagement or who choose to stay in their bedrooms. We
 also saw records of birthday celebrations, themed events and opportunities for people to spend
 time with others.

 The service supports people to communicate in their preferred language where needed. Welsh-
 speaking care staff work in the home and bilingual signage and information support people to use
 the Welsh language. Welsh culture is also promoted through recognising Welsh festivals, sport,
 and history through activities and themed events in the home, such as St David’s day.

 People and relatives told us they feel comfortable raising concerns with care staff and the manager.
 The provider seeks out and values feedback from people and their relatives about the home.
 Records of resident meetings, relative feedback records and quality assurance processes show
 managers seek people’s views and respond to comments and issues raised appropriately. The
 meetings discuss the accommodation and environment of the home, activities provision, meals and
 day-to-day living arrangements with people. The manager ensures that any issues raised at these
 meetings are revisited at subsequent meetings to ensure people are happy with the outcomes of
 any actions taken.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care staff provide person-centred care and support that reflects people’s needs and preferences.
Personal plans include information about people’s life histories, communication needs, health
conditions, routines, preferences and health and wellbeing related risks. Records show care staff
deliver care as planned, and review personal plans regularly and update them when people’s
needs change. Care staff use individualised risk assessments and monitoring tools to support safe
care. Risk assessments we viewed are appropriately detailed, linked to relevant personal plans and
provided clear guidance for care staff about how to keep people safe.

We saw positive and respectful interactions between care staff and people during the inspection.
Care staff spoke with people in a calm and friendly way. They supported people discreetly with
personal care and encouraged independence where possible. We saw care staff support people to
eat and drink respectfully and attentively, communicating well and working at each person’s pace.
People and relatives describe care staff as kind, caring and approachable. Feedback shows people
have positive relationships with care staff and feel comfortable asking for support.

Records show care staff monitor people’s health needs and seek advice from health professionals
when needed. Records show care staff monitor people’s nutritional needs, and make referrals
about falls prevention, pressure area care and changes in physical health. Records show care staff
respond to changing needs and take action to reduce risks. The manager ensures all staff follow
appropriate policies and procedures to keep people safe from harm and abuse. Staff receive
training in safeguarding people and understand their responsibilities in keeping people safe.

People are supported through good medication management processes in the home. Medication
administration records show medication is administered appropriately and in line with people’s
prescriptions and care plans, by trained and competent staff. The manager ensures regular
medication audits are completed, with any emerging issues addressed promptly and appropriately.
Records show any covert medication plans are reviewed regularly, and relevant professionals and
representatives are involved in decision making with people.

The service has good hygiene practices. Care staff receive regular infection prevention and control
training, and domestic care staff work in the home each day. Cleaning schedules support routine,
deep and high-level cleaning across the premises. People and care staff speak positively about the
cleanliness of the home. We saw good supplies of cleaning equipment and personal protective
equipment, which care staff used and stored appropriately during our visit. The service has a Food
Hygiene Rating of 5, which is the highest rating available.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a clean, comfortable and welcoming environment. The home has a range of
communal areas where people can spend time with others if they wish. Communal lounges have
comfortable seating and space for wheelchairs and specialist seating. The dining room supports
family-style dining for people who prefer this. The provider has installed large bi-foldable doors to
the courtyard and plans to improve the outside area so people can dine outdoors in warmer
weather. Call bells and equipment are accessible to people who need them.

People have access to safe and attractive outdoor spaces. The home is surrounded by well-
maintained gardens with boarders, shrubs and flowerbeds. They employ staff to maintain the
garden and records show people have a say in how it looks. There are accessible pathways for
people who use wheelchairs, and we saw garden seating people can use also. There is a small
sunroom or conservatory with doors that open onto a lawned area if people wish to sit in a more
sheltered space and still engage with the outdoor environment. The manager showed us photos of
people also enjoying walks along the local promenade and an ice cream with care staff.

People can be assured the provider ensures the premises comply with relevant legislation and
national guidance on health and safety, fire safety and environment. The RI monitors the safety and
upkeep of the premises through audits and regular walk rounds to inspect the home. The manager
monitors the environment of the home daily during their walk rounds. Records show regular fire
alarm tests, water safety checks, window restrictor checks, electrical and gas safety testing, and
servicing of moving and handling equipment take place. Maintenance audits and records show
continued investment in the environment, including replacement flooring and equipment when
needed. Some bedrooms have been redecorated recently, and new flooring has been installed on
the ground floor since the last inspection. The provider plans to redecorate bedrooms and replace
flooring across the service. Care staff use maintenance logs to report concerns. The manager and
maintenance staff review these logs regularly to ensure all actions are completed. Larger or more
complex jobs are prioritised appropriately to ensure contractors are contacted promptly when
specialist work is needed.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People receive good care and support because the provider ensures good systems are in place to
monitor the quality and safety of the service. Audit records show the manager regularly reviews
records and practice around medication, falls and accidents, care delivery and infection prevention
and control arrangements. They also gather feedback through resident meetings, relative meetings,
surveys and day-to-day discussions with staff and people in the home. The manager considers
issues raised and respond to concerns and suggestions, addressing them promptly. Governance
records show the manager discusses feedback about activities, the environment and people’s
experiences of care with staff and the RI as part of ongoing quality improvements.

Care staff told us the manager supports them and they had good understanding of safeguarding
procedures. Supervision records and staff meeting minutes show the manager provides
opportunities for staff to discuss their practice, their training and people’s needs in the meetings as
well as considering their own wellbeing. Care staff describe a positive working culture and say they
have confidence in management arrangements and the quality of care in the service.

People are supported by staff recruited through safe recruitment and vetting processes.
Recruitment records show the provider obtains appropriate references and disclosure and barring
service checks before staff start work. The manager oversees staff training to make sure care staff
have the skills and knowledge to meet people’s needs. They also monitor professional registration
requirements for registered nurses and other care staff where needed.

Quality assurance processes help the provider identify areas for improvement. Leaders monitor the
service, identify issues and take action to address them. The provider gathers information through
feedback from people, relatives and care staff, and through regular RI visits. The RI records their
findings in regular reports and produces twice-yearly quality of care review reports. These reports
describe activity in the service, summarise feedback and identify required improvements. The
reports could more clearly show how monitoring, learning and feedback lead to continuous
improvement and service development. However, they currently meet regulatory requirements and
show leadership and management arrangements support day-to-day care and positive outcomes
for people.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
