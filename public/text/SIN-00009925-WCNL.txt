Summary:

 Well-being is rated good as people experience positive well-being outcomes. They benefit from
 caring relationships with care staff who know them well, promote independence and provide
 opportunities for meaningful engagement. People appear comfortable, settled and receive support
 to maintain relationships with those important to them.

 Care and support is rated as requires improvement because care planning, record keeping,
 medication management and risk oversight do not consistently demonstrate people receive safe,
 effective and person-centred care in line with their assessed needs.

 Environment is rated as requires improvement because environmental oversight and fire safety
 arrangements are not consistently effective in identifying, monitoring and responding to risks,
 reducing assurance that people are protected from harm.

 Leadership and management is rated as requiring improvement because governance, quality
 assurance, regulatory oversight and workforce monitoring arrangements are not consistently
 effective in identifying concerns, driving improvement and ensuring compliance.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive well-being outcomes because they receive support from care staff who
 know them well and promote their well-being. Feedback from people, relatives and professionals is
 largely positive. During inspection people appeared relaxed, comfortable and settled within the
 home. We observed warm, caring and respectful interactions throughout the inspection. Care staff
 demonstrate a good understanding of people's preferences, routines and life histories, helping to
 create positive relationships and a welcoming atmosphere. We observed people who appeared
 confident in approaching care staff to seek support when required. We saw care staff engaging with
 people in a patient and friendly manner, taking time to provide reassurance, encouragement and
 companionship throughout the day.



 People are supported to maintain their independence, make choices and remain connected to what
 is important to them. Care staff demonstrate an understanding of people's abilities as well as their
 support needs and encourage people to maintain daily living skills wherever possible. Care records
 show a focus on promoting independence. Family members tell us they are welcomed into the
 home and can visit regularly. People remain connected with those important to them and the
 service promotes an open and inclusive culture. Throughout inspection, we observed people
 making choices about how they spend their time, where they wish to sit and engage, and their day-
 to-day routines.



 People benefit from opportunities for meaningful occupation and social engagement. Activities take
 place within the home and feedback regarding activity provision is overwhelmingly positive. People
 spoke positively about the activities available, and we saw evidence of organised events and
 outside entertainment. In particular, the activities coordinator is highly regarded by people, relatives
 and staff, and is consistently identified as a strength of the service. We observed positive
 engagement and enthusiasm around activities, demonstrating the contribution they make to
 people's quality of life and overall well-being. The activity programme provides opportunities for
 social interaction, enjoyment and stimulation, helping people maintain interests and reduce the risk
 of social isolation.



 There are opportunities to further enhance people's experiences and well-being. Some relatives
 told us people would benefit from increased opportunities to access the community and make
 greater use of outdoor spaces. We found the garden was not fully accessible for people with
 mobility difficulties and is not routinely used by all living at the service. The importance of

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement



People benefit from caring and positive relationships with care staff who know them well and
understand their needs. Feedback from people, relatives and professionals regarding the care staff
is overwhelmingly positive. People describe them as kind, supportive and approachable, with
comments including “Lovely carers.” During inspection, people appeared comfortable in the
presence of care staff who demonstrated a good understanding of people's routines, preferences
and support needs. There is evidence of staff promoting independence through encouragement
and support with daily living skills.



People's needs and risks are generally identified; however, personal planning is not consistently
comprehensive or person-centred. Whilst some records provide assurance of assessed needs
being reflected in care planning, gaps are identified within others. We found examples where there
are no dedicated plans to guide care staff in areas such as communication and medication support.
During inspection we found people’s personal plans do not always reflect current practice and
contain inconsistencies relating to healthcare information and decision-making arrangements. This
limits assurance care staff have access to accurate and up-to-date information to support people
safely and consistently.



Recording systems do not consistently demonstrate people's assessed needs are being met.
Personal plans identify support needs relating to personal care, continence and oral health;
however, daily records are incomplete and cannot be relied upon.



People are supported to manage risks; however, oversight of risk management is not consistently
effective. Significant risks, including falls, mobility difficulties and cognitive impairment, are
identified and measures are implemented to reduce harm. However, falls documentation is
incomplete and there is limited evidence of analysis, evaluation of interventions or learning from
incidents. Professional involvement is referenced throughout records, but supporting
documentation is not always available to demonstrate recommendations, actions taken or
outcomes achieved.

Medication management and oversight require improvement. We identified concerns relating to
controlled drug records, stock discrepancies, medication administration records, missing
signatures, handwritten entries, storage and temperature monitoring arrangements. These findings
reduce assurance that medicines are managed safely.



Outcomes for people require improvement because care planning, recording, medication

       Environment               Requires Improvement



People live in a clean, comfortable and welcoming environment which supports their well-being.
Investment has been made to improve the home, particularly through the refurbishment of
downstairs communal areas. During inspection we observed a clean and homely environment, with
comfortable communal spaces available for people to enjoy. People appear relaxed within their
surroundings and relatives speak positively about the overall atmosphere of the home.
Systems are in place to monitor the safety and suitability of the environment; however, they are not
consistently effective. Outcomes for people require improvement because environmental oversight
arrangements do not consistently identify and address concerns.



During inspection, we identified several fire safety concerns which had not been recognised
through routine monitoring processes, including fire doors not closing fully into their frames when
released. These findings reduce assurance that environmental risks are being effectively identified
and managed. Fire safety arrangements also require strengthening. We are unable to obtain
sufficient assurance that fire safety systems are being routinely reviewed and evaluated and that
the fire risk assessment is comprehensive. Records available during inspection do not demonstrate
regular fire drills or ongoing review of emergency evacuation arrangements. Given many people
living at the service would require assistance to evacuate in an emergency, robust fire safety
arrangements are essential. During the inspection, we identified several fire safety concerns which
had not been recognised through routine monitoring processes. These findings reduce assurance
that environmental risks are being effectively identified and managed.



The outdoor environment is not fully accessible for people with mobility difficulties, which limits
opportunities for some individuals to independently access and enjoy the garden. Whilst plans are
in place to improve accessibility, feedback from relatives and care staff suggests that many people
living at the service do not routinely use the outdoor areas. During the inspection, our observations
were consistent with this feedback. As access to outdoor space can support well-being,
independence and social engagement, we expect the provider to continue progressing plans to
improve accessibility and maximise opportunities for people to benefit from the outdoor
environment.



Outcomes for people require improvement because fire safety management and environmental
oversight arrangements are not consistently effective in identifying, monitoring and responding to
risks. Whilst the home is clean, welcoming and benefits from significant investment, we expect the
provider to make improvements in these areas.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



Leaders promote a positive culture and support care staff to provide care and support to people.
Care staff mostly speak positively about management, describing managers as approachable,
responsive and visible within the service. Care staff told us they feel listened to, can raise concerns
and understand safeguarding procedures and escalation processes. Positive feedback includes
comments such as, “Support from management is good as gold.” Care staff also describe positive
working relationships and effective teamwork, demonstrating a culture where they feel supported to
carry out their roles and provide care for people living at the service.



Systems are in place to monitor and improve the quality of the service; however, these are not
consistently effective. Governance arrangements include medication audits, care plan audits, team
meetings, supervision processes and complaints monitoring, but cannot be relied upon to be
effective. Managers demonstrate a commitment to developing the service through refurbishment
projects and ongoing investment in the environment. However, outcomes for people require
improvement because quality assurance systems do not consistently identify concerns affecting the
quality and safety of care. Audits in key areas, such as medication and care and support fall short
of the expected standard, impacting on the risk to people receiving a service.



Governance information is not always used effectively to evaluate performance and drive
improvement. The responsible individual (RI) has a regulatory duty to visit the service and report
their findings to the provider. The reports were not made available to us when requested, limiting
assurance regarding RI oversight of the service. Complaint records do not consistently
demonstrate investigation outcomes, actions taken, whether complaints are upheld or how learning
has informed service improvement. As a result, opportunities for organisational learning, trend
analysis and service development are not always maximised. These findings limit the provider's
ability to obtain accurate assurance about the supervision and management of the service, quality-
of-care people receive and effectively drive sustained improvement. We expect the provider to
make improvements in these areas.



Oversight of legal and regulatory responsibilities requires strengthening. Whilst systems are in
place to monitor Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS), records do not consistently contain key
information such as application, authorisation and expiry dates. As a result, effective oversight of
people subject to DoLS authorisations cannot be readily demonstrated, reducing assurance that
legal safeguards are being effectively monitored.

Workforce monitoring arrangements are not consistently effective. Care staff value the support they
receive from managers and describe positive team working. However, supervision arrangements
are not consistently delivered in line with the provider's expectations, and some staff experience
significant gaps between supervision sessions. Care staff and relatives also raise concerns about
staffing pressures and the impact this has on activities and engagement opportunities.



Overall, outcomes for people require improvement because governance, quality assurance,
regulatory oversight and workforce monitoring arrangements are not consistently effective in
identifying risks, driving improvement and ensuring compliance. Whilst leaders are visible,
supportive and well regarded, we expect the provider to make improvements in these areas.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
