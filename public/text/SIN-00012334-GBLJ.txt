Summary:

 Coalbrook Care is a welcoming service providing accommodation and personal support for adults
 with a range of support needs. Set within spacious grounds in Pontyberem, the home offers a calm,
 nurturing environment where people feel valued and able to live meaningful, positive lives. The
 atmosphere is warm and inclusive, and people are encouraged to enjoy fulfilling daily experiences.

 People’s well-being is supported by a caring and committed staff team who know them well. We
 observed warm, respectful interactions and a relaxed, homely atmosphere where people appeared
 happy, comfortable and engaged. People are supported to maintain relationships, helping them
 achieve good wellbeing outcomes.

 People receive good care and support from staff who understand their needs and deliver person-
 centred care. Staff demonstrate a clear knowledge of people’s preferences and routines, and
 people’s health needs are recognised and supported with appropriate professional involvement.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good well-being because they are supported by a staff team who know them
 well and promote their dignity, choice and independence. We observed staff interacting with people
 in a warm, respectful and meaningful way. People appeared relaxed and comfortable in the
 service, and we saw positive engagement, including smiles, laughter and social interaction.

 People have choice and control over their day-to-day lives. During the inspection, we observed
 people choosing how and where they spent their time. For example, some people were being
 supported to attend a day centre, others were gardening in the conservatory, and some were
 independently tidying their rooms. People are given choice, and are supported to make decisions,
 which helps them to feel listened to and valued.

 People maintain positive relationships with staff and others. Care staff know people’s routines,
 preferences and communication styles well. People’s communication needs are outlined in
 personal plans and staff demonstrated this knowledge in practice, including responding
 appropriately to non-verbal communication.

 People are supported to take part in meaningful activities. We observed people taking part in
 activities within the home, such as crafts and social interaction, and we read in daily records that
 people regularly access community opportunities. These opportunities support emotional well-being
 and reduce social isolation.

 People’s physical and emotional health needs are supported. We read in care records that people
 have access to health professionals and their needs are monitored. We observed staff responding
 appropriately to people during the inspection. Risk assessments we reviewed show people are
 supported to remain safe while maintaining independence. These are also written in an easy-read
 format, allowing people living in the home to be fully engaged in their care planning.

 People are protected from harm and abuse. We spoke with staff, who demonstrated a clear
 understanding of safeguarding procedures, and we read policies and training records that support
 this. People experience a service where they feel safe, valued and supported to live meaningful
 daily lives.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that is person-centred and responsive to their individual needs.
Care plans provide staff with clear guidance on individuals’ daily routines, health needs, and
preferences, including support with personal care, nutrition, mobility, and overall wellbeing. We saw
that people, their families and relevant professionals are involved in care planning and reviews,
helping to ensure care remains responsive to individuals’ needs and preferences.

On the day of the inspection, we observed positive interactions between staff and people, with care
provided in a respectful, patient and dignified manner. Staff showed a good understanding of
individuals’ needs and preferences. For example, when one person became unsettled, staff
adapted the planned activities for the day to reflect the individual’s emotional needs. A staff
member was heard reassuring the person, saying, “That’s okay, I’ll come with you.” Staff took time
to explain the revised plan and checked the person’s understanding, offering reassurance such as,
“We’ll go at your pace, there’s no rush.”

People are supported to maintain their independence where possible, with staff encouraging choice
and involvement in day-to-day decisions. We saw staff prioritising the individual’s wellbeing and
taking steps to ensure they felt comfortable and involved in decisions about their day.

Risk assessments are in place and cover key areas such as mobility, nutrition, and environmental
safety. These support staff to manage risks appropriately while promoting people’s independence.

People’s health needs are monitored, and referrals to external professionals are made when
required. Care records indicate that support is delivered in line with individuals’ assessed needs.

The service provides opportunities for social interaction and engagement through activities such as
birthday celebrations and recognising special events, including VE Day. We saw photographs of
recent VE Day celebrations, which demonstrated how people are supported to take part in
meaningful occasions that promote their emotional wellbeing. People appeared relaxed and
comfortable in the presence of staff and received care in a supportive environment. Overall, people
are supported safely and with care and compassion.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that provides a range of features which support comfort,
independence and a sense of belonging. People’s rooms are personalised and reflect their
individual preferences, helping to create a homely and familiar space. Communal areas are
available for people to relax, socialise, or watch television with others, while quieter areas give
people the option to spend time alone or enjoy activities privately. The kitchen is a shared and
active space, and we observed people enjoying time together.

The environment promotes a strong sense of community and reflects the lives of the people who
live there. Throughout the home, people’s artwork and crafts are displayed, alongside group
photographs of individuals living at the service. These personal touches contribute to a warm and
welcoming atmosphere, helping the setting feel like a family home where people’s identities and
experiences are valued.

We saw that communal spaces were well-used and supported meaningful interaction. The
conservatory provides a bright and pleasant area for people to spend time, and we observed that it
is actively used for activities such as growing tomatoes. This offers people opportunities to engage
in purposeful tasks and enjoy shared interests. The garden area is accessible and provides
additional space for people to spend time outdoors, supporting wellbeing and offering a change of
environment.

On the day of the inspection, the environment appeared clean, tidy and well maintained. Furniture
and equipment were arranged to support people’s mobility and independence, allowing individuals
to move safely around the home. Appropriate safety measures are in place, including regular
environmental and fire safety checks, which help to ensure the premises remain safe for people
living in the service.

Staff supported people to use different areas of the home according to their preferences. For
example, a staff member was heard asking, “Would you like to sit in here or go out to the garden
for a bit?”, promoting choice and control over how people spend their time.

Overall, the environment is welcoming, personalised and supports people’s comfort, independence
and wellbeing.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



The service benefits from a committed and approachable management team who are visible and
provide day-to-day support to staff and people living at the service. Staff spoken with described the
manager and Responsible Individual (RI) as supportive and accessible. Comments included, “The
manager is really approachable, we can go to her if we need anything,” and “We work well as a
team and support each other.” This contributes to a positive working culture within the service. The
ethos demonstrated by the leadership team has a clear and positive impact on the wellbeing of
people living at the service, supporting a caring and inclusive environment.

There are arrangements in place to support oversight of the service, including RI visits and quality
assurance processes. We saw that RI visits are taking place and include engagement with people,
staff and, where possible, families, to gather feedback about the quality of care provided. Quality of
care reports are produced, which reflect consideration of feedback and provide an evaluation of the
service. The service has developed an action plan and has identified areas for further development.
This demonstrates an awareness of where improvements are needed and a willingness to drive
change. Management show a reflective approach to their practice and dedication to the people they
support. Some progress has been made in response to the previous Area for Improvement (AFI),
particularly in relation to staff training, and there is recognition of the need to continue to strengthen
workforce development and compliance.

We discussed with the RI the importance of ensuring all staff meet regulatory requirements,
including appropriate registration with Social Care Wales and completion of mandatory and
specialist training. We also highlighted the need for management arrangements to fully meet
registration requirements. The provider has recognised this, and we have identified this as an Area
for Improvement (AFI) and expect the manager to be appropriately registered by the next
inspection. Overall, the service shows a clear willingness to improve and build on its existing
strengths, underpinned by a committed and reflective leadership team. Improvements are needed
to ensure a suitably qualified and registered manager is in place, to provide effective oversight and
avoid any potential impact on the quality and consistency of care provided to people. The RI has
provided assurances that this will be addressed as soon as possible. An Area for Improvement
(AFI) remains in place to support this

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
