Summary:


 The Willows is a care home in the residential area of Brackla, Bridgend, offering care and support
 for up to 4 people.


 People experience excellent well-being outcomes. They are supported to maintain their health and
 well-being, are treated with dignity and respect and feel listened to. People have autonomy over
 their daily routines and enjoy a broad range of activities. The care and support delivered to people
 is good. Care staff are knowledgeable about people’s lives and their routines and preferences.
 They have enough information to provide people with the right care at the right time. There are
 robust systems in place for safeguarding, medication management, and infection control. The
 physical environment is good, being safe and supports the needs of people who live there. We rate
 the leadership and management at the service as excellent. There is strong management oversight
 and expectations of standards within the home are communicated clearly. Care staff have the
 correct training, recruitment practices are effective, and governance and oversight systems are in
 place to support the running of the service. At the last inspection improvements in both care and

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being at the service because they are listened to, respected and
 involved in decisions that affect their daily lives. Weekly house meetings and key-worker sessions
 give people regular opportunities to express their views and influence how their care is planned. A
 professional told us “staff involve people in decision-making” and that their “voice is central to
 planning and day-to-day choices, which has had a really positive impact” on the people in the
 home. People choose from a wide range of meaningful activities. We saw examples of people
 going on trips to favourite sports clubs, holidays, and taking part in enjoyable activities both within
 the home and the local community.


 There is a warm and natural rapport between people and care staff. Care staff communicate with
 patience and kindness and understand how to meet people’s communication needs highly
 effectively. This promotes trust and helps people feel secure. People described the home as feeling
 “like a family”, highlighting the strong bonds and sense of belonging shared within the household.
 These positive relationships create a strong and supportive culture where people feel valued, safe
 and able to thrive.


 People are protected by robust safeguarding arrangements. Care staff know their responsibilities
 and understand how to report concerns. Both care staff and family members told us they feel
 confident the manager responds promptly to any issues raised. People are supported to maintain
 important relationships, and we heard how the home has built positive relationships with families
 including support during challenging times. The location of the service allows people easy access
 to the community and, when appropriate, care staff encourage people to go out independently.
 Staff provide reassurance when needed, but they prioritise choice and respect people’s autonomy.
 This helps to maintain dignity and promotes independence and has a significant impact on people’s
 outcomes.


 People contribute to decisions about their home environment. Bedrooms are personalised and
 comfortable, helping people feel settled and at home. Communal areas are warm, welcoming and
 well-decorated, providing pleasant spaces for people to spend time together. During our visit,
 people appeared relaxed and told us they enjoy living at the home. The large garden is
 well-maintained and used actively. People take part in gardening projects and take pride in growing
 vegetables, which supports both physical and emotional well-being. The overall environment is
 clean, safe and organised in a way that supports people’s needs. It contributes positively to
 people’s comfort, security and sense of well-being.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive consistently good quality care and support. Personal plans are thorough and direct
care staff on how to support people to achieve their well-being outcomes. Additional assessments
enhance the quality of support by giving practical, tailored guidance. We saw how the approach
used by the care staff has significantly supported a person in better managing their own behaviour.
Care staff receive a good level of training to meet people’s needs. The staff team is stable and
experienced and they know people well. Care plans and risk assessments are reviewed regularly,
and people are encouraged to contribute to this process. “About Me” plans are in place and are
being further developed, including translated versions. This strengthens people’s voice and makes
future plans more person-centred.


We saw a whole-team approach to “supporting and doing with” rather than “doing for”, to promote
people’s independence. Language in plans now reflects this shift and we saw and heard the
Manager supporting this approach throughout the service. People have a named key worker and
know who this is; key-worker meetings capture people’s views, wishes and choices. Relatives
speak positively about the support offered. One family member described their relative as “the
happiest they have been,” noting positive change at the service.


Care staff complete daily recordings that give a clear account of support provided. Care
documentation is reviewed monthly and update it where needed. Records show the manager deals
promptly and appropriately with incidents affecting well-being. Where people lack capacity to make
specific decisions, Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) authorisations are in place.


Medication is managed safely. Records are robust, audits are regular, and staff undertake
competency checks. People are supported to attend health appointments, with timely referrals
made when issues arise. People are actively involved in meal planning and preparation, with
attention to specific dietary needs. Infection prevention is effective. The home holds a Food
Standards Agency rating of 5 (very good). Cleaning schedules are in place, PPE is readily
available, and staff apply safe practices, to maintain clean communal areas and support people to
keep their private spaces to a hygienic standard.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is well-maintained, comfortable and equipped to meet their
needs. The home is warm, welcoming and well-lit, creating a positive atmosphere as soon as
people enter. People also benefit from access to a large garden, which provides space for outdoor
activities and relaxation. There is ample parking for people’s accessible vehicles, as well as for staff
and visitors, which supports ease of access for everyone coming into the home.


People have choice and control over how they personalise their bedrooms. We saw bedrooms
decorated in ways that reflect people’s personalities, interests and comfort preferences. This helps
people feel settled and promotes a sense of ownership over their living space. The service provider
ensures people have suitable furnishings and equipment to meet their needs, and we saw
examples of equipment that supports people’s independence and well-being.


Most bedrooms have ensuite facilities, with three of the four rooms offering this provision. The
accessible ensuite bedroom downstairs supports people with mobility needs and promotes dignity
and independence. Across the home, the environment is clean, safe and organised in a way that
supports people to live comfortably. The layout enables people to move around freely and make
use of communal areas as they choose.


The environment is secure and well maintained. Care staff greet visitors on arrival, and they sign in
and out in a visitors’ book. We saw evidence of an ongoing schedule of servicing of facilities and
utilities, with appropriate regular safety checks completed. Fire alarms and emergency lighting are
regularly tested. The service provider has an in-house maintenance team, to whom the manager
can raise any maintenance issues, and they are responded to promptly. Potentially hazardous
items, such as medication and Control of Substances Hazardous to Health (COSHH) materials are
stored securely. The environment is clean and tidy, with care staff completing domestic and laundry
tasks with and for people living at The Willows, depending on their level of independence.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management at the service are excellent. The management team sets clear
expectations about the standard of care and support people should receive, and care staff told us
the manager has “high aims”. People and staff described significant improvements since the
manager came into post. The service no longer uses agency staff and a stable core team now
provides consistent care. The manager is highly visible within the home and leads by example. The
service has a detailed Statement of Purpose that clearly outlines how people will be supported,
alongside an accessible service guide for people living in the home.



Recruitment processes are safe and robust. People living in the home are involved in staff
interviews, ensuring their views inform decision-making. Staff told us they feel valued, supported,
and “proud to work at the service”. They described positive morale and a strong sense of
teamwork. The manager also promotes wider wellbeing support and has introduced an ‘extra mile’
award to recognise staff contributions. Each staff member has also been provided with an
essentials kit, which they appreciated.



A structured induction process helps ensure new staff are well prepared for their roles. Training
compliance is strong. Mandatory online training is fully completed, and face-to-face training is
progressing well, enabling staff to deliver care and support effectively. Care staff told us there is a
lot of training, but that it is useful and good quality. Supervision and appraisals are up to date.
Records show these have developed significantly, from brief, task-focused discussions into
reflective and meaningful conversations; they are highly effective. We saw that practice issues
were addressed promptly and in a supportive manner, helping staff understand what needed to
improve and how to achieve this. Care staff said the improved supervision process helps them feel
supported and confident in their roles. The service has extensive organisational policies and
procedures, and we saw a monthly focus on a “policy of the month” to refresh knowledge and
practice within the team.



Management oversight of the service is highly effective. The provider completes comprehensive
audits across all areas of practice to monitor quality and identify improvements. The RI
(Responsible Individual) is a regular presence in the home and engages frequently with both staff
and people, in addition to the required quarterly visits. The most recent 6-monthly quality of care
reports have improved significantly; they are clear, detailed and include strong feedback, defined
actions and an improvement plan. We saw committed improvements in leadership and

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
