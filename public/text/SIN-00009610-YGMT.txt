Summary:

 Pentre House is a Local Authority residential home providing care and support to older adults with
 residential care needs. It is located centrally within Pentre.


 Well-being is excellent because people are supported to live meaningful, independent and fulfilling
 lives. People are at the centre of decisions about their care and support. They benefit from strong
 relationships  with care  staff who know them  well and promote  their  rights, strengths and
 aspirations. Care and support is good because people receive personalised, outcome-focused care
 which  reflects  their  needs,  preferences  and  wishes.  Detailed  care  planning,  effective
 multidisciplinary working and strong safeguarding arrangements support people to remain safe and
 healthy. The environment  is  excellent because people  live  in an  exceptionally welcoming,
 comfortable and homely setting which enhances their quality of life. Leadership and management is
 good because strong, values-led leadership, and good governance and quality assurance systems
 support positive outcomes for people. Managers demonstrate a strong commitment to continuous
 improvement and actively involve people and care staff in the development of the service. A stable,
 skilled and well-supported staff team provides consistent care and contributes to a positive culture
 throughout the home.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 Well-being is excellent because people are supported to live healthy, safe and meaningful lives
 with high levels of choice, control and independence. People are at the centre of care planning and
 are encouraged to make decisions about their daily routines, activities, visitors and how they spend
 their time. Care staff consistently treat people with dignity, kindness and respect and promote their
 rights, strengths and aspirations. Positive risk-taking is managed extremely well, helping people
 maintain independence while remaining safe. Resident meetings and surveys provide meaningful
 opportunities to influence the service, and advocacy is arranged proactively when required. People
 benefit  from  varied  activities,  strong community  links and  excellent access  to  healthcare
 professionals, supporting their physical, mental and emotional well-being. Care staff understand
 people's communication needs and work closely with families and professionals to achieve positive
 outcomes. The service demonstrates an excellent commitment to promoting Welsh language and
 culture and is making strong progress towards the Active Offer. We observed a warm and positive
 atmosphere where people appeared relaxed, engaged and happy. People told us  "It’s brilliant
 here…I feel safe. I've got everything I need - what more could I ask for" and “Can't fault them. It’s
 lovely and clean…my family are so pleased I'm here”. Families and visiting professionals told us
 "The staff go above and beyond for residents, organising a variety of activities and outings", “Staff
 are friendly and caring, nothing is too much”, and “it’s a warm, professional and supportive service
 that places the needs and wellbeing of residents at the centre of everything it does”.


 Safeguarding is embedded throughout the service. Effective systems identify risks at an early stage
 and support vulnerable people to remain safe. Strong partnerships with external professionals help
 ensure concerns are managed  appropriately and promptly.  Information about safeguarding,
 complaints and raising concerns is readily available, and people remain involved in decisions
 affecting their safety and well-being. Management oversight is robust and there is clear evidence of
 learning from incidents and safeguarding events to strengthen practice.


 People's well-being is greatly enhanced because individuality, identity and personal history are
 valued and respected. Equality, diversity and inclusion are actively promoted, including creative
 initiatives which encourage people to learn about and celebrate different cultures. People maintain
 important  relationships  with  family and  friends  in an  exceptionally warm and welcoming
 environment. Positive risk management supports inclusion rather than restricting opportunities,
 helping people remain  active and  involved  in  their communities. Strong peer  relationships
 contribute to a friendly and socially active atmosphere.

People live in accommodation which supports their well-being outcomes exceptionally well. The
home is highly personalised, welcoming and maintained to a very high standard. Investment and
refurbishment are continually ongoing and completed to an outstanding standard. Bedrooms reflect
people's preferences and promote a strong sense of ownership and belonging. Equipment and
adaptations support safety, comfort and independence throughout the home. People are consulted
about environmental improvements, ensuring developments reflect their wishes and enhance their
quality of life. The home is safe, clean, comfortable and accessible, with access to outdoor spaces
and opportunities to enjoy the wider community.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care and support is good because people receive consistent, person-centred care which meets
their needs, promotes  their independence and supports  positive outcomes. Care plans are
comprehensive, detailed and developed with people, reflecting their strengths, preferences and
outcomes. Plans provide clear guidance for care staff and are reviewed regularly to ensure they
remain accurate. Detailed risk assessments support safe care while promoting independence. We
observed warm and respectful interactions between care staff and people, with a relaxed and
homely atmosphere throughout the home. Care staff know people well and provide support in line
with their wishes and preferences. Mealtimes are enjoyable, unhurried and offer choice.


People are protected from harm because safeguarding arrangements are well established. Care
staff understand their safeguarding responsibilities and reporting procedures. Management works
effectively with partner agencies to safeguard people and takes prompt action to reduce identified
risks. Incidents, accidents and safeguarding concerns are reviewed to identify learning and improve
practice. Management promotes an open and transparent culture and responds appropriately when
concerns are  raised. Deprivation  of  Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) arrangements are managed
appropriately, best interest processes are followed, and advocacy is considered whenever needed.
Consent and mental capacity issues are managed  well, helping people remain involved  in
decisions about their care and support.


Systems are in place to support the administration, storage and monitoring of medication. The
service maintains strong links with district nurses, advanced practitioners and pharmacy services to
support  people's  health needs. Medication  administration  records are accurate and  reflect
prescribed medicines. Care staff receive medication training and competency assessments to
ensure they remain knowledgeable and competent. The medication room is secure, clean and well
organised. Controlled drug storage and recording systems are good, and clear arrangements
ensure medication changes are communicated promptly to relevant care  staff. Management
demonstrates a positive approach to learning from any issues. Regular audits help ensure correct
medication stocks and help identify opportunities for further improvement.


The service maintains good infection prevention and control practices. We found the home to be
very clean throughout, and laundry and clinical waste arrangements were generally managed
appropriately. Care staff have access to suitable supplies and equipment to support safe practice.
Effective auditing and monitoring arrangements help ensure standards remain consistent. The
service has a transparent approach to reporting concerns and uses oversight processes to identify
issues.

       Environment                              Excellent



The environment is excellent because people benefit from an exceptionally high-quality home
which enhances their well-being, promotes independence and provides a comfortable, welcoming
place to live. The environment is a significant strength of the service. Recently refurbished areas
have been completed to an outstanding standard and go beyond what would be expected.
Improvements demonstrate  creativity, innovation and a genuine commitment and passion to
improving people's quality of life with the resources available. Management actively involves people
in decisions about improvements, ensuring developments reflect their preferences and wishes. The
service is developing a dedicated facility to support people with their end-of-life journey, following
collaborative work with health professionals. This demonstrates a proactive and compassionate
approach to supporting people and their families at an important stage of life.


The environment meets people's needs extremely well and supports positive outcomes. Lifts, stair
lifts and appropriate adaptations help people move around the home safely and independently.
Bedrooms are personalised to reflect individual tastes and preferences, promoting a strong sense
of ownership and belonging. People have a choice of several attractive and welcoming communal
areas where they can socialise, relax or take part in activities. Private spaces are available for
visitors and confidential conversations, supporting people's privacy and dignity. Outdoor areas are
easily accessible and were being used by people during our visit. The overall design of the home
creates a warm, inclusive and homely atmosphere where people appear comfortable and at ease.


The service is clean, free from clutter and maintained to a high standard. Bathroom and toilet
facilities are creatively decorated, being sufficient to meet people's needs and promote dignity and
independence. Temperature, lighting and comfort levels throughout the home are appropriate and
contribute positively to people's well-being. Effective maintenance arrangements ensure repairs are
completed promptly and the environment remains safe and welcoming. We found health and safety
files, maintenance records and servicing documentation are well organised and up to date.
Personal emergency evacuation plans and emergency arrangements are managed appropriately,
helping ensure people remain safe. The continued investment and commitment to improving the
environment help people achieve excellent outcomes and enjoy a high quality of life.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management is good because people benefit from strong leadership, effective
oversight and a positive values-led culture which supports people’s outcomes. The management
team has a strong understanding of the service and the people living there, demonstrating a clear
commitment to providing high-quality, person-centred care. We received very positive feedback
about the manager and deputy manager, who are visible, approachable and knowledgeable. Care
staff told us "They are approachable, they are always willing to help staff, even if it is last minute,
very family orientated, they are visible and don't just sit in the office all day," and "Pentre House has
been going from strength to strength and this is due to the management. They really care about
staff and individuals alike." Effective governance arrangements are in place, including regular
quality of care reviews by the Responsible Individual and detailed oversight visits to consult with
people and staff. An audit programme covers key areas such as medication, care planning, health
and safety, infection control, fire safety, and the environment. The management team complete
regular monitoring checks and use findings to identify improvements and drive development.
People are involved in shaping the service through resident meetings and surveys, with feedback
influencing  activities,  trips,  decoration and  refurbishment  projects.  Effective communication
systems support continuity of care, and incidents, accidents and medication errors are reviewed to
promote learning and improve practice. The service actively promotes learning through  staff
champion roles, additional training opportunities, and regular staff meetings where lessons learned
and service developments are discussed.


People are supported by a stable, skilled and well-trained staff team. Training compliance is good
overall, with learning opportunities reflecting the needs of people using the service, such as falls
training, manual handling and safeguarding. Care staff have access to additional development
opportunities beyond mandatory training, and all are registered with Social Care Wales.
Supervision, appraisal and induction arrangements are well established and support care staff to
develop their knowledge and skills. Recruitment records generally demonstrate safe recruitment
practices. Staffing levels observed during the inspection appeared to meet people's needs, with
support being calm and unhurried. A large pool of bank staff means agency staff do not need to be
used, helping provide continuity of care. Feedback from care staff was overwhelmingly positive
regarding leadership, support and opportunities for professional development, contributing to a
motivated workforce. Feedback included "I love it here…we all work together well…the staff team is
fab", “I feel well trained and feel able to go on more training… they always offer training to us when
opportunities come up”, and "Pentre House is a lovely home. The care and support provided to all
residents is phenomenal. There is so much thought and passion that goes into constantly updating
and improving the home to ensure residents feel like they are at home".

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
