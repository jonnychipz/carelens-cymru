Summary:

 The service provides a positive, inclusive environment that effectively supports people’s well-being,
 independence, and quality of life. Interactions between staff and people are warm, respectful, and
 unhurried, contributing to a calm atmosphere and promoting emotional well-being. The environment
 is comfortable, homely, and well maintained, with access to both indoor and outdoor spaces that
 support relaxation and engagement.

 Care and support are delivered to a strong standard, with staff demonstrating a strong
 understanding of individuals’ needs, preferences, and risks. Care is provided with dignity and
 respect, and relationships between staff, people, and families are a key strength of the service.

 The service benefits from strong leadership and a stable, experienced workforce. Managers are
 approachable and foster a culture of openness, learning, and continuous improvement. Staff feel
 supported and valued, which contributes to high morale and consistent care.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good well‑being in a positive and inclusive environment. Many people have
 good control over their day-to-day lives and are supported to make choices that matter to them. We
 saw a relaxed and flexible approach to routines, particularly at mealtimes, where people were
 offered choices of food and could eat at their own pace. People were free to come and go as they
 wished, supporting independence and personal control. Daily interactions were gentle, unhurried,
 and person-centred, contributing to a calm and supportive atmosphere. There was frequent
 laughter and meaningful engagement between staff and people, reflecting respectful relationships
 and promoting emotional well-being. A good range of meaningful activities are available, supporting
 people to engage in things they enjoy and helping maintain a positive sense of well-being and
 quality of life.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. We saw people experience a safe and
 supportive environment where they are comfortable and at ease. The warm, respectful interactions
 observed between staff and people contribute to a culture where individuals feel secure and
 valued. Positive relationships and consistent care staff presence help ensure people’s needs are
 understood and responded to effectively. Relatives told us they feel welcomed and confident in the
 service, and they have positive relationships with managers and staff.

 People are supported to build safe and healthy relationships. The service demonstrates a strong
 family feel with warm, meaningful relationships evident between staff, people, and their relatives.
 People are supported to maintain and develop important relationships with relatives and others.
 Regular newsletters provide useful information and help keep people and relatives informed and
 engaged with the service. Connections with the local community are well established, enabling
 people to access local resources and maintain links outside the service. People are supported to
 go out regularly, in line with their preferences, which promotes social inclusion and supports them
 to remain active members of their community.

 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. The environment effectively
 supports people’s well-being and enhances their quality of life. People have access to pleasant and
 well-maintained outdoor spaces, including a courtyard, patio, and a small garden. These areas
 provide  opportunities  for  relaxation,  social  interaction, and  time  outdoors, which  contribute
 positively to physical and emotional well-being. Indoor spaces are calm and welcoming, supporting
 a homely atmosphere where people feel comfortable.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes. Care staff
know people well and provide support that is dignified, respectful, and responsive. We saw warm
and meaningful interactions, with staff taking time to engage with people in a person-centred way.
This supports positive outcomes and helps people feel valued. People were relaxed and
comfortable in the presence of staff, and we saw interactions that were unrushed. A relative told us,
“The staff go above and beyond, not only caring for residents with compassion and dignity, but also
taking the time to build meaningful relationships with their families.” This shows the positive impact
relationships have on people’s experience of care.

Personal plans and risk assessments are well developed and provide clear and useful guidance for
staff to support people safely while promoting independence. Care staff demonstrate a good
understanding of people’s individual needs, risks, and preferences, and can describe how they
tailor support accordingly. Care staff manage risk in a positive way, enabling people to maintain
control over their lives while remaining safe. The service works effectively with other professionals
to support people’s health and well-being, ensuring advice is followed and incorporated into care
delivery. A professional told us “The home involves us and work in tight collaboration” confirming a
consistent and effective approach to partnership working.

Safe systems for medication management are in place. Appropriate medicines policy and
procedure exist, and staff complete regular audits. Overall, medication administration records are
completed accurately. Medicines are stored securely in a locked cabinet and storage temperatures
are recorded and monitored to ensure safety. Confidential documents related to people’s
medication are stored securely in a locked cabinet. We reviewed medication charts with no gaps
identified in sampled records and we saw appropriate controlled drugs checks.

People’s risk of infection  is minimised. People are supported in a clean and well-maintained
environment that promotes their health and well-being. The service demonstrates a good approach
to hygiene and infection control, helping to protect people from risks. Care staff present well and
maintain appropriate standards, contributing to a safe and comfortable setting.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is well maintained and provides a comfortable, welcoming, and
homely setting. The overall presentation of the service is positive, and this helps people feel
relaxed, well cared for and content. We saw a calm and unhurried atmosphere, with people moving
freely between areas of the home and spending time in communal spaces of their choosing. This
supports independence, social interaction, and inclusion. Communal areas are inviting and used
well, enabling people to engage with others or spend time quietly as they prefer. Bedrooms are
nicely decorated and individualised to people’s preferences.

People benefit from access to both indoor and outdoor spaces which enhance their well-being.
Outdoor areas, including a courtyard, patio, and a small garden, are accessible and provide
pleasant environments for relaxation and social engagement. These spaces offer opportunities for
people to enjoy fresh air and spend time outdoors, supporting both physical and emotional well-
being.

The provider has systems in place to maintain the safety of the environment. Staff complete regular
safety and environmental checks to support the ongoing protection of people and staff.
Where appropriate, the service provides suitable adaptations and equipment to meet people’s
individual needs. Furnishings and resources are thoughtfully chosen to reflect people’s preferences
and promote comfort. This includes the use of specialist beds, call systems, and moving and
handling equipment to support safe and effective care. Bathroom, shower, and toilet facilities are
designed to maintain privacy, dignity, and accessibility for all.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance and oversight. The service is well-led and demonstrates
a positive, inclusive culture. Leaders and managers are visible, approachable, and knowledgeable,
providing clear direction and effective oversight. Staff described managers as supportive and
accessible, enabling staff to seek guidance or raise concerns confidently. This fosters a culture of
openness and trust, where staff feel valued and supported in their roles. The leadership approach
is strong and effective, contributing to a well-organised service where people benefit from
consistent and reliable care.

There is a stable and experienced workforce, with many care staff having worked at the service for
a long time. This continuity supports the development of meaningful relationships and a strong
understanding of people’s needs. Care staff spoke positively about their roles and consistently
reported high levels of job satisfaction, with comments reflecting that they enjoy working at the
service and value the supportive team culture. Observations on the day highlighted a relaxed and
positive atmosphere, with warm, respectful, and enjoyable interactions between staff, people, and
colleagues. This contributes to a welcoming and supportive environment and demonstrates high
staff morale.

A culture of learning and continuous improvement is evident within the service. Leaders promote
reflective practice, including following incidents such as medication errors, to improve practice and
outcomes for people. Care staff are supported to develop their roles through guidance and ongoing
support from managers. This proactive approach ensures that learning is embedded in practice and
contributes to maintaining safe and effective care.

Engagement with families is a strength of the service. Relatives provided positive feedback about
communication and felt well informed and involved in their relatives’ care. Systems are in place to
gather feedback, including the use of a feedback box, supporting ongoing improvement and
responsiveness to people’s views. This ensures that people and their representatives have
opportunities to influence the service and that their experiences are valued.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
