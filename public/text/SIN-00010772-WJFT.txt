Summary:


 Plas Cwm Carw Care Home is in the centre of Port Talbot, close to shops and other amenities. The
 provider has taken action to improve standards of care and support, as well as the overall
 management of the service. As a result, people experience good well-being as they receive safe
 care and have consistently positive experiences. They benefit from a varied programme of activities
 and regular opportunities to connect meaningfully with others.

 Although progress has been made, standards of care and support require improvement. Medication
 and infection control procedures have improved, and people receive timely input from medical and
 specialist services. This helps them to stay healthy. However, the care planning and review
 process requires improvement. This is to ensure people’s needs and goals are accurately reflected
 in personal plans and care staff have access to clear and consistent guidance.

The home and grounds are secure and appropriately maintained. The accommodation has a
homely, welcoming feel and a good range of facilities to support activities of daily living. Many
environmental improvements have been made, including the redecoration and development of
communal areas and the renewal of some furniture and furnishings.

Leadership and management arrangements have improved significantly, although some areas
continue to require improvement. New managers have built trust by listening to feedback and
making positive changes. They promote an open and supportive culture, which has resulted in
more effective teamwork and improved staff practice and morale. Staff receive appropriate
induction, training and informal support, although managers require more time to improve
supervision and appraisal processes. Administrative systems also require improvement to ensure
managers have timely access to all the records they need to manage the service effectively.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to maintain good health and well-being and have control over their care.
 Care staff are visible and attentive to people’s physical and emotional needs and will promptly refer
 concerns to the relevant agencies and professionals. People contribute to decisions regarding their
 care and support, so their needs and wishes are clearly understood. However, the service must
 make improvements to ensure personal plans are revised following reviews and accurately reflect
 people’s current needs and goals. The service holds regular staff, resident and family meetings,
 allowing people to share their views about home life. Based on recent feedback, the service is
 working to improve the laundry system and the quality and range of meals on offer.

 People feel happy and relaxed in their home as they go about their daily routines. Care staff create
 a calm environment that puts people at ease. The home has suitable facilities to cater for people’s
 day-to-day needs, and these are well-maintained. People told us, “We feel happy here… staff are
 nice” and “It’s brilliant – there is nothing of concern here”. People have fun taking part in a variety of
 activities. For example, they dressed up for Ascot Day to watch the horseracing, place friendly bets
 and enjoy sweet treats together. The home clearly advertises its activities and events, which
 include regular church services, weekly choirs, celebrations to mark special occasions and live
 entertainment. Currently, people do not regularly access the community, although the service has
 secured a minibus to support outings. These will be facilitated once suitable drivers are available.

 The running of the home has significantly improved, helping to ensure people consistently receive a
 safe and reliable service. Care staff understand their safeguarding responsibilities and help to keep
 people safe through close observation and effective risk management. Managers promote an open
 and transparent culture, so people, their representatives and staff feel able to raise concerns and
 make suggestions to drive up standards. When needed, managers liaise with the Local Authority
 safeguarding team and other professionals to reduce risks to people’s safety and well-being. The
 manager informed us about the recent withdrawal of a routine Occupational Therapy service, and
 the provider is considering how to manage risks associated with this. Administration systems
 require improvement to enable managers to view staff recruitment and disciplinary records easily
 and deal with issues effectively.

 People connect well with others. Personal plans include details about how people communicate
 and what relationships are important to them. People spend time with family and friends in both
 private and social settings. They also benefit from regular interactions with care staff, who are
 respectful and generally sensitive towards their unique characteristics. There is no current demand
 for services in Welsh, although the Welsh language and culture are promoted.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement




The service has taken action to improve standards of care and support, but further improvements
are needed. We previously identified that people’s health and well-being were at risk because care
staff did not always understand how to safely meet their needs. Practice has since improved, as
people receive appropriate care and support to promote their health and independence. People
appeared well cared for, although care staff must remain vigilant with regards to maintaining good
nail care. Care staff know people well and understand how to manage risks to their safety and well-
being. They anticipate people’s needs, closely monitor their activity and encourage them to
maintain a suitable diet.

However, personal plans do not always accurately reflect people's needs. We found that some
contained conflicting or historic information, as they had not been revised properly following
reviews. This requires improvement as risks to people’s health and safety are increased if care staff
do not have clear guidance on how to effectively meet their needs. We expect the provider to make
the necessary improvements. We also found that risks are not consistently incorporated into
personal plans and important information can be difficult to find. Additionally, various methods are
being used to document care intervention. The manager told us of plans to improve the care
planning and review process and address inconsistencies in record-keeping. This will involve
workshops to refresh staff’s knowledge and support their practice.

People receive timely intervention from medical and specialist services. Care staff make referrals
when there are concerns about people’s health and ensure professional advice, health tests and
appointments are followed up appropriately. Care staff receive regular updates about people’s care
and support needs during handovers and daily ‘flash’ meetings. Handover arrangements require
review as care staff are currently expected to attend meetings shortly before or after their shifts.

The service stores and manages medicines safely. Medication is reviewed and adjusted when
needed and staff maintain accurate records regarding medication administration and stock levels.
This helps them to identify and address any issues quickly. We observed some medicines being
given at regular intervals but outside allotted timeframes. The manager assured us administration
times would be reviewed where necessary, with input from the relevant professionals. The findings
of internal audits show that medication practices are continuously improving. There are also plans
to further develop practice in relation to the application and recording of topical medicines.

Standards of hygiene and infection control have vastly improved, helping to reduce the risk of cross
contamination and infection. We observed the home and equipment to be clean and hygienic.
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is easy to access, and we saw staff using this appropriately.
Records show that staff receive regular infection control training. The service was awarded a food
hygiene rating of 4 (good) in March 2026.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is bright, spacious and homely. The entrance area is welcoming and includes a
coffee shop, where people often spend time with visitors. People also enjoy one another’s company
in the lounges and dining rooms, where they complete various daily activities. People move freely
between private and communal areas, as able and desired. There are some dementia friendly
features and themed areas, which offer stimulation and help people orientate to their surroundings.
Bedrooms are also personalised and reflect people’s needs and preferences. The home is
decorated to a good overall standard, although this could be further enhanced by refining finishing
touches to the woodwork and paintwork.

People are benefitting from improved facilities. Suitable locks have been installed to bathroom
doors, promoting people’s safety, privacy and dignity. Storage systems are better organised, with
equipment and other items stored safely. Some lounge chairs and bedding are also being replaced.
We saw people using the specialist equipment they need to help keep them safe and comfortable.
However, some mattresses required adjusting as they were not at the correct settings to provide
optimal support. We discussed with the manager the need for care staff to perform and record
mattress checks correctly, ensuring any issues are addressed promptly. The service has recently
invested in a new range of higher-specification mattresses, including automatic air alternating
mattresses that provide regular pressure relief and help to reduce the risk of skin breakdown. The
manager told us extra air conditioning units have also been purchased to help regulate the
temperature in warmer parts of the building.

The accommodation and facilities are safe and well-maintained. Additional safety checks have
been introduced to ensure the building and grounds remain secure. Two maintenance officers carry
out general repairs, redecoration and routine health and safety checks. Utilities and equipment are
serviced and inspected within recommended timescales to ensure they are in good working order.
The manager informed us that plumbers have recently resolved issues with the central heating.
Staff are ensuring wooden panels are properly secured after accessing pipework, reducing the risk
of people being exposed to hazards. This was an issue identified at the last inspection. Work to
declutter the environment is ongoing and includes disposing of old, unused equipment. However,
staff must take care not to discard rubbish bags where they may pose a fire safety risk.

People have regular opportunities to spend time outdoors in the home’s two gardens. We saw
people socialising in the main courtyard, where they appeared relaxed and content while listening
to music and enjoying refreshments. The gardens are accessible and include seating and sheltered
areas. We also saw that people have spent time planting flowers in the main courtyard, adding
interest to this area. The gardens are generally well-presented, although patio areas would benefit
from refurbishment as part of future investment plans.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



The management of the service has significantly improved since the last inspection. The service
was not previously being operated with sufficient care, competence or skill, and this was impacting
on people’s care. A permanent nurse manager and clinical deputy have since been appointed and
are providing strong and effective leadership. A staff member said, “Couldn’t ask for anyone better”.
Staff have a better understanding of people’s needs and deliver care safely. New staff receive an
appropriate induction and training before starting work, and managers have addressed staff
conduct issues via disciplinary procedures. Quality assurance and audit systems also show that the
service is making steady improvements. Staff, families and professionals have observed better
standards of care, higher staff morale and a more positive working culture. Staff told us, “It’s nice to
come to work again” and “It’s getting better and better”. A relative also said, “Things are immensely
better… They’re changing all the things they need to now, you don’t need to ask”. The manager
remains committed to further improving the service and embedding these positive changes.

People are at risk of being cared for by staff who may not be suitable to continue working in their
role, and this requires improvement. Recruitment and disciplinary records are not always available
at the service and often need to be obtained from other departments within the company, which
can cause unacceptable delays. Failure to keep these records up to date and in good order could
delay Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks and result in a disjointed approach to
managing staff disciplinary issues. We expect the provider to make the required improvements.
The administrator is aware of the records that are not currently available at the service and is
working to rectify this. We also found that timescales are not always being met for responding to
complaints and completing six-monthly quality reviews. The Responsible Individual (RI) and
management team must manage these processes more effectively and ensure administrative tasks
are completed in a timely way.

Staffing levels are reviewed and adjusted as needed to meet people’s needs. The staffing position
has improved following the recruitment of permanent nurses and care staff, and good progress is
being made with regards to filling remaining vacancies. Staffing shortages are covered by existing
staff or agency workers who know the home well. Regular team meetings give the manager and
staff opportunities to discuss issues, share updates and agree actions. A staff member described
the manager as “straight talking – will act on issues straightaway”. Records show that staff
complete mandatory and specialist training to support their practice. Managers have recently
implemented a system to track staff’s registration with Social Care Wales, confirming that very few
are outstanding. Staff supervision and appraisal processes continue to require improvement as
they are not taking place at the required intervals and the quality of records is inconsistent. The
manager has imminent plans to address this. We expect the provider to take action and will follow
this up at the next inspection.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
