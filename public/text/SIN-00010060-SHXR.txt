Summary:

 Pen-coed is a residential care home for adults situated in a village in Pembrokeshire, close to the
 well-known coastal towns of Tenby and Saundersfoot. Up to twenty-five people can be
 accommodated in a two-storey building.

 Overall people achieve good wellbeing outcomes and benefit from care that is compassionate,
 respectful and focused on their individual needs. People are supported to maintain their health,
 remain connected to those important to them and engage in experiences that enhance their quality
 of life.

 Care and support are good. People receive good quality care and support from a stable, caring and
 knowledgeable staff team. Strong healthcare partnerships and positive relationships between staff
 and people contribute to positive outcomes for people living at the service. While improvements are
 required in some areas of care planning and documentation, these issues are recognised by
 management and are being addressed and have not impacted on the good quality care people
 receive.

 The environment is good. People live in a safe, comfortable and supportive environment that

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People living at Pen-coed Residential Care Home are supported by a caring and committed staff
 team. During our inspection, we observed many positive interactions between staff and people
 living at the home. Staff speak to people respectfully, patiently and in a warm manner. There is a
 relaxed atmosphere throughout the service and people appeared comfortable in the presence of
 staff. Staff have a good understanding of individuals' needs, preferences and routines and support
 people to make choices in their day to day lives.

 People and their relatives consistently spoke positively about the quality of care they receive. One
 person told us, "There's nothing to complain about. The staff are very nice, nothing is too much
 trouble." Another person described the staff as "perfect" and said they loved living at the service.
 Relatives were equally complimentary. One relative told us, "The most important thing is the staff
 and they're brilliant. Everybody here today was here when xxxx arrived." Another stated, "The care
 is second to none. I wouldn't move them anywhere else”.

 People are supported to maintain their physical, mental health and emotional well-being. The
 manager has strong links with local health services ensuring people have timely access to
 additional support when required. Care staff know people well and notice any changes in their
 presentation and needs meaning people receive the right support when they need it. Relatives
 described examples where staff had identified health concerns promptly and acted quickly to
 access medical intervention resulting in a positive outcome. A district nurse told us they had "no
 issues" with the service and described staff as always willing to assist. They also confirmed there
 are sufficient staff available to support nursing interventions safely.

 People's social wellbeing is promoted through activities and meaningful experiences, and they are
 supported to continue doing things that matter to them, as much as possible. The manager has
 introduced a "wish experience" initiative which aims to support people to participate in experiences
 that are significant to them based on their personal histories and interests. For example, one
 person who had previously worked as a jockey was supported to attend a race meeting after the
 manager arranged access through local contacts. Another individual was supported to purchase a
 replacement guitar because music had played an important role throughout their life.
 Although outcomes for people are positive overall, recent staffing shortages have reduced
 opportunities for staff to spend one-to-one time with people. The manager acknowledges the
 challenges created by vacancies and explained that newly recruited care workers are due to
 commence employment shortly. We were provided with assurances that this would improve staffing
 resilience and increase opportunities for personalised support.

People are supported to maintain relationships that are important to them. Visitors are welcome at
any time and relatives told us communication from the service is very good. One relative said the
manager regularly telephones with updates and follows up with messages if they cannot be
reached. Families report feeling involved and informed about significant events affecting their
relatives.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good care and support delivered by staff who know them well and understand their
individual needs, preferences and routines. A stable workforce means staff have developed trusting
relationships with those they support. Feedback from people, relatives, staff and professionals
consistently indicates that care is delivered in a compassionate and person-centred manner. One
relative said, “"Everyone here today was here on day one. You never see a different face”. Another
stated, "The majority of the staff are still here from when my mother first moved in four years ago."

Robust admission arrangements are in place to help ensure people's needs can be appropriately
met before they move into the service. Information is gathered from health and social care
professionals, hospital teams, family members and the individual themselves where possible. This
information is used to inform care planning and support staff to understand people's needs from the
point of admission. Personal plans cover a wide range of areas and appropriate risk assessments
are in place to support staff to provide care safely while promoting people's independence.

Relatives and people living at the home speak positively about the food provided. One person told
us, "The food is good, I can't complain, you can have more or less whatever you want”. People's
dietary needs and risks relating to nutrition are assessed and monitored. Meals are freshly
prepared and local suppliers are used where possible.

Medication management arrangements are generally safe. Medication is stored securely and staff
administering medicines receive appropriate training and competency assessments. People
requiring covert medication have supporting documentation and GP involvement. We identified
opportunities to strengthen the medication policy by providing more detailed guidance regarding
covert medication, PRN (as required) medicines and homely remedies. Strengthening policy
guidance would provide greater assurance that practice remains aligned with current best practice.

People are protected from abuse and neglect through effective safeguarding arrangements. Staff
have a clear understanding of safeguarding procedures and can explain how they would respond if
concerns arise. Staff receive safeguarding training and staff feel confident that management would
take appropriate action if concerns were raised. The service also works closely with health and
social care professionals to manage risks and safeguard individuals when concerns arise.

Overall records are up to date and relevant. Recording systems in some areas could be
strengthened. While there is evidence of regular day-to-day communication with families, records
do not always demonstrate this. The manager acknowledged this and provided assurances that
people and relatives would be involved more consistently in documented reviews moving forward.
We also identified some inconsistencies in care records and monitoring charts. While these issues
have not resulted in adverse outcomes, they limit the service's ability to fully evidence the quality of
care provided and evaluate people's progress towards identified goals. The manager immediately

       Environment                         Good



People live in a welcoming and comfortable environment that supports their wellbeing. Bedrooms
are personalised with photographs, furniture and possessions that reflect people’s lives and
interests, creating familiar surroundings. Communal areas, including lounges, a conservatory and
accessible gardens, provide opportunities for social interaction, activities and quiet relaxation
according to individual preferences. We observed people moving freely around the home and using
these spaces confidently and comfortably.

The provider has continued to invest in improving the environment. Recent work has included
resurfacing the car park, improving the entrance area and creating a new porch and reception
space. Relatives recognised these improvements and spoke positively about the investment. One
relative told us, "It's clear they have spent a lot of money improving the front. It looked quite run
down before they did the work."

The manager described several ongoing environmental improvement projects, including an
innovative development designed to enhance the experiences of people living with dementia. Plans
are in place to create a train carriage themed experience within an area adjoining the conservatory.
The project will utilise visual technology and themed surroundings to allow people to experience
virtual journeys and reminiscence opportunities. The proposed development demonstrates
creativity and an understanding of the importance of sensory stimulation, meaningful occupation
and dementia-friendly design principles.

People have access to appropriate equipment and adaptations to promote safety, mobility and
independence. Handrails are provided throughout the home; specialist equipment is available when
required and outdoor spaces can be accessed safely depending on staff availability. Environmental
safety systems are generally effective, with evidence of routine maintenance, fire safety checks,
servicing programmes and Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans being in place. The home is
clean and tidy throughout and staff demonstrate good infection prevention and control practices.

Some indoor areas require further attention and upgrading. Parts of the building would benefit from
redecoration, several window handles require repair, and the flooring is worn and torn in one place.
Additionally, following changes within the domestic team, cleaning schedules are not being
followed as consistently as usual, although standards of cleanliness and hygiene remain good. The
manager is aware of these issues and provided assurances that they are going to be addressed.

The manager maintains environmental monitoring systems and completes routine checks of the
premises. Overall, we found people were living in an environment where health and safety risks
were generally identified and managed appropriately.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People living at Pen-coed Residential Care Home benefit from a service led by an experienced and
visible manager who is well regarded by relatives, staff and professionals. Feedback during the
inspection demonstrated a positive culture focused on person-centred care, openness and
improving outcomes for people. A relative told us, “They (manager) is excellent, communication is
good" and another said, “If there is anything I need to know ‘X’ (manager) will contact me”.

The manager has a good understanding of the people they support and their health conditions and
described initiatives designed to improve wellbeing and promote meaningful outcomes. These
include supporting people to achieve personal wishes and maintain community connections
through social, cultural and fundraising activities. A relative told us, "X (manager) is excellent,
communication is good. If I miss a call from them, they will send me a message. ‘X’ is very
knowledgeable about dementia, understands and knows how to manage people".

Care staff speak positively about management support and describe an open and approachable
leadership style. One staff member told us, “I’ve always been supported since I've been here. The
door is always open, and ‘x’ (manager) always acts on anything we ask”.

Governance arrangements are generally effective. Electronic systems are used to monitor audits,
training, supervision and operational tasks and will highlight any overdue tasks. The Responsible
Individual (RI) also monitors the managers audits and will be made aware of any overdue tasks.
Medication audits, environmental checks and other monitoring activities are completed regularly.

Training compliance is strong and supervision and appraisal records show care staff receive
ongoing support and opportunities for development to ensure they have the necessary skills and
knowledge for their role. Care staff undergo strict vetting processes before they are recruited and
are registered with Social Care Wales, their professional body.

Workforce stability is a notable strength, with several staff having worked at the service for many
years. Relatives and staff identified consistency of staffing as contributing positively to people's
experiences and relationships within the home. However, the service has recently experienced
staffing vacancies which has placed additional pressure on staff and management. While people’s
care needs continue to be met safely, additional staffing would improve opportunities for one-to-one
support and meaningful engagement. The manager confirmed that three new care workers have
been successfully recruited, two of whom were already familiar with the service through agency
work.

The RI has effective oversight and governance arrangements in place, and the six-monthly reports
highlight areas of strength and where improvements can be made to improve the standard of care

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
