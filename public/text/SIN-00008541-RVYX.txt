Summary:

 Beechlea is a small care home situated in a village close to Bargoed and provides accommodation,
 care and support for up to four adults.

 People’s well-being and the care and support they receive are both rated as good. People are at
 the centre of their care and are treated with dignity, kindness and respect. Their choices,
 independence and individual outcomes are promoted, with personal plans reflecting their needs
 and wishes and are regularly reviewed. People receive their medicines as prescribed, with some
 aspects of medication management identified for further improvement.

 The environment and leadership and management of the service are both rated as good. People
 live in accommodation that supports their independence, safety and sense of security. Improved
 environmental oversight is required to ensure people continue to live in a safe and well-maintained
 home. The service is generally well managed, and quality assurance arrangements continue to
 develop to support effective monitoring and improvement. The RI is strengthening their oversight to
 provide greater assurance about the ongoing quality and safety of the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good emotional and physical well-being at Beechlea. They appear comfortable
 and settled in their surroundings and are treated with kindness, dignity and respect by a caring and
 attentive staff team. People benefit from positive relationships with staff and enjoy the
 companionship of others. Friends and relatives are welcomed and can visit when they wish,
 supporting people to maintain relationships that are important to them. Staff support people to
 access their local community and wider opportunities, including holidays abroad. People have
 opportunities to engage in activities and interactions that are meaningful to them, including planned
 activities and informal, spontaneous interactions with staff. People appeared relaxed and
 comfortable in the company of staff, demonstrating positive relationships and a sense of belonging
 within the service.


 People are supported to make choices and exercise as much control as possible over their daily
 lives. Staff were heard and observed offering people choices, and personal plans reflect individual
 likes, dislikes and preferences. People’s language preferences are identified as part of the
 assessment process and support is provided accordingly. People have opportunities to express
 their views about the service through arrangements such as keyworker meetings and visits from
 the Responsible Individual (RI).

 There is focus on promoting and maintaining people’s emotional and physical health. This includes
 access to GP services and other health and social care professionals, with staff supporting them to
 attend appointments. Referrals are made promptly when there are changes in people’s health,
 demonstrating a responsive approach to maintaining well-being.

 There are systems in place to help protect people from abuse and harm. The service assesses
 people’s care and support needs and any associated risks to their health and well-being. Care staff
 are trained to meet the needs of the people they support and are aware of their safeguarding
 responsibilities. There are whistle-blowing and safeguarding policies and procedures in place to
 further support practice.

 The accommodation generally supports people’s well-being. People have access to communal
 areas where they can spend time together, while their bedrooms provide private space and are
 personalised with belongings reflecting their individual tastes and preferences. People also have
 access to outdoor garden space. Routine fire safety and health and safety checks are completed to
 help maintain a safe environment.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that helps them achieve their personal outcomes and promotes
their well-being. Staff provide support with warmth, compassion and patience and demonstrate a
good understanding of people’s individual communication needs and preferences. They adapt their
approach to enable people to understand, engage and express themselves. We observed positive
and meaningful interactions, with people appearing relaxed and reassured by the staff supporting
them.


Personal plans provide a comprehensive and individualised overview of people’s needs,
preferences, and backgrounds. These plans include social histories, reflect people’s voices, and
guide staff in delivering consistent and responsive care. Plans are reviewed regularly, although
records do not always clearly demonstrate how people, or their representatives, have been
meaningfully involved in the review process. Risk assessments are in place to identify and manage
potential risks to people’s safety and well-being. Going forward, risk assessments will be reviewed
alongside personal plans to ensure they remain current and relevant.


Keyworker meetings provide people with further opportunities to discuss their care, preferences
and experiences of living at the service. These meetings focus on what matters to people, their
individual wishes and the outcomes they would like to achieve. People’s health is monitored and
they are supported by care staff who know them well and notice any changes quickly. This results
in timely referrals to healthcare professionals supporting people to maintain their physical health
and any advice or recommendations are followed.


Improvements are required in medication management and oversight. Although records indicate
people receive their medicines as prescribed, concerns relating to storage temperatures, updating
personal  plans, medication  audits and  staff competency checks mean  there  is  insufficient
assurance that medication practices are consistently safe and effective. This has the potential to
adversely affect people’s health and well-being outcomes. This is an area for improvement, and we
expect the provider to make improvements before the next inspection.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is suitable for people’s needs and provides a comfortable and homely place to
live. People’s bedrooms are of a sufficient size and are personalised according to their individual
preferences, with photographs, belongings and items of importance helping to create a sense of
familiarity and belonging. Communal areas provide opportunities for people to spend time together
and take part in activities. During the inspection, we observed people using communal areas and
they appeared comfortable and relaxed. People have access to an outdoor area with seating which
can be enjoyed during warmer weather.



There are appropriate arrangements to help keep people safe and secure. Access to the home is
controlled, visitors are required to make themselves known on arrival, and staff verify their identity
before allowing access. Fire safety equipment is checked regularly, including testing of fire alarms
and emergency lighting. People’s personal emergency evacuation plan (PEEP), summaries will be
developed further to clearly identify the individual support they require to evacuate safely in an
emergency. External contractors undertake required servicing and safety checks, including gas and
electrical testing. Food hygiene standards are maintained to a high level, as evidenced by the
service achieving a Food Standards Agency rating of 5.


Improvements are required in the service provider’s oversight of the environment. The home is
generally clean and maintained to an acceptable standard; however, sealant and tile grouting within
a bathroom and areas of flooring near the front door require replacement. The provider gave
assurance these issues would be addressed and any underlying causes identified to reduce the
risk of recurrence.

Routine internal environmental and health and safety checks are not documented to demonstrate
effective oversight of the premises. Consequently, there is insufficient assurance that systems are
sufficiently robust to routinely identify, monitor and address environmental issues promptly, to
ensure people live in a safe and well-maintained environment. This is an area for improvement, and
we expect the provider to make improvements before the next inspection.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service has governance arrangements in place which support its day-to-day operation and
help people achieve their individual well-being outcomes. The Statement of Purpose (SoP) and
guide to the service provide information about the care and support people can expect. The
manager is experienced, registered with Social Care Wales and supported by a deputy manager.
The Responsible Individual (RI) maintains oversight of the service through regular visits, which
include opportunities to speak with people and staff.



Overall, people are supported to achieve their well-being outcomes; however, quality assurance
and monitoring arrangements require strengthening. The Quality-of-Care report does not provide a
sufficiently comprehensive evaluation of the service, with no analysis of information from audits, RI
visits and environmental monitoring. Greater oversight and scrutiny are required across medication
management, environmental and health and safety monitoring, and staff support and development
to ensure areas for improvement are identified, appropriate action is taken and improvements are
sustained.


Currently, quality assurance arrangements do not provide sufficient assurance that emerging risks
are identified, and learning is used effectively to drive and sustain improvements in the quality and
safety of people’s care and support. Outcomes for people may not continue to be good because
the RI does not have sufficient oversight of the quality of the service. This is an area for
improvement, and we expect the provider to make improvements before the next inspection.



People are supported by a generally stable staff team, with sufficient staff available to meet their
needs at the time of inspection. Staff feel well supported and confident they have the skills needed
to carry out their roles. They complete relevant core training, and care staff are appropriately
registered with Social Care Wales. Staff receive regular formal supervision, providing opportunities
for support and development. However, supervision records do not always accurately reflect
discussions held. The provider continues to strengthen recruitment and induction arrangements to
ensure appropriate pre-employment checks are completed, and induction is structured and aligned
with the All Wales Induction Framework (AWIF). The service provider is taking steps to strengthen
the quality of supervision records, formal supervision arrangements for the deputy manager, and
improve opportunities for staff to meet collectively as a team.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
