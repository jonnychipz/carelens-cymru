Summary:
 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes because their care is person centred and individual
 needs, likes and dislikes are considered fully. Care staff understand what matters to individuals and
 consistently anticipate their needs and preferences. People benefit from a wide range of
 meaningful activities that significantly enhance their overall well-being.

 Care and support are excellent. Care staff know people extremely well and deliver care with
 kindness, dignity, and genuine warmth. Their strong relationships with individuals ensure that
 support is tailored, responsive, and consistently of a very high standard.

 The environment is good, offering a warm, comfortable, and welcoming home that meets people’s
 needs reliably. The provider carries out regular maintenance and repairs, ensuring the setting
 remains safe and supportive for those who live there. The environment meets people's needs
 effectively and contributes positively to their overall quality of life.


 Leadership and management are excellent. The service is very well-led, with a strong and positive
 culture that promotes the well-being of both the people supported and the staff team. Staff have
 robust training, recruitment practices are effective, and governance and oversight systems are in

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People achieve excellent wellbeing outcomes because care staff know them well and consistently
 place their wishes, preferences and choices at the centre of support. We observed warm,
 respectful and meaningful interactions throughout the inspection. Care staff spoke with people in a
 kind and compassionate manner, encouraged independence and responded positively to requests
 for assistance. People told us they feel respected, valued and safe. Several people described the
 service as excellent and said staff always provide the support they need. Relatives confirmed staff
 treat people with dignity and maintain effective communication. People experience exceptionally
 positive outcomes because care staff create a culture that promotes dignity, respect,
 independence, inclusion and meaningful engagement.

 People enjoy extensive opportunities to participate in activities that reflect their interests and
 promote social inclusion. Three activity coordinators provide a varied programme of meaningful
 opportunities including community outings, pet therapy, reminiscence sessions, arts and crafts,
 exercise groups, music, quizzes and one-to-one engagement. The service has strengthened
 activity provision since the previous inspection and now develops activity programmes to reflect
 people's preferences. Community links with local schools, colleges, community organisations and
 visiting entertainers provide additional opportunities for people to remain connected with the wider
 community.

 Care staff promote choice and control in daily life. People choose when they get up, go to bed,
 receive visitors and participate in activities. Care staff support people to make informed decisions
 about meals through pictorial menus and personalised dietary arrangements. We saw numerous
 examples of the service tailoring support to individual preferences, religious beliefs and personal
 routines.

 People benefit from highly personalised approaches that enhance quality of life. Comprehensive
 ‘About Me’ profiles help staff understand people's histories, preferences and aspirations. Activity
 staff use this information to develop meaningful opportunities that promote emotional wellbeing,
 confidence and social interaction. We found evidence of people achieving outcomes that matter to
 them and maintaining interests that support their identity and sense of belonging.

 People's health and wellbeing needs are supported  effectively.  Staff provide positive dining
 experiences and encourage independence wherever possible. Meals are well presented, choice is
 promoted and specialist diets are catered for appropriately. We observed mealtimes that were
 calm, sociable and person centred. People had access to refreshments throughout the day and told
 us they enjoy the food provided. People described the food as “Lovely”, “Plentiful” and “Well made”.

       Care & Support                           Excellent




People receive care and support of an exceptionally high standard because staff understand their
needs, preferences and personal goals. Personal plans are detailed; person centred and focus on
achieving outcomes that matter to people. Care records contain comprehensive information on
health needs, communication, nutrition, mobility, personal preferences, social interests and
emotional wellbeing. Staff use this information to deliver care that is tailored to each person's
circumstances and aspirations. People told us, “I was lucky to get a place here, it’s got a good
reputation”, “All very good, if you want anything they are there” and “Staff are really helpful,
wonderful”.

People and their representatives actively contribute to care planning and reviews. People and
relatives consistently told us they are involved in discussions about care and support and receive
regular updates from staff. Records demonstrated clear evidence of consultation and involvement
in reviews. This helps ensure care remains relevant and responsive to changing needs.

People benefit from access to healthcare services when required. The service maintains strong
relationships with general practitioners, specialist nurses and other professionals. Staff make timely
referrals and act on professional advice. Care records demonstrated regular monitoring of health
conditions, specialist interventions and effective coordination of care. Professionals provided
positive feedback regarding the quality of support people receive. We spoke with a visiting health
professional who told us they had no concerns and any advice given is always followed

Medication management arrangements are robust. Staff follow clear procedures for storage,
administration, recording and auditing. Competency assessments support safe practice, and
regular audits provide effective oversight. Medication administration record (MAR) charts contain all
required information and are completed correctly with signatures when medication has been
administered. Controlled drugs are also appropriately stored and recorded. Records show regular
contact with health and social care professionals when needed.

The service implements effective measures to protect people from harm. Care staff demonstrate a
clear understanding of safeguarding, can recognise potential concerns, and follow appropriate
reporting  procedures.  Care  staff  reported  feeling  confident  that  management  responds
appropriately to any issues raised. The service maintains accurate records of incidents and
accidents and completes detailed  risk assessments to support safe practices while enabling
individuals to take positive risks. People and their families confirmed that they feel safe and well
supported at Picton Court. A relative told us, “It’s a weight off my shoulders her being here”. The
service maintains effective relationships with safeguarding partners and keeps relevant records.
Training compliance and staff knowledge provide reassurance that people are protected from harm.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a good environment that is safe, comfortable and well maintained. The provider
maintains effective systems to ensure the premises remain suitable for people's needs. We found
the service clean, tidy and free from unpleasant odours. Bedrooms and communal areas were
furnished, decorated and maintained to a good standard, creating a welcoming environment for
people and visitors.

People personalise their bedrooms with personal belongings, photographs and items of importance
to them. This helps people maintain their identity and feel at home. We saw comfortable communal
lounges, dining areas, quiet spaces and a conservatory available for people to use. The service
also provides dedicated facilities including assisted bathrooms and a hairdressing salon.

The provider maintains robust arrangements to manage health and safety. We reviewed evidence
of regular servicing, maintenance and testing of key equipment and systems. Fire safety
arrangements were comprehensive and included routine drills, testing and servicing. The provider
also completed checks relating to hoists, slings, lifts, water safety, electrical systems and other
essential equipment. These arrangements help ensure risks are identified and managed
appropriately.

People have access to outdoor areas that support wellbeing. The dementia garden provides safe
access to fresh air, gardening opportunities and meaningful activity. Raised flower beds, accessible
pathways and seating areas encourage participation and social interaction. Patio spaces adjacent
to communal lounges also offer opportunities to enjoy outdoor environments.

The provider ensures hazardous substances are stored securely and maintain appropriate infection
prevention arrangements. Laundry facilities operated effectively with clear separation of clean and
dirty items. Domestic staff followed cleaning schedules and maintained high standards of
cleanliness throughout the home.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management arrangements are highly effective and contribute significantly to the
excellent outcomes experienced by people. Management promote a strong values-based culture
that places people at the centre of service delivery. Staff consistently describe the management
team as approachable, supportive and committed to achieving positive outcomes for people.

The provider maintains extensive governance and quality assurance systems. Regular audits
monitor all aspects of service delivery, including care planning, medication management, infection
prevention, staffing, training, accidents and incidents. Management use information gathered
through audits to identify trends, drive improvement and strengthen practice. Quality reviews
demonstrated clear oversight of service performance and a commitment to continuous
improvement.

The Responsible Individual (RI) maintains regular oversight of the service and exceeds minimum
regulatory requirements through additional visits and engagement with people and staff. Feedback
mechanisms include surveys, meetings, audits and quality reviews. Management actively seek and
act upon feedback to improve outcomes and service delivery. This year’s annual survey is currently
in process, and we saw posters around the home with QR codes for staff and relatives to access.

Staff receive structured induction, supervision, appraisal and extensive training opportunities.
Training compliance levels are high and included specialist subjects relevant to people's needs.
Care staff spoke positively about opportunities to progress their careers, gain qualifications and
participate in professional development programmes. The provider supports leadership
development, nurse training, student placements and partnership working with universities.

Staff retention is strong and turnover remains low. Management maintain staffing levels through
effective workforce planning and dependency assessments. Care staff told us they feel valued and
recognised for their contribution through wellbeing initiatives, recognition schemes and
opportunities for progression. This contributes to positive morale and continuity of care for people.

The service demonstrates a culture of innovation and improvement. Examples include research
partnerships, implementation of new technology, workforce development initiatives and projects
designed to improve outcomes for people. Leaders show ambition for the service and actively
explore new approaches to enhance care and support. The service has trialled specialist TOTO
turning mattresses designed to improve comfort, support pressure care and enhance wellbeing.
Feedback from the trial was reported to be positive, with benefits identified for both people using
the service and staff responsible for providing care and support. Management reported positive
outcomes from this initiative and plans are in place to expand its use within the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
