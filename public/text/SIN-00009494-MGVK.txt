Summary:

 Llys Meddyg is a care home with nursing for 18 older people, most of whom have dementia. The
 service provides accommodation for females only and the provider is focussed on meeting the
 needs of the local community.

 People experience good well-being because their physical and mental health and emotional needs
 are closely monitored and well-being needs are met. Some excellent practice was seen such as
 tailored, person-centred activities and a high level of supervision to prevent falls. People and their
 relatives praise the level of dignity and respect afforded. People have as much control over their
 day as they can with daily routines and choices of food and activities respected and catered for.

 Care and support is good; people are well cared for and are happy in their home. They are treated
 with kindness and genuine compassion. People feel they are listened to and are happy they can
 communicate in Welsh if they prefer as there are Welsh speaking staff.

The environment is good because the layout suits the people who live here. A maintenance person

Leadership and management is good because there are systems in place to ensure continued

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. Care planning information includes
 details about people’s preferences and choices on how their care is to be delivered. Choice is
 evidenced at mealtimes; although the menu shows the main option of the day, people can select
 alternatives and we saw this was provided. The cook knows people well so can prepare their
 favourite meals. One relative shared how the cook makes their mother her favourite deserts
 because they know she will enjoy it. Choice is evident in people’s uptake of the meaningful
 activities arranged for them. The activities organiser takes time to read through ‘This is me’
 documents to ascertain people’s interests so she can tailor activities to suit everyone. One person
 unable to engage in activities with others, likes the activities organiser to read from the Bible at her
 bedside and have hand massages. Photographs evidence people’s enjoyment of an excellent
 range of activities including interactive games that encourage movement, arts and craft and baking.
 One resident recently won an art competition judged by the local mayor. The management team
 supports the Alzheimer’s Society Dementia Friend’s initiative and aims to share their newly
 developed onsite café with carers of people living with dementia in the local community.

 Relatives and staff consistently praised the level of dignity afforded to those living in the home and
 we observed positive interactions of genuine warmth and compassion. Care is described as very
 person centred, delivered according to people’s preferences. One person chooses to have beauty
 treatments and hair dressing from people they have used prior to living in the home.

 People are safe and protected from harm because they know who to speak with if they have any
 concerns. Visiting is unrestricted and people have privacy with their guests, therefore able to raise
 any concerns if they had any. All the people we spoke with had nothing but praise for the care.
 They enjoy the companionship provided by the staff and the friends and family who visit.

 The accommodation supports people’s well-being outcomes as there is space to do what they
 wish. A choice of lounges affords peace and privacy with visitors and a space for socialising with
 each other. A secure garden provides opportunities to sit in the sun or shade in the shelter
 provided.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive the quality of care they need to keep them well and help them live their life as they
wish. Care planning is comprehensive and considers people’s wishes and needs. Plans are kept up
to date as they are reviewed monthly. People know the carers well because some have worked at
the service for years. Where agency staff are used, the service endeavours to use the same people
so residents are supported by staff with whom they are familiar. Relevant agencies are consulted to
ensure the most appropriate care and support is provided including the provision of mobility aids
and guidance from mental health services.

Records show people’s health and wellbeing is closely monitored. Weight, nutrition and skin
integrity records are kept, guidance from health professionals is recorded and referrals to other
services are made when required. Oral healthcare is reflected in care plans, included in personal
care audits and supported through staff training and supervision discussions. There are Welsh
speaking staff working in the home so people can converse in their first language of either Welsh or
English. People told us they are happy with the care, staff are kind and caring and they feel they
live their life how they want to. Similarly, relatives praised staff for their kindness and how safe
people feel in their care. We saw evidence of genuine compassion in records of daily activities;
regarding one person’s engagement and enjoyment of a particular fun activity, one staff had written
‘it was good to see her beautiful smile’ and referring to someone as a ‘lovely lady’. One relative
shared how staff moved their mother’s furniture in her room after they noticed she kept turning
herself towards the window, and her bed is now positioned so she can see outside.

People are protected from abuse and harm because staff have had safeguarding training and are
guided by safeguarding policies. The premises is kept secure, with visitors having to sign in and
there are few falls because people in the lounge are always supervised. Relatives told us they can
visit at any time without appointment. There is an additional small lounge and a tearoom for people
to talk to their relatives in privacy.

The management of medication is taken seriously with audits completed in house every month and
separate checks undertaken by the community pharmacist. Only trained staff administer the
medication, and regular competency checks are carried out on the process. We saw accurate
administration records and that drug stock balance is routinely checked.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment with appropriate, maintained facilities. The environment is homely,
warm and clean, and nicely decorated. Each bedroom is personalised to some degree, including
photographs and other things that matter to the individual. There were two shared bedrooms when
we visited which exceeded the ratio specified in the regulations. However, one other bedroom was
unoccupied, so this was swiftly resolved. There are two lounges to choose from, and a room
designed as a café where people can take refreshments with their relatives if they wish. Most
people take their meals from overbed/chair tables where they are seated, although we saw
photographs of dining tables arranged in the lounge for celebratory meals on special day such as
birthdays and Mother’s Day. There is a shower on each floor. We saw an additional shower room
needs refurbishing and flooring in the first-floor corridors need replacing; the manager confirmed
this work is planned. The garden provides a secure space for people to sit out in good weather.
Two shelters provide shade from the sun if needed.

The home is kept safe and records evidenced all safety checks and inspections of gas, electricity,
fire, water, lifts and other equipment have been carried out at the required frequency. Inhouse
checks are also carried out on water safety, fire safety and other health and safety areas.
Recommendations previously made by the Food Standards Agency have been completed, and the
provider is eager to ensure compliance and safety for all. The maintenance person is prompt to
resolve any repairs or renewals required, and environment health and safety risk assessments are
carried out.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to stay well and live how they wish because staff are guided by
comprehensive care plans and know them well. Records evidence management monitor
compliance of practice, processes and the building to keep people safe. Continued interaction
between staff and those they support ensures requests and preferences are heard and met quickly.
We spoke with residents and relatives who feel they are listened to and that their wishes are
respected. The RI (responsible individual for the home) visits at least monthly and carried out their
own checks; they also complete biannual quality of care reviews and report on what the service is
doing well and where improvements could be made. They have good oversight of the service.

Staff are safely recruited because they are vetted prior to employment. References are sought and
criminal record checks are carried out. All staff undergo an induction period, shadowing more
experienced staff while they complete mandatory training. A range of training is provided; records
show this is ongoing and many staff are due to attend further refresher training. All staff have
completed moving and handling including practical training and the majority have received first aid
training.

Supervision and appraisal meetings with staff, together with an open-door policy and regular staff
meetings, provide plenty of opportunity to discuss work, concerns and career plans. Staff know the
whistleblowing policy and what they need to do if they are unhappy with any aspect of residents’
care. A policy of the month is posted on the notice board, and all staff must sign to confirm they
have reviewed these. The manager and management team have undertaken additional training in
dementia to gain the expertise they require to meet people’s needs and are disseminating this to
other staff.

Staff are happy working for the service. They shared they do not feel rushed and do feel listened to.
For example, we read a copy of the team minutes in which staff had suggested additional staff in
the morning would be beneficial and this was arranged. They feel their work is acknowledged and
the newly implemented ‘employee of the month’ initiative will reinforce this. One staff shared how
the manager commended them for their attributes and suggested their skills and personality would
be suited to providing meaningful engagement and activities for people living here, a role they
thoroughly enjoy. All staff spoken with describe the RI and manager as approachable, saying they
can speak to them at any time.

 Areas identified for improvement
 Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
 immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
 action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


 Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
 people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
 Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
 improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
 and outcomes for people have improved.


 CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
