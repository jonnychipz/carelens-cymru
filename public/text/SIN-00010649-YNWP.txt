Summary:

 People experience good well-being outcomes; they are treated with dignity and respect and have
 activities available to them. People are supported to stay as healthy as possible, with systems in
 place to protect people in an environment that meets their needs. People are treated as individuals
 and can live their lives how they wish.

 Care and support are good. Detailed care documentation is in place to support people’s needs. The
 service has good systems to safeguard people, and good systems to reduce the risk of the spread
 of infection. Care staff treat people with kindness and compassion. Personal plans are an accurate
 reflection of the individual and people receive appropriate care and support.

 The environment is good and meets people’s needs and outcomes, enhances their well-being, and
 is safe. Sandford is well decorated throughout and has enough communal space. People’s rooms,
 as well as communal areas, are kept clean and free from hazards. People can personalise their

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have choice and control over their daily lives. Care workers support people to make
 everyday decisions. They understand people’s preferences and know what is important to them.
 The provider undertakes detailed pre-admission assessments and receives information from health
 care professionals prior to people moving into the home. People’s personal plans focus on their
 likes and dislikes and are goals and outcomes focused. We saw people have a positive experience
 at mealtimes, their dignity is respected, and alternative meal options and meal sizes are offered. At
 breakfast we saw people having several different options of their choice. A relative told us “The
 food is exceptional…they have roast dinners. My relative has two breakfasts.” People confirmed
 they or their relative receive good support which enhances their experiences throughout the day,
 which we witnessed. One relative told us “Staff are really good, some are amazing. We were
 looking for a home for our relative closer to us however we don’t want to move them as they are
 happy and settled.” Another told us, “My relatives health has improved significantly since being
 here.” Whilst a relative said “Nowhere is perfect but this is as perfect as  it can get.”  People’s
 independence is encouraged as they are offered choice wherever possible. People have their own
 personal routines and engage  in  activities organised by both the  activities co-ordinator and
 managers. External entertainers also visit the home on a regular basis. People told us they enjoy
 the activities, which was confirmed by what we observed. One relative told us, “There is enough
 going on, there are always activities going on, they are always varied and have themed days.”

 People are protected from harm and neglect. Care workers receive training in safeguarding, to
 protect vulnerable adults and appropriate training to support people effectively. There are also
 policies and procedures in place to support this. Care workers have a good understanding of their
 responsibilities and know how to report any concerns they have. Personal plans and risk
 assessments are in place and reviewed regularly. People told us that they are happy and feel safe
 in their home. One person said, “They look after me really well. If I’m not happy, I will speak to the
 manager. Staff here are marvellous…I am very happy here.” The manager is visible during the
 day, is aware of people’s requirements and speaks with them regularly. Recruitment of care
 workers is robust.

 People are supported to maintain their physical and emotional well-being. There is a positive
 culture amongst care staff. There is a consistent staff team which provides good continuity of
 support for people. We saw friendships formed with people enjoying time in the lounges and dining
 area. People told us, managers, including the deputy manager and manager are “Approachable”
 and “Very supportive.” Individual personal plans are in place which reflect their choices and how to
 maintain their safety.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are protected from abuse and harm. People are supported to be safe and have risk
assessments in place where needed. People we spoke with confirmed they feel safe at Sandford. A
professional told us, “I have no safeguarding concerns or issues to raise. It is busy here and people
are not sat around in a circle after breakfast which is a good sign.” Whilst a relative said “I give this
place 10/10. They are fabulous and take good care of my relative…If I had a problem I could go to
the manager, he is very approachable.” The provider understands the legal requirements of caring
for vulnerable people and makes referrals to the local authority safeguarding team when required.

People experience good care and support which ensures they meet their well-being outcomes.
Care is outcomes focused and delivered in a way which promotes the development of routine and
structures in line with people’s preferences. Personal plans are goals and outcomes based, give a
clear picture of the person, and are reviewed regularly. Care staff follow these to ensure good
quality person-centred care and support is delivered. People’s outcomes are clear, and they are
supported by care staff to ensure their outcomes and goals are met.

Throughout the inspection, people’s requests for support were met promptly with good quality care
being delivered throughout the day and care staff responded to call bells in a timely way. We
observed positive interactions between people and care staff, who clearly know the people they
support, this was confirmed by those we spoke with. People are well kempt and treated with dignity
and respect. This was also confirmed by what we saw and what people told us.

People are referred for appropriate appointments at the right time and recommendations for care
and treatment by other professionals are conducted as directed. This was confirmed by people we
spoke with as well as records we viewed. We saw personal plans are updated with any changes in
health conditions and there are clear protocols to follow to maintain people’s health and wellbeing.
Health monitoring, such as food and fluid charts, are completed in line with personal plans.
People’s medication is safely managed, administered as prescribed and stored securely. Only
appropriately trained care staff can administer medication.

The provider is working towards the Active offer of the Welsh Language. Personal plans, and other
documentation can be translated into Welsh upon request. People’s language preferences are
discussed prior to moving into the home and some Welsh speaking care staff are employed.

       Environment                         Good



Sandford is situated on the outskirts of Prestatyn town centre, in a residential area. The home is set
over two floors with two lifts provided. Sandford is clean, comfortable and provides an environment
where people’s well-being and outcomes are met. The decoration and flooring in both people’s
rooms and communal areas are of a high standard. Improvements to the service have been made
and include parts of the communal areas which have recently been re-decorated and re-equipped,
some bedrooms have been improved, and the dining room has been renovated. There are
communal areas where people spend time in the company of others, alone, or people can remain
in their own room should they wish to have privacy. People have their own rooms and can
personalise their room to their preference with their own belongings, which helps people to feel at
home. Communal areas, bathrooms, and toilet facilities are kept clean. Externally there is a
courtyard which has been improved which people can use. It is secure and is a positive
environment which people can enjoy.

People live in an environment which is safe, with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and
equipment. There are safe and effective systems in place to maintain and manage the
accommodation to consistently meet people’s needs. Regular servicing and environmental checks,
such as checks of electrics, gas and water are undertaken to ensure the safety and well-being of
people using the service. Care staff are trained in fire safety, and regular fire checks are
undertaken, to keep people as safe as possible. We also saw a schedule of planned works for the
home and saw evidence that works had and were to be conducted and that significant investment
has been made to the home. Investment and improvement is ongoing.

Sandford has been awarded a score of five by the Food Standards Agency, the highest score
available which suggests standards of cleanliness and hygiene in the kitchen are very good. The
housekeeping team follow cleaning schedules to ensure the home is kept clean and tidy, which we
observed it is. Laundry facilities are suitable for the size of the home and there are systems in place
to reduce the infection control risks. People we spoke with told us both clothes and rooms are kept
clean.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People live at a service which is well organised and has good leadership oversight. The provider
has established a stable team of care staff who have daily support from a manager, deputy
manager and Responsible Individual (RI). A regional manager also visits the service regularly. One
member of staff told us the staff team is really good. Another carer told us “I have worked here for
several years, and I love it very much….it is a great team, and we always help each other out.
Morale is good and the manager is amazing; he encourages us to speak to him.” There are regular
meetings between the manager and the staff team to keep them informed. Care staff and people at
the service gave positive feedback about the manager, saying they are “Supportive and
approachable.” Another member of the care staff team told us the manager was “Really good,” and
the deputy manager was “Fantastic.” These positive sentiments about managers at Sandford was
also reflected in conversations had with relatives.

There are effective systems in place to monitor quality. This is conducted through managers
comprehensive audits of different areas of the service undertaken regularly, visits by the RI, and
Regional Manager, and gaining feedback from people and their representatives through the
service’s own quality assurance questionnaire. The RI creates reports following visits to evidence
what areas they have looked at and what actions are needed. There are policies and procedures in
place to guide staff in how to support people effectively and safely, and these are reviewed and
updated as required. People and staff can be assured they have access to relevant information.

People are supported by care staff who are safely recruited, well trained and professionally
registered. Pre employment checks are conducted before a person begins working at the service,
which includes a disclosure and barring service (DBS) check and obtaining appropriate references.
This is to ensure people are safe and appropriate to work with adults at risk. Staff complete an
induction, and all staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. We reviewed
training records and found staff complete mandatory training in several areas including dementia
awareness, moving and handling, positive behaviour support, safeguarding, and medication. All
staff receive regular one to one supervision and appraisals. This helps identify any training needs,
performance, and concerns to support ongoing development and ensure people receive the
support they require. Care staff confirm training they receive is appropriate for the people they
support. Care staff are employed in appropriate numbers; this was confirmed by records we saw,
staffing numbers on the day of inspection, and what stakeholders told us.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
