Summary:

 Heddfan provides care and support for people with learning disabilities. The service offers
 accommodation for up to five people and is located in the town of Merthyr Tydfil. The home is
 within walking distance of a range of local amenities, services, and community facilities, enabling
 people to access and participate in community life.

 People’s well-being outcomes are excellent. Staff provide person-centred care and support that
 promotes people’s rights, choice, and independence. They have a thorough understanding of the
 people they support, including what is important to them and the most effective approaches to
 meeting their needs. Staff have developed positive and trusting relationships with people, enabling
 them to deliver care that is responsive and tailored to personal preferences. Care documentation is
 outcome-focused, clearly identifying people’s goals, aspirations, and the support required to help
 them achieve the best possible outcomes.

 Leadership and management is good. The service is well led and promotes a positive, person-
 centred culture that places the well-being of both people receiving support and staff at its core.
 Effective leadership and management arrangements ensure the service operates smoothly, with
 governance and quality assurance systems in place to monitor performance, drive continuous

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People’s views at Heddfan are actively sought, valued, and used to inform the care and support
 they receive. People are encouraged to make choices about their daily lives and are supported to
 exercise as much control as possible over decisions that affect them. People personalise their
 bedrooms to reflect their preferences and individuality and are actively involved in planning their
 routines, meals, and activities. The service promotes meaningful participation in a wide range of
 educational, vocational, and leisure opportunities that reflect people’s interests, aspirations, and
 goals. We saw positive examples of people being supported to engage with their local community,
 including one person attending a weekly work placement at a local McDonald’s and another
 undertaking a volunteering role with St John’s Ambulance. These opportunities help people
 develop skills, build confidence, and maintain valued community connections. On the day of the
 inspection, we observed people actively planning an upcoming holiday, demonstrating how the
 service supports people to make decisions, pursue personal interests, and enjoy experiences that
 enhance their well-being and quality of life.


 People are treated with dignity, respect, and kindness and have developed excellent relationships
 with care staff. During our inspection, we observed numerous positive interactions between staff
 and the people they support. Staff communicated in a warm and respectful manner, taking time to
 listen to people and respond to their wishes and preferences. Our observations demonstrated staff
 hold people in high regard and are committed to promoting their rights, independence, and well-
 being. People spoke positively about their relationships with staff and expressed high levels of
 satisfaction with the support they receive. Comments included, “The staff are all very good, I love
 living here” and “I love it here because I like the people I live with and the staff.”


 People are protected from harm, abuse, and neglect. Staff are recruited in accordance with
 regulatory requirements, with all the required pre-employment checks undertaken. Staff receive a
 comprehensive programme of training relevant to the needs of the people they support. Training
 includes key areas such as safeguarding, medication management, and the Mental Capacity Act,
 equipping staff with the knowledge and skills required to provide safe and effective care and
 support. The service has established effective mechanisms to enable people to raise concerns,
 express their views, and provide feedback about the support they receive. Staff understand their
 safeguarding responsibilities and are confident in responding to concerns appropriately.


 People live in an environment which supports their well-being. There are sufficient communal areas
 which are clean and comfortable. People’s rooms are personalised to their preference. The home is

       Care & Support                       Good



Personal plans contain information to enable staff to provide people with care and support that is
tailored to their individual needs, preferences, and wishes. The plans are person-centred and
clearly identify people’s personal outcomes and aspirations, along with guidance on how staff can
best support them to achieve their outcomes. We found personal plans are reviewed in line with
regulatory requirements, ensuring information remains reflective of people’s changing needs and
circumstances. The service uses a range of methods to evidence progress towards personal
outcomes. For example, photographs of trips, activities, and daily living tasks are incorporated into
reviews, providing meaningful and accessible evidence of people’s achievements, experiences,
and participation in activities that are important to them. This approach helps to celebrate
successes and demonstrates how people are being supported to achieve their desired outcomes.

People are supported to maintain their health. People’s health needs are clearly detailed within
their personal plans, providing staff with the information they need to deliver appropriate care and
support. The service maintains comprehensive records of healthcare involvement. Appointments
with healthcare professionals are documented, and relevant medical correspondence is retained on
file, ensuring information is accessible and contributes to effective continuity of care. Appropriate
support is available for people with medication needs. We found medication is stored securely and
administered in accordance with prescribers’ instructions. The service has a robust medication
policy in place, and staff receive training to ensure they are competent to manage and administer
medication safely. In addition, regular medication audits are undertaken to monitor practice, identify
any issues, and ensure medication management remains safe, effective, and in line with best
practice.


Care  staff receive training in infection prevention and control (IP&C) to minimise the risk of
infection. IP&C forms part of the service’s mandatory training programme, ensuring staff have the
knowledge and skills required to protect the health and well-being of the people they support. In
addition, the service has an infection prevention and control policy in place, which provides staff
with guidance on safe working practices. We completed a visual assessment of the environment
and identified no concerns in relation to cleanliness or infection control. The home is clean, tidy,
and well maintained throughout. We also found the service had an adequate supply of personal
protective equipment (PPE), which staff use appropriately to reduce the risk of cross-contamination
and help maintain a safe environment.

       Environment                         Good



People have access to a range of communal areas where they can socialise with others and
participate in activities. These include a main lounge and dining area, a kitchen, and quiet lounge.
All communal spaces are clean, comfortable, well maintained, and suitably decorated, creating a
welcoming atmosphere for people living at the home. People have their own bedrooms, which are
personalised to reflect their individual tastes, preferences, and interests. Some bedrooms benefit
from en-suite facilities, while accessible communal bathrooms are available for those who do not
have en-suite facilities. The home also has a safe and accessible outdoor area, providing additional
space for relaxation and leisure activities. People spoke positively about the environment and told
us they were happy living at the home. Comments included, “I love my bedroom and the rest of the
house” and “The house is lovely.”

The service demonstrates a commitment to maintaining the environment and its facilities to a good
standard. We found appropriate arrangements are in place to ensure the premises are safe, well
maintained, and fit for purpose. Up-to-date safety certification was available for key utilities and
systems, including gas, electricity, and fire safety equipment, providing assurance that essential
safety checks are completed as required. There is a current fire risk assessment in place, and
regular fire drills are conducted to ensure both people living at the home and staff understand what
action to take in the event of an emergency. These measures help to promote people’s safety and
preparedness. The kitchen has been awarded a Food Standards Agency food hygiene rating of
three, indicating hygiene standards are generally satisfactory. In addition, staff follow established
cleaning schedules to ensure the home remains clean, tidy, and comfortable for the people who
live there. Our observations confirmed the environment is well maintained and systems are in place
to support the ongoing safety and cleanliness of the home.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider’s oversight and governance arrangements promote a positive culture and support the
delivery of good-quality care and support. The Responsible Individual (RI) undertakes regular visits
to the service to maintain oversight of service provision and to ensure the service is operating in
accordance with its aims, objectives, and regulatory requirements. A quality-of-care review is
completed every six months to evaluate the performance of the service. We reviewed the most
recent quality of care report and found it provided a balanced assessment of the service, identifying
areas of strength alongside opportunities for improvement. This reflective approach supports
continuous improvement and helps to ensure the service continues to meet the needs of the people
it supports.

The service has robust recruitment and vetting procedures in place to ensure staff are suitable,
competent, and safe to work with vulnerable people. Personnel records we reviewed demonstrated
all required pre-employment checks have been completed. These checks included obtaining
references from previous employers, verifying employment histories, and completing Disclosure
and Barring Service (DBS) checks. Newly appointed staff are required to complete a structured
induction programme, which helps them develop an understanding of their role, the service’s
values, and the care and support needs of the people they support. We saw all care staff were
registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator for the social care sector in Wales. This
provides assurance staff meet the required professional standards and are accountable for their
practice.

People achieve positive outcomes because the provider is committed to ensuring skilled,
knowledgeable, and competent staff are available to meet their needs. During our inspection, we
observed sufficient staffing levels. Staff we spoke with told us they felt staffing levels were
appropriate. Staff receive training relevant to the needs of the people living at the home, ensuring
they have the skills and knowledge required to deliver safe, effective, and person-centred care.
Training records show staff are up to date with their training requirements. Staff also receive
regular supervision and support, helping them to reflect on their practice, develop their skills, and
maintain good standards of care. Feedback from staff indicated they felt valued and well supported
by the management team. One member of staff told us, “As a member of staff I feel fully supported
on a daily basis whether it is work related or a personal issue.” Another said, “I feel very supported
by the home manager and fellow team members.” These comments, together with our observations
and a review of records, demonstrate a positive and supportive working culture where staff feel
empowered to perform their roles effectively. This contributes to improved outcomes for people,
who benefit from care and support provided by a stable, skilled, and motivated workforce.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
