Summary:

 Duffryn House is a care home which supports people with a learning disability and other associated
 needs. It is located in a residential area of Aberdare.


 Well-being is excellent because people achieve outstanding outcomes important to them, and are
 supported to live meaningful, independent and fulfilling lives. Strong relationships, positive risk-
 taking and excellent community involvement help people maintain choice and control. Care and
 support is good because people receive personalised, outcome-focused care from skilled care staff
 who understand their needs and preferences. Effective safeguarding, medication management and
 infection prevention arrangements help keep people safe. The environment is good because
 people live in a comfortable, safe and personalised home which meets their needs and promotes
 their well-being. Leadership and management is good because effective governance, oversight and
 quality assurance systems support positive outcomes for people. A stable, well-trained and well-
 supported staff team provide consistent care.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 Well-being is excellent because people achieve outstanding outcomes which are important to them.
 People live healthy, safe and fulfilling lives with extensive support to achieve their aspirations.
 People make meaningful choices about their daily lives and positive risk-taking  is embedded
 throughout the service, creating a variety of opportunities. We saw people engaged in various
 activities  tailored  to  their  interests and  maintaining  strong community connections through
 volunteering and social opportunities. These opportunities have considerably improved people's
 confidence and  well-being. Care  staff know people exceptionally  well and understand  their
 communication needs, emotional well-being and how best to support them. There is very strong
 partnership working  with  health  professionals, which helps manage and  maintain  people’s
 emotional well-being and supporting them to remain in their home.


 People are protected by strong safeguarding arrangements which promote their safety and well-
 being. People told us they feel safe and know who to speak to if they are ever worried. Care staff
 recognise changes  in people's well-being and respond promptly when concerns  arise.  Staff
 understand  safeguarding  responsibilities,  reporting processes and  escalation  arrangements.
 Robust  risk management systems help keep people  safe. Management maintains  effective
 oversight through electronic monitoring systems, audits and incident reviews. Measures to promote
 learning from incidents is very strong and embedded at the service.


 People experience positive relationships and meaningful  lives because  their  identity,  history,
 strengths and individuality are recognised and valued. There is a strong culture which promotes
 equality,  diversity and  inclusion. People maintain important family relationships and develop
 friendships and social networks within their communities. Community integration is a significant
 strength and people remain active members of their local area through volunteering, activities and
 community engagement. Robust financial management arrangements provide a good balance
 between protecting people and promoting financial independence.


 People live in accommodation which supports their well-being outcomes. The home is welcoming,
 personalised and reflects people's preferences and interests. Bedrooms are personalised and
 people tell us they are happy with their home. The property is well maintained and supported by
 health and  safety arrangements. People  benefit from an  accessible garden and  frequent
 opportunities to access wider community spaces, with the home well-located to local amenities.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care and support is good because people receive personalised care and support which meets their
needs, promotes their outcomes and helps keep them safe. People receive care and support from
care staff who know them well and understand their needs, preferences and aspirations. Personal
plans are person-centred, detailed and focused on achieving outcomes important to people. They
provide clear guidance for care staff on supporting people and managing risks. Plans are co-
produced through reviews, keyworker meetings and family involvement. People have timely access
to healthcare professionals and specialist services. Detailed risk assessments and behaviour
support plans are reviewed regularly, and daily records demonstrate people’s progress. We saw
warm, respectful and natural interactions between care staff and people, who appeared relaxed
and comfortable in their home. People told us “I’m happy – I can do everything I want” and “They
absolutely love living at the home and staff go above and beyond. Nothing is too much trouble for
them.”


People are protected by a positive safeguarding culture. Care staff understand their safeguarding
responsibilities. People told us they feel safe living at the service. Incident management systems
are appropriate and subject to management oversight. Risks are managed through detailed risk
assessments,  behaviour  support  plans and  specialist  guidance. We found  restrictions  are
proportionate and people's independence is actively promoted. Consent processes are in place,
and people are involved in decisions about their care and support wherever possible.


People’s medication is managed safely through robust systems and effective oversight. Care staff
complete  six-monthly competency assessments  to  help ensure  safe  practice and  receive
medication training more generally. Medication administration records are generally accurate and
completed  appropriately. Medicines  are  stored  securely and  safely, and arrangements  for
controlled drugs are appropriate. PRN ‘as needed’ medication protocols provide clear guidance for
care staff and outcomes are recorded. Strong auditing arrangements help maintain good standards
and systems ensure care staff are informed promptly of any changes to people’s medicines.


People are protected from the risk of infection because the service adheres to established infection
prevention and control practices. The home is clean and tidy throughout, and appropriate cleaning
materials and infection control supplies are readily available. Care staff are knowledgeable about
infection prevention and control procedures and training compliance for infection prevention and
food hygiene is strong. Effective auditing arrangements help maintain standards and identify any
issues promptly.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is good because people live in a safe, comfortable and well-maintained home
which supports their independence, privacy and well-being. The service meets people’s needs and
supports positive outcomes. Bedrooms are large, spacious and personalised to reflect individual
preferences, helping people feel comfortable and at home. People told us they are happy with their
rooms. There are a variety of communal spaces, including lounges and dining areas, which
enables people to spend time socialising with others or enjoying quiet time independently. The
environment  is accessible  for the people currently  living at the service. The garden  is well
maintained, accessible and actively used by people, providing opportunities to spend time outdoors
and enjoy fresh air.


The home is tidy, uncluttered and generally well maintained, creating a welcoming and comfortable
environment for people and visitors. The service is appropriately lit and maintained at a comfortable
temperature. Effective security arrangements help keep people safe while supporting their privacy
and dignity. Personal information is stored securely, helping protect people's confidentiality.


The provider has effective systems in place to maintain the environment and manage risks to
health and safety. Maintenance, servicing and health and safety records are up to date. Fire safety
arrangements are well managed and support the safety of people living at the home. Weekly water
temperature monitoring and flushing arrangements are completed to reduce environmental risks.
Contingency plans are available to support continuity of care during significant service disruptions.
These arrangements help ensure people live in a safe environment where risks are identified,
monitored and managed appropriately.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management  is good because  effective governance, oversight and  staffing
arrangements support people to achieve positive outcomes and receive consistent care. The
service benefits from effective leadership, governance and quality assurance arrangements. Three-
monthly Responsible  Individual  (RI)  visits  to the service are robust. As well as evidencing
consultation with people and staff, the RI visit reports include audits, observations, record reviews
and action planning. They also identify areas for improvement as well as strengths, helping drive
service development. A wide range of audits are completed at the service, including medication,
safeguarding, infection control, and health and safety. The use of audit systems provides ongoing
oversight and supports  effective governance. Information sharing between the  staff team  is
effective. Team meetings take place  regularly and include discussions about safeguarding,
medication, staffing, and updates on people's care needs. Policies and procedures are reviewed
and up  to  date.  Safeguarding  concerns,  incidents and  disciplinary  matters  are managed
appropriately, and there is positive evidence of learning from incidents. Regular engagement with
families, keyworker reviews and staff meetings help ensure feedback is gathered and acted upon.
Regulatory documentation, including the Statement of Purpose, is compliant.


People benefit from support provided by a skilled, knowledgeable and stable staff team. Training
compliance is high and training reflects the needs of people living at the service. Specialist training
courses include epilepsy management, autism awareness, positive behaviour support, and learning
disability support. All care staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator, and
receive regular supervision. We saw evidence of completed induction programmes, including the
All Wales Induction Framework. Recruitment processes are robust and staff files contain the
required documentation. Staffing levels appear appropriate. The service is able to draw upon the
wider organisation when additional staffing support is required, for example to cover annual leave
and sickness. This helps provide continuity of care for people. Care staff demonstrate a strong
understanding of people's needs and important areas of care such as safeguarding, behaviour
support and infection control procedures. Staff spoke positively about the support they receive from
managers. We were told “The manager is good. They help on the floor, and we can approach
them” and “The management team are very proactive and supportive of employees.” There is a
strong culture of staff development and internal promotion, with several staff progressing and
promoted within the organisation. Staff morale appeared positive overall, and people benefit from
continuity of care because staff turnover is low. Care staff described “the friendly and supportive
team” and another said, “Everyone works as a team and we’re friendly.”

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
