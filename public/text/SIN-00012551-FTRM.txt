Summary:

 Llannerch Grange is in a quiet residential area, within walking distance of the beach and local
 shops and amenities.

 People experience excellent well-being because they receive highly personalised support that
 promotes choice, independence and positive relationships. They shape their routines, pursue
 interests at home and in the community, and take positive risks such as volunteering, work, sport,
 cooking, gardening and planning a holiday. People feel safe, know how to raise concerns and live
 in a homely environment which reflects their identities and gives them ownership.

 Care and support is good because staff understand people well, work with professionals and
 respond to changing needs. Personal plans and risk assessments are detailed, reviewed regularly
 and linked to people’s goals. Medicines, infection prevention and environmental safety are
 managed effectively.

 People live in a good environment because the home is comfortable, welcoming, well maintained
 and personalised. People contribute to its appearance and use the indoor and outdoor spaces in

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People have excellent control over their lives because support is highly personalised, aspirational
 and focused on what matters to them. They make meaningful choices about their day, are actively
 involved in decisions about safety and are empowered to pursue activities and goals that enhance
 their independence, confidence and well-being. Personal plans, activity planners and risk
 assessments strongly reflect people’s interests, strengths and aspirations. Staff promote a positive
 culture of independence and well-managed risk-taking, using interest profiles and monthly key-
 worker meetings to turn people’s ideas into achievable outcomes. This highly effective approach
 has led to significant outcomes, including increased independence, progress in sport and a return
 to employment through shared planning and proportionate risk assessment. People are extensively
 supported to live active and fulfilling lives. They play a meaningful role in shaping opportunities and
 contribute to daily life through cooking, gardening and practical projects. People work, shop, swim,
 attend martial arts classes, use local leisure facilities and enjoy theatre and cinema trips. These
 varied and enriching experiences support people to learn, develop and maintain a strong sense of
 purpose. People are also empowered to make informed health choices, including reducing
 medication gradually with staff and professional support where this reflects their wishes.

 People feel safe, listened to and confident their views are acted upon. They know how to raise
 concerns, and staff and the manager respond promptly when issues are shared. Staff understand
 safeguarding procedures and how to escalate concerns. Accessible information about complaints
 and deprivation of liberty safeguards helps people understand their rights. Individual and group
 meetings provide regular, meaningful opportunities for people to share views in ways that suit their
 communication needs.

 People are exceptionally well supported to develop and maintain positive relationships. They see
 family and friends, celebrate important events and join activities with people from other services run
 by the provider. Shared cooking, gaming and gardening promote companionship, equality and
 community within the home. We observed warm, respectful and humorous interactions between
 people and staff. Staff actively recognise people’s achievements, build confidence and purpose,
 and promote identity and culture. People can speak Welsh with fluent staff, and key-worker
 meetings are held in Welsh for those who prefer this.

 The environment significantly enhances people’s well-being. People’s rooms are highly
 personalised and decorated to their preferences. Communal areas and gardens include objects,
 pictures and ornaments that are important to people, and people influence how the home looks and
 feels. This creates a homely, personalised environment that reflects people’s identities and gives
 them a strong sense of ownership.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support which reflects their needs, preferences and personal
goals. Assessments and plans include detailed social, family, physical and mental health
information. Staff review plans through monthly key-worker meetings and regular formal reviews.
People contribute their views and we saw some people sign records to confirm they agree with
plans. The provider is working on ways to ensure records more clearly reflect how people co-
produce plans and how specialist input, training and learning improve each person’s experience
and sustained outcomes over time. Plans include employment, travel, sport, cooking, family contact
and greater community independence, rather than focusing only on safety. Staff know people well
and communicate changes through handovers, meetings and supervision.

Individualised risk assessments give staff clear guidance on how to support people. Behaviour
support records describe triggers, preventative approaches, responses and recovery strategies.
Staff adapt information to people’s communication needs. Examples include a communication
passport written in people’s own voice and risk plans using symbols and pictures. Professional
input includes GPs, social workers, psychologists, community mental health professionals and
deprivation of liberty representatives. During periods of mental ill-health, the provider ensures
specialist assessment, medication advice and consistent personalised support helps people
become settled and resume ordinary activities.

People are protected from harm and abuse. Staff receive training in how to safeguard people from
harm and can describe safeguarding, recording and escalation procedures confidently. Plans
address risks linked to exploitation, relationships, mental health, leaving the service and potential
harm to people and others. Records show appropriate liaison with safeguarding services and other
professionals. The provider also reviews incidents and shares organisational learning.

Medicines are managed safely. The records we sampled had no gaps. Staff use clear protocols for
medicines taken when required, complete regular stock checks and undertake regular audits. Staff
receive yearly training and competency assessments. The provider is introducing electronic
medicine records in stages to ensure staff remain competent and implementation is successful.

The home is clean and infection risks are managed effectively. Staff complete cleaning schedules,
hand-hygiene checks and monthly audits. Food hygiene practices are good and personal protective
equipment is available and used appropriately. People help with cooking, laundry and cleaning.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a good quality environment which supports privacy, independence and well-being.
People told us they feel comfortable and safe, can move around freely and have no concerns about
the home. Each person has a single en-suite bedroom personalised with photographs, books,
posters, ornaments and other belongings. Two lounges, a dining room and accessible outdoor
areas give people choices about where to spend time together or use quieter areas. Shared areas
are pleasantly decorated, and people influence future changes. The property feels homely and
comfortable. The garden provides space for planting, social events, relaxation and smoking, with
suitable paving, seating and controlled access.

People have a strong sense of ownership and influence how the environment develops. They have
made planters and seating for the front garden, where they grow vegetables and herbs, and display
ornaments that are meaningful to them. Following feedback, the manager has commissioned
individual portraits reflecting people’s personalities and choices. Photographs in shared areas
celebrate people’s interests, activities and achievements. People also use the environment to build
practical skills and enjoy meaningful activities. They plan meals, shop for ingredients, cook, help
clean shared areas, maintain bedrooms and complete laundry with support when needed.

The provider manages environmental and health and safety risks effectively. The manager
completes regular checks, the RI reviews arrangements during routine visits, and electronic audit
systems allow senior leaders to monitor the home remotely. Fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency
lighting and escape routes are checked and serviced regularly. Personal emergency evacuation
plans are available and reviewed. Current servicing records were available for gas appliances,
central heating, electrical installations and portable appliances. Routine checks include window
restrictors, water temperatures and general maintenance. The provider maintains the building, has
maintenance systems and daily checks in place, and keeps the home decorated to a good
standard in line with people’s wishes.

Security arrangements balance safety with people’s rights. The front door is unlocked during the
day, and staffing levels allow people to come and go with support in line with individual plans. The
door is secured at night to keep people safe. Relevant legal safeguards, risk assessments and
contingency plans are in place, including for emergencies, loss of utilities and threats to the
property.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management are good. The service has a positive, person-centred culture where
people’s choices and outcomes are central to practice. People know the manager and find leaders
approachable. Staff describe the manager and RI as visible, supportive and responsive, and say
their views and experience are valued. They describe strong teamwork across day and night staff,
feel able to raise concerns and receive support after difficult incidents. This helps people receive
consistent, respectful care.

Governance systems provide effective oversight of key areas of service delivery. Monthly
management reports cover safeguarding, incidents, medicines, legal restrictions, staffing, training,
complaints, audits, notifications, premises and health and safety. The RI visits regularly, meets
people and staff, reviews care records, safeguarding issues, incidents, complaints, training and fire
safety, and checks the environment. Routine audits cover infection prevention, staffing, care
records, activities and health and safety, with actions recorded where needed. Electronic systems
allow senior leaders to monitor the service between visits, and quality of care reviews provide
further assurance twice a year.

Leaders have systems in place to gather feedback and inform service improvements. People share
views through resident meetings, key-worker sessions, questionnaires and direct contact with the
manager and RI. Staff meetings consider people’s goals, current needs and ideas for development.
The provider also compares practice with findings from external and regulatory inspections of its
other services and involves staff in improvement ideas. Recent developments include making
people’s achievements more visible and commissioning personalised portraits.

Leaders respond appropriately to risk and changing needs. When the manager is absent, senior
staff lead the home, with additional support from regional managers and the RI. Incidents are
reviewed through governance meetings and lessons are shared across the provider’s services,
although evidence of how wider learning changes outcomes for people at this service could be
clearer. The RI is working on improving how feedback and learning is incorporated into action
planning on improvements as part of their quality of care reviews.

Staffing arrangements meet people’s needs. Rotas show no gaps, the workforce is very stable, and
the service rarely uses agency staff. Recruitment records show required identity, reference,
disclosure barring service and registration checks, alongside induction, probation and mentoring for
new staff. Staff receive regular supervision and annual appraisals. Staff training reflects people’s
needs and includes safeguarding, medicines, positive behaviour support, de-escalation, mental
health, dysphagia, first aid and record-keeping. Practical training helps staff apply least restrictive
approaches. Reminders are sent when refresher training is due, and staff told us they feel
confident, skilled and well supported by colleagues, the manager and the RI.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
