Summary:

 People are well supported in Pembroke Lodge and receive consistent care from a stable staff
 group. The rating for well-being in the home is “good” as people have a voice and choices in their
 care. People can access activities and socialisation and are supported to stay in touch with family
 and friends.

 People receive care which is planned according to their individual needs. The rating for care and
 support is “good” as people have good health monitoring and can access health care advice in a
 timely manner. Medicine processes are sound and are regularly audited. Accidents and incidents
 are appropriately reported to Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW), and local authorities. Processes are
 put in place to mitigate further risks.

 The environment in the home is rated as “good”. This is because maintenance work has been
 carried out, and is ongoing, to improve the environment for people’s comfort and safety. Safety
 checks are in place to ensure people’s health and safety and to ensure equipment used for
 people’s care is safe to use.

 The Responsible Individual, (RI) visits the home regularly to ensure good standards are
 maintained. Leadership and management are rated as “good” for Pembroke Lodge as there is good

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People’s rights are protected in the home as they have a voice and daily choices. Personal plans
 show evidence of person-centred care. People’s personal choices and preferred routines are
 carefully recorded. Personal plans are on a PC system and are kept up to date, staff can input care
 as they give it making it a contemporary record. People can access certain activities and
 entertainment to provide them with socialisation and stimulation. Risk assessments are in place for
 each person and are kept up to date to mitigate risks and keep people safe. Care staff and nurses
 are up to date with safeguarding training and are aware of local protocols to protect people from
 abuse and neglect. Menus seen demonstrate choice and variety. We saw people can access
 regular snacks and drinks. A pictorial menu is on the dining room wall to aid people with their
 selections. We observed people were up and dressed in the lounge and were attended to by staff.
 People and staff have a good rapport, and people are treated with dignity and respect. People can
 go out and about with assistance. People are enabled to remain in touch with friends and family.
 They can sit outside in good weather as the grounds of the home are regularly maintained.
 People’s first language choices and day to day preferences are recorded in their personal plans.
 We saw positive responses to the RI’s quality of care survey from families with comments such as,
 “great care” and “my family are very grateful for the care given.” We saw the provider has improved
 the environment in the 3 years since they purchased the home, this is an ongoing process. The
 rooms seen were compliant to regulations, clean, and people can personalise them if they wish.
 Health and safety safeguards are in place.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care which is planned according to their individual needs. Senior staff assess
people prior to admission into the home to ensure the home can meet their needs and objectives.
Personal plans show good health monitoring and people can access health appointments in a
timely way. Plans are updated regularly and as people’s conditions change. People’s
communication needs are recorded including any sensory needs such as hearing aids and first
language choices. People’s preferred outcomes and routines are recorded including how these are
to be met. Specialist needs are also recorded in Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs),
to ensure people’s safety. Core risk assessments are made, and regularly updated, to enable
people’s health and safety.

People experience continuity in their care. The home has a full complement of staff and rarely uses
agency staff meaning care staff, nurses and people know each other well. We observed care staff
and nurses can anticipate people’s needs and deliver care sensitively. The service is working
towards the Welsh language offer. Methods of offering reassurance to alleviate anxiety are
included in personal plans to ensure people can access care appropriate to their needs.

The service works well with local authorities and CIW. Quality markers such as falls are audited to
ensure good standards and mitigate risks. The service reports falls, incidents and anything which
disrupts the smooth running of the service appropriately to CIW and local authorities.

There are good systems for storing and administering medications in the home. Medicines are
audited to ensure good practice. The service can access good stocks of prescribed medications for
people in a timely manner.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment which meets their needs and objectives. Work has been completed
to improve the home, which is ongoing. The hygiene rating for the kitchen has gone from 1 to 4 due
to staff’s diligent work and new fridges and equipment have been purchased for the kitchen. Staff
receive training in food hygiene in line with legislation. Health and safety risk assessments are in
place and are updated regularly. People can access the equipment needed for their care. The lift
and equipment used for care are regularly serviced. Utility certificates and home insurance are in
date. The home presents as clean and tidy and walkways and fire exits are free from obstructions.
People’s rooms are compliant to the regulations and can be personalised. Fire bells are tested
weekly and staff receive training to evacuate people safely. We saw the trees in the car park have
been pruned back and there are areas for people to sit out in good weather. There is a
maintenance person employed to ensure the upkeep and maintenance of the home. Housekeeping
staff are employed to ensure the home is clean and has good infection control. Entrances to the
home are locked and people sign in and out for accountability and a record in the event of a fire or
emergency of any extra people within the home. There are car parking areas to the front of the
home with sufficient space for ambulances if required.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has good governance arrangements in place to ensure the smooth running of the
service. The RI visits regularly and considers the views of residents, family and staff regarding the
service. The RI provides a quality report regarding the home in line with the requirements of the
legislation. The RI considers people’s well-being, the care and support provided, environmental
needs and maintenance and managerial issues such as staffing requirements, training and
supervision. The manager feels well supported by the RI and can access training required for their
role. Service audits conducted by senior staff are discussed with the RI and any actions to be taken
to maintain good care standards. People have information regarding the service prior to using the
service, this information is frequently reviewed to ensure its accuracy.

Nurses and care staff are safely recruited. We saw from staff records appropriate employment
checks are in place and in date to ensure they are appropriate to work with adults at risk. Checks
are made to ensure nurses and care staff are qualified and registered with the relevant governing
body. Care staff and nurses receive regular training and supervision suitable for their role. Staff
said they are happy working in the home and senior staff are available and approachable. A nurse
told us, “I love it here,” and told us there is a link nurse for training and external trainers who come
into the home who are interesting. The service retains good numbers of staff to ensure continuity of
care for people.

The provider invests in the home; the building and grounds are maintained to a good standard to
provide dignified surroundings for people. People are offered meals made with fresh ingredients
and can access snacks and drinks as required. Staff are safely recruited, and investment is made
in appropriate training. Equipment is replaced as required as are soft furnishings, towels and
bedding.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
