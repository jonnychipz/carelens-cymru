Summary:

 Cartref Care Home is a service located in the village of Henllan, near Newcastle Emlyn, providing
 care for up to 36 adults. The service supports older people, including people living with dementia,
 and promotes Welsh language and culture.

 Well-being requires improvement. People are generally positive about the care and support they
 receive and staff know people well. However, there is limited evidence people and their
 representatives are consistently involved in planning and reviewing of their care and support. Care
 records do not always reflect people's preferences, wishes and personal outcomes.

 Care and Support requires significant improvement. Concerns exist regarding care planning, risk
 assessment and the oversight of care delivery. Care records do not consistently provide assurance
 people's needs are accurately assessed, planned for and reviewed. Concerns relating to
 medication management further reduce assurance that people consistently receive safe and
 effective care.

 The Environment requires significant improvement. People do not consistently benefit from an
 environment where risks are identified and reduced. There is insufficient assurance that health and

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People are generally positive about the care and support they receive and are treated with dignity
 and respect. Positive interactions take place between staff and people living at the home and
 people appear comfortable within their surroundings. People have access to healthcare services
 when required and there is evidence of engagement with external professionals to support their
 health and well-being. People are supported to maintain important relationships with family and
 friends and Welsh language and culture continue to be promoted within the service.

 People do not consistently experience care and support that is planned and reviewed in a way that
 reflects their individual wishes, preferences and personal outcomes. Care records do not always
 contain sufficient person-centred information. There is limited evidence that people and their
 representatives are routinely involved in the development and review of personal plans. "This is
 Me" documentation was not consistently completed, and resident meetings were not taking place.
 This limits opportunities for people to formally share their views and influence the care and support
 they receive.

 Opportunities for consultation and engagement are not consistently evident, and records do not
 always demonstrate how people's views influence the development of the service. Whilst activities
 are available within the home, there is limited evidence they are consistently planned around
 individuals' interests, preferences and outcomes. People are not routinely supported to access
 opportunities within the wider community. Information regarding advocacy and complaints was not
 readily available, reducing assurance that people are fully supported to express their views,
 exercise choice and understand how to raise concerns. People benefit from respectful
 relationships, support to maintain important connections and access to healthcare services when
 required. Improvements are needed to ensure people are consistently involved in decisions about
 their care and support and that records and practices fully reflect their needs, preferences and
 desired outcomes. As a result, outcomes for people require improvement and we expect the
 provider to make improvements.

                                    Requires Significant
       Care & Support
                                       Improvement


People are supported by caring staff who treat them with dignity and respect. Positive interactions
take place between staff and people living at the home and healthcare professionals are involved in
supporting people's health and well-being. The newly appointed manager has recognised areas
requiring improvement and has started reviewing care documentation and strengthening care
planning arrangements.

People do not consistently receive care and support that is planned, delivered and reviewed in
accordance with their needs and personal outcomes. Personal plans do not always provide
accurate and person-centred guidance for staff and some records lacked sufficient information
regarding people's preferences, routines, outcomes and support needs. This reduces assurance
that care is consistently delivered in accordance with people's individual needs and wishes.

Risk management arrangements are not consistently effective. Risk assessments are not always
reviewed following incidents or changes in need and do not consistently reflect people's current
circumstances. Records do not always demonstrate how incidents, accidents and falls are
evaluated or how learning has informed future care and support arrangements.

Limited evidence was available to demonstrate people and, where appropriate, their
representatives are routinely involved in developing and reviewing personal plans. “This is Me”
documentation was not consistently available to support staff understanding of people's
preferences, life histories and what matters to them. Records did not consistently demonstrate how
people and their representatives were involved in decisions about their care and support.

Safeguarding, medication management and falls management arrangements do not consistently
provide assurance that risks are recognised, reviewed and managed effectively. Records and
oversight arrangements did not always demonstrate appropriate analysis of incidents, learning from
events or timely escalation to other parties where required. Medication audits and competency
assessments had not effectively identified or addressed concerns.

The provider does not consistently demonstrate that staff receive appropriate training, supervision
and support. Training and workforce records did not provide sufficient assurance that staff had
completed relevant training or that all workers requiring registration with Social Care Wales had
completed registration.

Whilst improvements have commenced, significant shortcomings remain in relation to care
planning, risk management, medication management, safeguarding and workforce oversight.
Improvements have not yet become embedded in practice or resulted in sustained positive
outcomes for people. People cannot be assured that care and support is consistently safe, effective
and person-centred. Outcomes for people require significant improvement and we have therefore

                                    Requires Significant
       Environment
                                       Improvement


People live in a home that is welcoming, clean and tidy, and bedrooms are personalised to reflect
individual preferences. Suitable equipment is available to support people's assessed needs and
communal areas provide opportunities for people to spend time with others. Food hygiene
arrangements remain “Very Good” and necessary equipment servicing arrangements are largely in
place. The provider has also taken action to strengthen security arrangements at the main entrance
and plans are in place for further improvements.

People do not consistently benefit from an environment where risks are effectively identified,
monitored and reduced. Environmental and health and safety oversight arrangements do not
provide sufficient assurances that concerns are identified promptly and addressed within
appropriate timescales. Records relating to environmental checks, maintenance and monitoring
processes did not consistently demonstrate effective oversight of risks.

The provider could not consistently demonstrate that actions arising from health and safety,
maintenance and environmental monitoring processes had been completed and reviewed. This
reduces assurance that risks to people's safety and well-being are effectively managed. There was
also limited evidence of routine environmental monitoring checks, including some maintenance and
safety records, not being completed and available for review.

Environmental hazards were identified throughout the home. Flooring and carpeting in some areas
of the service were worn and in need of replacement and presented avoidable trip hazards for
people living at the home. Other areas of the environment required repair, maintenance and
redecoration. These issues are of particular significance given the number of people living with
dementia and those with mobility difficulties.

Arrangements relating to the security of the front entrance door did not provide assurance that
identified risks are being effectively managed. Previous concerns regarding security arrangements
resulted in reliance on a doorbell system to alert staff when the entrance was used. The provider
has subsequently strengthened arrangements by ensuring the door remains locked and has plans
in place to install an electronic keypad entry system. These actions are welcomed and demonstrate
recognition that additional measures are required to reduce risk.

The provider is not able to consistently demonstrate that effective systems are in place to identify
environmental concerns, assess risk and ensure timely action is taken. This reduces assurance
that people are protected from avoidable harm within their living environment and that the
environment consistently supports their safety and well-being. As a result, outcomes for people
require significant improvement and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


The newly appointed manager demonstrates a good understanding of the improvements required
within the service and has already started to implement changes in key areas. The manager is
open to feedback, responsive to concerns raised and committed to improving outcomes for people
living at the home. Positive working relationships have been established with external professionals
and partner agencies.

Governance and oversight arrangements do not consistently provide assurance the service is
operating safely and in accordance with regulatory requirements. Quality assurance systems have
not been effective in identifying, monitoring and addressing concerns relating to care planning, risk
management, medication management, safeguarding and environmental safety. Several issues
identified during the inspection had not been recognised through existing audits or provider
oversight arrangements.

The provider does not consistently demonstrate effective quality assurance systems are in place to
monitor the quality and safety of the service. Audits had failed to identify concerns within care
records, medication administration processes and environmental risk management. This reduces
assurance that people benefit from a service which is regularly monitored and improved.

Records relating to staff supervision, appraisal and support are not consistently available. Training
records were not available to provide assurance that staff had completed training relevant to their
roles and responsibilities, including training required to support people safely and effectively.
Inspectors were not assured that all care workers required to be registered with Social Care Wales
had completed registration. Records did not provide sufficient assurance that appropriate DBS
checks had been completed and maintained in line with regulatory requirements. These shortfalls
reduce assurance that staff are appropriately supported, monitored and equipped to meet people's
needs safely and effectively.

The Responsible Individual could not demonstrate that oversight arrangements were sufficiently
robust to identify concerns, monitor compliance and drive improvement across the service.
Regulatory responsibilities had not been consistently met and provider oversight had not identified
or addressed several of the concerns found during the inspection. This reduces assurance that
effective arrangements are in place to monitor the quality and safety of the service and ensure
compliance with regulations.

Whilst improvements have commenced, they remain at an early stage and have not yet become
embedded in practice. Significant shortcomings remain in relation to governance, oversight,
workforce development and quality assurance arrangements. As a result, people cannot be

There has been a change in the management of the service since our last inspection. The new

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
