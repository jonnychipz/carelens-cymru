Summary:

 Glyncornel Nursing Home provides nursing care and support to 16 people, some of whom also
 have dementia care needs. The service is located in Llwynypia, a small valleys community, with
 transport links and amenities nearby.

 The wellbeing outcomes of people living in Glyncornel Nursing Home are good. This is because
 people are supported by a small, consistent team of nurses and care staff who know them well and
 can anticipate their needs and preferences where needed.

 The care and support provided at the service is good. Care documentation details care needs and
 risks to people’s health and wellbeing. Care staff record daily care interventions and follow advice
 given by health professionals. Infection control practices have improved since our last inspection.

 The environment people live in is good. Although the building requires further refurbishment and

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have choice and control over their daily lives. Care staffsupport people to make everyday
 decisions. They understand people’s preferences and know what is important to them. Care staff
 offer people options throughout the day, with food, activities, and where they want to spend their
 time. There is an activities co-ordinator who works full time at the service. Most activities are held in
 the service, with people who can, participating in baking, crafts and singalongs. The co-ordinator
 knows people well and tries to find activities that will trigger happy memories for them. Many people
 receive all their care in bed and so cannot take part in the group activities. The co-ordinator told us
 she tries to spend time with people one-to-one in their bedrooms, chatting with them and doing
 sensory activities such as hand massages.

 People are protected from harm and neglect. All care staff have up to date safeguarding training
 and there is an up-to-date safeguarding policy in place at the service. Individual risk assessments
 identify possible harm to individuals or others and the intervention needed from care or nursing
 staff to minimise these risks. Relevant incidents, or concerns, are appropriately referred to the
 Local Authority and guidance implemented.

 People are supported to maintain their physical and emotional well-being. There is a warm, friendly
 and supportive culture amongst staff. We observed caring and respectful interactions between staff
 and the people they are supporting. Visitors are welcomed and made to feel at home. We saw a
 regular visitor to the home chatting with care staff, who appeared to know them well and were
 asking about their family, to engage not only the visitor but also their loved one. Nursing and care
 staff told us how much they value the people living in Glyncornel and enjoy the job of supporting
 them. Relatives and representatives are kept up to date with any changes in needs or behaviour.

       Care & Support                       Good



People give good feedback about the care and support they receive at Glyncornel. One person told
us “The girls are great; they treat me well”. Another person was talking to the RI during our visit and
said: “we’re treated like kings here”. A staff member told us: “the care is really good here; all of the
staff really care”.


People receive good care and support which helps them achieve their personal outcomes. Care
documentation is stored electronically, and nursing and care staff update people’s notes throughout
the day.  All aspects  of care are recorded, such as  daily tasks, food and  fluid intake and
repositioning. Care plans and risk assessments contain all the necessary information for staff to
deliver the right care at the right time, in the way people prefer. A ‘resident of the day’ system is
used to fully review people’s care and environment every month. The manager is working to
increase consultations with families for care plan reviews.

People are referred for appropriate care and treatment with external professionals such as speech
and language therapists, dieticians or occupational therapists at the right time. Recommendations
for care and treatment by these professionals are incorporated into people’s care plans and the
guidance followed by nurses and care staff on a day-to-day basis. People receive their medication
as prescribed in accordance with national guidelines and service policy. Medication is consistently
stored as required to ensure its efficacy and safety. Nurses administer medication to all people
living in the home. Medication processes are audited regularly to identify any possible errors and
act accordingly, and there is a medication policy in the home to underpin good practice.

People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because the premises and
equipment are kept clean. At the time of the last inspection, some improvements were required with
infection control processes in the service. During this inspection, all issues had been addressed,
and the service was being kept clean and tidy by nursing and care staff, domestic and laundry staff.
Personal protective equipment (PPE) is used for close contact care and health interventions and is
disposed of correctly.

       Environment                         Good



The service is in an old building, which requires refurbishment and redecoration. Some
redecoration work has already been completed since the new service providers took over. External
contractors have been employed to undertake further work. There are plans to create an accessible
and pleasant outside space for people to use.


The service is set across two floors, with all bedrooms on the first floor. There is a lift available for
people to access all authorised areas of the home freely. The building is secure to unauthorised
access, and visitors must make themselves known to staff on arrival. Areas which may contain
hazardous items, such as the medication room, kitchen and laundry, are kept locked. There is a
communal lounge and separate dining room where people can socialise if they wish. People’s
bedrooms are decorated in a homely way and are personalised with their belongings. Specialist
equipment is available to those who need it. There have been improvements since the last
inspection to ensure all equipment and facilities are suitable for people to use.


Health and safety checks are completed by the full-time maintenance worker on site. Regular
servicing, maintenance and immediate repairs of facilities by either the maintenance worker or
external contractors ensures the safety and wellbeing of people using the service. The manager
has oversight of any health and safety issues in the home and acts on these appropriately.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from good leadership and management, and the oversight and governance of
the service is effective. A new manager has recently joined the team, but prior to that the
Operations Manager for the service provider was based in Glyncornel supporting the staff where
needed. The Responsible Individual (RI) has been a consistent and visible presence in the service,
and has built up good relationships with staff, people living at the service, and their families.
Monitoring reports and bi-annual quality of care reports have been completed by the RI as required,
identifying what is working well, and any areas that require further work.


People are supported by suitable numbers of staff to be able to meet their needs. The service has
recently recruited to the care staff team and has bank staff who can be called upon to cover
sickness and leave. Staff are safely recruited, trained and supported. We looked at a sample of
staff recruitment files and saw they contained all the pre-employment checks in respect of any
person working in regulated services. All staff are working with current Disclosure and Barring
(DBS) checks and are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator.


Staff receive training and supervision to support them in their roles. The manager advised she is
building networks with other professionals and organisations to be able to offer staff additional face
to face training. A regular supervision and appraisal schedule is in place, enabling staff to have
one-to-one sessions with the manager to discuss their professional development and any issues
they may be having.


There is good communication within the staff team. Handovers are completed from every shift.
Nursing staff keep a diary with tasks and appointments that require follow up. There are regular
staff meetings scheduled for all departments of staff.


Policies and procedures are up to date and accessible to staff in the service, to underpin good
practice. The manager is highlighting important policies to staff, ensuring their understanding of the
processes that should be followed.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
