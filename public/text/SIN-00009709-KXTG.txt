Summary:

 Leadon Court Nursing Home on the outskirts of Cwmbran provides nursing and residential care for
 people living with or without dementia. The local amenities include a pharmacy, newsagents, GP
 surgery, fish and chip shop and a Community Hub.

 Staff movement at the service has led to some instability with increased use of agency staff and
 low staff morale. A new manager was appointed in March 2025. Shortfalls in management systems
 were identified prior to this inspection which the manager is working to address. The safety of
 people living, working, and visiting the service was prioritised.

 We rated wellbeing as good because people are encouraged to make everyday choices and are
 given opportunities to take part in regular activity which they enjoy.

 People’s care and support is more robustly monitored with improved audits. Personal plans have

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 The service seeks to develop positive relationships with people to have a greater insight their
 needs and enable them to have a voice, choice, and control. Improvements in personal plans set
 out what is important for each person’s care and support and directs care staff how to assist them.
 People are offered and given everyday choices such as selecting meals and drinks, where to
 spend their time and participation in activities. People’s views of the service are sought and used to
 improve the service. There is a move towards more regular resident and family meetings to enable
 people to have more input in the running of the service. Management recognise the use of agency
 staff has affected the service and expect recruiting permanent staff will develop continuity and
 boost staff morale. Staff training is earmarked to develop a professional and competent workforce
 at the service. The majority of people speak positively about Leadon Court and the care and
 support they receive. One person told us, “The care my relative receives is first rate all the staff go
 out of their way to look after them and always make all visitors feel welcomed, I can’t speak highly
 enough of this home.”

 People have regular opportunities to engage in meaningful activities. Activity workers provide a
 programme five days a week. One person told us they, “Like the entertainers who visit Leadon
 Court, the food and enjoy the garden.” For people who prefer one to one stimulation staff can
 accommodate this. People are supported to maintain contact with their family, friends, and practice
 their faiths. Previously people visited the local community which is to be restarted. The provider
 does consider peoples language and cultural preferences but recognise their efforts to promote
 Welsh Language and culture require strengthening.

 People are safe and protected from harm. The service provides people with a secure environment.
 Arrangements are in place which ensure risks to people are promptly identified and addressed.
 Care and nursing staff complete safeguarding training and have a clear understanding of how to
 report matters of a safeguarding concern. There are effective mechanisms in place to make sure
 the premises comply with health and safety requirements.

 People live in accommodation that supports their wellbeing. The service is safe, clean, and well
 maintained. Aids and adaptations are available to enhance people’s wellbeing and support their
 independence. People are able to spend time alone and or communally engaging with others.
 People can access the garden.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes, but
they continue not to be engaged in the process. Qualified staff complete a pre-assessment with
people and their representatives before agreeing to provide a service. The service gathers
important information, noting preferences, well-being outcomes, routines, and beliefs. This process
is currently being strengthened with a review of pre-assessment documents. The personal plan
details each person’s care and support needs, and we found plans sufficiently inform care staff.
Personal plans focus on what people can do for themselves, a strengths-based approach means
daily outcomes are met in-line with people’s preferences and routines. Whilst staff recognise the
benefits of people’s engagement in care plan reviews, they do not have the opportunity to
contribute. This was identified at our last inspection. Every person now has a named nurse
allocated to them to make sure in the future, for those who want to contribute to the review of
personal plans, along with their representatives this will take place. Outcomes for people require
improvement because people are not being consulted about whether their outcomes are being fully
met, and we expect the provider to make necessary improvements.

People are treated with dignity and supported to be healthy and remain safe. Systems work well to
ensure people’s liberties are protected and they are cared for in the least restrictive way by trained
care staff. Individuals’ health is monitored to enable the service to act responsively to any change
in a person’s needs. Accidents and incidents are monitored and evaluated to mitigate risks. The
service works collaboratively with health professionals to support people living at the service. We
saw staff interact with people in a kind, compassionate and respectful way. The majority of people
are complimentary about the service and told us “All staff give confidence that your loved one is in
good hands.” “The staff seem to know the residents and genuinely care about them.”

People receive their medication as prescribed. Medication is stored to ensure ifs efficacy and
safety. Staff receive training to ensure they have the necessary skills to administer medication
safely to people. Routine medication audits take place which assess the storage and administration
of medicines.

People’s risk of infection is minimised because service effectively assesses, manages, and
prevents the risk of infections. Care staff are appropriately trained and competent in using personal
protective equipment (PPE) and infection control practices. Oversight and review systems are in
place to ensure people receive good quality care and support.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment.
Leadon Court offers people a warm, clean, comfortable, and well-furnished place to live. People’s
bedrooms are personalised to reflect their families, past history and hobbies. Communal areas are
comfortable, bright, and spacious which ensures people can enjoy activities and socialise together.
There are sufficient toilet and bathroom facilities throughout the service and specialist equipment
such as hoists available for those who need it. Outdoor spaces are safe and attractive. The garden
offers people an opportunity to sit outside with family and friends during warmer weather. Easier
access to the garden is being considered. A bench is earmarked for the front of the property as
people told us they enjoy sitting and spending time there.

Regular servicing, maintenance and repairs of facilities ensures the safety and well-being of
people. The service manages risks to people and risk assessments are up to date. There are
domestic and laundry staff at the service to ensure good standards of hygiene and cleanliness are
maintained. There is a rolling schedule of servicing in place to ensure that equipment such as
hoists and the lift are functional and safe for use. Fire equipment is checked, and alarms and
lighting are tested regularly. Water systems are checked for temperature regulation and legionella.
Every person living at the home has a personal emergency evacuation plan specific to their support
needs and staff undertake routine fire drills. External contractors are used when required to test
services such as gas and electricity. The service displays a rating of four by the Food Standards
Agency, which is ‘good.’ Records viewed showed effective environmental oversight of the service.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Oversight and governance arrangements foster a positive and compassionate culture. We saw
effective governance systems in place to support the running of the service. The Manager is
suitably qualified for the role and registered with Social Care Wales (SCW), the social care
workforce regulator. They know people well and demonstrate a commitment to providing a good
quality service. The majority of staff told us there is good communication and they are consulted on
proposed changes in working practices. This results in people having confidence in how the service
is managed and in the leadership team.

The service provider oversees the quality of care through effective audit processes. Quality
monitoring systems have been revised to include detailed analysis of care and support provided
and action plans to improve the quality of services for people. Visits to the service by the RI and
feedback from people is on-going. This information is used to drive improvements at Leadon Court.
The organisations policies and procedures are currently under review. In addition, the admission
pack for prospective residents is being updated. The process will ensure guidance for staff align
with current legislation and national guidance.

The service provider’s processes for recruitment is robust and records were available for us to
view. The service completes regular checks to ensure all staff are suitable to work in the service.
We found complete records relating to Disclosure and Barring checks, and registration information
to professional bodies. Those eligible are registered with Social Care Wales (SCW), the workforce
regulator and the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC). All newly appointed staff receive an
induction which includes training and shadow shifts to ensure they can perform specific care tasks.
Staff have access to training opportunities and have completed an appropriate level of training.
Staff we spoke with feel supported by their manager and records confirm that they receive regular
one to one supervision sessions.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
