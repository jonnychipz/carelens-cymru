Summary:

 Coed Isaf is a service for older people with nursing care needs who live with dementia, located on
 the outskirts of Llandudno.


 People’s well-being is good because their health and well-being is well-maintained; they receive
 support to achieve their personal outcomes and lead comfortable lives, getting the right care at the
 right time. The service provides people with opportunities to take part in a range of activities and
 can exercise choice. Systems are in place to help safeguard people in an environment which
 supports their needs. Care and support is good, with people and families providing very positive
 feedback about the service. Personal plans are person-centred and kept under regular review.
 Effective systems are in place to manage medication and infection control, and to safeguard
 people. The environment is good, being well-maintained, safe, and meeting the needs of people.
 Significant investment has been made in recent months and is evident throughout, which enhances
 people’s experience  of  living there. Leadership and management  is good. Governance and
 oversight systems support the smooth running of the service, with staff happy working there. Care
 and nursing staff have appropriate training and are recruited safely.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good well-being at Coed Isaf because they are treated with dignity and respect
 and are supported to achieve their outcomes. People live safely and healthily with control over their
 lives. Feedback from people and their representatives is overwhelmingly positive. The service
 captures and  documents  important  person-centred  information  about  people’s  past,  their
 preferences and wishes, on admission. This information informs people’s plans. Where people
 have difficulty communicating or making decisions, the service works closely with people’s families,
 representatives and professionals to ensure decisions are in the person’s best interests, with the
 service having made significant improvements in this area. The service maintains very good
 contact with relatives, keeping them informed and involved, and offering a warm and welcoming
 environment. People receive information about what to expect from the service via a service user
 guide, which includes information on how to make a complaint. Family meetings take place, where
 views are sought and issues regarding the service can be discussed. People get the right care at
 the right time. The service refers health concerns appropriately and follows guidance.


 Systems are in place to help protect people from abuse and neglect. Recruitment procedures are
 safe, with checks completed before staff begin work. Risks to people and care staff are managed
 through risk assessments in the care planning process. The service is environmentally secure.
 Families say they feel confident the service is safe and can visit whenever they wish.


 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. People can do the things they
 wish, with a variety of regular activities and opportunities arranged for them, such as arts and crafts
 and visiting entertainers. People spend time in areas of the service of their choosing with people
 they wish to be with. The service employs an activity coordinator, who arranges and engages
 people  in  different  activities. The service  is inclusive and encourages family and friends to
 participate when marking significant events and occasions, such as birthdays. The service  is
 working towards the Welsh Active offer, capturing language preferences, having a Welsh board in
 place, and having several Welsh-speaking staff available. Welsh cultural events are marked and
 celebrated.


 People live in an environment that supports their needs. Coed Isaf is set over three floors, providing
 support to older people with nursing needs who live with dementia. The home is welcoming, has a
 calm atmosphere, staff are visible, and is well equipped to meet people’s needs. Bedrooms are
 comfortable and personalised, with several communal areas and access to attractive garden
 grounds. The overall environment is pleasant, clean and well-maintained.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care and support at Coed Isaf is good because people are treated with kindness, empathy and
compassion. We saw people are well-settled in their environment, with care staff interacting in a
calm, respectful manner. We saw positive social engagement throughout the service, with people
spending time together, families visiting regularly, and mealtimes being a pleasant and relaxed
experience. People and their families speak positively about the service, telling us “the staff are
lovely and kind”, “it’s wonderful – I can visit any time of day or night”, “the staff are very patient –
they listen”, and “they’re lovely, all of them –  I can drop in whenever and always get a warm
welcome.” Qualified staff at the service assess people’s needs prior to them moving in. Personal
plans are detailed and person centred, documenting how people  like to be supported. Risk
assessments identify issues and how to manage these. Plans are kept under regular review. Daily
notes and monitoring charts track people’s progress and confirm people receive support according
to their personal plans.


Processes are in place to help protect people from harm. Care staff undertake safeguarding
training and trust management will respond appropriately  if they report any issues. Records of
incidents and accidents are kept, with risk management plans guiding care staff in supporting
people safely. People and families tell us they feel the service is safe. Where people lack mental
capacity to make decisions about their care and accommodation, the service ensures Deprivation
of Liberty Safeguard (DoLS) applications are made.


Systems are in place to support the safe storage and administration of medication. Medicines are
well managed by  nursing  staff,  stored  securely and  administered  according  to  prescriber
instructions. Records are up to date and free from errors. Monthly medication audits ensure
supplies  are  accurate.  Appropriate  authorisations and  documentation  are  in  place where
medication must be managed covertly. Staff receive training in safe medication handling and
administration. Clinical oversight is robust and much improved, with good systems to monitor
people’s health such as their weight, nutrition and physical health.


Infection control is well managed, with effective measures in place to reduce risks. Care staff have
access to protective equipment and use  it as necessary,  for example when supporting with
personal care  tasks. Care  staff receive  infection  control  training,  with an up-to-date  policy
supporting staff practice. Domestic staff complete cleaning routines, with clear records evidencing
oversight and action taken to address any issues. Laundry is managed appropriately. Clinical waste
is managed and disposed of correctly. The kitchen achieved a Food Hygiene Rating of 5, which
means standards of food hygiene are ‘very good’.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is good because people live in homely accommodation that is safe and meets
their needs. People benefit from a warm, comfortable, welcoming,  well-lit environment which
promotes their independence and maintains their dignity. The service is set over three floors,
located in a residential area on the outskirts of Llandudno. It is tidy and uncluttered, and we saw
high standards of cleanliness throughout. Only authorised persons can enter, with  all visitors
required to sign in upon arrival and when leaving. There are two comfortable lounge areas, where
people often spend their time, with various other areas around the service where people also
choose to sit and spend time. There are sufficient toilet and bathing facilities, some bedrooms with
ensuite facilities, and mobility aids available where needed. We saw the storage of larger items,
such as hoists, has been well considered and adaptations made so they are stored out of sight. A
dining area provides a space where people can enjoy their meals. The kitchen  facilities are
appropriate for the service. Bedrooms are a good size and provide comfortable spaces for people.
They are  highly personalised  with people’s  furnishings and important items  reflecting  their
preferences. People are provided specialist equipment to meet their needs, such as profiling beds,
call bells and sensor mats. These are in good working order and well-maintained. An attractive
garden area is available to the front, with seating available for people and areas where people can
walk with support.


We saw significant investment has been made in recent months, with works ongoing during the
inspection visit. Where works have been completed in areas such as the lounges, hallways and
bedrooms, they enhance the environment and support people’s well-being. Walls have been re-
plastered and re-painted, new flooring has been fitted, furnishings and wood trims have been
upgraded, and new furniture purchased. While this work is not yet complete, the improvements are
evident and done to a very good standard. We were shown plans for the prioritisation of further
works at the service.


The service takes measures to ensure the home environment is as safe as possible. Equipment is
stored appropriately. Hazardous substances are safely stored in locked cupboards. All bedrooms
and bathrooms seen are  fitted with window restrictors. Fire exits are free from obstructions.
Records confirm the regular testing and servicing of gas, electricity, and water facilities. Equipment
checks and maintenance take place. Fire safety checks are  routinely undertaken. Personal
emergency evacuation plans are in place to support staff to understand the level of support people
require in the event of an emergency.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management  of the service  is good because the service provider ensures
governance arrangements support the smooth running of the service, with suitable numbers of
skilled and  qualified  staff  in  place. Strong governance and  oversight  practices have been
developed. The responsible individual (RI) undertakes their statutory duties by comprehensively
documenting quarterly visits to engage with both residents and staff, visits often and is at the
service regularly. They complete quality of care reviews every six months, with actions identified to
support ongoing service development. Management auditing and quality assurance systems are
extremely robust and much improved upon, helping to monitor performance and identify areas for
improvement. We saw an abundance of auditing around  all areas of the service, including a
manager daily walkaround, monthly safety calendars to analyse incidences of  falls, monthly
medication audits, monthly environmental audits, and infection control audits. All audits are well-
documented, with analysis of these helping to inform an ongoing home improvement plan. This is
kept under regular review by the RI and other directors. The service also gathers the views of
people and their representatives, with feedback from family members confirming their views are
sought and they feel listened to.


The service manager promotes a positive ‘open door’ culture where staff told us they feel valued
and work as one team. Care staff provided positive feedback about working at the service, telling
us they like working there, feel well-supported and can approach service leaders with issues, who
will then act on these. Care staff comments include “the nurses and seniors including the manager
is approachable” and “I appreciate the opportunity to learn and develop new skills”. Policies and
procedures outline staff roles and service delivery. These are in line with regulation and up to date.
The service whistleblowing procedure forms part of the safeguarding policy, with staff reporting
they feel confident in raising concerns  if necessary. The service is open and transparent, making
the required notifications to Care Inspectorate Wales and other relevant bodies, including the local
authority Safeguarding Team.


Staff are qualified and trained to meet a wide range of needs. Training records confirm ongoing
training in essential areas of care, with nursing staff receiving additional training relevant to their
role. Staff report feeling well-supported and confident in their roles and demonstrate a good
understanding of their responsibilities. Recruitment practices are thorough and compliant with
regulatory  standards,  with  staff  files  containing  all  required  documentation. These  include
Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS)  certificates and proof of  identification. Care  staff are
registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. Nurses are registered with the Nursing
and Midwifery Council. Newly appointed staff undergo an induction and probation period to ensure
suitability for their roles. Supervision supports staff in terms of performance management and
professional development. The  staff team  is stable and well-established. Staffing levels are

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
