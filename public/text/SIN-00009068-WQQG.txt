Summary:

 People experience good well-being outcomes at Allerton Lodge, are treated with dignity and
 respect, and have a choice of activities available to them. People are supported to stay as healthy
 as possible, with systems in place to protect people in an environment that meets their needs.
 People are treated as individuals and can live their lives how they wish.

 Care and support are good. Detailed care documentation is in place to support people’s needs. The
 service has good systems to safeguard people, and good systems to reduce the risk of the spread
 of infection. Care staff treat people with kindness and compassion. Personal plans are an accurate
 reflection of the individual and people receive appropriate care and support.

 The environment is good and meets people’s needs and outcomes, enhances their well-being, and
 is safe. Allerton Lodge is well decorated throughout and has enough communal space. External
 areas are secure. People’s rooms, as well as communal areas, are kept clean and free from

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have choice and control over their daily lives. Care workers support people to make
 everyday decisions. They understand people’s preferences and know what is important to them.
 The provider undertakes detailed pre-admission assessments and receives information from health
 care professionals prior to people moving into the home. People’s personal plans focus on people’s
 likes and dislikes and are strengths and outcomes focused. We saw people have a positive
 experience during lunch, their dignity is respected, and alternative meal options and meal sizes are
 offered. People have good support which enhances their experiences throughout the day, which we
 witnessed, and people confirmed. People’s independence is encouraged as they are offered choice
 wherever possible. People can get up and go to bed whenever they wish, get choice in regards
 their meals and where and how they wish to spend their time. People have their own personal
 routines and engage in a wide range of activities of their choice. An activities co-ordinator is
 employed four days a week. Activities include baking, games, music, and exercises. External
 entertainers also visit the home on a regular basis. People told us they enjoy the activities, which
 was confirmed by what we observed.

 People are protected from harm and neglect. Care workers receive training in safeguarding, to
 protect vulnerable adults and appropriate training to support people effectively. There are also
 policies and procedures in place to support this. Care workers have a good understanding of their
 responsibilities and know how to report any concerns they have. Personal plans and risk
 assessments are in place and reviewed regularly. People told us that they are happy and feel safe
 in their home. The manager is visible during the day, is aware of people’s requirements and speaks
 with them regularly. Personal plans and risk assessments are in place to help reduce risks and
 keep people safe. Recruitment of care workers is robust.

 People are supported to maintain their physical and emotional well-being. There is a positive
 culture amongst care staff. There is a consistent staff team which provides good continuity of
 support for people. We saw friendships formed with people enjoying time in the lounge, dining area,
 and communal spaces. Individual personal plans are in place which reflect their choices and how to
 maintain their safety.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are protected from abuse and harm. People are supported to be safe and have risk
assessments in place where needed. People we spoke with confirmed they felt safe at Allerton
Lodge. The provider understands the legal requirements of caring for vulnerable people and makes
referrals to the local authority safeguarding team when required. Managers monitor safeguarding
referrals. Applications are made to the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) team and are
renewed appropriately. This ensures that placements at Allerton Lodge are lawful where people
lack the mental capacity to make decisions around their care and accommodation needs.

People experience good care and support which ensures they meet their well-being outcomes.
Care is outcome and strength focused and delivered in a way which promotes the development of
routine and structures in line with people’s preferences. Personal plans are strengths and
outcomes based, give a clear picture of the person, and are reviewed regularly. Care staff follow
these to ensure good quality person-centred care and support is delivered. People’s outcomes are
clear, and they are supported by care staff to ensure their outcomes and goals are met. Throughout
the inspection, people’s requests for support were met promptly with good quality care being
delivered throughout the day and care staff responded to call bells appropriately. We observed
positive interactions between people and care staff, who clearly know the people they support, this
was confirmed by those we spoke with. People living at Allerton Lodge are well kempt. We spoke
with people who told us they got their clothes cleaned quickly. People confirmed they were
supported and spoken to appropriately and treated with dignity and respect.

People are referred for appropriate appointments at the right time and recommendations for care
and treatment by other professionals are conducted as directed. This was confirmed by people we
spoke with as well as records we viewed. We saw personal plans are updated with any changes in
health conditions and there are clear protocols to follow to maintain people’s health and wellbeing.
Health monitoring, such as food and fluid charts, are completed in line with personal plans.
People’s medication is safely managed, and medication is stored securely. Only competent care
staff can administer medication.

       Environment                         Good



Allerton Lodge is situated in a rural location on the outskirts of Holywell. The home is set over two
floors with a stairlift provided. The environment is clean, comfortable and provides an environment
where people’s well-being and outcomes are met. The decoration and flooring in both people’s
rooms and communal areas are of a good standard, with parts of the communal areas having
recently been re-decorated and new flooring has been laid. There are several communal areas
where people spend time in the company of others, alone, or people can remain in their own room
should they wish to have privacy. People have their own rooms and can personalise their room to
their preference with their own belongings, which helps people to feel at home. Bathrooms and
toilet facilities are kept clean and equipped with specialist equipment for those who require it.
Externally the back garden is laid to grass. There is a seating area with tables, and areas where
plants are looked after, which ensures that it is a positive environment which people can enjoy.

People live in an environment which is safe, with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and
equipment. There are safe and effective systems in place to maintain and manage the
accommodation to consistently meet people’s needs. Regular servicing and environmental checks,
such as checks of electrics, gas and water are undertaken to ensure the safety and well-being of
people using the service. Care staff are trained in fire safety and regular fire checks are
undertaken, to keep people as safe as possible. We also saw a schedule of planned works for the
home and saw evidence that works were to be conducted.

Allerton Lodge has been awarded a score of four by the Food Standards Agency, which suggests
standards of cleanliness and hygiene in the kitchen are good. The housekeeping team follow
cleaning schedules to ensure the home is kept clean and tidy, which we found it is. Laundry
facilities are suitable for the size of the home and there are systems in place to reduce the infection
control risks. People living at the home told us their rooms are kept clean.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People live at a service which is well organised and has good leadership oversight. The provider
has established a stable team of care staff who have daily support from a manager, and regional
manager. One member of staff told us that staff work well as a team. Other staff and people living
at the service gave positive feedback about the manager, saying they are “supportive and
approachable.” There are effective systems in place to monitor quality. This is conducted through
audits of different areas of the service, visits by the RI, regional manager and gaining feedback
from people and their representatives and relevant stakeholders. Managers have a comprehensive
suite of audits which are completed on a regular basis, and we were able to evidence actions are
identified and addressed in a timely way. The RI creates a detailed report following visits to
evidence what areas they have looked at and what actions are needed. The quality-of-care report
summarises all the information gathered and shows performance of the service in the past and
improvements to the service for the future. People and staff can be assured they have access to
relevant information regarding Allerton Lodge.

Alerton Lodge is working towards providing a service in Welsh, with signage in place, and
documentation being available in Welsh upon request.

People are supported by care staff who are safely recruited, well trained and professionally
registered. Pre employment checks are conducted before a person begins working at the service,
which includes a disclosure and barring service (DBS) check and obtaining appropriate references.
This is to ensure people are safe and appropriate to work with adults at risk. Staff complete an
induction, and all staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. We reviewed
training records and found staff complete mandatory training in a number of areas including
dementia care, moving and handling and medication administration. All staff receive regular one to
one supervision and appraisals. This helps identify any training needs, performance and concerns
to support ongoing development and ensure people receive the support they require. Care staff
confirmed training they received was appropriate for the people they supported. Care staff are
employed in appropriate numbers; this was confirmed by records we saw, staffing numbers on the
day of inspection, and what stakeholders told us. Regular meetings are held with staff, residents
and their families which ensures the service is aware of the views of stakeholders in regards the
running of the home.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
