Summary:

 The Bay Nursing Home is a residential service for adults, located in the seaside town of Tywyn.
 People have good access to the promenade and high street.

 People experience good wellbeing outcomes because they receive a good standard of care and
 support which meets their needs. People are encouraged to make choices about their daily lives
 and have the opportunity to engage in meaningful activities.

 People live in a good environment which is well maintained and safe. The provider has invested in
 the service which is ongoing.

 Good leadership and management arrangements are in place which provide oversight of the
 service and measure quality. Leaders identify areas to be developed and take action to address the
 feedback people give them.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have choice and control over most aspects of their daily lives. People choose when to get
 up and go to bed, where to spend their time, what they want to do and what they want to eat and
 drink. People told us they like the activities on offer and play games, sing, have massage and do
 gardening. The activities coordinator said they have the resources they need and has spent time
 getting to know people so they can arrange things people enjoy. People are treated with dignity and
 respect. Care staff are kind and patient with people, and we observed many warm interactions.
 One person told us they had recently moved here and were “much happier.” Another said, “the staff
 are excellent, if you can find a higher word I’ll agree with you.” We spoke with another person who
 said, “the staff are lovely and help me when I ask.” The manager keeps a record of compliments,
 and we saw many sincere messages of thanks. We also saw records of positive feedback from
 visiting professionals.


 People are protected from abuse and neglect. Staff complete safeguarding training and there are
 policies in place to support this learning so people can access information about what to do if they
 have a concern. We saw good records of incidents, accidents, and safeguarding issues, which are
 reported appropriately and reviewed  to ensure the appropriate action has been taken. The
 manager demonstrates very good practice when assessing capacity and supporting people to
 make informed decision about their care and support. People have risk assessments in place to
 help reduce risks and where restrictions are required to keep people safe, this is appropriately
 documented and monitored. People have access to advocacy services and representatives to
 support with their rights.

 People are supported to develop and maintain relationships and be part of their local communities.
 We saw people have visits from family and friends during the day, as well as receiving phone calls.
 People told us they enjoy spending time together and said, “we look out for each other”. The
 manager and staff make sure people are supported to go out into the community and for walks by
 the beach. People are looking forward to a charity coffee morning which local businesses have
 supported. Many people living at The Bay have lived and worked in the small seaside community,
 and this remains important to them.

       Care & Support                       Good



People experience good outcomes because they receive the care they need, when they need  it.
We saw evidence of the service contacting other health and social care professionals for further
support, which includes occupational therapists, podiatrists, dentists, opticians, and psychiatrists.
On the day of inspection, one person was going out to attend a hospital appointment and later the
service  administrator arranged  transport  for another person  to be able  to attend a  clinic.
Throughout the day, people’s requests for support were met promptly and staff responded to call
bells immediately. We saw good records of any care interventions and recording of professional
guidance. We were able to evidence good care being delivered as people who are considered at
high risk of skin damage remain well. People can receive some support in their preferred language.
There is good promotion of the Welsh language within the service. Documents are available
bilingually and we saw prompts  in communal  areas  of key words and  phrases. Welsh
conversations were heard throughout the inspection. People’s personal plans are detailed and
consistently include their preferences, routines and things which are important to them. Records
are reviewed on a regular basis to ensure information remains current and relevant.

People experience a pleasant mealtime experience. The dining room is spacious, and tables are
fully set with cloths, napkins, cutlery, cups, saucers, and menus. People have a choice of meals
which are nutritionally balanced and meet a variety a dietary requirements. Where people have
experienced weight loss, this has been addressed, and people receive supplements  if needed.
People told us they enjoy the food and have choice about what to eat. We observed positive
interactions during lunchtime, where staff were attentive and patient when supporting people.

Medication  is managed extremely well within the service. We observed excellent medication
practice during the day. Medication is ordered, received, stored, and dispensed safely. Organised
storage means medication  is easily  identified  for each person and detailed guidance about
administration is available in medication files. We found medication records to be complete and
counts  to be  correct. The manager conducts  audits  of medications  to ensure  safety and
compliance.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a service which is clean, safe, and accessible. People can access several communal
areas, in addition to their rooms where they can spend time alone, with others or with visitors.
Communal spaces meet the needs of people because they are accessible and can accommodate
any moving and handling equipment people need, including specialist seating so people are not
restricted to their rooms. People’s own rooms are clean, bright, and well ventilated, personalised
with their own possessions. People who choose to spend time in their room, have appropriate
seating and a table for comfort, along with a call bell in case they need help. People have access to
equipment which supports their wellbeing and promotes independence. Where it is needed, people
have specialist beds, seating, and manual handling equipment, supported by occupational therapist
guidance. All equipment is clean and regularly serviced to make sure it is safe to use.

People are safe living at The Bay as the provider has a strong approach to the management of
Health and Safety. Regular audits of the environment are carried out to identify any areas which
require work, and the responsible individual (RI) looks at the environment as part of their quarterly
visits. Regular servicing of gas safety, electrical and fire safety is completed and any actions
addressed. The provider has made investments in the environment of the service which continue to
be ongoing, which include an extension and refit of some of the ground floor areas. This will
improve the kitchen and laundry areas, and facilities for staff.

The housekeeping team work hard to ensure the service is clean throughout. Infection prevention
and control procedures are carried out, following health board guidance. Waste is appropriately
managed, and we observed personal protective equipment (PPE) is available for staff when
needed. A recent inspection by the Food Standards Agency, awarded the service a level 5 rating,
the highest which can be achieved. This indicates a high level of cleanliness and food safety
compliance.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People live at a service which is well organised and has good leadership oversight. The manager
has established a stable team of staff who have daily oversight from a team leader and a nurse .
One member of staff told us they felt the team is “the best it has ever been.” Other staff and people
living at the service gave positive feedback about the manager, saying they are supportive and
approachable. There are effective systems in place to monitor quality. This is conducted through
audits of different areas of the service, visits by the RI and gaining feedback from people and their
representatives. Audits are completed on a regular basis, and we were able to evidence actions are
identified and addressed in a timely way. Further quality assurance is provided by the local
authority and health board, and we saw positive feedback from professionals following a visit. The
RI conducts regular visits of the service and creates a detailed report of what areas they have
looked at and what actions are needed. The quality-of-care report summarises feedback from
people, and it is positive to see the action which has been taken following feedback from people.
For example, the provider has implemented new roles at the service to ensure people have
companionship and the opportunity to take part in activities. Policies and procedures have been
reviewed, as have important documents such as the statement of purpose and guide to the service.
People and staff can be assured they have access to relevant information.

People are supported by care staff who are safely recruited, well trained and professionally
registered. Pre employment checks are conducted before a person begins working at the service,
which includes a disclosure and barring service check (DBS) and obtaining appropriate references.
This is to ensure people are safe and appropriate to work with adults at risk. Staff complete an
induction, and the All-Wales Induction Framework, or equivalent to register with Social Care Wales,
the workforce regulator. We reviewed training records and found staff complete mandatory training
in a number of areas including Dementia care. The manager has consulted with the practice
development to team to book further training which ensures staff are knowledgeable about the
identified needs of the people they support. Clinical staff complete regular training and
competencies in order to retain their professional nursing registration and to provide skilled care to
people in the service. All staff receive regular one to one supervision conducted through discussion
and observations of skills. This helps identify any training needs to support ongoing development.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
