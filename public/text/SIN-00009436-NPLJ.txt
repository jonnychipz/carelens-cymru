Summary:

 Rhoslan is a residential care service providing care and support for people aged 18 and over.
 Based in the seaside town of Rhos-on-Sea, the service is close to local amenities and the beach.

 People experience excellent wellbeing outcomes because the service places people's health,
 wellbeing, and quality of life at the centre of daily practice. Staff know people well and support them
 to maintain their independence, make choices and participate in meaningful activities, which reflect
 their interests and preferences.

 Care and support are excellent because staff provide tailored, person-centred care which responds
 to people's individual needs. Personal plans accurately reflect people's wishes, strengths and
 desired outcomes.

 The environment is excellent because it is welcoming and comfortable. The home is thoughtfully
 designed, accessible and maintained to a very high standard, providing people with a safe,
 pleasant and homely place to live.

 Leadership and management are good because leaders provide strong and effective direction
 which promotes a positive culture throughout the service. Effective governance arrangements help
 maintain consistently high standards of care and drive continuous improvement for people living at

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent




 People experience excellent wellbeing outcomes because they have choice and control over their
 day-to-day lives and play an active role in decisions about the care and support they receive. Care
 staff know people well and take time to understand their individual preferences, life histories,
 interests and aspirations. Resident feedback provides opportunities for people to influence life
 within their home, contribute ideas and shape activities and events. Care staff create a culture
 where people feel listened to, respected and encouraged to express what matters most to them.
 The service consistently places people at the centre of decision-making and supports them to
 maintain as much independence as possible. Care staff work proactively with health and social
 care professionals to identify changing needs at an early stage and respond promptly to ensure
 people receive the right support. Professionals shared examples of people recovering from illness,
 regaining confidence and achieving greater independence through the high standard of care and
 support provided.

 A dedicated activities coordinator demonstrates passion and commitment to providing opportunities
 which enhance people's wellbeing and quality of life. The service offers a varied programme of
 activities, including music, arts and crafts, exercise classes, themed celebrations, and community
 events. We saw staff use activities therapeutically to support communication, reduce distress and
 identify changes in cognition, mood and behaviour. People maintain important relationships and
 stay connected to their families, friends and local community. Families and friends are welcomed
 into the service and encouraged to take part in celebrations and events. One relative told us, “My
 dad is very happy at Rhoslan. he feels safe and his needs are well met.”

 Care staff promote and uphold people's dignity, individuality and rights. They support people to
 maintain their identity, pursue their interests and celebrate important life events and achievements.
 Care staff regularly review personal plans and update them promptly when needs change, ensuring
 support remains personalised and responsive. People told us staff are kind and caring. During our
 visit, we observed warm, positive and respectful interactions between people and care staff, which
 enhanced wellbeing. There are bilingual signs across the service with some staff who speak Welsh.
 People live in accommodation which meets their needs and bedrooms are personalised. People
 have access to equipment and facilities which are regularly maintained to ensure their wellbeing.
 Outside space is accessible and safe.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People experience excellent care and support which is personalised and delivered by skilled care
staff who understand their individual needs, preferences and aspirations. Care staff develop
detailed care plans which describe people's health needs, personal histories, routines, choices and
desired outcomes. People and their families contribute to planning and reviewing care if they wish.
Care staff encourage people to maintain existing skills and independence wherever possible.
Professionals described care planning as “Excellent” and told us staff demonstrate a very good
understanding of people's needs. External professionals said the service communicates effectively,
shares information promptly and works collaboratively to achieve the best outcomes for people.
One professional told us, “As a professional I have peace of mind when I place service users at
Rhoslan that they will receive a high level of care, and support to achieve a positive outcome” Care
staff work closely with healthcare professionals. They monitor and escalate changes in people's
health and seek timely advice when required.

People and their families speak positively about the support they receive. Relatives describe staff
as approachable, caring and flexible in responding to individual needs. People are protected from
harm because staff understand their safeguarding responsibilities and respond appropriately to
concerns. Leaders make timely referrals to relevant agencies when required and oversee effective
safeguarding processes.

Medication is managed safely because trained and competent staff follow robust procedures for the
storage, administration and recording of medicines. Leaders monitor practice through regular
internal audits and competency checks to ensure staff maintain high standards. During the
inspection, we found medication practices were safe and well managed. In addition, a recent
independent pharmacy audit demonstrated compliance with relevant requirements and provided
further assurance to demonstrate medicines are managed safely and effectively. Effective infection
prevention and control practices help maintain a safe and clean environment, reducing risks to
people's health and wellbeing.

       Environment                              Excellent




People live in an excellent environment which enhances their well-being, independence and quality
of life. The service is welcoming, spacious and thoughtfully designed to create a warm and homely
atmosphere. Communal areas are comfortable, well-presented and provide opportunities for both
social interaction and quiet relaxation. The provider has invested in the environment and continues
to develop creative spaces to enrich people’s daily lives. Innovative themed areas throughout the
service provide meaningful opportunities for engagement, reminiscence and social interaction.
These include unique features such as a cinema room, fish and chip shop-themed area, flower
shop display and other decorative lounges and spaces. The environment promotes independence
and enables people to move safely throughout the service. The service has recently invested in a
call bell monitoring system to strengthen quality assurance arrangements by providing oversight of
response times. We observed staff responding quickly to call bells throughout our visit, ensuring
people received timely support when required. A refurbishment programme demonstrates a
proactive commitment to maintaining high standards and enhancing people’s experience of living at
the service.

The service is very clean, comfortable and safe. Staff take pride in maintaining high standards of
cleanliness and presentation. One relative commented the service is “always very clean, tidy” and
praised the quality of the environment available to residents. People benefit from access to a well-
maintained outdoor area at the rear of the home. The space provides opportunities for people to sit,
relax and enjoy dining outdoors if they choose. People spoke fondly about the garden and told us
they valued having access to a pleasant outdoor environment. Regular servicing, maintenance and
repairs of facilities and equipment ensures the safety and well-being of people using the service.
We saw regular checks are made both internally and by external contractors. Care staff attend
regular fire drills and have training, so they know what to do in emergency situations. Training is
also given to care staff on the use of equipment including moving and handling to minimise the risk
to people. We saw this equipment is regularly serviced.

       Leadership & Management               Good




Leadership and management are good and the service benefits from strong and effective
leadership which promotes a positive culture and achieves good outcomes for people. The
registered manager is respected by professionals, families and staff and demonstrates a clear
commitment to delivering person-centred care. Professionals consistently described the leadership
within the service as excellent. One professional commented, “The Manager's overall knowledge in
relation to the residents is exceptional”, while another stated, “The Manager and their team are
always one step ahead if there are changing needs in people.” Leaders have established effective
governance systems to support continuous improvement and maintain oversight of the service.
They use audits, resident feedback, professional surveys and regular monitoring activities to
evaluate performance and identify opportunities to further develop the service. Care staff can raise
concerns, and records show leaders investigate concerns thoroughly and share outcomes
appropriately. Leaders actively record and review compliments, demonstrating a culture where
feedback is valued. The Responsible Individual (RI) and other managers in the organisation visit
the service and take time to speak with people and review records and the environment.
Information is collated into a quality-of-care review which identifies what is working well and areas
to develop. Records show any actions identified are quickly addressed. Timely notifications are
sent to relevant authorities in the event of incidents.

People benefit from a stable, skilled and well-supported workforce. Leaders follow safe recruitment
practices, including obtaining Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks, references and
professional registration checks, where required. Staff receive a comprehensive induction, ongoing
training and opportunities for professional development. Training compliance is high and reflects
the needs of people living at the home. Staff told us training opportunities are good and help them
provide safe and effective care. Leaders ensure there are enough suitably qualified and trained
staff on duty to meet people's needs. During our visit, we saw staff present throughout the home
and responding promptly to people who required support. Although staff were busy, they provided
timely care and support. Leaders support staff through regular supervision, annual appraisal and
team meetings, creating opportunities for feedback, reflection and shared learning. Staff spoke
positively about the support they receive from colleagues and managers. They described the
manager as approachable and visible. The service works closely with external professionals and
has developed strong partnership arrangements.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
