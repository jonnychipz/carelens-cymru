Summary:

 People achieve positive outcomes because they receive compassionate, person-centred care from
 a care staff team that knows them well and is committed to promoting their well-being. During our
 inspection, people and relatives spoke positively about the service and consistently described staff
 as kind, respectful and responsive. We observed positive interactions throughout the home and
 found people were relaxed, comfortable and treated with dignity.

 People receive care and support that reflects their individual needs, preferences and personal
 outcomes. Care staff work effectively with healthcare professionals to support people's health and
 well-being and respond promptly when needs change. Care planning arrangements provide staff
 with clear guidance to deliver consistent and personalised support.

 People  live in a clean, safe and comfortable environment that enhances their quality of  life.
 Bedrooms are personalised and communal areas provide opportunities  for social interaction,
 meaningful engagement and relaxation. The provider maintains the premises to a good standard
 and ensures appropriate equipment and safety measures are in place to support people's well-

Effective leadership and management underpin the quality of the service. The management team

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Many people achieve positive well-being outcomes because care staff support them with kindness,
 respect and compassion. People told us they enjoy living at the service and feel safe. Comments
 included, “It’s very pleasant”, “I couldn’t ask for anymore” and “I feel quite safe.” Relatives also
 spoke positively about the care provided and described staff as caring, respectful and attentive. We
 observed people seeking reassurance, support and conversation from care staff and saw them
 respond in a patient and caring manner. This demonstrates people receive support from care staff
 who know them well and understand their preferences.

 People have opportunities to exercise choice and maintain control over their daily lives. Personal
 plans contain information about routines, preferences, communication needs and how care staff
 should support people to achieve their desired outcomes. People told us they are involved in
 discussions about  their care and  relatives confirmed they are consulted where appropriate.
 Personal plans could be further enhanced with more historical ‘This is Me’ information. Care staff
 support people  to access health services and maintain  their health and well-being.  Visiting
 professionals and the GP told us referrals are timely and appropriate and recommendations are
 followed.

 People would benefit from increased opportunities for meaningful occupation and community
 engagement. Although we observed people enjoying activities during the inspection and plans are
 in place for a new activities co-ordinator, people and relatives told us activities had been limited in
 recent months. The provider has already begun taking action to strengthen this area.

 People receive nutritious meals and can choose from a varied menu. We saw home-cooked meals
 being prepared and found dietary needs were clearly communicated between care and kitchen
 staff. Most people spoke positively about the food on offer and told us they have good choice and
 portions. The service offers a variety of meals, and hold a food hygiene rating of five, which is ‘very
 good’.

 People are protected from abuse and neglect. The building is secure, and visitors sign into and out
 of the home. This ensures there is an accurate record of who is in the building at any one time.
 Risks to individuals are included in personal plans and risk assessments, which are regularly
 reviewed. Equipment such as a call bell system, and sensor mats are available.  All staff receive
 safeguarding training and demonstrated a good understanding of their responsibilities. Information
 about advocacy, complaints and raising concerns is available within the service user guide.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive consistently good care and support because staff understand their needs and
provide support in line with detailed personal plans. We reviewed a sample of care records and
found comprehensive assessments, risk assessments and care plans that provided clear guidance
for care staff. Plans included information relating to health conditions, communication, mobility,
nutrition, personal care, skin integrity, emotional well-being and preferred routines. Care staff
recorded specific actions needed to promote people's comfort, dignity and independence. People
are protected from harm through effective risk management arrangements. Care staff complete
comprehensive  risk assessments and  review them  regularly. Safeguarding procedures  are
understood and implemented by care  staff, who demonstrated confidence in recognising and
reporting concerns. The Local Health Board undertook their annual review between May and July
2026, with positive feedback given.

People and their representatives contribute to care planning and review processes. Records
demonstrate regular reviews and evidence the involvement of relatives and representatives. Care
plans reflect people's individual preferences and included person-centred information to help care
staff understand how best to support them. We saw very good examples of person-centred care.
Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of people's health needs and preferences. Daily
records are  detailed and show  staff monitor important aspects  of care  including  nutrition,
repositioning, continence care and general well-being. People have access to healthcare services
when  required. Health  professionals spoke  positively about communication and  partnership
working. We spoke with a visiting GP who told us they had no concerns, they confirmed referrals
are made promptly and professional advice is followed. Health monitoring systems support care
staff to identify concerns early and take appropriate action.

Medication is stored, managed, and administered safely. Medicines are stored securely in locked
medication rooms and administered in line with the prescribers’ instructions. Electronic medication
records  support  safe  administration  practices  and  provide  clear  oversight.  Medication
administration record (MAR) charts contain all required information and are completed correctly
with signatures when medication has been administered. Controlled drugs are also appropriately
stored and recorded. We saw evidence staff receive training on the administration of medication to
ensure they remain  sufficiently  skilled. The completion of routine medication audits ensures
practice remains safe and effective. Records show regular contact with health and social care
professionals when needed.

Infection control is well-managed, with appropriate measures in place to reduce risks. Staff have
access to and use personal protective equipment. An infection control policy guides staff on how to
manage outbreaks and protect people. The domestic team follow thorough cleaning routines, and
laundry processes are designed to minimise infection risks. Clinical waste is disposed of safely and
correctly.

       Environment                         Good



People  live in an environment that supports their well-being outcomes. The service provides
comfortable, welcoming and well-maintained accommodation. Throughout the inspection we found
the home clean, fresh and free from unpleasant odours. People and relatives spoke positively
about the environment and described it as pleasant and homely.

People personalise their bedrooms with photographs, furniture and possessions that are important
to them.  This  helps  maintain  identity,  familiarity and  comfort. Bedrooms  reflect  individual
preferences and supported people's sense of ownership and belonging.

People have access to a range of communal areas where they can socialise, participate in
activities or spend time quietly. The service provides lounges, dining areas, a cinema room and a
reminiscence area  ‘Curiosity  Corner’. We found communal spaces  bright, comfortable and
appropriately furnished. Garden areas were attractive and offered opportunities for people to spend
time outdoors when the weather permits.

People are supported through the availability of suitable equipment and adaptations. The provider
maintains equipment through regular servicing and testing programmes. We reviewed records
relating to  fire safety, electrical systems, water safety, hoists and  lifts and found appropriate
arrangements were in place. Safety features including call bells, window restrictors and monitoring
equipment supported people to remain safe.

The provider has effective systems to identify and manage environmental risks. Regular health and
safety checks, maintenance programmes and environmental audits support the ongoing safety of
people, visitors and staff. Emergency arrangements, including personal emergency evacuation
plans and a contingency plan, are available to support people in the event of an incident.

The environment promotes  dignity, privacy and independence. Bathrooms and  facilities are
accessible, and people can move around the home safely. Bilingual signage supports orientation
and accessibility. To support a specific individual Arabic signage is used for bathrooms.

We identified a small number of areas where the environment could be further enhanced. Storage
arrangements for some equipment, and the second-floor dining room felt cluttered at times due to
the layout and furniture used. However, these observations had a negligible impact on people and
did not detract from the overall quality of the environment.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from a service that is led by an experienced management team and supported by
established organisational governance arrangements. Staff, people and relatives spoke positively
about leadership within the service. Many described managers as approachable, supportive and
responsive. Staff consistently told us they felt listened to and valued. We were told, “Great place to
work, everyone is welcoming”, “Personally I enjoy the job, the residents make the job enjoyable”,
“This is my first time working in a care home and I love it here, the staff I work with are like family
and made me feel at home” and “We work like a family”.

Quality assurance systems provide effective oversight. Managers complete a range of regular
audits relating to medication, care planning, health and safety, infection prevention and control,
accidents and incidents and staffing. Governance arrangements operate at local, regional and
organisational levels, helping leaders monitor quality and drive improvement. The provider gathers
feedback through surveys, discussions and quality monitoring activities. Complaints are recorded
and addressed in accordance with organisational procedures. Opportunities remain to strengthen
some governance arrangements, including maintaining a central safeguarding log, increasing the
frequency of staff meetings and enhancing oversight of call bell response times.

The provider maintains good systems for supporting and developing staff. Training compliance
levels were high across a range of mandatory subjects and staff described training opportunities
positively. Supervision compliance exceeded 90% and staff confirmed they receive regular formal
support. Recruitment arrangements support the employment of suitable staff. The provider’s HR
department confirmed that all required pre-employment checks before staff begin work, including
Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks, identity verification, and references. These checks
help ensure staff are suitable to work with vulnerable people. Care staff hold registration with Social
Care Wales (SCW), and nurses maintain current professional registration.

The service has experienced increased staffing pressures more recently associated with supporting
people who require enhanced supervision. There has been some recent agency staff use; the
provider is block booking regular staff to ensure continuity. Senior management recognise this and
are actively reviewing staffing arrangements, including appointing a new activities co-ordinator and
considering additional staffing resources. The provider demonstrated awareness of areas requiring
development and a commitment to continuous improvement.

The service has a strong vision and ethos. Its aims, values, and delivery of support are set out in
the statement of purpose in a transparent way. A written guide is available for people in the service,
containing practical information about the service, and the support provided.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
