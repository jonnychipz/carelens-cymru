Summary:

 People experience good well-being outcomes because they are happy living at the home, they get
 on with the care workers who support them, and they praise the standard of care they receive.
 Relatives speak highly of the care provided and the communication they receive from the manager.
 People can receive their care in Welsh or English, according to their own individual preferences,
 because many of the care workers are bilingual.

 People have control over their day to day lives because they have choices available to them and
 their decisions are respected. Personal plans are in place; they are created with people and record
 their individual needs and preferences. Care workers are guided by the personal plans, and this
 means people receive good care, in the way they want to be supported. Activities are provided and
 people can choose if they wish to participate or not.

 People are supported to maintain their physical health; medication management is good and

The environment of the home meets the needs of the people who live there. It is clean, homely,

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 The service provides the Welsh language active offer. People can receive their service in Welsh, if
 this is their preference, as the service recognises the Welsh language and culture as an important
 part of people’s identity. People, and their relatives, told us being able to receive their service in
 Welsh is important to them. We heard people speak Welsh with each other, and with care workers,
 and people told us this was something they valued.

 People have positive relationships with the care workers providing their support and have made
 friends with other people living at the service. Every person we spoke with praised the care they
 receive, and relatives also spoke highly of the support provided. People told us they were made to
 feel welcomed when they first moved into the home and this had made it easier for them to settle
 and feel at home. People told us; “Staff bendigedig, clên a ffeind” (“Fantastic, jolly and kind staff”),
 “Staff yn lyfli yma” (“The staff here are lovely”), “Mae nhw dda hefo fi” (“They are good to me”),
 “Dim byd yn ormod o drafferth” (“Nothing is too much trouble”). We saw people had made friends,
 whom they spend their time with and sit with at mealtimes. Relatives told us they are always made
 to feel welcome when they visit

 Some activities are provided which allow people the opportunity to take part in games, quizzes and
 gentle physical exercises. People told us they enjoy these greatly and they look forward to the chair
 yoga sessions. Relatives and people told us they would like to see more activities provided as
 some people feel “The day can feel long sometimes”. We discussed this with the manager who told
 us they try to offer regular activities, and they will speak with people about what they would prefer.

 The accommodation is homely, bright and promotes people’s sense of well-being. All areas of the
 home are clean, well-presented and maintained. There are accessible gardens and outside seating
 areas available so people can spend time outside, if they want to. Health and safety checks are in
 place and overall, the home provides a safe environment for people to live in. We found work is not
 always completed promptly to address safety issues identified. This is an area for improvement,
 and we expect the service provider to take action.

 Care workers support people  to be healthy and  well. People’s physical and mental health
 conditions are known when they move into the service. People’s plans reflect how each condition is
 managed. Relatives told us their family member’s health had improved since moving into the home,
 with one person supported to regain their ability to walk. A balanced diet is provided, and a choice
 of food is available.

       Care & Support                       Good



Medication is safely managed, stored and administered. The manager and care workers told us the
recent changes made to medication management at the service has led to improvements being
made. We saw people receive their medication as prescribed, which supports them to manage their
health conditions and to stay well. Medication is stored safely, and arrangements are in place to
ensure medication is ordered and administered in a timely manner. Medication policies are in place
which record how medication is managed at the service. Care workers receive medication training,
and their competency is tested by the manager.

The manager creates personal plans with people, and their relatives. We saw personal plans
include people’s views about how they want to be supported and their preferred daily routines.
People confirmed care workers respect their choices, and they are aware of their individual likes
and dislikes. Personal plans are strength based; they record clearly what people can do for
themselves so each person can continue to be as independent as possible. Risk assessments are
used to manage known risks to people’s health and safety and are regularly reviewed and updated
following any changes.

The service provider has good safeguarding arrangements in place to keep people safe from harm.
Care workers receive safeguarding training and understand their responsibility to protect people
from abuse and neglect. The manager and RI oversee safeguarding matters appropriately. People
told us they feel safe living at the service and feel able to raise any concerns they may have.

Infection control measures are good at the home and as a result people are well protected from
infections. Infection control training is provided, and we saw care workers following good practice
whilst carrying out their duties. Arrangements are in place for the safe storage of clinical waste and
infection control audits are completed by the manager daily.

       Environment                         Good



Support is provided in a clean, homely and well-presented environment. Visitors to the care home
are unable to enter the home without staff authorisation. This protects people living at the home.


There is a choice of communal lounges and dining rooms available so people can choose where
they want to spend their day and where they want to have their meals. We saw some people prefer
to have their meals in their own rooms, and this was accommodated. Bath and shower rooms are
accessible, with lifting equipment provided if people require this assistance.


People’s own rooms are personalised with their own items from home. We saw each room was
different and reflected each person’s family life and their interests. People told us they are happy
with their rooms where they feel comfortable.


There is accessible garden and a decking space available, so people can spend time outside if
they wish. We saw the garden is well-kempt and seating is available. People and their families told
us they appreciate having pleasant outdoor space available to them, especially when they bring
younger family members to visit. We saw people going for a walk around the outside areas and
they told us they enjoyed being able to keep physically active.


Health and safety checks are in place; however, action is not always taken promptly to address
areas of the environment which are identified as being risks to people’s safety. Records show the
manager completes regular health and safety checks and the servicing of gas and electrical
appliances take place as required. The fire alarm is tested weekly, and the system is serviced as
required. During our visit we saw work was underway to address risks which had previously been
identified within the environment, and the RI provided assurances the work would be completed
over the coming weeks. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the service provider to
take action.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Overall, the provider has good arrangements in place to oversee the quality of the care provided
and to ensure the home is running smoothly. Records show the manager and RI undertake regular
audits to monitor the quality of service provided and proactively identify any areas of the service
which require improvements to be made. The RI visits the service on a formal basis every three
months and undertakes a review of the service provided, which they record in a report. We found
the service provider does not always react in a timely manner to improve the safety of the
environment, when risks have been identified. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the
service provider to take action.


Robust recruitment processes are in place, so people can be assured the care workers who
support them have been assessed as being of suitable character for their roles. Records show care
workers are provided with appropriate training and one-to-one supervision sessions which occur
regularly. Staffing levels are sufficient to meet people’s needs, and people told us they receive their
care and support when they need it, most of the time. Care workers feel supported in their roles
and they enjoy their work. We saw kitchen and housekeeping staff are employed so care workers
can focus their attention on supporting people.


People’s views are sought regarding the developments they would like to see at the service. The
service provider has arrangements in place to formally review the quality of the service, and this
involves speaking with people who use the service, their families and commissioners. The RI
undertakes a falls audit as part of the review, and they look at the action taken in response to
individual’s falls. We discussed this with the RI and explained how the audit should also consider
any themes, patterns and trends emerging from the falls in total, to ensure any possible action
required to improve falls management is identified and addressed.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
