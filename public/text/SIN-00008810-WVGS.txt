Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive well-being outcomes because they are supported to maintain choice,
 independence, relationships and meaningful lifestyles. Inspection evidence demonstrated people
 are involved in decisions about their daily lives, supported to manage risks positively and
 encouraged to participate in activities that matter to them. People have opportunities to maintain
 relationships with family, friends and their wider community, helping them remain connected and
 valued.

 People reported positive experiences of living at the service. Relatives consistently described
 family members as happy, settled and well cared for. One relative stated their family member had
 improved since moving into the home and described the service as “wonderful,” whilst others
 reported people were “delighted,” had “no worries” and were supported to maintain independence,
 dignity and choice. Families expressed confidence that people were safe and treated with respect.

 There was strong evidence that people are protected from abuse and neglect. Safeguarding
 systems, risk management processes and staff knowledge were well established. Inspection
 evidence demonstrated that concerns were identified, reported and managed appropriately, with
 support from external agencies where required. Feedback from professionals highlighted positive
 safeguarding practice, effective risk management and good multidisciplinary working.

 People benefit from meaningful opportunities to engage in activities and social interactions that
 enhance well-being. During the inspection, people were observed participating in exercise and
 entertainment activities within a positive and enjoyable atmosphere. Activities were varied and
 person-centred, with opportunities for outings, hobbies, social engagement and maintaining
 important relationships. Feedback highlighted that activities and social interaction contribute
 positively to quality of life and emotional well-being.

 The environment supports well-being outcomes by providing safe, comfortable and accessible
 accommodation. People have access to communal areas, private spaces, outdoor facilities and
 equipment that help them maintain independence and enjoy daily life. Relatives consistently
 described the home as clean, welcoming and supportive of people’s well-being.

 Strengths included positive risk management, strong safeguarding arrangements, meaningful
 activities, personalised support and overwhelmingly positive feedback from relatives and
 professionals. No significant concerns were identified within this theme. Welsh language provision
 continues to be developed; however, the service demonstrated a commitment to promoting
 bilingual resources and supporting Welsh language needs.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive person-centred care that reflects their assessed needs, preferences and outcomes.
Care files evidenced comprehensive assessments, care plans, risk assessments, regular reviews
and timely referrals to health professionals. People’s communication, nutrition, hydration, mobility
and skin integrity needs were effectively monitored and managed.

People are protected from harm through effective safeguarding arrangements, trained staff and
established risk management processes. Safeguarding records demonstrated appropriate action,
partnership working and oversight, while visiting professionals spoke positively about safeguarding
practice, risk management and multidisciplinary working.

Medicines were managed safely, with appropriate arrangements for storage, administration,
auditing, staff training and management oversight. Inspection evidence demonstrated compliance
with relevant standards and effective medicines governance.

People benefit from a clean environment and effective infection prevention and control measures.
Staff followed good hygiene practices, PPE was available and used appropriately, and systems
were in place to minimise the risk of infection.

People benefited from meaningful activities, social engagement and support to maintain health and
well-being. Observations identified a positive atmosphere, with people participating in activities
including exercise sessions, entertainment and community opportunities. Residents were offered
choice, encouraged to maintain relationships and supported to take part in activities that mattered
to them. Feedback from relatives consistently described people as happy, settled and well cared
for. Professionals also commented positively on staff knowledge of residents, communication and
partnership working.

Strengths included personalised care planning, effective healthcare engagement, strong
safeguarding arrangements, robust medication management and positive outcomes reported by
relatives and professionals. Families described communication as excellent and expressed
confidence in the care provided.

Concerns were limited. One care file contained an expired Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards
(DoLS) authorisation awaiting local authority renewal, and some feedback suggested additional
staffing during busy periods may further enhance support. These issues were being monitored and
did not significantly impact outcomes for people.

Overall, evidence demonstrates people receive safe, effective and responsive care that promotes
well-being, protects them from harm and supports them to achieve positive outcomes.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a safe, comfortable and well-maintained environment that supports their well-being
outcomes. The home provides a range of clean, welcoming communal areas, including lounges, a
conservatory and a secure garden with seating, planting and areas for social interaction. Bedrooms
observed were personalised, accessible and equipped to meet individual needs. People and
relatives consistently described the home as clean, comfortable and homely, with relatives
reporting the environment was “spotless” and supported their family members’ well-being.

The provider has effective arrangements in place to identify and manage environmental risks. The
premises were secure, with controlled access systems, CCTV and secure outdoor areas. Health
and safety systems, environmental risk assessments, servicing arrangements and maintenance
records demonstrated ongoing oversight of safety. Fire safety arrangements were well managed,
with current risk assessments, maintenance records, staff training, fire drills and personal
emergency evacuation plans in place. Equipment including lifts, hoists and specialist aids were
appropriately maintained and available to meet people’s needs.

Observation of the environment found communal areas, bathrooms, laundry facilities and external
spaces to be clean and generally well maintained. Infection prevention and control measures were
embedded, cleaning schedules were in use and adequate supplies and waste management
arrangements were evident. People had access to safe outdoor space, multiple communal areas
and facilities that promoted independence, choice and social interaction. A full-time maintenance
officer provided ongoing maintenance support and there was evidence of recent refurbishment
work.

There were a small number of issues identified during the inspection. The front of the property
would benefit from some cosmetic improvement. A kitchen secure cabinet for the storage of
chemicals that may cause harm was temporarily unlocked whilst in use and staff were reminded to
ensure storage remained secure. Some bedroom door locks were not functioning correctly and
required repair to promote privacy. The home was also experiencing boiler-related issues affecting
hot water provision; however, hot water remained available during the inspection and the manager
submitted the required notification. One washing machine was awaiting repair, although alternative
arrangements ensured laundry services continued safely.

Overall, evidence demonstrates that people benefit from an environment that is safe, secure, clean
and suitable for their needs. Identified concerns were minor, promptly addressed or subject to
ongoing management oversight, and did not significantly impact people’s safety or well-being. The
environment supports positive outcomes through effective maintenance, risk management and
facilities that promote comfort, dignity and quality of life.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People experience positive outcomes because the service is well led, with clear governance
arrangements, effective oversight and systems that support assessment, care planning, monitoring
and review. Leaders are visible, approachable and responsive, and staff understand their roles,
responsibilities and lines of accountability. The Responsible Individual maintains regular oversight
through statutory visits and six-monthly quality reviews.

Quality assurance processes are established and include audits, quality reviews, stakeholder
feedback, complaints monitoring and service improvement planning. RI visits to the service and
quality of care review records were in place, a quality assurance policy was available, and the
annual return had been submitted and published. Evidence demonstrated that information gathered
is used to review practice and support continuous improvement.

People benefit from a stable and experienced workforce. Recruitment processes were robust,
staffing levels were assessed as sufficient and agency staff were not routinely used. Staff receive
supervision, appraisal and training, with management maintaining oversight of workforce
development. Staff reported feeling valued, supported and provided with clear direction.

Feedback from residents, relatives, staff and visiting professionals was overwhelmingly positive.
Relatives described management as approachable, responsive and communicative, while
professionals reported improvements in leadership, effective partnership working and confidence in
the manager. Stakeholders consistently described the service as person-centred, supportive and
well managed. These arrangements help people feel safe, respected, listened to and involved in
decisions about their care.

The service demonstrates a culture of continuous improvement. Previous inspection
recommendations relating to training oversight, health and safety monitoring, medication
management and quality assurance had been addressed. Minor areas for development included
reviewing and updating the Statement of Purpose, improving how overall training compliance is
presented and ensuring equitable access to development opportunities for all staff. Some
respondents also suggested additional staffing during busy periods and increased community
opportunities for people using the service.

Overall, leadership and management arrangements provide strong assurance that people receive
safe, high-quality, person-centred care within a well-governed service that promotes accountability,
quality improvement and positive outcomes.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
