Summary:

 Bodelwyddan is a residential care home located in Rhosneigr, a coastal village on the west coast of
 Anglesey. The home is close to the beach and within easy reach of local amenities, including
 shops, a post office and community facilities.

 Well-being requires improvement because the service does not consistently ensure people’s well-
 being through effective care planning, meaningful activities and up-to-date risk assessments.
 Systems do not clearly demonstrate how staff plan, monitor and review people’s outcomes or
 support individuals to engage in activities and maintain their interests. People do benefit from
 respectful interactions, choice, and effective communication.

 Care and support is good because people experience positive relationships with care staff who
 provide attentive and compassionate support. Care staff understand people’s needs and deliver
 care which meets their day-to-day requirements, helping people feel safe and well supported.

 The environment is good because the service provides a safe, clean and a well-maintained setting

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People living at Bodelwyddan experience warm, caring relationships. Care staff demonstrate
 kindness, respect and a genuine commitment to supporting individuals. During the inspection, we
 observed positive and meaningful interactions, and people and their families consistently describe
 care staff as attentive and compassionate. Care staff know people well and support them in ways
 which promote comfort, familiarity and trust. A stable workforce contributes to continuity of care and
 helps build strong, supportive relationships.

 People exercise choice in their day-to-day lives, including routines and meal preferences. Care
 staff respond positively to individual wishes where possible. Communication is effective, including
 through the use of the Welsh language where appropriate, supporting people’s sense of identity
 and inclusion. These factors contribute to a positive lived experience for many individuals and
 reflect a caring culture within the home. While people experience aspects of good well-being in
 practice, the service does not consistently evidence how it plans, reviews and sustains this. Care
 staff do not regularly update care plans and outcome-focused documentation to reflect people’s
 current needs, preferences and aspirations. This limits assurance the service actively plans and
 monitors people’s well-being.

 There is limited formal structure around activities. Although some activities take place, for example
 singers attend. Care records do not clearly show how care staff support people to engage in
 activities, maintain interests or achieve personal goals. Recording systems do not consistently
 show how care staff promote independence, choice and participation in daily life. There is also a
 clear gap between the care we observed and the care staff record, meaning the service cannot fully
 evidence how it achieves well-being outcomes.

 The service gathers feedback from people and families largely informally, which provides limited
 evidence of structured engagement such as meetings or surveys. This reduces assurance that
 individuals’ views consistently inform care delivery and service development. The provider
 demonstrates a positive and open attitude towards improvement and acknowledges the need to
 strengthen these areas. Leaders show a clear willingness to develop more structured approaches
 to activities, care planning and engagement with people and families. With improved systems and
 clearer documentation, the service has the potential to evidence the good care it already delivers
 and further enhance people’s well-being.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive care and support from care staff who demonstrate attentiveness, respect and
responsiveness to their individual needs. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of the
people they support and provide care in ways which align with people’s preferences and routines.
Feedback from individuals and families is positive. People tell us the care they receive is good and
they are happy with the support care staff provide. People also tell us they feel safe. One family
member says, “I am very happy with the care that my mum gets,” and another tells us they can
contact the owner or manager at any time to discuss anything. We saw staff delivering care in a
calm and compassionate manner.

 People experience a positive health and wellbeing because their healthcare needs are met. Care
staff seek support from healthcare professionals when required and respond to changes in people’s
health needs. Care staff understand safeguarding processes drawing on their training and
experience to respond to any concerns appropriately. Care staff administer and store medication
safely. We found records of medication administration to be consistently fully completed. Infection
prevention and control practices are effective and support a safe environment. Care staff
demonstrate a clear awareness of people’s needs, drawing on their knowledge, experience and
familiarity with individuals. This supports continuity of care and helps ensure staff meet people’s
day-to-day needs. Care and support would be strengthened through improvements to care
planning. While people experience positive outcomes, care plans are not consistently in place or
regularly updated. Despite this, people appear comfortable and well supported, and care staff show
commitment to providing good care.

The service provides a safe, clean and generally well-maintained environment that supports
people’s safety, comfort and independence. The premises meet the needs of the people living
there. Bedrooms are personalised and reflect individuals’ preferences, which helps create a homely
and welcoming atmosphere. People access spaces which support both independence and social
interaction, and the environment enables people to move around safely with appropriate support.
Staff carry out regular maintenance checks to ensure the environment remains safe and functional.

       Environment                         Good




The service provides a safe, clean and generally well-maintained environment to support people’s
safety, comfort and independence. The premises are suitable for the needs of the people living
there, and during the inspection we observed infection prevention and control standards are
maintained throughout the home.

Bedrooms are personalised and reflect individuals’ preferences, contributing to a homely and
welcoming atmosphere. People have access to spaces which support both independence and
social interaction. People can move around safely with appropriate support. Maintenance systems
are in place, and regular checks are undertaken to ensure safety and functionality.

Environmental risk management systems are evident, including the use of Personal Emergency
Evacuation Plans (PEEPs), which demonstrate awareness of people’s needs in emergency
situations. These systems support the safety of individuals and reduce risks within the environment.

The environment is generally comfortable, welcoming and meets people’s needs. People live in
homely surroundings which support their safety and wellbeing. While most areas are well
maintained, some aspects of décor and furnishings show signs of wear, these do not impact on
people’s safety but present opportunities to further enhance the overall presentation and comfort of
the homes. The provider demonstrates a proactive approach to maintaining a safe environment
and responds to maintenance needs as they arise.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



The service benefits from visible, approachable and committed leadership. Staff, people and
families speak positively about the management team and describe them as supportive and
responsive. Leaders promote a caring culture within the home and demonstrate a commitment to
people’s care. Managers maintain regular communication with staff, including through staff
meetings, and create an environment where staff feel able to raise concerns or ask questions. Care
staff receive supervision and appraisal, which supports their development and practice. This
approach provides a positive foundation for further development.

Improvements are needed to quality assurance processes. Governance and management systems
are not consistently and effectively used to provide robust oversight of the service. The provider
has not yet developed a fully structured quality assurance framework and uses limited auditing
processes. As a result, leaders do not always identify or address issues such as gaps in care
planning, risk management and record keeping through formal systems. This reduces assurance
the service consistently monitors and manages risk.

The service needs to develop systems for gathering feedback. The service relies mainly on
informal feedback from people and families and does not routinely use structured methods such as
meetings or surveys. This limits the service’s ability to show how it uses people’s views to inform
improvement. The service has some gaps in policy governance. The service does not fully align all
policies with Welsh legislation. Recruitment activity is limited, as staff remain in post and turnover is
low. However, the provider does not consistently maintain complete recruitment records. Records
show gaps in application information and required references. The provider has requested
references, but they are not available at the time of inspection. All staff have up-to-date disclosure
and barring service (DBS) checks in place. However, incomplete recruitment documentation does
not fully demonstrate the provider follows robust recruitment procedures in line with regulatory
requirements. This is a requirement to ensure care staff are safe and suitable to work with adults at
risk.

Leaders have implemented some systems, but they have not yet fully embedded them to
consistently assure quality and compliance. The provider shows a clear willingness to improve, and
with stronger governance systems, structured audits and improved oversight, the service is well
placed to develop further.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
