Summary:

 Woodfield Care Home is a nursing home set in a countryside location, providing care and support
 to people with nursing needs. We found outcomes for people are Good across all four inspection
 themes: Well‑being, Care and Support, Environment, and Leadership and Management. People
 experience positive well‑being outcomes and are supported by knowledgeable and motivated staff
 who know them well. Care and support is person‑centred and delivered with kindness and respect.
 Families and professionals gave consistently positive feedback about the quality of care. People
 live in a clean, safe, and homely environment that supports comfort, dignity, and well‑being.
 Leaders are visible and approachable and promote an inclusive culture. While some systems
 require strengthening, these have not impacted on people’s outcomes.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good well‑being outcomes and are supported to live comfortably and
 meaningfully at Woodfield Care Home. We observed people spending time in communal areas
 throughout the day, with staff providing sensitive and respectful encouragement to support
 engagement and social interaction. The communal areas were calm and welcoming, and people
 appeared relaxed and settled. Staff consistently offer people choice in their daily routines, including
 where they spend time and how they engage with others, and people are involved in decisions
 about how they wish to be supported. Interactions between staff and people are warm and
 respectful, clearly supporting people to feel valued and listened to.

 People are supported to engage in meaningful activities. Increased staffing levels have enabled a
 greater focus on engagement and social interaction, allowing staff to spend more time supporting
 people in ways that matter to them. Woodfield Care Home provides responsive and spontaneous
 activities throughout the day, alongside a more structured programme. Activities include listening to
 music, singing, Montessori‑based activity stations and pet therapy, which aim to promote wellbeing
 through meaningful stimulation, engagement, and social interaction.

 The promotion of Welsh language and culture is evident. Some staff are learning and using Welsh
 phrases and greetings, which supports people’s identity and sense of belonging. Families spoke
 positively about the atmosphere within the home, with one family member telling us, “When I visit,
 there is always the warmest welcome from all staff. They are so attentive and caring.”

 People are supported to live healthily and safely in a secure and homely environment. Staffing
 levels are appropriate to meet people’s needs and support them safely. Safeguarding training is
 mandatory for all staff, and appropriate Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) are in place for
 people who lack capacity. Security arrangements help keep people safe, and the provider
 continues to invest in the service to further enhance outcomes for people.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive consistently good, person‑centred care and support that reflects their assessed
needs, preferences, and personal outcomes. Daily care records demonstrate that people’s needs
are met and show continuity in how care is delivered by staff who know people well. Records
evidence that care is provided in line with personal plans, and appropriate referrals are made to
health and social care professionals. Effective communication with external services supports
timely and coordinated care when required.

Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of people as individuals. Care staff spoke
knowledgeably about people’s life histories, preferences, routines, and emotional needs, and
described how these inform the way care is delivered. We observed care provided in a manner that
consistently upheld dignity, respect, and individuality. Staff adapt their communication to meet
people’s needs, including offering reassurance and emotional support where required. Most people
were observed spending time outside their rooms and engaging in communal areas, supported by
staff who knew them well. A staff member told us that care at Woodfield Care Home is “Given
compassionately and professionally” which reflected what we observed.

Personal plans are strengths‑based and outcome‑focused, providing clear guidance for staff on
how to support people effectively. Plans include information about routines, preferences, and
communication needs. People’s personal plans are informed by their interests and preferred
approaches, supporting consistent and appropriate responses from staff and promoting positive
outcomes. Care plans are supported by relevant risk assessments and appropriate equipment,
such as sensor mats where required. Records show that plans and risk assessments are reviewed
regularly and that agreed actions are generally followed. Some areas of documentation require
further strengthening, including the development of people’s life histories and improved consistency
in recording outcomes within some care documents. The service has acknowledged these areas
and has plans in place to address them.

Feedback from families, staff, and professionals was overwhelmingly positive. Families told us they
feel confident and reassured that their relatives’ needs are met. One family member said they
“Would not hesitate to recommend the service,” while a visiting professional described the care
provided as “Of a high standard.”

People’s medicines are managed safely and effectively, supported by clear policies, accurate
records, and regular audits. Medicines are stored securely, temperatures are monitored, and
confidential documentation is maintained appropriately. People are protected from the risk of
infection through effective infection prevention and control arrangements. Staff demonstrate good
understanding of infection control practices, and the environment is clean, well maintained, and
supported by regular oversight.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a clean, safe, and homely environment that promotes comfort, dignity, and
well‑being. The home has a welcoming and relaxed atmosphere, and we observed people making
good use of communal areas throughout the day to relax, socialise, and engage in activities. These
areas were well presented and supported people’s independence and social interaction. Bedrooms
are personalised, inviting, and reflect people’s individuality, contributing positively to people’s sense
of home.

The service has two outdoor spaces, an internal courtyard and a decked area which people can
access. The internal courtyard provides natural light and an attractive shared space, and there is a
small outdoor seating area that people can access with the support of care staff.

The environment is well maintained, and continued efforts are made to keep the service in a good
state of repair. Leaders have worked to address recommendations from previous inspections, and
staff and professionals told us they have noticed improvements. Relevant adaptations and
equipment are in place to meet people’s needs, including specialist beds, call systems, and moving
and handling equipment. Bathrooms, showers, and toilets are designed to support privacy, dignity,
safety, and accessibility.

The provider ensures risks to health and safety are effectively managed. The service has a secure
entry system and maintains a visitors’ book to support safety. Mandatory fire safety checks are
completed routinely, and certificates for gas, fire detection, and electrical equipment are up to date.
The service has acknowledged the recommendation to introduce fire evacuation drills and has
confirmed plans to implement these to provide further assurance of people’s safety.

Appropriate arrangements are in place for the safe storage and control of hazardous substances
(COSHH), with items stored securely and risk assessed. Overall, the provider maintains a safe,
clean, and well‑organised environment, supported by effective systems and good staff compliance
with safety procedures.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management at Woodfield Care Home are visible, approachable, and responsive,
contributing to a positive and inclusive culture. Staff consistently described managers as supportive
and told us they feel able to raise concerns and seek guidance. One member of staff told us that
leadership and management are “Always available, you can go to them with anything and they will
help.”

Professionals told us communication with management is good and that recommendations from
reviews are acted upon. Families also provided very positive feedback, telling us they feel confident
in the care provided, value being kept well informed, and gain peace of mind knowing their relatives
are safe and well cared for. One family member described Woodfield Care Home’s management as
“Very efficient and caring.”

People are supported by staff with the appropriate skills, knowledge, and experience to meet their
care and support needs. We sampled staff files and saw safe recruitment practices in place,
including appropriate background and Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks, which are
renewed as required. New staff are supported through an effective induction process, and staff told
us they feel valued and supported in their roles.

During the inspection, we noted that systems intended to support staff, such as regular supervision
and annual appraisals require strengthening, as gaps were identified. Some staff also told us
access to some face‑to‑face training could be improved. However, staff continued to refer positively
to the management team. We were assured that the service would guarantee staff are in receipt of
regular supervision and appraisals to ensure that staff are fully supported and in turn able to
effectively continue to deliver good care to people.

We also identified some gaps in Responsible Individual visits and quality of care reviews. These
gaps have not adversely impacted outcomes for people or staff, which is reflected in the
overwhelmingly positive feedback received from staff, professionals, and families about leadership,
management, and the continued improvement of the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
