Summary:

 Romilly Nursing Home provides care and support for people, some of whom live with dementia,
 and many require nursing care.

 ‘Wellbeing’ is rated ‘excellent’ because people feel valued, empowered, and supported to live well
 by a knowledgeable, caring, and highly committed staff team. Inspirational, inclusive activity
 programmes have a positive impact on people’s physical and mental health.

 ‘Care and support’ is rated ‘good’ because care is delivered by a kind staff team in accordance with
 detailed personal plans. Improvements have been made to monitor people’s health.

 ‘Environment’ is rated ‘good’ because it meets people’s needs and supports positive outcomes.
 Oversight and maintenance of the environment are strong. Outdoor space is limited but access to
 the wider community is arranged.

 Leadership and management’ is rated ‘good’ because governance arrangements are effective,
 quality assurance systems are strengthening, and a supportive culture promotes safe, consistent
 care.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People living at Romilly Nursing Home experience a high quality of life and are supported to
 achieve strong wellbeing outcomes. Throughout the inspection, people consistently told us they
 feel safe, valued, and in control of their day‑to‑day lives. The service demonstrates a strong
 commitment to promoting both physical and emotional wellbeing, underpinned by a kind, inclusive
 culture.

 People are supported to make meaningful choices about their care and how they spend their time.
 We observed staff offering people options in relation to personal care, daily routines, and activities.
 One person told us, “I wander around myself and go downstairs if I want,” while another explained
 their preference to remain in their room was fully respected.

 The service has developed an inspirational and inclusive programme of activities promoting
 physical health, mental wellbeing, and community connection. Activities are tailored to people’s
 interests and abilities, including meaningful one‑to‑one engagement for people cared for in bed.
 One relative shared, “They decorated the home and we were able to have mum’s 90th birthday
 party here.” Another person told us, “The carers take me to the park,” highlighting positive access
 to the community.

 People feel listened to and involved. Communication with families is open and effective, with
 regular reviews and prompt updates when needed. A relative told us, “Communication is really
 good. If anything is wrong, they will call.” People also demonstrated confidence in raising concerns,
 telling us, “I can speak to all of them, including the manager.”

 Health and wellbeing are proactively supported. Staff show strong knowledge of people’s individual
 health needs and aspirations, working closely with professionals to prevent deterioration and
 promote healthier outcomes. One family described the care as “Excellent,” while another told us,
 “It’s not the swankiest place but the standard of care is really good.” We saw an example of
 exceptional support for a person aiming to lose weight, resulting in improved health and
 confidence.

 Relationships are warm and respectful, and people told us they feel genuinely cared for. People
 consistently described staff as “kind,” “patient,” and “wonderful.” One person said, “Staff treat me
 very well,” and another shared, “My family and friends can come anytime.” The provider is making
 exceptional efforts to strengthen the Welsh Active Offer, with visible bilingual signage and staff
 confidently using basic Welsh phrases, further supporting people’s sense of identity and belonging.

       Care & Support                       Good



People living at the service receive care and support which is responsive and focused on promoting
their overall wellbeing. There has been a marked improvement in the way people’s health needs
are monitored and managed. Robust systems are now in place to track people’s physical health,
including weight, nutrition, skin integrity and mobility. This has enabled staff to identify concerns
earlier and involve relevant health professionals in a timelier way. Monitoring information is used
effectively to inform care decisions, supporting people to maintain or improve their health
outcomes. People are supported to attend routine appointments. We saw improvements to the
dining experience, with staff providing more attentive and respectful support during mealtimes. We
saw people mostly receive sensitive and warm interactions while tasks are being undertaken. The
service has made significant investment in social and engagement opportunities. These
opportunities have a positive impact on people, helping them to remain socially connected, feel
valued and enjoy everyday life.

The service provider ensures people have good outcomes as personal plans contain sufficient
information. Care staff are guided by care plans containing detailed information supporting staff to
provide safe and appropriate care. Some of these are exceptional, based on strong assessments
and risk assessments. Choice is emphasised in plans, with people encouraged to retain as much
control as possible over their daily routines. Some strengthening is required to ensure care
planning is consistently based on people’s own desired goals. Review documentation does not
consistently demonstrate the person’s involvement. Daily records have improved in accuracy to
reflect the care and support offered and received.

Medication systems are safe. Trained and competent nursing assistants provide routine medication
administration. Qualified nurses provide an expanded range of medication administration and
treatments, such as injections. There is sufficient stock, good recording systems and we saw
responsive care when one person requested pain relief. There is clear monitoring of people who
receive medication ‘as and when required,’ recording the effectiveness.

Infection prevention measures are followed. We saw personal protective equipment (PPE) such as
gloves and aprons were readily available and used appropriately.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a home which meets their needs, supporting wellbeing and independence. The
building is bright, warm and welcoming. Signage helps people to navigate to areas of the home and
there is easy access to various floors through use of a passenger lift. Corridors are wide and
communal areas are well lit. Bedrooms viewed during the inspection were personalised and
reflected people’s preferences, life histories and sense of ownership. Rooms include sinks with hot
and cold running water, with some being ensuite. Where needed, specialist equipment such as
profiling beds, airflow mattresses and hoists are available to support mobility, comfort and health
needs. Although outdoor space on site is limited, people are actively supported to access the wider
community. Regular, supported visits to nearby parks and local amenities help ensure people
benefit from fresh air, exercise and time outdoors. This demonstrates a commitment to promoting
wellbeing beyond the physical environment of the home.


Communal living areas promote wellbeing. We saw the lounge and dining room used to support
social interaction and meaningful activity. The home reflects Welsh identity and culture through
bilingual signage, décor and artwork created by people living at the service. Reception areas
provide a positive first impression for visitors, and families are able to visit easily. Staff support
people to maintain contact with families in ways that respect their preferences.


Strong systems are in place to monitor and maintain the environment. Regular checks of gas,
electricity and equipment are completed, and fire safety arrangements are effective. Firefighting
equipment is in place and within test date. Security arrangements ensure people are protected
while still able to access areas safely where appropriate. Prompt responses to equipment
breakdowns demonstrate the provider’s awareness of the impact the environment can have on
people’s comfort and wellbeing. Cleanliness standards across the home are good. Cleaning
schedules and checklists are in use, particularly in shared facilities such as bathrooms and toilets.
We observed some natural wear and tear of furniture surfaces, and decor. These did not present a
risk to people and are consistent with a busy, well-used home. The provider has recognised these
issues and has already planned for redecoration works, alongside refreshing older equipment and
addressing minor repairs.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People who live at Romilly Nursing Home benefit from leadership and management arrangements
which provide effective oversight, promote stability, and support continuous improvement. The
service has demonstrated a strong commitment to learning, and has taken timely, constructive
action to address areas previously identified as requiring improvement.


The provider has clear governance arrangements in place. Policies and standard operating
procedures are available, and staff have access to these. Current practice is aligned with the
service’s statement of purpose and supports the delivery of good quality care. There is an
emphasis on openness, transparency, and learning. Regular Responsible Individual (RI) visits and
resulting reports provide assurance and drive improvement.


Leadership within the service is visible and approachable. People, relatives and staff described a
positive, welcoming culture, supported by an open‑door approach from managers. Concerns and
complaints are managed professionally. Leaders show a willingness to work collaboratively with
external professionals, which has resulted in improvements to people’s health and wellbeing
outcomes.


Audit and monitoring systems have significantly strengthened since the last inspection. The service
now uses a wide range of audits to monitor care quality and safety, including medication, nutrition,
skin integrity, nurse call response times and environmental checks. These systems are used
effectively to identify risks, inform decision‑making and improve practice; for example, by adjusting
workflows to ensure essential care such as repositioning is not missed during staff handover time.
Monitoring arrangements include regular review of the dining experience, supporting improvements
in the practice of care workers.


Staffing arrangements support safe, timely care. There are sufficient numbers of staff, supported by
a robust recruitment process, high‑quality induction and comprehensive training programme. Staff
are well supported through supervision, appraisal and development opportunities, including
leadership and vocational qualifications. Staff spoke positively about feeling valued, supported and
being encouraged to develop their skills. Many acknowledge the improvements made within the
service around meal times and activities. All staff show a good level of knowledge to support their
practice, confirming the quality of learning opportunities provided through the internal training
coordinator and access to accredited external trainers. This training includes coverage of specialist
subjects to suit people’s needs.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
