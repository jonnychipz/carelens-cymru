Summary

Forestry House provides care and support for six young people between the age of five and
nineteen. Young people are supported to achieve positive well-being outcomes and care
staff are motivated and inclusive in developing strategies to increase young people’s
engagement.

Since the last inspection the service provider has focused on the suitability of their service
for young people and sustaining and improving the areas of development identified in 2021.
The overall governance and management of the service is a good standard and provides
assurances it is committed to continuous improvement and development.

Work is required to continue improving the environment, with particular attention to
bedrooms and bathroom facilities and the manager advised there is a development plan in
place.

Well-being

Young people are provided with a guide to the service, which is user friendly and covers
most required information, however it does not support young people to access the right
information when they need it as the guide does not include guidance on how to raise a
complaint and important contact information for the Responsible Individual and Care
Inspectorate Wales (CIW). Young people told us they would not know who to speak to or
how to raise a concern.

The service provider promotes the physical and emotional well-being of young people,
registering them with universal health services including a General Practitioner (GP),
Dentist and creating individual health plans. Care staff promote healthy habits and routines,
and records demonstrate routine welfare checks are completed for young people spending
time in their bedrooms. Young people are encouraged to attend education, although not all
young people attend consistently. As a result, the service provider has sought alternative
educational support for young people who have disengaged form mainstream school.

Care staff ensure young people’s individual circumstances are considered and take
proactive action to ensure they do things which matter to them. Young people are
supported to practice their religion and transport is provided to ensure they can celebrate
their religion within a community they are comfortable in. When young people raise
concerns over their placement, the service advocates on their behalf through Local
Authority meetings and in consultation with other external professionals to ensure their
voice is heard.

Young people are cared for by a team of care staff who have completed safeguarding
training and understand their role in protecting young people from harm. The manager
completes good quality safeguarding records including a detailed chronology and evidence
of the outcome from the Local Authority Safeguarding Board is documented. Safeguarding
referrals are submitted in a timely manner and CIW are notified appropriately. Young
people told us they feel safe at Forestry House.

The service regularly engages young people in house meetings where topics such as
behaviours, Christmas and menus are discussed. Care staff should ensure the focus of
meetings are on the well-being and voice of young people and not a negative discussion on
incidents or behaviours, this has been raised by the Responsible Individual within their
regulatory visits.

Care and Support


The service provider completes pre-admission paperwork, including transition visits to
provide assurances to young people and Local Authorities they can provide young people
with their assessed care and support needs. Provider assessments are completed which
offer detail of young people’s needs and these are reviewed. However, further development
is needed to ensure records detail how care staff will support young people’s outcomes.
Compatibility risk assessments are completed and evidence how risk will be mitigated to
ensure the safety of young people. External professionals told us Forestry House is a good
service.

Young people have personal plans which are outcome focused and detail clear actions on
how care staff will support them to achieve their well-being outcomes. Records include a
child friendly plan written in the voice of young people and an outcome status log which
celebrates the achievements they have made. The manager and care staff complete three
monthly evaluations where they review and plan young people’s outcome plans and
although engagement from young people is limited care staff actively try to capture their
voice throughout.

Risk assessments are completed at the point of admission and records include possible
triggers and both preventative and reactive strategies to keep young people safe from
harm. Records demonstrate risk assessments are reviewed appropriately and when new
risky behaviours are identified, further risk assessments are completed.

Although young people receive keyworker sessions regularly, more work is needed to
ensure this time is planned, purposeful and supports them to achieve their well-being
outcomes. Care staff encourage young people to have control over their day- to -day life by
completing their own activity planners, supporting their independent living skills, and
completing monthly feedback surveys on topics such as their safety and the menu. Young
people complete food shopping with the support of care staff and many prepare their own
meals. Care staff have implemented incentive charts to support young people to achieve
positive outcomes and consequences appeared appropriate, however some feel
consequences can be too harsh.

 Environment


Forestry House is a five-bedroom home, with the addition of an independent flat. There are
large grounds which are suitably maintained. Effort has been made to provide an
environment conducive of a family home, specifically in the downstairs, communal areas
with soft furnishing and colourful paintings. Young people told us they like living at Forestry
house. Work is required to improve the upstairs rooms.

Young people are supported to personalise their bedroom’s; however, we saw limited
personalisation of rooms and most bedrooms appeared unkempt. Storage for young people
is limited and support is not provided to organise and take care of their belongings, however
young people told us they have everything they need in their bedrooms. Damage has been
caused to the bathroom which, although reported to maintenance has not been rectified.
The bathroom is unkempt and has many areas of damage and urgent attention is required
to ensure young people have access to a clean, suitably functioning bathroom. The
Responsible Individual has provided CIW assurances environmental improvements will be
completed in the new year.

The independent flat is currently not in use due to extensive damage and is undergoing a
full refurbishment. The service provider told us there are no plans for any young people to
use the flat in the near future.

Maintenance records evidence care staff take appropriate action to ensure the environment
promotes the well-being of young people and risk of harm is mitigated. However,
maintenance works are not always completed in a suitable time frame or finished to a good
standard. Health and Safety checks including fire evacuations are completed regularly and
visitors are required to sign into the visitors book.

Leadership and Management


Forestry House supports young people with a full-time manager and a full complement of
care staff, providing consistency of care and supporting positive relationships. There is a
senior management structure in place including the Responsible Individual and a Regional
Manager who are present and supportive within the service. Care staff spoke positively
about the Responsible Individual and Regional Manager, and the encouraging and positive
culture which is demonstrated by all who work at the service

Young people can be assured they are cared for by care staff who have been subject to
safe recruitment checks. The service provider completes regulatory checks prior to the
appointment of new care staff including identification checks, verification of references and
checks to the Disclosure and Baring Service (DBS). New care staff complete a corporate
inhouse induction, however many care staff provided feedback to CIW stating the content is
not always appropriate or purposeful for those working with care experienced young
people.

Improvements have been made to the training and supervision of care staff and the service
now provides young people with a complement of fully trained care staff in line with the
Statement of Purpose and bespoke training has been provided to ensure care and support
needs of young people can be met. Young people can be assured care staff are supported
and supervised and are reflective in their practice to continue developing their skills and
knowledge. A focus on praise, well-being and development is evidenced throughout
supervision records. Care staff told us supervision sessions are purposeful and concerns
about practice are managed appropriately.

The Responsible Individual visits the service and completes their regulatory reports.
Regulatory reports evidence consultation with young people, care staff and external
professionals and identify strengths and areas for improvement. Actions are listed and
reviewed during each period of visits. Not all care staff understood the role of the
Responsible Individual but stated they would be confident in speaking to them or the
Regional Manager if they were concerned about young people or the service provision.
