Summary:

 Clydach Court is a residential care home in Tonypandy, operated by Rhondda Cynon Taff County
 Borough Council. The service is set over two communities, providing support to older adults with
 care needs who live with dementia in one, and supporting adults with a learning disability in the
 other.


 Well-being is good because people are supported to experience positive outcomes which promote
 their overall well-being. Care and support requires improvement because, while people receive
 support from care staff who understand and respond to their individual needs, they do not always
 protect and promote people’s safety. The environment is good because the provider has taken
 action to improve safety, security and oversight within the home. Leadership and management is
 good because effective leadership and strengthened governance arrangements are driving
 improvements across the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Well-being is good because people experience positive outcomes, with new measures introduced
 by the service supporting their health and well-being. We saw people appear comfortable, settled
 and  appropriately  supported.  Interactions between care  staff and people are  positive and
 respectful. Care staff respond appropriately to individual needs and provide support in a patient and
 considerate manner. People can move freely around the home and receive support in line with their
 assessed needs. Visitors were present during the inspection, demonstrating people are supported
 to maintain relationships which are important to them.


 The service has taken action to address recent issues. Robust arrangements are in place to
 improve the security of medicines and reduce risks to people's health and well-being. Additional
 monitoring and oversight arrangements have been introduced  for people requiring enhanced
 support, helping to ensure care needs are reviewed appropriately. Appropriate safeguarding
 arrangements assist people to maintain their safety. Records and discussions with managers
 demonstrate appropriate engagement with safeguarding processes when concerns have arisen.


 The home is clean, tidy and suitably maintained. Environmental improvements to address issues
 identified previously have been completed, including securing areas of the home which should not
 be accessed by people without support. Overall, people receive support which promotes their
 comfort and quality of life.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement



Care and support has been considered proportionately during this inspection, with the inspection
activity focusing on the areas  identified through our pre-inspection analysis and  regulatory
oversight. An area for improvement was identified under this theme at the previous inspection. This
forms part of CIW's regulatory history for the service. The evidence gathered during this inspection
was not sufficient to determine whether the area for improvement has been fully addressed.  It
therefore remains subject to further regulatory consideration.


We saw people appeared clean, appropriately cared for and settled. Interactions between care staff
and people are  positive,  respectful and responsive. People can move around the  service
independently,  with  documentation  seen  confirming  proportionate  checks  and  monitoring
undertaken by care staff as required. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of people's
individual needs and provide support in a manner which promotes their well-being. We saw people
are supported to maintain relationships with those who are important to them. The service is open
to visitors, with family members present during the inspection.


There have been a number of improvements and developments at the service. The service has
introduced a range of measures to improve oversight and reduce risks to people around medication
management. These include targeted CCTV monitoring, enhanced monitoring of controlled drugs,
and additional audits and checks by management and senior care  staff. Records inspected
demonstrate these arrangements are operating in practice.

       Environment                         Good



We have not considered this theme in full. Our findings for this theme are based on the specific
areas considered during this inspection. The environment is good because the service provides a
clean, safe and well-maintained environment. The service has taken action to address concerns
identified previously, whereby health and safety risks were not reduced as far as reasonably
practicable. Rooms which were previously identified as not being secure are now locked and
appropriately secured, reducing risks to people and promoting their safety. This is an improvement
acted upon since the last inspection.


The home appears clean, tidy and generally well maintained. The service has introduced CCTV in
key areas of the home. This provides an additional safeguard for people and supports management
oversight of the service. This demonstrates the provider has taken proactive action to reduce
identified risks and strengthen arrangements to help keep people safe. We found people benefit
from an environment which is secure and supports their well-being.

       Leadership & Management               Good



We have not considered this theme in full. Our findings for this theme are based on the specific
areas considered during this inspection. Leadership and management is good because leaders
have taken effective action to address previous concerns, strengthen oversight arrangements and
drive improvements across the service.


The management team described improvements in leadership capacity and support arrangements,
including the appointment of an additional deputy manager. New management meetings have been
introduced, helping governance actions to be progressed and monitored effectively. Management
peer  supervision arrangements have been  established,  providing  opportunities  for  support,
reflection and shared learning.


We found significant progress has been made in relation to staff training. Training records have
been improved and provide a clear overview of training completed and when training is due.
Managers have established training compliance expectations and systems are in place to monitor
outstanding training. We saw evidence any outstanding training has been booked and there is a
clear plan to continue improving compliance. Supervision arrangements are organised and well
managed, with responsibility shared across the management team. Records demonstrate care staff
are receiving regular supervision. The Priority Action Notice issued at the previous inspection in
relation  to  staff  training and supervision has been addressed. We are assured appropriate
arrangements are in place to support sustained improvement.


Managers demonstrate a good understanding of areas requiring further development and are
progressing actions  relating  to care planning,  infection prevention and  control,  training and
governance. Policies identified as being required at the previous inspection are now in place. This
is an improvement acted upon since the last inspection.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
