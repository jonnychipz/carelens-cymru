Summary:


 People living in Bryn Eithin receive sensitive care from a consistent care staff team. People’s well-
 being is rated as good as care is centred on their individual preferences and objectives. Activities
 are regularly arranged for people to help them socialise and maintain their interests. People have a
 voice and have daily choices in the home. The meals are varied and people told us they enjoy
 them.

 Bryn Eithin offers people a homely, pleasant environment. There are spacious grounds to walk
 around and sit out in good weather. The environment is rated as good as the home is maintained to
 a good standard. Health and safety regulations are met to keep people safe. The home presents as
 clean and tidy. People told us they are happy with their rooms and can have their own things
 around them.

 People have planned care in a timely way to keep them as healthy as possible. The care and
 support in Bryn Eithin are rated as good as people receive care which meets their needs and

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People feel they have a voice regarding their care and daily lives. People can choose what to do
 with their day and have varied meal choices. Kitchen staff visit people, and offer menu choices,
 they get to know people’s preferences and can tailor the menu accordingly. People are offered a
 selection of activities according to their personal preferences. We saw people chatting in the lounge
 or having visits from family and friends. Some people chose to spend time in their rooms and said
 they were comfortable watching TV and reading. Families gave positive feedback in quality-of-care
 questionnaires. One family were particularly pleased that their relative is eating better since living in
 the home. The person told us they are happy in the home, and the food is, “lovely”. We saw people
 can have visitors as they want and witnessed good rapport between care staff and visitors.
 People’s personal plans include details of their personal history, their likes and dislikes and their
 individual care objectives.

 People’s personal plans evidenced people receive personalised care from nurses and care staff
 who are trained and supported to enable good care standards. We saw people are encouraged to
 be as independent as they can be and to make personal choices. Care staff can anticipate people’s
 needs, and we witnessed good rapport between people and staff. People praised the meals
 provided and said they can access drinks and snacks as they want them.



 The service has procedures in place to safeguard the people living there. Care staff receive
 safeguarding training and are supported with updated policies and protocols to follow in the event
 of a concern. Staff spoken with are aware of local safeguarding procedures and how to access
 local authority teams to ensure people’s safety. Care staff said they can approach senior staff if
 they have any concerns regarding people’s care.

       Care & Support                       Good




People’s personal plans are written according to their individual needs and objectives. People’s
preferences, history and people and dates of importance are also recorded to enable person
centred care. Daily and nightly records are written in a clear, easy to read way and the records are
organised and easy to navigate. Health care visits and appointments are carefully recorded along
with any changes to the person’s care. People have access to health care advice in a timely way as
care staff know people well and can anticipate their needs.

People’s health needs are carefully monitored to keep them as healthy as possible. People are
consulted regarding their care and family input is also welcome. Care staff offer sensitive care and
demonstrate a respectful attitude towards people. People’s nutritional needs and weight is
monitored, and people can access nutritional food and drinks. People have activities and
conversations to stimulate their memory and encourage socialisation. Some staff can converse with
people in Welsh. People can have optician; dental and chiropody appointments as required and
can access hairdressing services. The home has a good reputation for care locally; district nurses
praise the care in the home and have placed their own relatives there.

Medicine administration and storage practices are good in the home. There is a good audit system
for medicines entering and leaving the home. Medicines are stored in a secure way, and the
temperature of the room and medicines fridge is monitored daily to ensure they are stored in an
optimal way. People are given their medicines individually and nurses sign for them when they see
they have been taken. Nurses receive medicine administration refresher training to ensure their
knowledge is up to date and to enable good practice. The manager said they receive good support
from the local pharmacy, and they can access medication for people in a timely manner.



There are good safeguarding practices in the home to guard against neglect and abuse. Care plans
are regularly audited to ensure good care standards. Staff receive safeguarding training and are
aware how to report any incidents or practices of concern. Senior staff report appropriate concerns
to Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) and other relevant authorities. Accidents and incidents are
recorded and appropriate actions taken.

       Environment                         Good




People live in a home which promotes their health and safety. The home presents as clean and tidy
and has spacious lounges for people to be sociable or enjoy some quiet in. Walkways and fire exits
are kept clear of obstacles. People can choose to spend time in their room or in the lounge areas to
socialise, engage in activities, eat meals in and receive visitors. The home is an older building
which is maintained to a good standard for people’s comfort and enjoyment. There are grounds and
gardens for people to sit out in good weather, walk around, and the space is used for a summer fair
which people enjoy. People can have a say in the décor of their room and can personalise them.
There are appropriate facilities in the home and people are happy with the laundry service
provided. The hygiene rating for the home is five, this is the highest rating possible. People can
access the equipment needed for their safe care; this is well maintained and serviced to ensure
their safety.




Health and safety checks and assessments are in place and up to date to enable people’s safety.
Utility checks and certificates are in place for water, gas and electricity. Weekly fire alarm checks
are conducted, and staff are trained regarding evacuating people in the event of a fire. People have
personalised personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs), to ensure their safe evacuation in the
event of an emergency. There is a maintenance person employed to ensure the upkeep and
maintenance of the home. Housekeeping staff are employed to ensure the home is clean and has
good infection control. Entrances to the home are locked and people sign in and out for
accountability and to maintain a record in the event of a fire or emergency of any extra people
within the home. There are car parking areas to the front of the home with sufficient space for
ambulances if required.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has good governance arrangements in place to ensure the smooth running of the
service. The RI visits regularly and considers the views of residents, family and staff regarding the
service. The RI provides a quality- of -care review regarding the home in line with the requirements
of the legislation, the RI considers people’s well-being, the care and support provided,
environmental needs and maintenance and managerial issues such as staffing requirements,
training and supervision. The manager feels well supported by the RI and can access training
required by their role. Service audits conducted by senior staff are discussed with the RI and any
actions to be taken to maintain good care standards. People have an assessment prior to
accessing the service to ensure the home can meet their needs. People have information regarding
the service prior to using the service, this information is frequently reviewed to ensure its accuracy.

Care staff are safely recruited. We saw from staff records that appropriate employment checks are
in place and in date to ensure they are appropriate to work with vulnerable adults. Checks are
made to ensure care staff are qualified and registered with the relevant governing body. Staff
receive regular training and supervision suitable for their role. Care staff said they are happy
working in the home and that senior staff are available and approachable. A care worker said they
are supported to give people good care and can have a voice in the running of the service. The
service retains good numbers of staff to ensure continuity of care for people.


The provider invests in the home; the building and grounds are maintained to a good standard to
provide dignified surroundings for people. People are offered meals made with fresh ingredients
and can access snacks and drinks as required. Staff are safely recruited, and investment is made
in appropriate training. Equipment is replaced as required as are soft furnishings, towels and
bedding.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
