Summary:

 People achieve excellent outcomes because they receive highly personalised care and support
 from a skilled and committed staff team that understands their individual needs, preferences and
 aspirations. People are happy, settled and actively involved in decisions that affect their daily lives.
 Care staff develop strong relationships with people. They use individual communication
 approaches to ensure people can express their views, make informed choices and maintain control
 over their lives. People benefit from meaningful opportunities that reflect their interests and
 aspirations and are supported to achieve positive physical, emotional and mental well-being
 outcomes.

 People receive a very high standard of care and support informed by comprehensive assessments,
 detailed care planning and effective partnership working with families and professionals. Care staff
 understand people’s needs well and tailor support to promote independence, choice and personal
 development. Care staff support people to access healthcare services, attend appointments and

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes because they are supported by care staff who know
 them well, promote their rights and encourage them to have control over their daily lives. During the
 inspection, we observed exceptionally positive interactions between people and care staff.
 Interactions were warm, respectful and person-centred. Care staff used people’s preferred methods
 of communication and took time to ensure people understood information and could participate in
 decisions affecting their lives. People appeared relaxed, comfortable and confident within their
 home and moved freely around the environment. People told us they were happy living at the
 service and felt safe.

 People are actively involved in decisions about their daily lives, activities, meals and personal
 goals. Weekly menus are planned with people and individuals can choose alternatives if they
 change their minds. Care staff encourage people to participate in meal preparation and everyday
 living tasks, supporting independence, confidence and life skills development. We found people are
 listened to and their views, wishes and preferences are valued and acted upon. Care staff support
 people to understand their rights, make informed choices and take positive risks in ways that
 promote well-being and personal growth.

 People benefit from a wide range of meaningful activities that reflect their interests and aspirations.
 Care staff demonstrate a strong commitment to understanding what is important to people and
 adapting support to help them achieve personal outcomes. People are supported to pursue
 individual interests and aspirations through meaningful experiences tailored to their preferences.
 People are encouraged to develop skills, pursue interests and participate in their local community.

 People receive extensive support to maintain and improve their physical, emotional and mental
 well-being. Care staff work effectively with health and social care professionals to ensure people
 access healthcare services when required. Health appointments are attended, health passports are
 maintained and care staff act as advocates where necessary. Communication needs are met
 through personalised approaches, including the use of specialist communication methods. People
 are supported to maintain important relationships with family, friends and professionals. Feedback
 from families and professionals consistently highlights the positive impact of the support provided
 and the progress people have made since moving to the service. People are happy, settled, safe
 and achieving positive outcomes because of the very high-quality support they receive.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive excellent care and support because care staff have a very strong understanding of
their individual needs, preferences, communication methods and personal outcomes. Before
people move into the service, managers complete comprehensive assessments and gather
detailed information from families, health and social care professionals, day services and others
involved in their lives. This supports effective transition planning and helps ensure the service can
meet people’s needs while promoting positive outcomes from the outset. People benefit from care
that is tailored to their individual circumstances and aspirations.

People are actively involved in planning and reviewing their care and support. Care staff use
personalised communication approaches to enable people to participate in decisions about their
lives. Personal plans are detailed and strengths based. They include information about people’s
preferences, routines, health needs, communication methods and desired outcomes. Families,
advocates and professionals contribute to care planning and reviews, helping to ensure care
remains responsive to people’s changing needs. We found people’s interests, wishes and goals are
reflected throughout their plans and support arrangements.

Care staff promote choice, independence and positive outcomes in everyday practice. People are
encouraged to develop skills, achieve personal goals and participate in meaningful activities. Care
staff adapt support to individuals and focus on opportunities rather than limitations. People pursue
personal aspirations, including accessing new experiences and developing confidence within their
local community, with support tailored to their individual goals. People benefit from structured and
meaningful opportunities that support independence, personal development and emotional well-
being.

People receive coordinated support from a range of health and social care professionals. Care staff
support people to attend routine and specialist health appointments and work proactively to
promote positive physical, mental and emotional health outcomes. Health passports are
comprehensive, regularly updated and used to support continuity of care. Care staff act as
advocates when required to ensure people’s views, wishes and needs are understood and
respected. People have access to the services and support they need to maintain and improve
their health and well-being.

People are protected from harm through highly effective safeguarding arrangements. Care staff
understand safeguarding responsibilities and follow the Wales Safeguarding Procedures. Risks are
identified, assessed and managed appropriately, helping people remain safe while maintaining as
much independence as possible. People are supported to understand their rights, raise concerns
and access advocacy where required. Medication management arrangements are robust.
Medicines are stored safely, administered correctly and regularly audited. Medication
Administration Records reviewed during the inspection were completed accurately, with no

independence.

       Environment                              Excellent



People benefit from an excellent environment that supports their well-being, independence and
quality of life. The home is exceptionally clean, well maintained and presented to a very high
standard throughout. We found the environment provides a warm, welcoming and homely
atmosphere that reflects the people living there. Communal areas are personalised with
photographs, artwork and ornaments that help create a sense of belonging and reflect people’s
interests, personalities and identities. People have access to a range of communal and private
spaces where they can socialise, take part in activities or spend time alone.

People live in accommodation that is tailored to their individual needs and preferences. Bedrooms
and self-contained living spaces are highly personalised and reflect people’s tastes, hobbies and
interests. We found people are actively involved in decisions about how their rooms are furnished
and decorated. This promotes ownership, choice and pride in their home. People appeared
comfortable and confident within their surroundings and were observed making use of the
accommodation available to them. The accommodation supports people to achieve positive well-
being outcomes and provides an environment where people feel safe, secure and valued.

The home provides opportunities for people to develop independence and participate in everyday
life. The kitchen is accessible, clean and well organised. People are encouraged to take part in
meal preparation and household activities that support the development of practical living skills.
Managers are planning improvements to communal areas to further promote independence and
social interaction. People have been involved in discussions about these proposed changes, and
their views have informed decision-making. This ensures the environment continues to reflect
people’s wishes and evolving needs.

People have extensive access to outdoor space that supports physical and emotional well-being.
The home is situated within large grounds that people use regularly for walks, relaxation and
recreational activities. Outdoor areas are used purposefully to support individual outcomes,
including emotional regulation and meaningful occupation. One person benefits from a self-
contained flat with direct access to a private garden area, providing additional independence while
maintaining inclusion within the wider home. Outdoor spaces are safe, accessible and enhance
people’s quality of life.

The provider has highly effective systems in place to maintain people’s safety. Fire safety
arrangements are robust and include regular fire drills, audits, risk assessments and Personal
Emergency Evacuation Plans. Health and safety systems are comprehensive and well monitored.
Maintenance records, safety certificates and environmental audits demonstrate a proactive
approach to maintaining the premises and equipment. Repairs and maintenance issues are
recorded and addressed promptly. Records show all required safety checks are completed and

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from excellent leadership and management arrangements that promote positive
outcomes, safety and continuous improvement. The service has recently transitioned from a
children’s home to an adult care service and has implemented effective governance systems to
support this change. Managers provide clear direction and maintain oversight of service delivery
through regular audits, monitoring systems and quality assurance processes. Records demonstrate
that managers identify areas for development, record actions and monitor progress to completion.
This helps ensure the service continues to meet people’s needs and maintain a very high standard
of care and support.

People are supported by a staff team that feels valued, listened to and well supported. Staff
consistently describe managers and senior leaders as approachable, visible and responsive. Care
staff and managers told us they feel confident seeking advice, raising concerns and discussing
practice issues. Supervision arrangements are effective and provide opportunities for reflection,
support and professional development. Records show staff receive supervision in line with the
provider’s requirements, and any gaps are appropriately recorded and monitored. This contributes
to a positive culture where staff are supported to provide high-quality care and support.

The provider has robust recruitment, selection and vetting arrangements. Staff files contain the
required recruitment documentation and employment checks, helping to ensure people are
supported by suitable and appropriately vetted staff. Staffing levels meet people’s assessed needs
and rotas are managed effectively. Where additional support is required, the provider uses trained
staff from sister services rather than agency workers. This promotes consistency and helps people
build positive relationships with staff who know them well. Staffing arrangements are reviewed
regularly and take account of people’s needs, risks and desired outcomes.

The provider demonstrates a strong commitment to workforce development. Training records show
high compliance in most mandatory and service-specific training areas. Systems are in place to
monitor completion rates, identify gaps and ensure staff maintain the required knowledge, skills and
competence to meet people’s needs. Newly appointed staff are supported through induction
programmes and professional qualifications. Staff told us they receive appropriate training and
opportunities to develop within their roles. Training arrangements help ensure people are supported
by a knowledgeable and competent workforce.

Quality assurance arrangements are comprehensive and support continuous improvement.
Managers and senior leaders complete regular audits across all aspects of the service. Audit
findings are analysed, actions are identified and progress is monitored. Feedback from people, staff
and professionals informs service development and improvement planning. Responsible Individual
visits provide additional oversight and confirm people are settled, safe and achieving positive

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
