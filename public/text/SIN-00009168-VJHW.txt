Summary:


 Pen Y Bryn is situated in 12 acres of well-maintained gardens and parkland, in the Summerhill area
 of Wrexham. The service provides nursing care for adults with a range of needs, primarily, but not
 exclusively, supporting people with acquired brain injuries, neurological conditions, and
 Huntington’s disease.

 Care and nursing staff treat people with dignity and respect, assisting them to maintain as much
 independence as possible. They know people well and assist them to achieve good wellbeing
 outcomes. They ensure people’s preferred routines are followed, and their health and wellbeing is
 prioritised. They have established relationships with health and social care professionals and work
 alongside these professionals to ensure people receive a consistently good standard of care and
 support.

People have access to beautifully maintained grounds, and a range of comfortable communal

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People living at the service experience a positive and inclusive approach to well-being, with a clear
 emphasis on choice, independence and meaningful engagement. Individuals are supported to
 make day-to-day decisions about their routines, personal care and how they spend their time. They
 are encouraged to maintain their independence and supported with positive risk taking. A social
 care professional told us care staff support people to be “as independent as possible”. People have
 detailed personal plans which identify preferences, communication needs and aspirations. These
 plans support staff to deliver good quality care that respects people’s wishes, including cultural and
 language preferences such as Welsh language provision. Welsh language classes are held weekly
 for residents and staff. People told us their preferences for male or female only staff are also met.

 People can engage in a range of activities on offer, with a strong offer of arts-based activities.
 There is a varied programme that includes arts-based initiatives, gardening, baking and exercise
 opportunities such as zumba. Partnerships with community organisations enhance these
 opportunities, enabling some individuals to showcase their work in the local community and
 fostering a sense of achievement and belonging. Access to assistive technology further enriches
 people’s daily experiences and helps maintain engagement.

 Relationships are clearly valued within the service. People are supported to maintain strong
 connections with family, friends and professionals, contributing to emotional well-being. Interactions
 between staff and residents are warm and respectful, and feedback consistently describes the
 environment as relaxed and supportive. One person we spoke with described the home as “relaxed
 and easy going.” The service also demonstrates strong links with the wider community, including
 local schools which provide meaningful social contact and reduce isolation.

 People report feeling safe and secure in their home. Safeguarding systems are robust, with clear
 processes for reporting concerns and ensuring appropriate action is taken. People who lack
 capacity to consent to their care and accommodation needs are protected by Deprivation of Liberty
 Safeguards, meaning they have access to a representative to ensure their voice is heard and rights
 upheld. This contributes to an overall sense of trust and security for individuals and their families.
 People told us they are confident to raise any issues with the manager. Relatives told us they have
 good communication with the service, and we saw relatives were well supported by the manager
 during our inspection.

       Care & Support                       Good





The service provider demonstrates a strong approach to delivering safe, effective and person-
centred care. Comprehensive pre-admission assessments ensure that people’s needs are fully
understood before they move in, forming a solid foundation for care planning. Personal plans are
detailed and cover a wide range of areas including health, communication, mobility, enrichment and
specialist needs. This enables staff to provide consistent support in line with each individual’s
wishes.

Care staff are committed to promoting independence and recognising what people can do for
themselves. Care plans are strengths-based and include evidence of positive risk-taking,
supporting individuals to maintain control over their lives. For example, people are supported to
make choices in their daily routines, and risks are managed in a way that balances safety with
autonomy. This person-centred approach enhances people’s confidence and well-being.

Care and nursing staff deliver care and support which is consistent with people’s personalised
plans. Regular reviews take place and involve families and professionals, ensuring that care
remains responsive to changing needs. They know people well and deliver support in a way that is
both respectful and responsive. They ensure people have access to the health care services they
need, and timely referrals are made to healthcare professionals when needed. For people with
complex care needs there are strong relationships with external professionals to ensure there is a
consistent and thorough approach to their care delivery.

The manager ensures there are well established risk management systems in place. Assessments
are in place for key areas such as falls, nutrition, pressure care and behaviour, and incident
reporting processes are used consistently. Records in the home show that incidents are reviewed
and learning is applied to improve care and reduce risk. Staff are knowledgeable about
safeguarding responsibilities and follow appropriate procedures. They are confident to raise any
issues or concerns.

People’s medication is safely managed. There are systems in place which are safe and effective,
with secure storage, regular checks and competent administration by trained staff. Controlled drugs
are stored securely, with two members of staff to witness their administration. Infection prevention
and control practices are also good, with a clean and well-maintained environment and appropriate
use of personal protective equipment.

       Environment                         Good





People live in a service which is comfortable and enabling. The premises are spacious, well
maintained and designed to meet the needs of people with varying levels of mobility. Accessibility
is good, ensuring people can move around their home safely and with confidence. They have
access to grab rails, wide corridors, and bilingual signage.

People are encouraged to personalise their bedrooms, creating spaces that reflect their identities
and preferences. Soft furnishings are carefully considered with people consulted on colour
schemes. This contributes to a sense of ownership and belonging. In addition to private spaces,
there are multiple communal areas that support social interaction and activities. For example, larger
lounge areas are used effectively to provide opportunities for people to come together in
meaningful ways. People also have access to quieter areas in which to relax or receive visitors.

The service is well equipped with specialist equipment to meet people’s needs, and there is
evidence that this is maintained appropriately. This ensures both safety and comfort for individuals
requiring additional support. The presence of an accessible and secure courtyard area offers a safe
outdoor space where people can relax and participate in activities in warmer months. The service
has extensive grounds, and beautifully maintained gardens, which can be accessed freely by
people who are independently mobile and not at risk of wandering. They provide opportunities for
recreation and connection with nature. For individuals able to access these spaces, either
independently or with support, this contributes positively to physical and emotional well-being.

Safety within the environment is well managed. Statutory checks and maintenance systems are in
place, providing assurance that the premises are safe and compliant. We saw certification of gas
and electrical safety checks. The service provider ensures any recommendations from external
surveys such as fire risk assessments are followed up. This structured approach supports a secure
and stable living environment for residents. The building is secure, with a visitor’s signing in book at
the entrance. We were required to make ourselves known to staff before we could enter the
building.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The service benefits from strong and compassionate leadership, which has established a positive
culture focused on people’s well-being. The manager is visible and actively involved in the day-to-
day running of the service, providing reassurance to both residents and staff. Feedback highlights a
caring and supportive leadership style that promotes trust and teamwork across the service.
Relatives told us they have a great deal of trust in the manager. They told us the manager is, “On
the ball with everything.”, and “I don’t class X as a manager, I class them as a friend.”

The service provider has well developed governance arrangements in place. There are systems in
place for auditing, quality monitoring and oversight, including regular visits by the responsible
individual (RI), supported by their nominated representative. The RI ensures they speak with
people and staff during their visits and reviews a range of records. A wide range of data is collected
relating to safety, care quality and staffing, which supports ongoing monitoring of the service.
Incident reporting and review processes demonstrate that management maintains awareness of
key risks and service performance.

The manager and RI are committed to maintaining and enhancing standards. Complaints and
compliments are recorded and responded to appropriately, with a notable volume of positive
feedback from families and professionals. This indicates a good level of satisfaction with the care
provided. The presence of a robust contingency plan further demonstrates preparedness and
organisational resilience.

The service provider ensures recruitment processes are thorough, with suitable individuals
appointed to deliver care and support. We saw evidence of recruitment checks, such as reference
and disclosure and barring service (DBS) checks. Care and nursing staff are required to register
with the relevant workforce regulators. They receive regular supervision and access to a range of
training opportunities, including specialist training relevant to people’s needs. Many staff are trained
in Huntington’s Disease, which is an area of speciality for the service. There is a clear emphasis on
professional development, with opportunities for progression and recognition of staff achievements.
This contributes to a motivated and skilled workforce. Staff consistently told us they enjoyed their
roles, and many staff have received long service awards, indicating there is a consistent and strong
staff team.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
