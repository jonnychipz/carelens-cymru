Summary:

 Llys Newydd is a residential home on the outskirts of Ammanford. Well-being is rated as good
 because people receive a person-centred service, that enables them to achieve their health and
 well-being outcomes. People have developed positive relationships with the caring staff team.

 Care and support is rated as good because people receive an effective service from a team of
 consistent and familiar staff. Care workers are guided by accurate and individualised personal
 plans that enable them to meet people’s needs.

 The environment is rated as good because people are relaxed and comfortable throughout the
 home. Improvements have been made to the building and the provider has a clear plan to continue
 these to enhance people’s well-being.

 Leadership and management is rated as good because the provider has effective oversight of the
 service. The onsite leadership team ensure people receive reliable and highly valued care from a
 team of dedicated care workers who are well supported by their managers.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are at the heart of the service and treated with dignity and respect. They are supported to
 identify their health and well-being outcomes, while also being encouraged to maintain their
 independence whenever possible. A representative of a person who lives at the service told us,
 “She’s safe, fed well and well looked after, it’s very reassuring”.

 People have as much control over their day-to-day lives as possible. They are supported to be
 involved in decisions that affect them, ensuring that their voices are heard. This approach helps
 people to take charge of their lives with confidence and assurance. A representative said “Things
 are very good, they look after her very well and they really understand her needs”.

 There is a strong sense of community within the home. Positive relationships between people living
 at the service are evident and contribute to a family atmosphere. We observed people engaging
 naturally with one another, sharing conversations and offering encouragement or support. This
 demonstrated people were comfortable in each other's company and encouraging one another's
 well-being. People appeared to know one another well and enjoy spending time together, creating
 a genuine sense of belonging.

 People can choose to take part in a variety of group or one-to-one activities and there is
 unrestricted visiting. People are supported to maintain and sustain existing relationships with
 family, friends and important people in their lives as far as possible. This is facilitated by staff who
 respect these relationships and the importance they hold in people’s lives.

 The service ensures people are, as practicably as possible safeguarded from abuse and neglect.
 People are provided with a secure environment where they feel safe. The service encourages
 feedback from people and their representatives to make sure every voice is heard. There are
 effective support systems to ensure any risks are promptly identified and addressed.

 People live in accommodation that supports them to achieve positive outcomes. The provider has
 improved the décor of the home and has a plan in place to further develop the building and grounds
 to better meets people’s needs.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are very happy with the service they receive; they are supported by a team of well-trained
and caring staff. We observed many familiar, friendly and patient interactions throughout the
inspection. A representative of a person who lives at the service told us, “He’s as happy as he can
be and that’s down to the staff”.

When commencing the service people’s care and support is co-produced between them, their
representatives and relevant professionals. Personal plans are individualised and outline how staff
should support people to achieve their well-being outcomes. The service works closely with partner
agencies, their advice and guidance is well documented in people’s personal plans. People's well-
being and safety is acknowledged by the service by identifying, managing and reducing potential
risks. These are comprehensively and clearly documented in people’s individual personal plans.

There is a family feel at the service, people have developed caring and compassionate
relationships with one and other. The service has dedicated staff to ensure people are offered
choices of enjoyable and stimulating activities. People engage in meaningful past times, such as
hair and beauty, exercise classes, arts and crafts and can enjoy small group activities in the garden
sensory room. Welsh language and culture is embedded in the service, people converse with each
other and many of the staff team in English or Welsh as they choose. Care workers communicate
with people bilingually and one said, “Being able to talk to people in their own language makes a
big difference for them”.

People experience care and support that is dignified and respectful. People have positive
interactions with staff, friends, family, and each other. People are supported to attend and
participate in activities related to health promotion, staff are aware of their role in this and the
current needs of each individual. Care workers told us, “The people are so nice and make the job”.
and “I love making a difference for the residents”.

People receive their medication as prescribed in accordance with national guidelines and the
service provider’s medication policy. Regular reviews of medications are completed in a timely
manner, involving people where appropriate and any relevant professionals. Monthly audits are
completed to ensure consistency of practice and good standards are maintained.

People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because premises and
equipment are kept clean and hygienic and there are clear roles and responsibilities. Food hygiene
practices are good and staff actively support people to enjoy an stimulating and sociable dining
experience.

       Environment                         Good



People are comfortable, happy and relaxed throughout the home. Individual rooms can be
personalised and people decorate them with items of their own furniture, ornaments and
equipment.

People access different communal and private spaces in which to spend time alone, socialise or
entertain visitors. Communal spaces are thoughtfully designed to meet the needs of people,
promoting independence and providing opportunities for private meetings, activities, and recreation.
A representative told us, “The lounge is really well set up in a way that helps interactions”.

Outdoor areas are safe, accessible and welcoming, allowing people to spend time outside
independently. People enjoy accessing the garden and the recent development of the sensory
room has made a positive impact on people’s well-being.

The provider considers people’s needs when designing the home to effectively promote social
interactions. They have replaced flooring, purchased new chairs, decorated communal areas and
have further plans in place to improve the overall environment to make a positive impact on
people’s well-being.

Safety is a key consideration at the service and staff access necessary equipment. The service
manages environmental risks; staff are familiar with the strategies and apply them consistently.

Regular servicing, maintenance and repairs to equipment ensures the safety and well-being of
people using the service. The environment is clean and well maintained to a high standard by a
committed and dedicated housekeeping team.

Security arrangements are in place to protect people without compromising their rights, privacy,
and dignity. This includes protecting personal property and providing appropriate access to and
from the premises.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider oversees the quality of care through their own detailed and effective auditing
processes. Information gathered from both internal and external audits guides quality review
reports and improvement plans.

The Responsible Individual (RI) visits the service regularly to talk with people, their representatives
and staff. Feedback from everyone involved is encouraged by them to support with assessing the
quality of the service and to consider any necessary improvements. People and their
representatives know how they can provide feedback about their experiences of the service.

The manager is well supported by their deputy and a team of senior staff. They have created a
knowledgeable and person focused culture, that enables people to live as well as possible. People,
their representatives and care workers have confidence in the manager and deputy, they can
discuss any concerns they have with them. A representative told us, “She’s (manager) on top of
everything. I’m happy to talk to her and she is always open and willing to chat to me”.

The manager ensures care workers have regular one-on-one supervision sessions at least
quarterly and an annual review to provide feedback and identify any training needs. Staff told us
the manager has a hands-on approach, is always available and helpful. When discussing the
manager a care worker said, “She leads from the front and isn’t afraid to come out and help us”.

The provider has very strong selection and vetting processes for hiring staff to ensure they are
suitably qualified and trustworthy. Care workers undergo routine and regular checks to ensure they
remain suitably fit to work in the service and are appropriately registered with professional bodies.
The service has developed an effective induction with a comprehensive support system to help
prepare new staff for the role.

The provider ensures all staff help to protect people and keep them safe. Staff told us they have
good communication with their manager and are confident to report any complaints or concerns if
needed.

Overall, people benefit from effective leadership and management; that promotes a positive culture.
Leaders value feedback and demonstrate a commitment to improving the service. Strong
governance arrangements, effective oversight and a dedicated staff team help ensure people
experience good quality care and support within a well-managed home.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
