Summary:

 The Ivy is a small residential care home in the Vale of Glamorgan. It provides care and support for
 four people with complex needs, including learning disabilities and autism.

 People’s well-being requires improvement. We observed care delivered with kindness, respect and
 compassion. However, the service did not consistently support people to maximise their
 independence, choice and meaningful engagement.

 Care and support requires improvement. Ongoing staffing pressures meant people did not always
 receive person-centred care in line with their personal plans. Personal plans were not consistently
 up to date, individualised or regularly reviewed. Records did not always evidence people’s
 involvement in reviews. This limited assurance that care reflected people’s wishes and created
 challenges for newer staff in understanding people’s needs.

 The environment requires improvement. The layout and limited communal space restricted
 opportunities for people to spend time together outside their bedrooms. Some health and safety

Leadership and management require improvement. Governance and oversight arrangements were

have the skills, knowledge and support they need to meet people’s needs safely and consistently.

We identified areas for improvement during this inspection and expect the provider to make

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement





 People are treated with dignity and respect. We observed positive and respectful interactions
 between care staff and people. People appeared relaxed and at ease. Staff listened to people,
 advocated on their behalf. However, opportunities for meaningful engagement were limited.
 Outcomes for people require improvement because the service did not consistently maximise
 opportunities for meaningful interaction, independence, choices and engagement. We observed
 people spending extended periods in their bedrooms. Staff focused much of their time on general
 household and administrative tasks and relied on monitoring equipment rather than engaging
 socially with people. This limited people’s day-to-day experiences and well-being. We expect the
 provider to make improvements.


 Bedrooms and bathrooms contain suitable furnishings and equipment to meet people’s needs and
 preferences. This includes specialist beds and appropriate moving and handling equipment.
 Bathrooms promote privacy, dignity, safety and accessibility. However, communal areas offered
 limited space and facilities for people to spend time together. Although staff told us people
 preferred spending quiet time in their bedrooms, this was not consistently reflected in care
 documentation. This limited assurance that people were actively supported to access shared
 spaces and social opportunities.


 People’s voices were not always fully represented. Records did not evidence how people were
 supported to access advocacy services when needed to support their rights and entitlements. Risk
 assessments were not reviewed consistently. Some lacked sufficient detail to clearly describe
 people’s needs and known risks, which limited staff understanding and risk management.


 People live in a clean and generally warm environment, which supports comfort. However, we
 found some areas of the home were draughty, which could cause discomfort. Health and safety
 monitoring systems were in place, but these were not sufficiently robust to reliably identify, prioritise
 and address environmental issues that could affect people’s well-being.


 People are generally safeguarded from abuse and neglect. Systems are in place to identify and
 manage risks, including safeguarding training for staff. However, safeguarding arrangements
 required strengthening. We identified an incident that had not been reported through the correct
 channels in a timely way. This indicated gaps in staff knowledge, management oversight and

Outcomes for people require improvement because safeguarding systems do not consistently

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement





People receive care that is kind, attentive and responsive to their individual needs. We observed
care staff provide reassurance appropriately. We observed warm, respectful interactions from care
staff that reflected people’s preferences. People access specialist health advice when needed. The
manager has long-standing knowledge of people and works closely with professionals to support
people’s physical health and well-being.


Outcomes for people require improvement because care planning and record-keeping systems
were not consistently effective in supporting personalised and safe care. Inaccurate, limited or
outdated records reduces staff understanding of people’s needs and preferences. We found care
documentation was disorganised and not always up to date. Care documentation did not
consistently evidence people’s or their representatives’ involvement in key choices, including
decisions about when to wake up, go to bed, and how to spend their time during the day. There
was limited evidence to show people were supported to access advocacy services when needed or
involvement with people’s representatives during reviews and decision making. During the
inspection, management began updating and streamlining records to better reflect people’s views,
choices and preferences. However, this remained an area of development and requires consistent
oversight to ensure improvements are sustained.


Outcomes for people require improvement because staffing arrangements did not always align with
assessed needs. Staff delegation and time pressures has affected people’s daily experiences,
because people have received less support to engage in meaningful activities during staff changes.
We observed staff spending significant time completing administrative and household tasks, which
reduced opportunities for social interaction and meaningful engagement with people. This limited
flexibility in how care was delivered and reduced opportunities for person-centred support. Staffing
levels have increased recently, and improvements are already being made; however, we will
consider the impact of this on people’s experiences and access to meaningful activities at the next
inspection.


People’s medicines are generally managed safely. People received their medication as prescribed.
Staff complete medication training and had their competency assessed. However, medication
governance requires strengthening. Staff had not consistently completed medication audits, and
competency checks were not always up to date. Care documentation does not clearly demonstrate
decision-making about medication timing, particularly where this could affect people’s sleep or daily
routines. We expect the provider to make improvements.

       Environment               Requires Improvement





People live in an environment that is generally clean, homely and personalised. Bedrooms are
homely and reflect people’s individual preferences. People choose their own décor, including
photographs, ornaments and paint colours. Some bedrooms have en-suite facilities, which supports
privacy and dignity when people receive personal care.

People access the mobility equipment they need to move around the home. Recent improvements
to laundry equipment mean staff no longer dry laundry in communal areas. This has increased
available shared space for people. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans are up to date and give
staff clear guidance on how to support people safely during an emergency.

Outcomes for people require improvement because the environment is not consistently safe,
accessible and well maintained. Gaps in environmental oversight has affected people’s
accessibility.

The layout and available space within the home present ongoing challenges. The garden is not fully
accessible for people who use a wheelchair. This limits opportunities for them to spend time
outdoors. There is limited space for people to spend private time with visitors, and most people
spend most of their time in their bedrooms. Communal areas are not fully accessible to everyone.
Staff store some equipment in shared living areas, which reduces social space and creates
potential safety risks. The recent upgrade to washing and drying equipment has eliminated the
need for laundry to be dried in communal areas, which is positive for people, as this reduced space
for people. During the inspection we saw the small living area being used to store wheelchairs and
to dry laundry on freestanding laundry airer. We observed the living area was frequently used as a
thoroughfare for staff and deliveries. This resulted in ongoing disturbance and notable draughts,
which can affect people’s comfort. Some areas within the home showed scuffing and damage and
require repair or replacement.

Oversight of health and safety arrangements is not effective. We found fire extinguishers were not
securely mounted to walls. This remained an outstanding action from previous audits and indicates
weaknesses in follow-through and monitoring. Systems to monitor and maintain the environment
and equipment were in place, but these were not consistently implemented.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement





Leadership within the service shows positive interpersonal strengths. Care staff and visiting
professionals consistently described the manager as caring, supportive and people-focused.
Professionals told us the service works closely with them to help meet people’s health needs.
These strengths support positive working relationships and contribute to a generally supportive
culture. However, leadership and governance arrangements were not consistently effective in
ensuring safe systems and regulatory compliance.


Outcomes for people require improvement because organisational oversight is not sufficiently
effective to ensure safeguarding concerns are consistently identified and reported. Staff did not
appropriately report a safeguarding concern, highlighting gaps in safeguarding knowledge,
escalation processes and the effective implementation of safeguarding policies. We expect the
provider to make improvements.


Outcomes for people require improvement because systems, documentation and the management
of notifiable incidents are not consistently effective or in line with regulatory requirements. Gaps in
management oversight and knowledge mean issues are not always identified, recorded or
escalated appropriately. We expect the provider to make improvements.


The responsible individual completes quality assurance checks and reports; however, these are not
sufficiently robust to meet regulatory requirements. Oversight processes did not consistently test
practice or identify gaps in service provision and did not reliably ensure compliance with regulatory
responsibilities.


Outcomes for people require improvement because staff training and induction arrangements are
not consistently effective, increasing the risk that care staff are not adequately prepared to carry out
delegated care tasks safely. Training records showed some mandatory and delegated health task
training was out of date. We expect the provider to make improvements.


Staffing arrangements is being strengthened to support positive outcomes for people. Care staff
told us time pressures and task-driven demands limited opportunities for meaningful engagement,
increasing the risk of care becoming task-focused rather than person-centred. The provider has
addressed this and staffing arrangements are improving. Care staff receive regular supervision, but

records did not consistently evidence reflective practice or professional development. Staff new to
the senior roles were unclear about the induction progress during the inspection.


Leaders acted immediately on issues identified during the inspection, but this demonstrated a
reactive approach to governance. Several issues had been recognised internally but were not
addressed in a timely way. Stronger, proactive monitoring and quicker action for people is required
to ensure risks are identified early and people consistently experience safe, person-centred care.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
