Summary:

 People living at Sketty House are able to achieve their well-being outcomes because they are
 supported to make choices, maintain important relationships and take part in a varied range of
 meaningful activities.

 Care and support is good because care plans provide detailed guidance about people’s needs,
 preferences and desired outcomes, and people have access to health professionals and safe
 support with their medicines. Some daily records did not consistently demonstrate that fluid
 monitoring, continence care and oral hygiene had been provided.

 The environment at Sketty House is good because people live in a clean, comfortable and
 generally well-maintained environment. Safety checks, equipment servicing and infection
 prevention arrangements support their well-being, although parts of the older building remain in
 need of refurbishment.

 Leadership and management is good because management are visible and supportive. Care staff

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to have choice and control over their day-to-day lives and receive care from
 staff who know them well. We observed positive and respectful interactions between people and
 care staff throughout the inspection. Care staff spoke with people in a friendly and familiar manner
 and demonstrated a good understanding of their individual needs and preferences. One person
 stated, “I enjoy living here; the staff are amazing”. People have access to personalised care
 information which helps staff understand how they prefer to be supported and communicate. We
 found examples of care plans containing clear information about people's routines, preferences and
 the support that works best for them.

 People are supported to maintain relationships and participate in activities that are meaningful to
 them. The service has invested considerable effort into enhancing well-being opportunities for
 people living at the home. We saw evidence of a varied programme of activities including themed
 events, social gatherings, walking groups and creative sessions. Care staff gave examples of
 supporting people to continue interests that were important to them, including reconnecting a
 person with a choir they previously attended.

 People are protected from harm and abuse through safeguarding arrangements and risk
 management processes. Care staff spoken with during the inspection showed a detailed
 understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities. We found systems were in place to identify
 and manage risks to people’s safety and welfare. Safeguarding concerns had been reported
 appropriately, and records demonstrated the service works with external agencies when concerns
 arise. Access to the home is controlled and measures are in place to help keep people safe.

 The service has strengthened  its key worker arrangements, promoting regular monitoring of
 people’s  well-being and  helping  to ensure  effective communication  with people and  their
 representatives. People and, where appropriate, their representatives are invited to participate in
 care plan reviews and are kept informed of any significant changes to their care and support needs.
 The service is working towards the Welsh Language Active Offer. Care staff are undertaking Welsh
 language lessons to ensure they can fully support Welsh speaking people living at the service.
 Bilingual signage  is also present throughout the home and Welsh holidays and culture are
 celebrated regularly.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that is personalised and responsive to their individual needs. We
reviewed a sample of personal plans and found they contain detailed information about people’s
health conditions, strengths, wishes, preferences and support needs. Care plans set out people's
desired outcomes and include associated risk assessments to guide care staff in delivering care
safely. We found that care plans and risk assessments are reviewed regularly and updated when
people’s needs change. Monthly review arrangements help ensure information remains current and
relevant. Feedback received from family and representatives was consistently positive. One family
member stated, “all the staff are very good; we are always kept up to date”.

People benefit from care that promotes their health, safety and well-being. Records demonstrated
people have access to health and social care professionals when required and specialist
assessments are sought to support complex needs. We saw examples of risk management plans
relating to mobility, nutrition, hydration, skin integrity, falls prevention and medication. Where
people require specialist equipment or additional monitoring, guidance is available for care staff to
follow. People informed us that care staff were “helpful” and “attentive”. One person stated, “the
care is very good here”.

People generally receive care and support that achieves positive outcomes; however, we identified
some areas where recording practices require strengthening. Whilst care plans and risk
assessments are detailed and routinely reviewed, we found inconsistencies in some daily care
records. For some people, records relating to fluid monitoring, continence care and oral hygiene
were not always completed consistently. This is an area for improvement and we expect the
provider to take action.

People receive their medicines in a safe and person-centred manner from trained and competent
staff. Medication support is tailored to individual needs and delivered in accordance with people's
care plans, ensuring their preferences, abilities and levels of independence are recognised and
respected. Records sampled demonstrated that medication risk assessments were in place and
reviewed regularly, providing care staff with clear guidance on how people should be supported
with their medicines. Medication Administration Records (MARs) were completed appropriately,
providing assurance that medicines had been administered as prescribed. We found medicines
were stored securely and appropriate arrangements were in place to maintain their safety and
effectiveness, including regular temperature monitoring and recording. These systems helped
ensure medicines were managed safely, whilst promoting people's health, well-being and individual
outcomes.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is safe, comfortable and generally supports their well-being
outcomes. The home provides a range of communal and private spaces where people can
socialise, receive visitors or spend time independently. We found the environment was clean and
well maintained, with suitable facilities and equipment available to meet people's needs. The
provider continues to invest in improvements to the home and has undertaken refurbishment work
in several areas since the previous inspection. Some communal bathrooms have been renovated,
and flooring replacement works continue as part of an ongoing programme of improvements.

People benefit from an environment that supports their safety. We found that systems are in place
to monitor and maintain the premises and equipment. Records showed regular servicing and
maintenance of specialist equipment including hoists, lifts and beds. Gas safety certification, fire
safety equipment servicing and other routine safety checks were completed and recorded
appropriately. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are available and reviewed
regularly to help ensure people can be supported safely in the event of an emergency. Weekly
maintenance walk-rounds are completed by the manager and maintenance staff to identify and
address environmental issues.

People are protected from environmental hazards because there are effective health and safety
arrangements in place. We saw cleaning products and substances hazardous to health are stored
securely. Kitchen safety checks, food temperature monitoring and fridge temperature records are
completed consistently, and the service holds a five-star food hygiene rating. We also found
suitable arrangements for infection prevention and control, including dedicated laundry facilities and
clear systems to separate clean and soiled items.
People have access to equipment and facilities that help them maintain their independence and
well-being. Managers told us the provider continues to invest in equipment and environmental
improvements to meet people’s changing needs. We found suitable equipment was available
throughout the home and servicing arrangements were in place to ensure equipment remains safe
and fit for purpose.

People's accommodation generally meets their needs and bedrooms can be personalised to reflect
individual preferences. We found efforts had been made to improve communal areas and maintain
the overall presentation of the service. However, some areas of the home continue to require
refurbishment, including one upstairs bathroom which had been identified at a previous inspection.
The provider has identified these areas and plans are in place for further improvement works. This
is an area for improvement and we expect the provider to take action.
People's accommodation meets their needs and bedrooms are found to be personalised to reflect
individual preferences. Renovations had been made to improve communal areas and maintain the
overall presentation of the service

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from a service that has effective day-to-day management and oversight
arrangements. The registered manager is visible within the service and care staff consistently
spoke positively about the support they receive. Care staff described the manager as
“approachable” and “understanding”. One staff member stated, “they really listen, and are always
there to give support”. We also found the Responsible Individual (RI) maintains a regular presence
within the home and engages with staff and people living at the service. Care staff described the RI
as “supportive”.

People's well-being is supported by leaders who are committed to developing the service and
improving outcomes. The manager told us there has been a particular focus on enhancing well-
being opportunities and strengthening person-centred practices since the previous inspection. We
saw evidence of ongoing quality improvement, including the development of the key worker role,
increased opportunities for consultation with people and representatives, and continued investment
in environmental improvements. The manager was recently recognised through the Together We
Care Awards, receiving a Rising Star award for leadership.

People are supported by care staff who are generally well trained and receive appropriate support
to carry out their roles. Staff described the training provided as good quality and told us they
receive a mixture of face-to-face and online learning. Care staff also told us supervision provides a
useful opportunity to discuss practice and share ideas.

Training records showed most mandatory training had been completed, with only a small number of
gaps identified. The service has systems in place to monitor training compliance and obtain
updates where required. Quality assurance arrangements are in place to monitor the service and
identify areas for improvement. We reviewed a recent Quality of Care Review and found it included
feedback from people, relatives and professionals and considered a range of areas relating to
service delivery. The manager completes regular audits and monitors activities across the service.
We saw evidence of routine maintenance checks, care plan reviews and other governance
processes intended to support oversight of the service.

People benefit from a positive culture where staff feel supported and able to raise concerns. Care
staff spoken with understood safeguarding and whistleblowing responsibilities and told us they
would raise any concerns with management. Team meetings, supervision arrangements and
informal discussions support communication across the service. Staff morale was described
positively overall.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
