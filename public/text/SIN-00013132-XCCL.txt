Summary:

 Brigadoon Care Home is a small, family-run service that provides a friendly and homely
 environment for people living here. The home is conveniently located close to local amenities in
 Llandudno.

 People’s wellbeing is good as they have many opportunities to socialise with others and do things
 they enjoy. People’s care and support needs are accurately reflected in risk assessments and
 personal plans, which are kept under review. Medication systems and standards of hygiene and
 infection control are mainly good.

 Care and support require improvement as the correct equipment is not in place and there is not
 enough staff to care for immobile people at night. People receive dignified and respectful care, with
 meaningful interactions with staff and their care is individually tailored to each person’s own
 individual needs.

Environment requires significant improvement as the provider has failed to act on external
professional advice and sustain compliance with the external condition of the grounds. The
environment is homely, clean and well-maintained. People are also benefitting from an ongoing
refurbishment and safety checks of the home.

Leadership and management require significant improvement. The Responsible Individual (RI) has
not ensured that effective organisational arrangements, governance systems and oversight
mechanisms are in place to support the safe and effective operation of the service. As a result,
there are shortcomings in the provider’s ability to monitor the quality of care, identify areas of
concern and drive continuous improvement.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to make choices about their daily lives and maintain routines that are
 important to them. We observed people choosing how and where they spend their time, where they
 eat their meals, and whether they wish to participate in activities. Records showed people can
 choose to eat in communal areas or in the privacy of their own bedrooms. People described the
 home as welcoming and told us they enjoyed living here. People praised the quality of the food and
 the friendliness of the staff. Care records and daily notes demonstrate staff have a good
 understanding of people’s preferences and provide support in accordance with their wishes and
 individual needs. We saw people have opportunities to develop and maintain friendships, spending
 time together in communal areas talking, socialising and taking part in games and activities.
 However, there is not enough staff on duty at night or the correct equipment in place to meet the
 needs of people who are not mobile.

 People are not always protected from abuse and neglect. Care staff told us they receive
 safeguarding training and are aware of local procedures to alert authorities if there are concerned
 about people’s care. Staff recruitment procedures need improvement. Staff do not have the skills
 or equipment to ensure people who are not mobile are cared for in a safe manner. The RI monitors
 the quality of the service however has not rectified the issues found at this inspection which is
 placing people, staff and visitors at risk of harm.

 People mainly live in accommodation that supports their wellbeing outcomes. We found the home
 to be clean, comfortable and homely, with spacious communal areas available for people to spend
 time in if they wish. People are encouraged to personalise their bedrooms with belongings and
 items that are important to them, helping to create a familiar and individualised living environment.
 The home has a dedicated dining area with attractively laid tables, providing a pleasant setting for
 mealtimes. However, improvements are required to ensure concerns relating to pest control are
 addressed in a timely manner. In addition, environmental audits need to be further developed and
 implemented effectively to identify, monitor and address any issues within the service at the earliest
 opportunity.

 People are not able to receive a full service through the medium of Welsh. However, some care
 staff are able to use basic Welsh phrases to support communication. People benefit from continuity
 of care provided by a stable staff team who know them well and understand their individual needs
 and preferences.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement



People live in a service where their care and support needs are largely met. We reviewed a sample
of personal plans and found these are developed in consultation with the person wherever
possible. The plans include a personal profile, detailing the person’s history, preferred routines, and
the people, activities and events that are important to them. Personal plans are reviewed at least
quarterly, or sooner if a person’s needs change, to ensure they remain current and reflective of the
support required. We saw evidence that people’s needs are assessed prior to admission to
determine whether the service can safely and effectively meet those needs. Core risk assessments
are in place to promote people’s safety and are subject to regular review.

People have good access to services that support their overall health and wellbeing. Records show
people receive support from a range of healthcare professionals, including dietitians, general
practitioners, district nurses, occupational therapists. Where people have specific care
requirements, we saw these are not being delivered in line with relevant guidance, including the
management of skin integrity. We found the provider does not have the required equipment and
night care staff to safely support immobile people with their manual handling needs. In addition,
there are insufficient staff on duty during the night to ensure people can be repositioned safely and
in accordance with their assessed needs. Daily care records contain multiple entries demonstrating
the support provided by care staff.

People are supported through effective medication management processes within the home.
Medication Administration Records demonstrate medicines are administered appropriately and in
line with people’s prescriptions by staff who have received relevant training. However,
improvements are required to ensure staff medication competencies are assessed and refreshed at
regular intervals, and all transcribed Medication Administration Records are checked and signed by
a second member of staff. Monthly medication audits are completed, and any issues identified are
acted upon promptly and appropriately to help maintain safe medication practices.

People benefit from effective hygiene and infection prevention and control practices. The service
does not employ designated domestic and laundry staff; care staff undertake cleaning duties each
afternoon to help maintain a clean environment. People and staff spoke positively about the
cleanliness of the home. We observed sufficient supplies of cleaning products and personal
protective equipment. The service has achieved a Food Hygiene Rating of 5, the highest rating
available, providing assurance that food hygiene standards are well maintained. Infection
prevention and control training is due to be delivered in August 2026 through the provider’s new
training provider.

       Environment

People spoke very positively about their personal bedrooms, which we found to be comfortable,
well-furnished and attractively decorated. Bedrooms reflect people’s individual preferences and
contain personal belongings and items of significance to them, helping to create a familiar and
homely environment. Rooms are arranged in accordance with people’s wishes and needs,
supporting their comfort, independence and wellbeing. People also have access to outdoor space,
including a small garden and a large patio area at the rear of the property. Staff told us these areas
are used during warmer weather for celebrations, social events and planned activities, providing
opportunities for people to spend time outdoors and engage with others.

People live in an environment where required maintenance is carried out, and safety checks of
equipment and appliances are arranged appropriately. We observed staff check our identity before
granting access to the home and ensure we signed the visitors’ book, helping to protect people
from unauthorised access to the service. Records show servicing and safety checks, including gas
and electrical safety inspections, are completed as required. Fire safety arrangements are in place,
with regular checks undertaken to ensure fire alarm systems, and firefighting equipment remain
effective and in good working order. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans are readily accessible.
We noted staffs fire safety and health, and safety training had recently expired. The provider
advised training has been scheduled for August 2026.

People are not consistently provided with an environment that is promoted through effective quality
assurance and maintenance systems. We observed the carport area contained foliage and the
clinical waste bins were overflowing. These issues had the potential to attract vermin and are of
particular concern given the ongoing vermin problem affecting the premises. The provider has not
taken timely action to address recommendations made by the pest control contractor. As a result,
opportunities to effectively manage and reduce the risk posed by vermin has not been fully
implemented. In addition, the external lean-to shed continues to require improvement. Despite the
inspectors raising previous concerns having been identified in this area, sufficient progress has not
been made to ensure this area is maintained to an acceptable standard. This is placing people’s
health and well-being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider
must take immediate action to address this issue.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


Arrangements for the oversight and governance of the service require strengthening to ensure
effective quality assurance and continuous improvement. The director is acting as interim manager
and is working towards the required management qualification. The RI and interim manager and
deputy manager are present in the home on most days and provide direct support to people when
required. The RI produces a three-monthly report detailing their findings. The provider also seeks
feedback from people, relatives and staff and produces a six-monthly quality of care review reports.
However, these reports do not demonstrate a robust analysis of complaints, audits, incidents or
notifications. As a result, there is limited evidence of lessons being learned or actions being
identified to drive service improvements.

The systems for monitoring and improving the quality of the service are not sufficiently effective. A
dependency assessment tool is not used to evaluate staffing levels when people's needs change,
which limits the provider’s ability to evidence staffing arrangements remain appropriate. In addition,
compliance had not been sustained in relation to environmental matters. Recruitment practices do
not consistently promote the safety and wellbeing of people living in the home. The regulator is not
always informed of notifiable events and medication competencies are not carried out on a regular
basis. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority
action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address this issue.

People are not consistently supported by staff recruited through safe recruitment and vetting
processes. The provider does not always obtain satisfactory references and application forms
before staff commence employment, which does not fully support safe recruitment practices. Some
staff mandatory training has expired. The provider informed us a new training provider has been
commissioned and training for all staff is scheduled to take place in August 2026. Staff receive
regular supervision, providing opportunities to reflect on their practice, discuss people’s needs,
review training requirements and consider their own wellbeing. Staff spoke positively about the
working culture within the home and told us they had confidence in the current management
arrangements and the quality of care being provided. However, improvements are required to
ensure all staff receive annual appraisals.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
