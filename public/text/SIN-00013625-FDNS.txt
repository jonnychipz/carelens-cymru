Summary:

 Maesy Y Wennol is a care home situated in the market town of Llanidloes, in Mid Wales. The
 provider is Shaw Health care. The home provides residential care with aspects of personal care,
 elderly frail care and dementia care for adults 65 years and above.

 People experience good positive wellbeing outcomes, receiving respectful, person-centred care
 which supports their emotional wellbeing and sense of belonging. Staff demonstrate kind and
 responsive interactions and the service promotes autonomy, choice and control, enabling
 individuals to lead fulfilling lives.

 Care and Support is consistently good, with evidence of a focus upon person-centred practice.
 Personal plans reflect individuals’ needs, preferences and what matters to them; however, reviews
 and the level of detail and quality is not yet consistent across the home. The provider has identified
 this as an area requiring further work and has begun strengthening processes.

 The environment is warm, clean and homely and supports people’s comfort and independence.
 There are ongoing structural and cosmetic improvements underway. Safety checks are completed,

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to achieve positive wellbeing outcomes, as staff understand and respond to
 their physical, emotional and social needs. They are treated with dignity and respect and staff
 consistently demonstrate a caring, patient and compassionate approach in daily interactions.
 Preadmission assessments are completed with representatives and with other relevant
 professionals as required. People are encouraged to maintain and build on their strengths, with
 positive engagement supporting a sense of value and belonging. Feedback from representatives
 we spoke with is positive describing staff as kind, professional and welcoming. We were told the
 activities coordinator is proactive in her role, taking initiative to organise and deliver engaging
 activities that meet people’s needs. She demonstrates flexibility in her working pattern, including
 availability to work alternative weekends to ensure continuity of provision. Representatives spoke
 highly of her, praising her commitment and approach. One representative told us “I am informed of
 all planned activities and I attend where I can to support my mother as she enjoys these activities”.


 Support enables people to exercise choice and maintain control over their daily lives, including their
 routines, activities and personal preferences. During the inspection, we observed staff actively
 promoting choice in everyday living. A varied program of meaningful activities such as gardening,
 community engagement and social events encourages people to remain active, engaged and
 connected. The service continues to enhance these opportunities by introducing new activity ideas
 and developing creative fundraising initiatives for the residents’ fund. Following feedback gathered
 through a “You Said, We Listened” survey, the service established Friends of MYW to strengthen
 the voice of residents and their families in shaping the home’s future. This inclusive group, made up
 of family members, residents and staff, provides a valuable platform for collaboration and shared
 decision-making.


 Staff provided reassurance and support in a timely and dignified manner. People appeared well
 presented and told us they are happy with the support from staff. Positive risk taking is encouraged,
 supported by appropriate risk assessments reflected in personal plans which promote
 independence and informed choice. Staff know people well and develop positive, trusting
 relationships, enabling people to feel safe and secure. Health needs are monitored appropriately,
 with access to healthcare services as required. Emotional wellbeing is supported through
 consistent respectful interactions and a calm environment. Representatives told us leadership team
 is visible and approachable, with an open-door policy.

There are effective safeguarding systems in place to protect people from harm. Arrangements
include training for staff, clear reporting processes and regular review of risks which support people
to feel safe. Where people are subject to Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS), these are
legally authorised, regularly reviewed and remain the least restrictive option to keep people safe,
demonstrating a strong commitment to upholding people’s rights. Measures are in place to ensure
manual handling practices are safe.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support firmly centred on their needs, preferences and aspirations. Most
people are independent with eating and drinking and the service ensures access to a balanced and
nutritious diet, including meeting specialist dietary needs. Mealtimes are well organised and
support people to eat in a calm, dignified environment, with flexibility to meet individual
preferences. People were offered choice, with alternatives available and feedback about the food
was positive. People who preferred to remain in their rooms had their choice respected and were
prioritised for service. We saw a dignified, unhurried mealtime and a calm environment, supporting
positive well-being and choice.



The service provider completes effective pre-admission assessments to ensure they can meet
each person’s needs alongside those already living at the home. Information is gathered from
people, their representatives and relevant professionals, with people meaningfully involved in the
process, helping to build positive relationships from the outset. Care is delivered in line with
assessed needs, preferences and routines, providing clear guidance for staff. Personal plans are in
place and support person-centred care; however, further work is required to ensure all plans are
consistently comprehensive, regularly reviewed and updated to reflect changing needs. We were
assured processes are being strengthened to address this.


Feedback from people and their representatives is routinely gathered through meetings and
discussions, with records demonstrating this informs service improvements. Staffing rota is
effective, enabling meaningful engagement with people while other tasks are managed by relevant
staff. Safeguarding arrangements are robust; concerns are acted upon promptly with appropriate
referrals made to relevant agencies. Medication is administered as prescribed, with safe storage,
regular reviews and improved working relationships with local pharmacies and GPs. The service
provider completes audits to maintain consistent standards, and there is a clear culture of
openness, with learning identified from incidents and near misses.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is safe and secure. Appropriate identification checks are in place and a visitor
signing-in book is used to ensure only authorised individuals enter the service. The care home is
clean, warm and welcoming, supporting people’s comfort and wellbeing. Bedrooms are
personalised and equipped to meet individual needs, including specialist beds and moving and
handling equipment, which is safely stored. Communal areas are well used to promote social
interaction, independence and meaningful activities, with opportunities for private use where
required. People are supported to make good use of the environment, including outdoor spaces for
activities such as gardening, reflecting personal interests and backgrounds.


Domestic staff maintain good hygiene standards throughout the day, with appropriate infection
control supplies readily available. The environment supports engagement and reduces anxiety
through the appropriate use of space and activities. However, some maintenance issues require
attention, including leaks, flooring and fire door works. While these had no immediate impact at the
time of inspection, they present potential risks if not addressed. We were assured a maintenance
plan is in place.


People are protected from the risk of infection through effective infection prevention and control
practices. The environment and equipment are clean and well maintained, and food hygiene
standards are good. The service has achieved a food hygiene rating score of 5, reflecting high
standards in food safety. Staff are appropriately trained and demonstrate a clear understanding of
safe practices, including the correct use of personal protective equipment (PPE), which was readily
available. Systems are in place to monitor and manage supplies ensuring people are safeguarded
from infection risks. Staff knowledge of infection prevention and control is strong following
attendance at Powys Health Board Health Champions Day, with learning shared across the team.

The service provider ensures the premises comply with current legislation and national guidance
relating to health and safety, fire safety and environmental health. A fire risk assessment is in
place, substances hazardous to health are stored securely and water safety checks, including
legionella risk assessments, are completed. Gas and electricity safety testing is carried out within
required timeframes by external contractors.

Fire drills are conducted regularly and designated fire marshals are in place to support safe
practices. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are in place and provide clear guidance
to ensure people can be safely evacuated in the event of an emergency.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from a visible and approachable manager who promotes a positive, person-
centred culture. The manager is not yet registered with Social Care Wales, the provider must
ensure registration is completed in a timely manner in line with regulatory requirements. The RI and
senior managers continue to provide effective oversight and support. The manager has settled into
the role and alongside the leadership team, has identified areas for improvement, including
strengthening person-centred plans. Staff describe the manager as accessible, responsive,
supportive and empathetic, with a clear values-driven approach that focuses on understanding
individual needs and contributes to a stable and inclusive environment. Staff told us morale has
improved, stability and continuity is visible. Maes Y Wennol will host the regional “Star” awards with
several staff and people nominated, celebrating their successes.


There is strong oversight of the service, with evidence of regular visits and direct engagement with
people by the RI. Quality of Care reports are completed in a timely manner and are analytical and
reflective, demonstrating effective monitoring of service delivery. The provider uses information
from audits, feedback and learning from incidents to drive continuous improvement, respond
promptly to issues and recognise good practice. Policies and procedures are up to date, with some
under review, and are consistently applied in practice. Risk management arrangements are robust,
with clear contingency plans in place to ensure continuity of care and support in unforeseen
circumstances.


Performance, compliance and outcomes are monitored effectively through regular audits with
findings used proactively to support service improvement. The RI and other senior leadership team
are visible and approachable, fostering positive relationships with people, staff and external
professionals. Feedback from people, their families/representatives and other stakeholders is
routinely sought and acted upon, demonstrating a commitment to learning, development and
continuous improvement. Staff told us they are happy working at Maes Y Wennol. Staff are highly
knowledgeable and understand safeguarding procedures and their responsibilities to protect people
from harm. They receive training in whistleblowing and feel confident in raising concerns. The
provider supports leadership development at all levels, promoting shared responsibility,
professional growth and a positive learning culture. Staff are familiar with the complaints policy and
statutory notifications are submitted as required.


Recruitment processes are robust and staff receive a comprehensive induction, including
shadowing and access to specialist training, alongside strong employment support. All care staff
are registered with Social Care Wales, hold the required qualifications and have a valid Disclosure

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
