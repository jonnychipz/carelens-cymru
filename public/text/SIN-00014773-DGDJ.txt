Summary:

 People’s well-being outcomes are consistently positive, reflecting the high levels of skill,
 compassion, and dedication demonstrated by care staff. Care staff provide person-centred support
 enabling people to achieve their personal outcomes, make informed choices, and maximise their
 independence. As a result, people are empowered to lead meaningful and fulfilling lives, with
 opportunities to exercise choice and maintain control over matters that are important to them.

 People receive an excellent standard of care and support. Care staff demonstrate a comprehensive
 understanding of people’s needs, preferences, and aspirations, enabling them to consistently
 deliver high-quality, person-centred care. Personal plans are exceptionally detailed, accurate, and
 regularly reviewed, ensuring they remain relevant, responsive, and effective in meeting people’s
 changing needs and desired outcomes.

 The environment is good. The detached home is set in a peaceful rural location, with well-
 maintained indoor and outdoor areas. Systems are in place to promote safety. Communal areas
 are welcoming, and people can personalise their bedrooms to reflect their preferences.

 Leadership and management arrangements are strong. Staff feel well supported and receive

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People’s views at Prince Llewellyn Farm are actively sought, respected, and used to influence their
 daily lives. People are provided with meaningful opportunities to express their views about the care
 and support they receive. Regular key worker meetings enable people to discuss matters important
 to them, including their care and support arrangements, and preferred activities. In addition, the RI
 engages directly with people on a regular basis to gather feedback and ensure their views are
 considered in the ongoing development and improvement of the service.

 People at Prince Llewellyn Farm are safeguarded from abuse, neglect, and harm through robust
 safeguarding arrangements and a strong culture of vigilance. Care staff are recruited in line with
 regulatory requirements, with all necessary pre-employment checks completed prior to
 appointment. Staff undertake a comprehensive training programme tailored to the needs of the
 people they support, including safeguarding, positive behaviour support, and other mandatory
 areas. Discussions with care staff demonstrated a clear understanding of their safeguarding
 responsibilities, with staff able to confidently recognise, report, and respond appropriately to
 safeguarding concerns. These arrangements help to ensure people receive care and support in a
 safe and protective environment.

 People are consistently treated with dignity and respect and are supported to make meaningful
 choices. Throughout the inspection, care staff were observed interacting with people in a warm,
 kind and respectful manner. People are encouraged and supported to make informed decisions
 about how they spend their time and the activities they wish to pursue. Detailed, person-centred
 activity plans reflect people’s hobbies, interests, and preferences, ensuring opportunities are
 tailored to their needs and aspirations. People actively participate in a range of social, educational,
 and vocational activities and maintain a strong presence within their local communities. The
 evidence reviewed demonstrates the service’s strong commitment to supporting people to lead
 active, meaningful, and fulfilling lives while promoting independence, choice, and inclusion.

 The environment at Prince Llewellyn Farm is well maintained, with effective systems in place to
 ensure essential utilities and fire safety equipment are regularly checked, serviced, and remain fit
 for purpose. The home offers ample communal space where people can socialise, relax, and
 participate in activities of their choosing within clean, comfortable, and welcoming surroundings.
 People’s bedrooms are personalised to reflect their individual preferences, interests, and identities,
 promoting a sense of belonging and helping them feel settled and at home within the service.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive care and support that is responsive, person-centred, and tailored to their individual
needs. Personal plans are comprehensive, regularly reviewed, and developed through meaningful
consultation with people, their families, and relevant professionals. The service demonstrates
strong partnership working with families and external agencies, which contributes to the delivery of
high-quality, personalised care. Feedback from family members about Prince Llewellyn Farm is
consistently positive, with relatives praising the standard of care and support provided by staff. Our
observations during the inspection reflected this feedback, as we saw staff interacting positively
with people and delivering care in a dignified, respectful, and compassionate manner.

Outcomes for people are consistently positive due to the effective and consistent implementation of
behaviour support strategies. Care staff are well trained in Positive Behaviour Support (PBS) and
demonstrate a strong understanding of individual needs. Where required, people have
comprehensive PBS plans that provide clear guidance for care staff on identifying triggers,
implementing proactive interventions, and reducing associated risks. As a result, behaviours of
concern are effectively managed, promoting people’s safety, well-being, and quality of life while
minimising the need for restrictive practices.

People’s health needs are effectively supported, with timely access to relevant healthcare
professionals when required. Records demonstrate healthcare appointments and medical
correspondence are consistently maintained and easily accessible. Care staff have access to
detailed and up-to-date information regarding people’s health needs through comprehensive
personal plans. A stable and experienced care staff team provides continuity of care, enabling them
to develop a strong understanding of the people they support. This helps them to identify changes
in people’s health or presentation promptly and seek appropriate advice or intervention from
healthcare professionals when necessary.

People receive their medication as prescribed. Robust systems are in place to support the safe
storage, ordering, administration, and recording of medicines. Medication Administration Records
(MARs) are accurately completed, containing all required information and appropriate staff
signatures. At the time of inspection, no people were prescribed controlled drugs; however, staff
have received relevant training, and clear procedures are in place within the medication policy
should this change. Effective arrangements are in place for the management of ‘as required’ (PRN)
medication, with clear guidance available. Care staff responsible for administering medication are
appropriately trained, and their competence is regularly assessed to help ensure safe medication
practices are maintained.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is well maintained and purposefully designed to meet both the individual and
collective needs of the people living at the service. The rural setting provides a peaceful, spacious,
and therapeutic environment that promotes well-being and enhances quality of life. The home
offers a variety of communal spaces where people can spend time with others and participate in
meaningful activities. These areas are clean, comfortable, and tastefully decorated, creating a
warm, welcoming, and homely atmosphere. People have their own bedrooms, which are
personalised in line with their individual tastes, interests, and preferences, promoting a strong
sense of ownership and belonging. The home also benefits from a large, enclosed garden,
providing a safe and accessible outdoor space where people can relax, socialise, enjoy
recreational activities, and maximise opportunities to spend time outdoors.

Appropriate arrangements are in place to ensure the premises remain safe, well maintained, and
suitable for the people living at the service. A dedicated estates team is responsible for the ongoing
maintenance of the home, carrying out routine checks, repairs, and essential upkeep to ensure the
environment remains safe and functional. Records reviewed confirm up-to-date certification is in
place for key areas, including gas, electrical, and fire safety systems. Robust fire safety
arrangements support people's wellbeing and safety. An up-to-date fire risk assessment is
available, and regular fire drills are conducted to ensure staff and people understand the actions
required in the event of an emergency. Infection prevention and control measures are effective,
with cleaning schedules implemented consistently to maintain a clean, tidy, and hygienic
environment throughout the home. The kitchen has achieved a Food Standards Agency rating of 3,
demonstrating satisfactory standards of food hygiene and cleanliness. Overall, the environment is
managed effectively and provides a safe and suitable place for people to live.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and governance arrangements are strong and support effective oversight of the
service. Regular internal audits, Responsible Individual (RI) visits, and robust monitoring systems
help ensure the service remains compliant, responsive, and focused on continuous improvement.
Quality assurance processes actively seek the views of people and staff, using feedback to inform
service development. Comprehensive records provide clear evidence of oversight, accountability,
and the provider’s commitment to maintaining high standards of care and support.

Care staff are well trained and equipped to meet the complex and individual needs of the people
they support. The training programme covers essential areas such as safeguarding, health and
safety, and positive behaviour support, alongside specialist training tailored to people’s specific
needs. Care staff are encouraged to achieve recognised health and social care qualifications,
further enhancing their knowledge and skills. Regular supervision and annual appraisals are
embedded within the service culture, providing opportunities for reflection, professional
development, and continuous improvement. This helps ensure staff remain competent, confident,
and able to maintain consistently high standards of care and support. Alongside traditional formal
support, care staff have access to an Employee Assistance Programme, which offers additional
benefits including a confidential counselling service for those who may require support.

People at Prince Llewellyn Farm are supported by competent and appropriately qualified care staff
who possess the skills, knowledge, and experience required to meet their individual care and
support needs. Safe and robust recruitment practices are in place to ensure staff are suitable to
work with vulnerable people. A review of personnel files confirmed all required recruitment
documentation was present, including satisfactory references and enhanced Disclosure and
Barring Service (DBS) checks. Following appointment, care staff complete a structured induction
programme, incorporating mandatory training and opportunities to shadow experienced colleagues
to develop their competence and confidence in the role. Care staff are registered with Social Care
Wales (SCW), ensuring they meet required professional standards and are accountable for
maintaining their knowledge, skills, and practice.

The service’s aims, values, and approach to providing care and support are clearly outlined within
the Statement of Purpose, ensuring transparency for people using the service and their
representatives. A written guide is available to people living at the home, providing accessible
information about the service, facilities, and support available. These documents are also produced
in an easy read format to promote understanding and meaningful engagement. Policies and
procedures are regularly reviewed and aligned with legislative requirements and recognised best
practice guidance, underpinning consistent service delivery and the provision of high-quality care
and support.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
