Summary:

 Ty Pentyrch is a care home located in a rural area of Cardiff. The service supports people living
 with a learning disability, those on the autistic spectrum and other associated mental health needs.

 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes at Ty Pentyrch because they are empowered to
 thrive, with numerous opportunities to maintain, develop, and explore their interests, strengths, and
 skills. This ensures people experience a strong sense of purpose, achievement, and belonging.

 People receive excellent care and support because the provider conducts highly effective
 assessments of whether they can meet the person’s needs alongside those of people already
 accessing the service, before offering a service.

 The environment at Ty Pentyrch is excellent because the service provider actively seeks out new
 technology and creative solutions to help people live with as few restrictions as possible. This
 allows people to live safely in the community and achieve their personal outcomes.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 With few exceptions, people are treated with dignity and respect. They are actively supported to
 identify their well-being outcomes and are encouraged to build on their strengths and abilities.
 People consistently achieve goals important to them. We saw examples of people accessing public
 transport independently for the first time, attending college, developing new skills and increasing
 their confidence and autonomy.


 People maintain and improve their physical, mental and emotional health through high-quality
 support provided by care staff. People are supported through monthly multidisciplinary meetings,
 where personal outcomes, incidents and health needs are monitored and reviewed. Records
 demonstrated positive progress across a range of health and well-being outcomes. With few
 exceptions, sustained improvements and reduced risks have resulted in people and involved
 professionals agreeing reductions in staffing support, reflecting increased independence and
 positive outcomes.


 People are well informed about their rights and receive effective support and encouragement to
 voice their views, wishes and concerns. People actively participate in resident and keyworker
 meetings, influencing decisions about their care, support and future goals. The service provider
 also facilitates a supported people forum, providing additional opportunities for involvement and
 feedback. Where appropriate, people have access to easy-read information, communication aids
 and accessible guidance, enabling them to understand information, express their views and
 exercise choice and control over their lives.


 There are frequent opportunities for people to maintain and develop meaningful relationships with
 family, friends and their local community. People complete weekly activity planners with care staff
 and told us they enjoy the wide range of opportunities available. Care staff actively support people
 to sustain and strengthen relationships through facilitating contact, and participation in community
 activities. For many people, these opportunities have enabled them to rebuild social connections,
 form new friendships and become more actively involved in their communities. As a result, people
 experience a strong sense of belonging, reduced social isolation, and improved emotional well-
 being and quality of life.


 The provider prioritises people’s well-being, ensuring accommodation not only meets their needs
 but also significantly enhances their quality of life. People frequently achieve outstanding outcomes

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive exceptionally personalised care and support tailored to their individual needs,
preferences and personal outcomes. Comprehensive assessments are completed before people
move into the service, helping to ensure support is planned effectively from the outset. Personal
plans and positive behaviour support plans are detailed, strengths-based and regularly reviewed,
providing clear guidance to promote people's well-being, independence and quality of life.


People achieve excellent outcomes because care staff work proactively and creatively to help them
develop skills, overcome barriers and maximise their independence. We saw evidence of people
successfully transitioning from hospital settings into the community, achieving outcomes important
to them and developing greater confidence, and choice in their daily lives. People are supported to
access education, employment-related opportunities, community activities and meaningful
relationships. Professionals spoke highly of the care and support provided and the significant
positive impact this has had on people's well-being, independence and quality of life.


The service demonstrates an exemplary approach to supporting people's physical, mental and
emotional health. People's health needs are closely monitored and reviewed, and they are
supported to access healthcare services and specialist professionals when required. Detailed and
up-to-date health passports help ensure continuity of care and coordinated support across
services. Records demonstrated sustained improvements in health and well-being outcomes for
people.


People's safety is promoted through proactive, person-centred and least restrictive approaches.
Risk assessments are comprehensive and support people to take positive risks whilst maintaining
their safety and well-being. Incident records demonstrate care staff use skilled and proactive
strategies to reduce distress and prevent escalation. Best-interest decisions are made
appropriately, and where required, Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) applications are
submitted. Safeguarding arrangements are robust, and care staff demonstrated a clear
understanding of their responsibilities to protect people from harm and abuse.


Medication is managed safely and effectively. Medicines are stored securely and administered by
trained and competent care staff. Regular audits, checks and competency assessments support
safe practice, while medication reviews help ensure treatment continues to meet people's individual
needs.


The service maintains effective infection prevention and control arrangements. The home was

       Environment                              Excellent



The environment at Ty Pentyrch has been purpose-built to enable people to live as safely,
independently and comfortably as possible. People have access to their own flat or studio-style
accommodation, promoting privacy, dignity and a sense of ownership. The service provider actively
seeks innovative technologies and solutions to reduce restrictions and improve people’s
experiences. For example, flats are equipped with specialised equipment, including robust anti-
ligature bathroom fittings, anti-tamper button-operated taps, magnetic window blinds, and
individually controlled underfloor heating and air conditioning. The environment is thoughtfully
designed to meet people's diverse needs and preferences, while supporting independence and
well-being.


People's privacy, dignity and independence are embedded within the design and use of the
environment. A variety of communal and private spaces enable people to choose how and where
they spend their time. People have access to an attractive and fully accessible garden, which
promotes relaxation, social interaction and well-being. People are encouraged and supported to
personalise their flats and told us they had chosen features such as paint colours and furnishings,
contributing to a strong sense of ownership and pride in their home. People consistently told us
they were happy living at Ty Pentyrch.


People's care and support needs are met through a highly effective safety management system
which anticipates and manages risks whilst maximizing freedom and choice. Risk assessments and
best-interest decisions are used positively to enable people to access activities, facilities and
personal possessions safely, rather than imposing unnecessary restrictions. For example, people
are supported to use daily items and equipment appropriate to their individual needs, while
environmental measures discreetly reduce identified risks. Daily environmental checks help ensure
the home remains safe, comfortable and responsive to people's needs, enabling them to live with
as few restrictions as possible.


The provider ensures regular servicing, maintenance and prompt repairs are completed supporting
the safety and well-being of people using the service. Comprehensive security arrangements
protect people and their personal property whilst maintaining their rights, privacy and dignity. These
measures support safe access to and from the service and provide people with confidence in their
home environment.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



The service benefits from strong and effective leadership which promotes a positive culture focused
on achieving the best possible outcomes for people. Comprehensive quality assurance and
monitoring systems are embedded throughout the service, including audits of medication, finances
and health and safety. The Responsible Individual (RI) completes six-monthly quality of care
reviews, informed by regular visits and feedback from people, relatives and involved professionals.
Information gathered through these processes is used proactively to drive continuous improvement
and ensure the service remains responsive to people's needs.


Leaders maintain effective oversight of the service and promote open and transparent
communication. Professionals and relatives told us they are kept informed of significant
developments affecting people using the service and spoke positively about communication from
the management team. The provider has established a strong learning culture and actively invests
in workforce development at all levels. Care staff qualifications, competencies and training are
closely monitored, with high levels of compliance achieved. Robust recruitment processes,
supported by regular audits, help ensure people receive support from a skilled, competent and
values-driven workforce. Care staff are encouraged and supported to register with the workforce
regulator, Social Care Wales.


Records reviewed during the inspection confirmed care staff receive regular supervision and
annual appraisals. Care staff told us these sessions are meaningful, reflective and support their
professional development. The provider promotes staff well-being through a range of initiatives,
including access to an Employee Assistance Programme, whistleblowing support and staff
recognition events. The diversity of the workforce is recognised and celebrated through staff
networking groups, including a multicultural network. As a result, care staff told us they feel valued,
supported and confident in their roles.


The provider demonstrates a proactive approach to workforce planning. Staffing levels and skill mix
are regularly reviewed and adjusted to reflect people's changing needs and the complexity of the
support required. Where workforce challenges have arisen, leaders have taken timely and effective
action to maintain service quality and continuity of care. Minimal use of agency staff and feedback
from care staff indicated the workforce was stable and well supported. This strong leadership and
management approach helps ensure people receive consistent, high-quality care and support from
a service continually striving to improve.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
