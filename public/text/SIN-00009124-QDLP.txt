Summary:

 Hollybank Care Home Ltd is a residential home providing dementia care for up to 21 older people.
  It is located in Wrexham.

 We found people’s well-being and care and support is good, because of the genuine and person-
 centred care and support people receive. The care provided is person-centred, with people’s
 physical, emotional and mental health needs well considered by care staff and management.
 People and relatives are involved in making choices and decisions about what they want to do and
 how they spend their time.

 The environment is good because it is personalised, meets people’s needs and is well-maintained.

 Leadership and management of the service is good as there is effective oversight of the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have control over their lives as much as possible, and they are treated with dignity and
 respect. Where people do not have capacity to make decisions themselves, these are made
 following the relevant mental capacity legislation and best interest guidance, involving relevant
 professionals and people’s relatives. People have a choice of activities to take part in, and we
 heard and saw people enjoying these during our inspection visit, with lots of laughter and cheering
 during a bean bag throwing game which was taking place. There is a menu provided for people to
 choose from, but  if people feel like eating something different they can. Resident meetings take
 place to gather people’s views regarding the care home,  for example choosing colours  for
 communal areas to be decorated in. We discussed that an introduction of relative meetings would
 be beneficial so relatives can advocate on behalf of their loved ones, the management team have
 confirmed they will work to introduce these.

 People value the support they receive and the warm relationships they have with care staff. People
 like living at Hollybank Care Home Ltd, telling us “They are lovely girls here”. One staff member told
 us, “We will have a cuddle with residents when they need it, we understand that some people don’t
 have families to give them that affection”. Relatives told us care staff know people well and provide
 flexible support, they also said they are made to feel welcome in the home. Support staff are
 proactive in responding to people’s mental and physical health needs and adjust their approaches
 accordingly. People were well presented during our inspection visit, with washed hair, clean clothes
 and nails.

 People are safeguarded from abuse and neglect and supported to be involved in safeguarding
 processes where  possible,   if  not  their  relatives  are  involved. The  service has a  strong
 understanding of mental capacity, best interest principles and codes of practice. Care staff receive
 training in safeguarding and policies and procedures are in place and followed. There are strong
 support systems to ensure any risks are promptly identified and addressed. Risk assessments are
 updated in a timely way, and steps are taken to reduce risks which are agreed together with people
 where possible. The provider learns from incidents and accidents, changing their processes and
 protocols when needed.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive effective care and support to achieve their well-being outcomes. The manager and
a senior carer assess people’s needs before they move to the service to ensure they can meet
these. The service gathers relevant information from professionals involved in people’s lives to
provide up to date and person-centred support. A visiting professional told us, “It’s lovely here, it’s
one of the better homes we come to. The communication with the home is good. Everyone in our
team likes coming here and we have no concerns.” Support is provided by a stable, dedicated and
experienced staff team who have an effective understanding of people’s needs. We observed
senior care  staff having  in depth  conversations  with  professionals  contacting  the  service,
evidencing an up to date and detailed understanding of people’s needs and a collaborative
approach to providing care and support. A visiting professional told us, “They are proactive in
escalating changing health needs and if we implement or recommend something different or new,
they are very good at following  it through.” Care staff understand how best to communicate with
people and how to support them to feel reassured and calm. People have positive and warm
relationships with care staff, we observed people laughing and smiling with care staff and having a
hug when they needed one. The management and care staff team have the knowledge and skills
required to anticipate people’s needs and to identify when their mood or mental health is changing.
People are supported to attend appointments without delay.

Personal plans are completed to a good standard. They are detailed and easy for care staff to
understand. There is a ‘summing up’ section in the personal plan which summarises people’s
current needs and the support they require. Risk assessments are updated as people’s needs
change. There are processes in place to ensure personal plans are up to date to ensure they are in
line with risk assessments. Reviews take place in line with regulatory requirements.

Medication processes are safe and robust. Medication audits are completed by the management
team to ensure valuable oversight and any actions identified are addressed in a timely way. The
medication administration record charts we viewed contained the required information and had
been signed correctly. Staff responsible for administering medication have their competency to do
so assessed and they receive training in medication administration.  If people are administered
medication covertly, the appropriate processes are followed.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a home which meets their needs. People’s rooms are spacious, personalised and
reflect their interests. People can choose the colour of the paint in their rooms if they wish. There
are lots of personal belongings and people’s rooms are clean, tidy and homely. The service
provider invests in the decoration and maintenance of the home to ensure it meets people’s needs,
and all rooms and communal areas are well maintained. There is an area in the home called ‘The
Old Curiosity Shop’ which has displays of old typewriters and reminiscent items that people can
look at. People can mostly choose where they eat their meals and can socialise in the communal
spaces if they wish or have privacy in their own rooms. We found the home to be clean and tidy in
all areas. The service provider has infection prevention and control policies, with good measures in
place. Moving and handling equipment is stored accessibly for those that need it, but safely out of
the way to prevent trips and falls. The front entrance to the property is securely locked and visitors
are required to sign in and provide identification on arrival.

People can be confident the service provider identifies and mitigates risks to health and safety. If
issues in the environment are identified, maintenance staff are available to address them. We saw
a maintenance log in place which documents environmental issues identified by staff, these are
dealt with in a timely way. The home has the highest food rating attainable. Routine health and
safety checks for fire safety, water safety and equipment are completed, and records show required
maintenance, safety and servicing checks for the gas and electrical systems are up to date.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service provider has effective arrangements, governance and oversight to ensure the smooth
operation of the service and good quality outcomes for people. The manager is experienced, and
the RI visits the service in line with the regulatory requirements. There are governance
arrangements in place, and we saw evidence of actions from audits being completed resulting in
improvements to people’s well-being outcomes. Spot checks take place during various times of the
day, including the middle of the night to ensure that actions have been taken forward from staff
meetings and the quality of care remains high. There are policies and procedures which contribute
to the successful running of the service and inform care staff of what is expected of them.

People are supported by care staff who are safely recruited, feel well supported and receive
training to carry out their roles effectively. There are strict selection and vetting processes in place,
so people know care staff are qualified and trustworthy. Care staff told us they like working at
Hollybank Care Home Ltd and feel well supported. Care staff told us, “We are like a family in the
service, the staff are all different ages, but we all work well together and “The Responsible
Individual is very good with us, she is fair, wants us to do our job properly and appreciates us”.
Care staff have formal supervision and annual appraisals in line with regulatory requirements and
feel able to approach the manager with any issues they may have. One member of staff said, “I
would happily say if I had any concerns or issues, the manager and the RI are approachable but
are also straight to the point and let us know if something needs to improve.” The provider ensures
care staff receive training appropriate to the needs of people. Care staff said, “They will support us
to progress and explore any particular areas of training we are interested in.” Training records are
reviewed and updated by the management team to ensure training compliance. Not all care staff
are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator, or are in the process of doing so.
The manager and RI are prioritising this and will ensure registration levels improve by the next
inspection. Care staff team meetings are held several times throughout the year, and discussions
take place about how the quality of care can be improved.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
