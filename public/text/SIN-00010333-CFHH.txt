Summary:

 Cae Glas provides nursing care and support to adults with mental health needs. The three-story
 home is on the main street of Denbigh town, so people have easy access to shops, cafes and
 pubs.
 Wellbeing is good because people live a safe and healthy life, doing things they choose to do. They
 are supported to be independent and achieve their wellbeing outcomes.
 Care and support require improvement. People praise the quality of care and level of freedom they
 have living in the home; they are particularly impressed with the support staff who they describe as
 genuinely caring and kind. However, medication administration practices do not comply with
 regulatory guidance; a qualified witness is required to evidence the administration of certain drugs,
 and this does not happen. This is an area for improvement.
 The environment is good. The provider has invested in the building, making bedrooms larger and
 including en-suite shower rooms in all of them. The dining room has been redecorated and there
 are plans to improve other areas.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely and have control over their lives. Their preferences are sought
 when they are assessed prior to admission and on an ongoing basis through care planning and
 reviews. One care plan records how a resident does not want 2 hourly, nightly checks. The
 management team have meetings with residents where they encourage them to express their
 views on matters such as food, activities and the home. The service also gains views through the
 use of surveys and one to one conversation daily. One person shared how they are vegetarian and
 the chef accommodates this well. People shared how they choose their own daily routines and
 times of daily activities. The services newly developed café bar allows people to take breakfast at
 any time over the morning, choosing their own meal and preparing it independently if they are able.

 Staff expressed their occasional frustration that people are reluctant to engage in activities. There
 is no designated activities co-ordinator at present, so support staff try to arrange meaningful
 activities and carry out their role as a support worker simultaneously. The chef enjoys arranging
 food events to celebrate days of significance such as Halloween, pride week, birthdays and
 holidays and also to sample international cuisine. The manager is currently reviewing activities,
 soliciting views on what people want to do so resources can be more effectively utilised.

 People are safe and protected in the home as staff are all trained on safeguarding of vulnerable
 people. We heard people speaking openly to the manager about their issues, evidencing they feel
 comfortable to do so. An advocacy service is available to people. Records show any decisions to
 reduce a person’s liberty have been made on the basis of a multi disciplinary decision and proper
 approval from the authority.

 The service supports and encourages people to maintain relationships with their friends and family.
 There is private space in the home to meet with friends and they are free to go out to local cafes,
 pubs and leisure facilities. Events are arranged with others in the community, such as pool
 competitions and watching football competitions. The service strongly promotes anti racism; we
 saw a notice board with easy read information on the matter and learned how police had visited the
 home to inform residents racism is a crime.

 The provider has invested in the accommodation to improve facilities for people living here.
 Bedrooms are bigger and all have en suite. There is a development plan for home which promises
 further refurbishment.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement




People receive the quality of care needed to help them achieve their outcomes. Care plans are
person centred and clearly identify people’s preferences regarding how they are to be supported.
Positive risk taking is supported by risk management strategies; care plans identify people’s
strengths and highlight potential hazards, detailing the actions to be taken by staff supporting them.
Plans show health needs are monitored and associated appointments attended. We saw reference
to psychiatric support, physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech and language team. The
management team have designated mouth care champions and ensured a mouthcare assessment
for each person. People told us they are happy with their care. One person said, ‘there is more
freedom here’ and, ‘they’re really good staff here’. Another person reiterated, ‘it’s far better here,
more open and lots more freedom’. The home has a Welsh language champion. We saw a poster
board devoted to Welsh language, such as regular phrases and greetings, and the chef confirmed
how the home celebrates important days in the Welsh cultural calendar with typical Welsh baked
goods and other food.

People are safeguarded by the service provider’s policies and staff knowledge about adult
protection and abuse. The management team require all staff to have the All Wales safeguarding
procedure app on their mobile devices.

Medication practices require improvement. There are lockable cabinets in each bedroom and the
templates for plans towards independence in self-medication have been designed. Correct
procedures dictate the administration of certain drugs must be witnessed by two signatories and
this is not currently the case. Drugs are counted by a second nurse daily when they come on duty,
so balances are checked and audits are regularly completed but the second nurse is not present
when drugs are administered. Outcomes for people require improvement because current
arrangements for administering drugs put people at risk, and we expect the provider to make
improvements.

There are infection control policies and processes in place and staff have received training in
handwashing. We saw handwashing information in the home and records show there is infection
prevention training for all staff.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that meets their needs. It is well maintained and we saw
considerable investment has been made to the layout of the building, particularly in bedrooms and
en suites; these are now more spacious and promote people’s privacy and dignity more effectively.
There are various rooms for people to spend their time, depending on what they want to do,
whether they want to play pool or watch television or partake in arts and crafts.

The dining room is inviting and spacious, background music and ambient lighting enhance the
dining experience. A separate kitchenette allows people to prepare their own drinks, breakfast and
snacks independently whenever they choose. A fridge is well stocked with popular canned drinks.

Records show compliance tests are up to date for electrics, gas, water, fire safety, lifts, and
equipment to ensure continued safety. A maintenance log shows any identified repairs are dealt
with swiftly. Weekly fire tests are carried out to make sure fire safety arrangements are

There is room for people to sit outside and socialise with each other and take barbeques in the
Summer.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
arrangements in place to ensure quality care is provided. The RI (responsible individual for the
service) has various arrangements in place to ensure they maintain good oversight of the services.
Audits are completed by the management team, and the RI visits the home every three months to
carry out checks of their own. They speak with people using the service and with staff, check
records and audits that have been completed. Reports are completed detailing findings of the visit
and any changes that need to be made. The RI also completes a biannual quality of care review of
the service, evaluating all the information collated throughout the previous six months, feedback
received about the service, the condition of the building, developments and areas for further
improvement. There is a clear plan of action for proposed changes.

Support staff are carefully selected to ensure they are suitable for the role. They are thoroughly
vetted and undergo an induction period including mandatory training and familiarising themselves
with the policies and procedures of the home. Staff shared how they feel very supported and
valued. There are monthly recognition schemes for staff and long service rewards. The provider
has an employee assistance programme, and each staff has an employee wellness plan. Staff
know their rights, what support is available to them and how to get this.

Staff told us their skills are recognised and shared with other services in the organisation. The chef
has had opportunities to develop. He feels trusted as he runs his own budget and purchases the
best local produce. His ideas are listened to, so he feels valued. The service supports charities
such as Mind and Mc Millan cancer support and fund-raising ideas are fully considered. Staff said
they find the manager very approachable and very understanding. One staff member said ‘the
manager goes out her way to make sure we are happy. I needed support for personal matter
recently and she was lovely. I felt loved.’

Another staff said, ‘I always love coming to work. I am so grateful managers are always happy to
support me’. They were particularly pleased when the manager agreed with a suggestion they
made recently and asked her to lead on this; ‘we have opportunities to develop’. Staff confirmed
they have regular supervision sessions during which work and career are discussed. They
appreciate the extra day off per year to celebrate their own birthday.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.

identified.
