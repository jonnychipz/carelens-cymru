Summary:

 The service is located in Brynmawr and provides accommodation and support for people with a
 range of physical and emotional needs. Since the previous inspection, leaders have made
 significant improvements across the service.

 We rated Well-being as Good because people are supported by caring staff who know them well
 and promote dignity, choice and meaningful engagement.

 We rated Care & Support as Good because care planning has improved and people receive care
 that is responsive to their individual needs, with appropriate involvement from health and social
 care professionals.

 We rated Environment as Good because people live in a safe, clean and comfortable home.
 Leaders have addressed many of the environmental concerns found at the previous inspection and
 continue to improve the living environment.

 We rated Leadership & Management as Good because governance, oversight and quality
 assurance arrangements have strengthened significantly. Leaders have demonstrated commitment
 and capacity to drive improvement and sustain positive changes.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive well-being outcomes because staff know them well and support them
 with dignity, respect and kindness. We observed warm, relaxed interactions throughout the
 inspection and saw people engaging confidently with staff who understood their routines,
 preferences and individual needs. Staff promote choice and encourage people to remain as
 independent as possible in their daily lives.

 Outcomes have improved since the previous inspection. Personal plans now contain more detailed
 information about people's life histories, relationships, interests and preferences. This helps staff
 develop a stronger understanding of what matters to people and supports a more person-centred
 approach to care.

 People have increased opportunities to take part in meaningful activities. Activity programmes
 include social, creative, recreational and wellbeing-focused opportunities such as baking, exercise
 sessions, quizzes, reminiscence activities and crafts. We saw evidence that staff make efforts to
 include people who prefer to spend time in their own rooms, helping to reduce isolation and
 promote engagement.

 People maintain important relationships with family and friends and receive support to access
 healthcare services when required. Records showed staff seek professional advice appropriately
 and generally follow recommendations from healthcare professionals. This supports people to
 maintain their health and well-being.

 The service promotes Welsh language and culture through bilingual signage and displays
 celebrating the local area and Welsh heritage. These features help people maintain connections
 with their community, culture and identity and contribute to a sense of belonging within the home.

 Opportunities remain to strengthen how the service records, monitors and evaluates personal
 outcomes. While people's wishes and aspirations are increasingly found, records do not always
 demonstrate how these outcomes consistently influence care planning, activity provision and
 reviews. Activity records mainly describe what took place rather than the difference activities make
 to people's well-being. Leaders recognise this and are continuing to develop more outcome-
 focused approaches to ensure recent improvements are embedded and sustained.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support from staff who understand their needs and respond appropriately
when circumstances change. Staff work effectively with healthcare professionals and seek advice
promptly when concerns arise. Records demonstrate timely referrals and positive multidisciplinary
involvement to support people's health and well-being.

The quality of care planning has improved notably since the previous inspection. Personal plans
now include more detailed information about people's histories, preferences, relationships and daily
routines. This provides clearer guidance for staff and supports a more personalised approach to
care. Records show plans are reviewed regularly and staff monitor changes in people's needs.
People have regular opportunities to review their care and support through dedicated one-to-one
discussions with their key worker. This helps ensure their views, wishes and personal outcomes
remain central to the support they receive.

People's health needs are monitored appropriately. Staff support people to access healthcare
services and follow professional recommendations. We saw evidence of ongoing monitoring where
risks had been found and records demonstrated appropriate responses following incidents and
accidents. Medicines are managed safely, with leaders completing audits to oversee practice.

People told us staff are caring, respectful and responsive. Throughout the inspection we observed
positive interactions and saw staff supporting people in a manner that promoted dignity, choice and
independence.

Although outcomes have improved, some records require further development to ensure
information remains fully consistent across assessments, risk assessments and care plans. In a
small number of records, documentation did not always clearly demonstrate how found personal
outcomes influenced day-to-day support arrangements.

The service has also strengthened its response to incidents and accidents. However, leaders need
to continue developing how they analyse recurring incidents, particularly falls, to demonstrate the
effectiveness of preventative measures and learning. Embedding these improvements will help
ensure care remains consistently outcome-focused and supports sustained positive outcomes for
people.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is safe, clean and comfortable. Leaders have addressed many
of the concerns found at the previous inspection and people now benefit from an environment that
better supports their safety, dignity and well-being.

The home is clean, tidy and generally well maintained. Infection prevention and control practices
have improved and staff store equipment and personal care items appropriately. Housekeeping
arrangements are organised and environmental oversight has strengthened. These improvements
help reduce risks to people's health and promote their dignity.

Leaders have addressed previous safety concerns and people can now access emergency call
systems throughout the home. This helps ensure people can seek assistance promptly when
needed. Regular safety checks and equipment monitoring support a safe environment and help
reduce risks to people's well-being.

People have access to a range of communal areas where they can relax, socialise and participate
in activities. Some communal spaces are warm and welcoming, with seasonal decorations helping
to create a pleasant atmosphere. Displays featuring the local area and well-known Welsh
performers promote Welsh culture and identity, while encouraging reminiscence and conversation.
People's bedrooms are personalised to reflect their individual preferences and interests. We saw
photographs, personal possessions and treasured items displayed throughout bedrooms, helping
people maintain a sense of familiarity, comfort and belonging.

Equipment is available to support people's assessed needs and mobility requirements and
communal spaces provide adequate room for daily living and social engagement.

While the environment has improved since the previous inspection, some areas of the home
continue to require attention. We found a small number of maintenance issues which were not
having a significant impact on people at the time of inspection. Leaders were aware of these
matters and had arrangements in place to address them through ongoing maintenance plans.
Some communal areas could also provide more accessible information about activities and events
taking place within the home to further promote engagement and participation.

Overall, people benefit from a safe, comfortable and improved environment. Leaders have made
good progress since the last inspection and are continuing to embed improvements to ensure the
environment consistently promotes positive outcomes for people.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management has improved significantly since the previous inspection. Leaders
have strengthened governance, oversight and quality assurance arrangements across the service.
As a result, people experience improved outcomes and benefit from a service that is better
monitored and managed.

Leaders have implemented more structured auditing and monitoring systems and have increased
oversight of care delivery, training and service performance. Quality assurance activities are
completed more regularly and records demonstrate action has been taken to address concerns
found at the previous inspection. This has contributed to improvements in care planning,
environmental safety and operational practice.

Training compliance has improved and systems used to monitor staff training, supervision and
competency are more effective. Staff receive regular supervision. Supervision and training
arrangements help staff develop the skills and knowledge required to meet people's needs and
provide safe care and support.

Leaders seek feedback from people, relatives and staff through meetings, surveys and day-to-day
engagement. The Quality of Care Review identifies strengths and areas requiring further
development and demonstrates leaders have a good understanding of the service and where
improvements remain necessary. Action plans are in place to support continued progress and
improvement.

There is evidence of increased management oversight of incidents, accidents, complaints and
safeguarding matters. Governance systems are more effective at identifying issues and supporting
timely action where improvements are required.

While significant progress has been made, leaders now need to focus on embedding and
sustaining the improvements achieved. Quality assurance processes would benefit from stronger
evaluation of information gathered through audits, incidents, complaints and feedback to better
demonstrate the impact improvements are having on people's experiences and outcomes.
Supervision records could also provide greater opportunities for reflection, challenge and
professional development.

Overall, leadership and management are good. Leaders have demonstrated commitment,
responsiveness and the capacity to improve the service. The improvements made since the
previous inspection provide a stronger foundation for positive outcomes, and leaders continue to
develop systems to ensure these improvements are sustained over time.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
