Summary:

 Maes Y Vaynor is a small residential home for four adults. The service focuses on supporting
 people to become as independent as possible by building on their strengths and developing new
 skills.
 Wellbeing is good because people live their lives how they wish with choice and control. People
 achieve their outcomes becoming more confident and independent.
 Care and support is excellent because people are extensively supported to live well and achieve
 self-directed outcomes. People are proud of their achievements and praise staff highly for their
 excellent support.
 The environment is good as it is spacious and provides people with the room to live how they wish
 and the privacy they need. Communal areas need decorating and carpets need replacing, but this
 is something the service has already taken steps to address.
 Leadership and management is good. The manager and RI (responsible individual for the service)
 know the service well because they work closely with staff and residents, routinely carry out audits
 of records and procedures, and check the quality of care. They know what is working well and
 where improvements need to be made.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives, supported by positive risk management.
 Personal plans are very person centred and detailed and include people’s likes, dislikes and
 choices on a range of daily living activities, hobbies and interests. Additionally, they provide clear
 information about how everyone prefers to be supported to achieve their outcomes. Each person is
 involved in their care planning and has signed to agree with the decisions and plans. They engage
 in meaningful activities that are designed around their own interests such as day trips, football,
 holidays, shopping, cooking and other activities that promote their independence. Privacy and
 dignity is respected; each person can lock their bedroom door; there are several bathrooms and
 toilets; staff have all undertaken training in dignity and diversity.

 Desired outcomes are recorded in monthly meetings with people’s keyworkers and together they
 plan actions to achieve these. These self-directed, ambitious, outcomes are consistently met
 because the team are outcomes focused and have built strong relationships with people they
 support. One person who would not take any public transport, now regularly takes a bus to see
 family, has been on a train and taken a plane to go on a holiday. A person is working towards self-
 management of their medication; a person with challenging behaviours has now settled and there
 are no longer any incidents; another person has established a healthier sleep routine which has
 enhanced the quality of their days.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect because they know how to raise concerns.
 People confirmed their trust of staff and the manager; they feel they can discuss anything they want
 with them. People feel they are supported in a way that keeps them safe; positive risk taking is
 encouraged, and they take responsibility for cleaning and cooking in their home.

 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. They join with others in the
 community for activities; one person is in the local football team. They use local facilities and shop
 locally as well as taking buses further afield. People are supported to keep in touch with and visit
 their family and have access to mobile devices to contact people.

 People are supported in a spacious, homely environment that allows them to be as independent as
 possible. They are involved in the cleaning of the home, each having their own responsibilities.
 Communal areas need redecoration. People recently highlighted this in a meeting. They were
 subsequently involved in selecting colours for the work which is due to start in September.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive excellent quality of care and support and achieve their personal outcomes. We saw
highly person-centred plans that focus on people’s strengths. Records show people access health
services including the service’s in-house psychologist. Plans are reviewed to check they are still
relevant and keyworkers separately review progress with personal outcomes and explore new
goals. Potential risks are assessed and actions put in place to keep people safe while they work
towards what are sometimes challenging outcomes. The service enjoys exceptional success in
supporting people to achieve their outcomes, some people having become sufficiently independent
to move on. One person proudly shared their progress towards independence saying, when they
doubted themselves, the manager would remind them how far they had come and ‘remind me I can
do this!’ Residents meet every month and minutes show their views are sought regarding activities,
meal arrangements, trips out and the environment. Recently people shared they feel the communal
spaces need redecorating; they were asked about colour preferences, and this has now been
arranged

People are protected from harm and abuse as staff have all received relevant training and follow
established procedures on whistleblowing and safeguarding. People shared they are happy to talk
to staff about any concerns. They said of the manager ‘she is fantastic; I trust her. She really
helped me to get where I am’. Competency checks are carried out to ensure training is embedded.

People’s medication is safely managed, aided by the new electronic system for logging
administration. This is a live database which can immediately identify if there are missed
medication or errors. We saw records of medication kept in the home including drug counts
reflecting medication taken and remaining balances. A monthly medication audit is undertaken to
ensure accuracy of records and other aspects of medication management. Staff are trained to
administer medication and their competency is checked. There are measured, risk assessed
arrangements to support people towards self-medication using a step-by-step approach. One
person told us ‘I trust them; they are helping me to do my own meds and I know they will tell me
when I am ready for the next step’.

People’s risk of infection is minimised by the service provider promoting good hygiene practices,
ensuring sufficient supplies are available to meet people’s needs. Records show there are routine
infection control audits and all staff are trained. The service has its own inhouse ‘champion’ on
infection prevention and control, and this helps ensure staff and people living in the home continue
to follow correct procedures.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment with appropriate facilities and equipment to help them achieve their
well-being outcomes. The kitchen is spacious enough for people to prepare their own meals with
support from staff if required, and there are three other communal rooms in which people can
socialise, dine, or watch television.

All bedrooms are personalised and have been recently decorated according to the resident’s
preferences. Communal spaces need decorating and carpets need replacing in the hall and stairs.
At the time of the inspection, plans were already underway to address these matters with a
decorator booked to complete works in September. Residents have been involved in choosing
colours for each room and were excited to tell me about the plans. There are lampshades missing
in some rooms and some fixtures in the kitchen are in need of repair or renewal. The responsible
individual expressed their commitment to addressing all the issues raised immediately.

All safety checks have been completed; there is an up-to-date electrical installation safety
certificate; water hygiene, gas and fire safety inspections have all been completed. Records show
fire safety checks are completed weekly and monthly, dependent on the feature being checked. A
health and safety audit was completed in 2025 with areas identified now addressed. People have
the equipment they need to remain as independent as possible. Outside, the garden and
courtyards provide a space for exercise and a shelter for those who smoke.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance and oversight to ensure the service runs smoothly and
provides effective, quality care. The RI visits the home at least every three months to carry out
checks and audits and speak with people using the service and staff. This provides a regular
oversight of how people feel using the service and provides assurances it is being run as expected.
After each visit, the RI completes a report of their findings and outlines any improvements. The
manager also conducts regular audits to ensure safe practices. Twice a year the RI must report to
the provider on their findings. Each report includes an evaluation of the service and outlines what
the service is doing well and where it could improve. Staff and people using the service are
encouraged to give feedback and we saw these are very positive. They praise the quality of care
and support and illustrate people are achieving their outcomes. Any incidents are carefully logged
so lessons can be learned and practice changed if necessary.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. We saw rotas, observed interactions and spoke with people
using the service. All confirmed appropriate members of staff, including on days people had
appointments and wanted to follow interests outside the service. The provider ensures adequate
staffing to accompany people on holidays and day trips.

Records show staff are thoroughly vetted prior to employment including criminal records checks
and references. Staff are inducted including completion of mandatory training and a probation
period. People told us how happy they are with the staff who support them, although they all have
their own individual staff with whom they feel more compatible. Keyworkers are matched with them
accordingly, to share interests, and to foster trust and open dialogue. Staff told us they feel well
supported and encouraged to develop within the service. They have an excellent relationship with
the manager and RI and feel trusted to carry out their role and use their initiative.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
