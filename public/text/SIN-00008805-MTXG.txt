Summary:

 People are generally treated with kindness, dignity and respect and live in an environment that
 supports their well-being. However, inconsistencies in care documentation and monitoring records
 limit assurance that people’s health, personal care and social needs are always met effectively.

 Care and Support is rated Requires Significant Improvement. There are continued inconsistencies
 regarding personal planning, medication management and record keeping. Personal plans do not
 always provide clear, accurate or person-centred guidance. Medication errors and weaknesses in
 oversight processes were also found. Due to the seriousness of these issues, Priority Action
 Notices were re-issued.

 The service provides a generally safe, clean and homely environment. Living spaces support
 people’s comfort and safety. Communal areas offer opportunities for social interaction and
 relaxation.

 Leadership and Management is rated Requires Improvement. Although some progress has been
 made since previous inspections, quality assurance and governance systems have not been
 effective in identifying and addressing ongoing concerns. Further improvements are required to

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People generally live healthily and safely at the service. However, improvements to personal
 planning and record keeping are needed to provide assurance that care is consistently delivered in
 line with people's assessed needs. While progress has been made since the previous inspection,
 care records do not always contain sufficient detail, and monitoring documentation is not
 consistently completed. We found variability in the recording and oversight of key areas such as
 nutrition, hydration, personal care and skin integrity, making it difficult to demonstrate that care is
 always provided as intended. In addition, alternative methods of communication are not always
 available for people who are unable to use call bells independently. This may result in delays in
 receiving support and reduce opportunities for staff to respond proactively to people's needs.

 People do not consistently receive support that promotes meaningful engagement, social
 connection and emotional well-being. Activity records seen show limited evidence of meaningful
 activities and opportunities for engagement are not always linked to individuals' interests and
 preferences. Staff interactions seen were at times, task-focused rather than relationship-centred.
 We did however see care staff interacting with people in a kind, calm and respectful manner,
 helping to support positive daily experiences and maintain people's dignity and well-being. We
 received positive feedback from relatives during our inspection who told us they are happy with the
 care provided to their loved ones. Relatives spoken with told us, “We think it’s going pretty well and
 are happy with it here” and “The care home and the staff are great, I don’t have any concerns”.

 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. The environment is clean
 and comfortable, providing people with a safe and homely place to live. A range of communal and
 private spaces enables people to spend time with others or independently, according to their
 preferences. Bedrooms are personalised and equipped with appropriate furnishings and equipment
 to support people's individual needs, comfort and safety. Regular maintenance and health and
 safety checks are undertaken to help ensure the environment remains suitable for those living in
 the service. While the environment generally promotes people's well-being outcomes, ongoing
 monitoring is needed to ensure standards are consistently maintained and further improvements
 are sustained.

       Care & Support
                                       Improvement


Care and support is rated as Requires Significant Improvement because people do not consistently
receive safe, effective and person-centred care. We saw significant shortfalls across several key
areas of care and support, including care planning, medication management and the reliability of
care records. While some improvements have been made since the previous inspections, these
have not been sufficiently embedded to provide assurance that positive outcomes can be
sustained. These shortfalls have been discussed with the provider, and they are committed to
making the necessary improvements.


People do not consistently receive care and support that is person-centred or enables them to
achieve their personal outcomes. Whilst personal plans are in place, we found inconsistencies in
the quality and effectiveness of care planning arrangements. Personal plans seen contain
contradictory information, lack sufficient detail and do not always provide staff with clear guidance
on how people wish to be supported. Some plans do not adequately reflect people's abilities,
preferences, communication needs or desired outcomes. Records relating to daily care and support
are inconsistent, reducing assurance that people consistently receive the care required to achieve
positive outcomes. Examples of this was seen in skin integrity checks, dietary monitoring and
personal care records. Improvements are needed to ensure care is personalised, responsive and
consistently focused on promoting people's well-being. Due to the seriousness and persistence of
these concerns, a Priority Action Notice has been re-issued to the service. The provider must take
immediate action to address this.


The provider has made efforts to strengthen medication systems and has introduced an electronic
medication system, with the aim to reduce errors and improve consistency and safety. However,
the management of medication remains inconsistent, and we identified ongoing medication errors
and recording omissions. Medication administration records contain gaps and inconsistencies
relating to prescribed treatments. We found examples where medicines had not been administered
in accordance with prescribing instructions and oversight systems have failed to identify concerns
through routine auditing processes. Some medication storage areas are not routinely temperature
checked, which could impact the quality of medicines. These findings indicate that medication
governance arrangements are not sufficiently robust and do not provide assurance that people
consistently receive their medicines safely, accurately and as prescribed. Outcomes for people
require significant improvement because there is a risk that personal care needs in relation to
medication will not be met sufficiently and we have therefore re-issued a Priority Action Notice. The
provider must take immediate action to address this.

       Environment                         Good



During this inspection, we focused on the areas of non-compliance identified during previous
inspections. However we saw the environment at Y Plas generally supports people’s well-being
and provides a clean, comfortable and homely place to live. Accommodation is appropriately
maintained, with regular health and safety checks undertaken to help ensure people remain safe.
Bedrooms are personalised and suitable communal areas allow people to spend time
independently or socially according to their preferences. Although the environment generally
promotes positive outcomes, ongoing monitoring is required to ensure standards are maintained
and improvements are sustained.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



Leadership and management of the service is rated as Requires Improvement because leadership
and management arrangements are established but are not consistently effective in delivering and
sustaining high-quality care. While improvements have been made in some areas, governance
systems have not reliably identified significant concerns relating to medication management, care
planning, risk management and record keeping.

The provider has systems in place to monitor the quality and safety of the service. However, these
arrangements are not consistently effective in identifying and addressing areas of concern. Whilst
the provider has implemented some improvements, including the introduction of electronic
medication records and revisions to documentation, these have not yet been sufficiently embedded
to provide assurance that improvements are sustainable. Medication errors, gaps in care records
and shortfalls in care planning, had not been identified through the provider's own quality
assurance processes. As a result, governance arrangements require further strengthening to
ensure risks are identified promptly and lessons are effectively learned.

Staff have access to a range of mandatory training opportunities and overall training compliance
has improved since previous inspections. Training courses have been planned for the forthcoming
months to improve training compliance further and increase staff knowledge and skill. However,
whilst staff complete required courses, records and observations indicate that learning is not
always translated into consistently safe and effective care. Inconsistencies in personal planning,
medication management and risk monitoring suggest that staff do not always have the necessary
knowledge and confidence to apply training effectively.

Arrangements are in place to support staff through supervision and appraisal. However, these are
not always sufficiently reflective or focused on improving practice. We found supervision
discussions are often centred on training completion rather than professional development,
accountability and reflection. We had mixed feedback from staff about the support they receive.
While there are committed staff working within the service, further improvements are required to
ensure the workforce is consistently supported, developed and effectively led.

The provider is receptive to feedback because of the inspection process and willing to engage with
regulatory requirements. The provider has taken steps to address previous concerns through the
introduction of electronic medication systems, revisions to care documentation and increased
oversight arrangements. Staff spoken with demonstrated a commitment to supporting people and
managers showed an understanding of the areas requiring further development. Further
embedding of improvement measures is now required to demonstrate sustainable improvement
and improved well-being outcomes for people living at the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
