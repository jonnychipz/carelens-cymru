Summary:

 Well-being is rated good because people are supported to live healthy, safe and meaningful lives
 with choice and control over daily decisions. People told the inspector they were happy living at the
 service. Individuals access community activities, maintain relationships and are supported through
 positive risk-taking. No safeguarding concerns, notifications or open compliance issues were
 identified. Areas for development include strengthening the Welsh language active offer and
 evidencing activities through photographs and displays.

 Care and Support is rated good because people receive person-centred care supported by current
 care planning, risk management and multidisciplinary involvement. Safeguarding, infection
 prevention and activity provision were compliant. Medication systems were generally effective with
 regular audits in place; however, medication recording issues indicate the provider should continue
 to demonstrate audit actions are reducing risk.

 Environment is rated good because accommodation is suitable, well maintained and supports
 people’s well-being outcomes. Most fire safety actions had been completed, with remaining works
 subject to clear plans and timescales.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to live healthy and meaningful lives with a good degree of choice and control.
 Evidence showed people are involved in decisions affecting their daily lives, supported to manage
 their finances where appropriate, and encouraged to take positive risks that promote independence
 and community participation. Care planning and risk management arrangements were found to
 support personal outcomes and well-being.

 People appeared safe and protected from abuse and neglect. Inspection planning and analysis
 identified no open safeguarding matters, concerns, notifications, non-compliance notices, or
 important notices associated with the service. Systems were in place to ensure concerns are
 identified and responded to appropriately, and safeguarding arrangements were assessed as
 compliant.

 People are supported to maintain relationships and take part in activities that matter to them.
 Individual activity planners are used, with activities tailored to people’s interests and preferences.
 Opportunities included gym attendance, swimming, walking, community outings, participation in
 daily household tasks, access to transport, and support to maintain contact with family and friends.
 Care Staff facilitated activities on an individual basis, and people were observed to be engaged and
 content.

 Feedback available to the inspection was positive. At the previous inspection, people using the
 service commented, “It’s good here, I like it,” while relatives described the service as “home from
 home” and reported positive changes in people’s well-being. During this inspection, people told the
 inspector they were happy living at the service. Visiting professionals also reported that Care Staff
 knew people well and provided caring support.

 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. Evidence indicated that
 accommodation meets people’s needs, appropriate equipment is available, and people’s views are
 considered when improvements or adaptations are made to the environment.

 Opportunities exist to further evidence and celebrate activities through photographs and displays,
 and to strengthen the Welsh language active offer should future needs arise. Residents’ meetings
 were not taking place, although individual consultation arrangements were in place.

 People experience positive well-being outcomes and are supported to live safely, maintain
 relationships, engage in meaningful activities, and exercise choice and control over their lives. The
 evidence provides good assurance that well-being outcomes are being achieved.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive person-centred care and support which promotes their wellbeing, safety and
independence. People told us they were happy living at the service, felt safe and said Care Staff
supported them in the way they wanted. Care records demonstrated that people’s assessed needs,
preferences, communication needs, health requirements and risks were considered when planning
and reviewing care.

Care plans, risk assessments, health records and reviews were generally current and reflected
people’s individual needs. Case tracking demonstrated good multidisciplinary involvement, timely
healthcare referrals, regular reviews, monitoring of outcomes and personalised support planning.
People were supported to participate in activities that mattered to them, including community
access, exercise, hobbies and maintaining important relationships. Care Staff knew people well
and described person-centred approaches tailored to individual needs and behaviours.

Safeguarding arrangements were established and Care Staff demonstrated a good understanding
of safeguarding responsibilities, reporting procedures and escalation routes. Care Staff received
safeguarding training and were aware of current risks affecting people they supported.
Safeguarding referrals, incidents and behavioural risks were reviewed through governance
processes and evidence showed learning from incidents resulted in changes to support
arrangements and risk management approaches. A previous safeguarding concern relating to
restrictive practice was investigated and concluded with no further action following multidisciplinary
review.

Most medication management arrangements were effective. Medication policies, audits, stock
controls, storage arrangements and Care Staff training were in place. Monthly medication audits
demonstrated ongoing oversight. However, the inspection identified controlled drug recording
discrepancies, missing signatures and evidence of previous medication errors. While corrective
actions had been taken, recurring medication audit findings indicate the provider should strengthen
assurance that audit actions are effectively reducing medication risks.

The service was found to be compliant with infection prevention and control requirements.
Appropriate PPE was available and used correctly, waste management arrangements were
suitable, cleaning systems were in place and Care Staff demonstrated an appropriate
understanding of infection control practice. The environment was observed to be clean and
organised, supporting people to live in a safe and hygienic environment.

People receive care and support that is personalised, responsive and focused on achieving positive
outcomes. Strong Care Staff knowledge, person-centred planning, safeguarding arrangements and
multidisciplinary working provide positive outcomes for people.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that generally supports their well-being and independence. People
told us they were happy living at the service, liked their rooms and friendships, felt safe and were
supported by Care Staff in the way they preferred. Bedrooms viewed were personalised, clean and
reflected people’s needs and preferences. The home was welcoming, clean and homely, with
suitable communal areas, equipment and facilities that supported positive outcomes.

The provider has maintained accommodation that is largely suitable for the people living at the
service. Internal communal areas were clean, accessible and appropriately furnished. People had
access to transport, activities, outdoor space and equipment that promoted choice, independence
and community involvement. Essential servicing arrangements, maintenance systems and health
and safety checks were in place.

However, several areas required improvement. External areas appeared untidy, with damaged
fencing, overgrown grounds, cluttered storage areas and temporary security arrangements. The
conservatory roof was leaking and presented a potential electrical hazard, requiring immediate
action. The provider has a two-year improvement plan to address refurbishment and maintenance
needs.

The provider has made significant progress in addressing fire safety concerns identified within the
January 2026 Fire Risk Assessment. Most actions had been completed or substantially
progressed, including improvements to compartmentation, signage, evacuation arrangements, fire
safety monitoring and emergency planning. This provides greater assurance that people are
protected from foreseeable risks.

Despite this progress, some important risks remained. Fire door remedial works had not yet been
completed and remained the most significant outstanding life-safety issue. Additional fire safety
actions, including aspects of smoke ventilation and fire safety assurance, remained ongoing. Fire
records were not always consistently maintained and further verification is required to confirm all
remedial works have been completed to an appropriate standard. The inspection also identified
general maintenance issues affecting the external environment and parts of the premises.

People live in a safe and generally suitable environment which supports their well-being outcomes.
The provider has demonstrated substantial improvement in fire safety and environmental oversight.
However, outstanding fire safety actions, maintenance issues and the condition of external areas
require continued attention to ensure the environment consistently meets expectations and
supports people's safety and well-being.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management arrangements support positive outcomes for people. People said
they felt safe, were supported as they wished and were happy with their accommodation. Care
Staff described a positive, person-centred culture and said they understood their roles, knew
people well and felt valued, trained and supported. They consistently reported that managers were
accessible, concerns were acted upon and Care Staffing levels enabled them to provide good care.

Clear management and Responsible Individual (RI) oversight were evident. Policies, Care Staff
records, recruitment checks, supervision and appraisal arrangements were generally compliant.
Governance systems reviewed incidents, safeguarding, complaints, medication, Care Staffing and
service improvement. Leaders responded to identified risks, including escalating safeguarding
matters and taking immediate action where environmental hazards were identified.

A structured framework included RI statutory visits, Quality of Care Review reports, audits,
governance committees, risk registers, KPIs and electronic monitoring systems. Evidence showed
improvement in cleanliness, Care Staff supervision, training compliance and implementation of
Nourish digital care management system used in adult social care. Previous improvement
requirements relating to personal plan reviews, health and safety records, supervision, appraisal
and training were recorded as achieved. However, further assurance is required regarding the
frequency of Regulation 73 visits, completion of quality questionnaires and whether audit actions
consistently reduce recurring risks. The quality assurance policy did not clearly specify audit
frequencies or tools for care plans, risk assessments and behavioural support plans.

The provider evidenced investment in electronic care, HR, training and incident-monitoring
systems, alongside a two-year premises improvement plan. However, substantial agency
expenditure, the maintenance backlog and outstanding fire-safety works require continued provider
oversight to ensure resources support sustainable improvements.

Care Staffing numbers met assessed needs during the inspection, recruitment files were
satisfactory and mandatory training compliance was high. Nevertheless, significant and increasing
reliance on agency workers across day and night shifts presents a concern regarding continuity of
care. The provider has explained the pressures and introduced recruitment measures, while
recurrent agency personnel provide some consistency. This should remain an area for
improvement and monitoring rather than non-compliance at this stage.

The service is well led and supports people well, with evidence of responsive leadership and
improvement. Further strengthening is required in audit assurance, RI visit evidence, workforce
stability and oversight of recurring risks.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
