Summary:

 Willowbrook House Nursing Home is in a suburb of Cardiff, with local amenities close by. People
 experience good well-being outcomes because the service provides consistency of care from a
 dedicated care team. People form strong bonds with care staff, who know them well. The residents’
 committee regularly meets with service leaders. People’s voices are informing and shaping service
 development.
 People experience a good standard of care and support and are complimentary of the service they
 receive. Most daily outcomes are met. The service is continuing to strengthen how they deliver care
 in-line with people’s preferences and needs, and ensuring daily records are an accurate account of
 the care delivered. People access healthcare professionals and medications are well managed.
 The environment is good, which supports people’s daily outcomes. It is clean, comfortable and
 decorated to a high standard. Access to well-kempt outdoors spaces, and ample communal areas
 supports well-being.
 Leadership and management is good. Since the last inspection the provider has enhanced the
 governance systems with mostly good effect. Organisational structure, responsibilities and
 accountability is clear. The responsible individual (RI) is accountable for the quality monitoring of
 the service. The RI and governance team are taking prompt and effective steps to strengthen their
 processes to address matters identified at inspection.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People are treated with dignity and respect. People are encouraged to have control over their day
 to day lives whenever possible. Suitable arrangements are in place for people who require
 additional support to make complex decisions. Many representatives told us the service listens to
 them and works well with those who know people best. Since the last inspection, the provider has
 re-established the residents’ committee, who meet with service leaders to share views and raise
 matters on behalf of people receiving a service. Meetings are recorded, and the committee is
 provided with service updates, progress on matters previously raised, and people’s requests are
 well-documented. We saw input from residents relating to mealtime preferences, menu choices,
 activities and events. This strong approach to listening attentively and providing clear information
 promotes inclusion and empowerment of people to have a say in service development.


 People are offered choices in daily activities during weekdays, whereas activities provided at the
 weekends are more relaxed. Many people take advantage of the opportunities to be involved in
 hobbies and events with others. This provides social stimulation in group settings, which are
 supported by a team of activity staff. Group activities are generally well attended. Activities are
 promoted and information is available enabling people to make a choice and plan their day. We
 met many people who are content to stay in their rooms where they do what they enjoy, such as;
 watching TV, reading, knitting, listening to the radio or receiving visitors. We found records about
 social and meaningful interactions to be limited, especially for those who do not leave their
 bedrooms. We discussed this with the provider, and they reflected on how to strengthen oversight
 of how they record people’s well-being outcomes when there is a potential risk of social isolation.
 The provider arranges for faith services to be held in line with the current religious needs of people
 living in the home.


 Overall, communication needs are met, with written information and care delivery available to
 people and representatives in English. The service is not delivering the Welsh Active Offer to
 provide a service in the Welsh language but there is no impact on people at the time of inspection.
 The staff team are developing a cultural focal point in the home to evoke memories and connect
 people to their Welsh heritage. The provider understands their responsibility under the regulations
 to develop the active offer.

       Care & Support                       Good





People receive care and support from a staff team who know them well, understand their individual
needs and consider what matters most to them. Information about the service is available to help
people and their families make informed decisions. A qualified staff member completes a
comprehensive assessment before admission to ensure the service can support their personal
outcomes. People are complimentary about the care and support they receive. They described the
staff team as kind, sensitive and good at their jobs. We saw engagement between people and staff
across the departments to be respectful, warm and caring. People benefit from receiving care and
support from a team which contain long serving, established and experienced care staff and
nursing colleagues. People told us it is important to them to see regular care staff working within
their communities.


People largely receive care and support which meet their needs and promotes positive outcomes.
We found some care records did not always contain sufficient detail about people's preferences
and some aspects of personal care. We also identified inconsistencies between daily records and
people's presentation, particularly relating to oral care, nail care, bathing or showering. People
receive support to wash daily, but not necessarily in line with their preferences for showering or
bathing as outlined in their personal plan. Some people told us they occasionally experience a
delay before care staff can attend to them during busy times of the day. We discussed this during
the inspection with the management team, resulting in them reflecting on how to strengthen
oversight of daily care delivery and staff deployment. While improvements are required, we did not
find evidence of harm or significant impact on people's well-being and will review progress at the
next inspection.


People access freshly prepared home cooked meals, and their input and feedback about menu
choices is collected through surveys and the residents’ committee. The service caters for people
with additional dietary requirements, assistance at mealtimes and complex nutritional support
needs. People’s weights and skin integrity are monitored with good effect, and professionals told us
the service is responsive to people’s changing needs and referrals are made promptly. The
provider maintains records relating to clinical input from visiting professionals and their feedback
about the service is complimentary. Other professionals commented the service works well with
them and follows clinical guidance and advice.


The management of medication is effective, with clear roles and responsibilities for ordering, stock
control, storage, administration and record keeping. Clinical auditing is working well to ensure
medication management is reliable, safe and meeting people’s needs.

       Environment                         Good





People live in a home which is highly decorated, well furnished, comfortable and welcoming. An
effective maintenance team ensures the home is well-maintained. People have access to the
garden. The outdoor space offers people an attractive and stimulating area in which to spend time.
During inspection, we saw people enjoying a garden fete, which was well attended. The provider
arranged craft stalls, refreshments, activity stalls and a raffle. We saw people and their visitors
enjoying the event and being in the outdoors together.


People live in an environment which meets their needs. They have access to a variety of different
indoor communal areas to spend time. Many bedrooms are individually decorated and personalised
with things of importance to people; a small number of rooms were less personalised. General
standards of cleanliness and hygiene throughout the home is good. But we found a small number
of items such as bed bumpers, bedding and equipment to be worn, which increases the risk of
infection and compromises people’s dignity. Overhead storage in people’s rooms required attention
to ensure the safety of people, staff and visitors. The registered manager took immediate action to
address the environmental matters identified during our observations. They have taken responsive
action to improve the auditing and oversight of the environment, deploying clear roles and
responsibilities for ensuring standards are maintained and issues addressed promptly.


The on-site hair salon is a much-valued facility. People told us they enjoy having their hair cut and
styled as often as they want. Large communal lounges provide ample room for people using
wheelchairs or comfy armchairs to use the space. The dining areas are well-presented. Tables are
laid with tablecloths, place settings, menu information and a condiment setting. This provides
people with a dining environment they would expect at a restaurant or café. The provider has
further developed people’s dining experience by deploying enough staff to the dining rooms and
making changes to the positioning of the tables to give people a more social and inclusive mealtime
experience.


The provider is investing in additional moving and handling equipment to ensure there is enough
readily available to support people’s daily outcomes. The provider’s monitoring processes identified
the need for more equipment. This aligned with our inspection findings and feedback from care
staff. Communal bathrooms with adapted baths and wet room facilities are available to people.
Storage cupboards containing supplies of bedding, consumables and cleaning products are locked
to keep people safe. Clinical rooms containing medicines and clinical supplies are secure.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The organisational structure is clear. The provider has invested in strengthening the leadership
team since the last inspection. The changes foster a positive and compassionate culture at the
service with staff commenting on the confidence they have in the leadership team. The RI is
accountable for the quality monitoring of the service. They are supported by the registered
manager, clinical leads and quality lead roles. We observed improvements since the last inspection
in auditing, which are positively impacting some aspects of care delivery. This coincides with the
investment made by the provider to transition to digital systems. Whilst we see good progress with
auditing, improved clinical governance and service development, this remains to be an area of
growth for the service. The RI completes formal visits to the service and produces a quality-of-care
review. The review captures highly detailed accounts of stakeholder feedback and provides a
candid account of what is working well, and people’s views on what could be better.


People receive continuity of care and support from a dedicated staff team. We found the service
experiences challenges with deployment of care staff to ensure people’s needs are consistently
met. The provider is proactive in developing new ways of working to ensure people do not
experience delays, whenever possible, in their care delivery.


Records relating to safe recruitment are mostly well managed. Disclosure and barring certificates
are up to date. Most care staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator, and
over half of staff hold or are working towards a relevant qualification. Staff receive online and
classroom-based training to complete core topics relevant to their role, and compliance is high.
Clinical staff are in the process of completing enhanced training to meet the needs of people. Care
staff receive regular supervision and appraisals, but records relating to induction and probation
were not available during inspection. The provider has taken prompt action to re-evaluate their
processes and introduce a more robust system for documenting the support and development of
new staff. Overall, staff told us they feel listened to, they value the changes made to the
organisational structure and appreciate how positively it is impacting the work environment. Care
staff feedback demonstrates teamwork is a strength of this service.


Policies are in place to meet the needs of the service. People are safeguarded from harm and
abuse because the provider evaluates safeguarding matters, concerns and complaints. They
address practice issues and develop the service when lessons are learnt. The provider is delivering
the service in line with the statement of purpose, which is a regulatory requirement.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
