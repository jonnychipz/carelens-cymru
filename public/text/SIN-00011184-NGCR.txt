Summary:

 Y Frondeg provides a home for people living with physical and learning disability. The home is
 situated close to Caernarfon Town.

 Wellbeing at Y Frondeg is excellent. People are supported to be as independent and active as
 possible and achieve their chosen objectives. A person told us their life has improved drastically
 since living in the home. The service offers a strong active offer of the Welsh language and people
 remain a part of the local community.

 Care and support are excellent. People have care planned with them and records are person
 centred and reflect people’s choices and preferences. People receive care from familiar staff who
 provide continuation in care. Medicine processes are well thought out and audited.

 The environment is rated good. People can personalise their bedroom surrounding themselves with

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. People are supported and encouraged
 to be as independent as they are able. People set their own objectives and are encouraged to be
 active. We saw a person going to attend a water aerobics class, other people attended day care in
 a sister home and had enjoyed going to the chip shop for lunch. People who chose to remain in the
 home were peeling vegetables and enjoying making soup. People are offered meaningful activities,
 a person had collections and hobby materials in their room which they showed us with pride.
 Another person had pets which they were able to care for themselves and take to vet
 appointments. We saw an example of a person having little activity or motivation on admission to
 the home to, in time, leading a full and colourful life, joining local groups and travelling. The home
 has a mini-bus and can take people to day care, events and days out. People are supported with
 positive risk management to achieve their personal objectives. A person said, “The manager and
 staff do a good job, we are all happy here.” The service is innovative, having a shop in town, called
 “Galwch Acw” (call around), where people have meaningful employment making and selling wax
 melts and other products. People are supported with their communication needs, care staff know
 people well and can anticipate their needs. The service offers a full active offer of the Welsh
 language. We heard people and staff conversing naturally in Welsh. People can access bilingual
 information regarding the service, which is also in easy read format, this fosters informed choice
 about the service offered.


 People are protected from abuse and neglect. Care staff have safeguarding training and are aware
 of who to contact should they be worried about anyone’s care. People’s personal plans contain risk
 assessments to keep them as safe as practicably possible. People are supported to raise any
 worries or concerns and communication with people’s families is good. People spoken with said
 they feel safe and well cared for in the home.


 People live in accommodation that supports their well-being outcomes. People personalise their
 rooms as they like and can have things of importance around them. Care staff take extra steps to
 ensure people are happy with their rooms helping to decorate and paint them according to the
 person’s choices. Bedrooms are disabled friendly and people can access equipment needed for
 their care.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes.
People receive personalised care from care staff who are familiar to them and know their needs
well, providing continuation in care. People are supported to remain in contact with family and
friends who are welcomed in the home, and communication with families regarding people’s care
and well-being is very good. People access medical reviews and advice in a timely manner and are
supported to maintain good health. People are as involved as able with planning their care and the
service is especially good at enabling people to reach personal objectives. Personal plans are
regularly reviewed and updated as required to ensure people receive appropriate care. People are
supported in the language of their choice; this is reflected in their personal plans. Most staff can
speak or understand Welsh and most of the people using the service speak Welsh. Signage
throughout the home and documents regarding the service, are bilingual. People are robustly
assessed and can access any specialist equipment needed for their care. Equipment is regularly
checked and serviced to ensure people’s safety. Personal plans contain an effective pen picture of
the person, preferred routines, likes and dislikes and methods to diffuse any anxiety behaviours.
People’s plans are very person centred and provide care staff with good care instructions.


People are protected from harm and abuse. People can access an advocate to ensure their rights if
they have no other representative. Senior staff are aware of the safeguarding referral process
should any incidents or accidents occur to people. People’s falls and accidents are documented
and reported appropriately. People have risk assessments in their personal plans to mitigate risks
for people as far as is reasonably practicable. Senior staff notify Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW), of
any issues affecting the smooth running of the service.


People’s medicines are effectively managed. Care staff receive training and competency testing
regarding medicines administration to ensure they can practice safely. People have frequent
reviews to ensure their medicines remain therapeutic. Medicines are stored in a safe way.
Medicines are robustly audited, good records are available showing the numbers of medicines
entering and leaving the home and the stock numbers of medicines available for people.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment. Y
Frondeg has been due for relocation since some years in favour of a new building and so upgrades
have not always been budgeted for. However, as this has been delayed, a budget for upgrades has
now been agreed. Senior staff say they can access funds to replace furniture and equipment as
required and we saw monthly checks are made of equipment and furniture. The home is bright and
colourful, and people are assisted to personalise their rooms to a high degree. There is a garden
available where people engage in activities such as growing vegetables, making bird boxes,
painting stones. There is a parking area, and emergency vehicles can easily gain access to the
home. The food hygiene rating for the kitchen is five (very good), this is the highest score possible
and demonstrates the home is compliant to legislation. The home presents as clean and tidy and
walkways are free of obstacles and trip hazards. Risk assessments are in place to ensure people’s
safety within the home. Fire checks occur weekly and care staff receive fire evacuation training.
People have PEEPS, (personal emergency evacuation plans), which are accessible to the
emergency services. The home has insurance; we saw the certificate is in date. Utilities such as
water, electric, gas are checked for safety by appropriate professionals as are electrical equipment.


People spoken with told us they are happy in the home. People said they feel safe in the home and
feel it is their home. People have their own personal rooms which are adapted to people’s personal
interests and taste. People are enabled to keep pets in their rooms and are given the autonomy of
looking after the pet and ensuring they are healthy. We saw that people’s rooms are clean and in
good order and were told that people help with cleaning their rooms, doing their laundry, and help
in the kitchen. Senior and care staff ensure people can engage in these activities as safely as
possible and enable positive risk taking to promote people’s independence.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance, and oversight to ensure smooth operations and high-
quality care. The RI visits the home regularly and provides visit reports as required by legislation.
Quality audits and reports are also conducted in line with legislation asking the opinions of service
users, families and allied health care professionals. We saw positive feedback received from
families. Responses were all positive with families being happy with the care and support and
stating they felt the activities offered to their relative are meaningful. Families said, “Staff are
welcoming, friendly, warm, caring.” “The staff present a warm welcome with a gentle, patient, but
thoroughly professional manner,” “the building layout is secure, simple, and non-imposing. The
décor is in good condition and lends a bright atmosphere.” “They are like a big, happy family.” A GP
visiting people at the home felt the care is good and care staff can advocate for people
knowledgably. The service has a good relationship with commissioners and are open and
transparent in their dealings. Senior staff said they are well supported by the RI and can access the
training needed for their role. Service policies are regularly reviewed and are accessible to staff to
guide their practice. The Statement of Purpose and information for people using the service are
regularly reviewed and are available in an easy read format. We saw the manager conducts
several audits to ensure the care is of good standard in the home, the results are shared with the
RI and care staff to ensure continuing improvements in the home.


People are supported by care staff with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. We saw records showing care staff are updated with mandatory
training and supervision which supports them in their role and enables good practice. Care staff
have annual appraisals and say they are supported to attend further training to advance their
career. Care staff are all registered with Social Care Wales the governing body. Apprentices are
welcomed in the home and some elect to stay as care workers or kitchen staff. Care staff files are
compliant to legislation and contain the required checks to ensure they can work with vulnerable
adults. Senior staff conduct sickness management with staff, staff say their support is excellent,
keeping in regular contact with them and helping source medical advice. The staff team suffered
the loss of a colleague; senior staff have provided time and space for memorial and for staff to talk
if they wish.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
