Summary:

 People’s well-being requires improvement because they do not always have choice and control
 over their daily lives or receive support that consistently promoted their rights, independence and
 personal outcomes. Records and observations demonstrate people’s physical, mental and
 emotional well-being needs are not always met in a consistent or person-centred manner.
 Furthermore, people are placed at risk of harm because management oversight of care and
 support is not sufficiently robust.

 Care and support require significant improvement because personal plans, risk assessments and
 supporting records do not always reflect people's current needs, preferences or risks. Records
 relating to personal care, nutrition, hydration, repositioning, falls and incidents are incomplete or
 inaccurate. Medicines management arrangements are not sufficiently robust to ensure medicines
 are received, stored, monitored and administered safely. Infection prevention and control measures
 are not consistently effective.

The environment required significant improvement as poor management of the premises places
people's dignity, safety and well-being at risk. The provider has not ensured the accommodation is
maintained to an appropriate standard, and a number of environmental concerns identified during
this inspection demonstrated a lack of effective oversight and maintenance.

Leadership and management require significant improvement as governance, oversight and quality
assurance arrangements are ineffective. The Responsible Individual (RI) has not ensured robust
organisational systems are in place to support the safe and effective operation of the service.
Management oversight is weak, with auditing, monitoring and quality assurance processes failing to
identify a range of concerns relating to personal planning, falls oversight, medicines management,
record keeping, risk management, training, staff deployment and the environment.

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People do not consistently have control over their day-to-day lives. A dependency tool indicates
 staffing levels are sufficient; however, the staff skill mixes and deployment arrangements are not
 always effective in meeting people's needs in a timely, person-centred manner. People do not
 consistently receive appropriate support with their oral hygiene needs and we are unable to
 determine if people had received their personal hygiene needs met. We saw a small number of
 soiled beds where appropriate action had not been taken by care staff. These issues were only
 addressed after being brought to the attention of management by the inspector. We also noted
 refreshments are not routinely offered throughout the day, and people are not consistently provided
 with choice. People's opportunities to engage in meaningful activities and maintain their well-being
 are inconsistent. In addition, all meals are served on plastic plates, which does not promote a
 dignified and homely dining experience.

 Systems intended to protect people from harm require improvement. Whilst the RI undertakes
 monitoring activities and there are some safeguarding arrangements in place, governance and
 oversight systems have failed to identify a range of significant concerns found during this
 inspection. As a result, people, care staff and visitors are exposed to avoidable risks, and the
 provider could not demonstrate that effective systems are in place to consistently identify, assess
 and mitigate potential harm. Care staff recruitment processes are robust, staff have received
 safeguarding training, and appropriate safeguarding policies and procedures are available to guide
 practice. However, these arrangements were undermined by weaknesses in care planning, risk
 management, inadequate staff deployment and management oversight.

 People do not live in an accommodation that consistently meets their needs or promotes their well-
 being. Whilst access into and out of the home is safe and systems are in place to monitor appliance
 testing, these arrangements alone do not provide assurance the overall environment is suitable,
 safe or conducive to positive outcomes for people. Considerable investment and urgent remedial
 action are required to ensure all areas of the home are maintained to an acceptable standard and
 able to meet the current and future needs of people living there. We identified a number of
 environmental concerns which demonstrate the provider has not ensured the premises is
 adequately maintained, clean or fit for purpose.

People's personal plans do not consistently provide an accurate, up-to-date, or comprehensive
record of their care and support needs. Whilst some documentation contained detailed information,
we found several care plans and risk assessments lacked sufficient detail and clear guidance for
care staff on how peoples' needs should be met safely and consistently. As a result, it is not always
evident from the care planning documentation what specific needs people have or how these
should be supported. People's aspirations, goals and desired outcomes are not recorded. This
means the service could not demonstrate that care and support were always person-centred or
reflective of people's current needs, preferences and outcomes.

People do not always receive care and support at the right time, in the way they preferred, or in
accordance with their assessed needs. We saw aspects of daily care, including bathing, showering,
personal hygiene and oral care, are often recorded retrospectively later in the day rather than at the
time care is provided. We noted a number of dry toothbrushes and dry shower and sink areas after
people had arisen, which did not provide assurance that records accurately reflected care
delivered. Records relating to nutrition, hydration and repositioning are not consistently completed,
with gaps and missing information identified. In addition, all people living in the service were subject
to repositioning charts regardless of their assessed level of risk, and there was no documented
rationale to support this practice. This meant records are not individualised and did not clearly
demonstrate that care was based on assessed need.

There have been a number of unwitnessed falls within the service. Although a falls safety calendar
is in use, there is limited evidence of management analysis or oversight of falls to identify patterns,
trends or required actions to reduce risk. Similar concerns were identified in relation to incident
monitoring, where records do not demonstrate effective management review or follow-up actions.
Furthermore, falls risk assessments and personal plans are not consistently reviewed and updated
following falls or slips. The lack of oversight of personal planning, risk assessments, daily care
documentation and falls management is placing people’s health and well-being at risk and we have
therefore issued a Priority Action Notice. The provider must take immediate action to address this
issue.

Confidential records are not managed in a secure manner. During the inspection, care records
were found on tables in communal lounge areas where they could be viewed by individuals who
were not authorised to access them. We had unrestricted access to these records, which increased
the risk of unauthorised disclosure of personal and sensitive information. The manager informed us
that the care record folders should have been stored in the locked cabinets provided on each floor;
however, this was not occurring consistently at the time of the inspection. This has been identified
as an area for improvement, and we expect the provider to take action. We will review these areas

Arrangements for the management of medicines are not sufficiently robust to ensure medicines are
stored, monitored and administered safely. Care staff have received training in medication
administration; however, medication competency assessments for three of the five staff members
are overdue. Systems for the management of medication are not sufficiently robust. Prescribed
medications are not consistently checked and signed into the service on receipt. In addition, there
is no process in place to monitor and record transdermal patch applications and checks for
individuals prescribed these medicines until this was brought to the attention of the acting manager.
The medication room requires a thorough deep clean and improved organisation to ensure it was
maintained in a safe and orderly condition. The current medication audit process is not effective.
The audit tool in use relates to a medication system that is no longer operational and has failed to
identify the concerns outlined above. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we
have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address
this issue.

Standards relating to infection prevention and control, environmental cleanliness, and overall
hygiene within the service require improvement. Although staff have completed infection prevention
and control training, some staff did not demonstrate a sufficient understanding of their
responsibilities in maintaining a clean and hygienic environment. We observed staff failing to
remove and dispose of personal protective equipment appropriately after providing care and before
assisting other people living in the home. Used disposable gloves were found discarded in general
waste bins within bedrooms, and a used continence pad contained within a disposal bag had been
left on the floor of an en-suite bathroom. Some beds had been made despite soiled bedding
remaining in use, and other furnishings that had not been appropriately cleaned or replaced. This is
placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority action notice.
The provider must take immediate action to address this issue.

                                    Requires Significant
       Environment
                                       Improvement




The home environment is functional; however, it lacked a homely, welcoming and personalised
atmosphere. Investment is required in several areas to enhance people’s quality of life and overall
living experience. The provider has acknowledged this and informed us improvements are planned
following completion of the home's extension project. However, current management oversight of
the environment is not sufficiently effective to identify and address concerns in a timely manner.
The provider had not fully considered current best practice in creating a dementia-friendly
environment. Communal areas lack features designed to promote orientation, stimulation and
meaningful engagement for people living with dementia. In addition, many bedrooms are sparsely
furnished and contained limited personal items, reducing opportunities for people to feel
comfortable, maintain their identity and feel at home within their living environment. One corridor
was excessively cold despite warm external temperatures on the day of the inspection. The acting
manager told us this was an ongoing issue, which they attributed to people adjusting the thermostat
settings.

The acting manager does not undertake internal and external audits of the premises, which would
enable them to identify any areas of concern. The first-floor kitchenette is in a poor state of repair
and cleanliness and does not reflect safe food hygiene practices. Significant cleaning is required,
and several kitchen items and appliances need replacing. Following the inspection, the provider
informed us these issues have been addressed. We also found inappropriate storage practices that
could compromise safety. A fire extinguisher cabinet on the first floor is being used to store bin
bags and disposable aprons. The fireplace area contains clutter and exposed wires, and several
fire doors are being held open using items such as hoist weighing scales, chairs and curtains. We
also observed a damaged dark red window covering, which detracted from the overall standard of
decoration and maintenance within the home and may frighten a person with dementia. Records
relating to fire safety arrangements highlight further concerns. The most recent fire drill identified
issues regarding the frequency of evacuation practice and the provider's ability to demonstrate
ongoing emergency preparedness. These findings demonstrate significant shortcomings in
environmental oversight, resulting in negative impacts on people’s well-being, health and safety,
and increasing infection risks. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have
therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address this
issue.

There are measures in place to promote the safety and security of the environment. Keypads and
alarm systems are fitted to external exits and selected internal doors, preventing people from
accessing hazardous or unauthorised areas without supervision. Visitors are unable to enter the
home without staff authorisation, and records are maintained of their arrival and departure times.

Passenger lifts, hoists and fire safety equipment are subject to routine servicing and maintenance.
Gas and electrical safety inspections are completed in line with regulatory requirements, and
records demonstrate that repairs are reported and addressed promptly.

Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans are regularly reviewed and are readily accessible to staff.
Staff receive fire safety training as part of their mandatory training programme and but the
management oversight of staff participating in regular fire drills to ensure they are familiar with
emergency procedures requires improving.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


Arrangements for the oversight and governance of the service require significant strengthening.
Systems in place to monitor the quality and safety of care are currently not sufficiently robust to
effectively identify, assess, and address areas of concern. Audits of key aspects of care and
support are either not consistently completed or lack sufficient scrutiny to drive improvement. The
RI undertakes regular visits to the home and maintains regular contact with the acting manager.
Three-monthly quality of care reports are completed following these visits. However, the provider's
quality assurance arrangements have not been effective in identifying or responding to several
issues found during this inspection.

We found the provider's oversight arrangements are largely reactive, with action taken after
concerns have been brought to their attention. Opportunities to identify and address issues at an
earlier stage have therefore been missed. Areas where provider oversight has not been sufficiently
effective includes the management of environmental concerns, the quality and completeness of
handover and daily care records. Oversight of staff deployment, medication management, staff
training, falls and incident management, and the promotion of people's personal hygiene and
confidentiality has also been omitted. These shortfalls demonstrate weaknesses in governance and
quality assurance arrangements and have the potential to impact people's well-being, safety, and
the quality of care and support they receive. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk,
and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to
address these issues.

Arrangements to ensure care staff are appropriately trained, deployed and competent to meet
people's needs requires improvement. While care staff had completed a number of mandatory
training courses, including manual handling, mental capacity, safeguarding and infection prevention
and control, not all staff have completed the training necessary to meet the specific needs of
people living in the home. Although training records identify training completed, we found gaps in
key areas of practice. There have been a number of unobserved falls within the home; however,
the provider was unable to demonstrate that care staff had received training in falls prevention and
management. In addition, people living at the service require support with catheter care, stoma
care and diabetes management, yet staff have not received appropriate training to ensure they can
provide this care safely and effectively. We also observed occasions when people are left
unsupervised for periods of time. Agency staff are not always provided with sufficient information
about people's needs and risks to enable them to provide safe and consistent care. Staff handover
records lack detail and quality, and management oversight of these records is not effective. This is
placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority action notice.
The provider must take immediate action to address these issues.

People are supported by a staff team that are recruited safely and receive regular supervision. The

provider follows appropriate recruitment procedures, including obtaining references and completing
Disclosure and Barring Service checks before staff commence employment. The service has a
range of policies and procedures in place to support the safe and effective delivery of care. Staff
demonstrate an awareness of these documents and told us they have opportunities to discuss
policies, working practices and professional development during supervision sessions. Care staff

concerns and issues. Another member of staff said they receive "good support from the manager"
and feel valued as part of the team.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
