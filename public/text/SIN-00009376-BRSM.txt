Summary:

 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes because care staff provide highly personalised
 support that promotes independence, choice, relationships and community inclusion. People live
 fulfilling lives, maintain important relationships and take part in activities that matter to them.
 People, relatives and professionals consistently described positive and excellent outcomes in well-
 being.

 People receive excellent care and support. Care staff know people exceptionally well and provide
 highly personalised support based on individual wishes, strengths and goals. Staff use positive risk
 management to maximise independence whilst keeping people safe. Strong partnerships with
 health, mental health and social care professionals help people access the right support at the right
 time. We found evidence of significant progress in people’s independence, communication,
 confidence and quality of life.

People benefit from a good environment that supports their comfort, independence and well-being.
The home is warm, welcoming and highly personalised. Accessible indoor and outdoor spaces
promote independence and social interaction. Ongoing investment and improvements help ensure
the environment remains safe, comfortable and homely.

People benefit from good leadership and management. Leaders promote a positive, inclusive and
person-centred culture. The manager provides visible leadership and actively involves people in
shaping the service. We found several examples of excellent practice, particularly in promoting
participation, staff support and continuity of care. However, leadership systems require further
strengthening to improve oversight, recording and information sharing of allegations of staff

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes because care staff provide highly personalised
 support that promotes independence, choice, safety, relationships and inclusion. People live
 healthy and fulfilling lives, remain in control of their daily routines and achieve outcomes that matter
 to them. We found strong evidence that people maintain and improve their physical, emotional and
 mental well-being because of the exceptional support they receive.

 People are extensively supported to make choices and direct their own lives. Care staff promote
 independence through positive risk management and support people to do the things they enjoy.
 People choose when they get up, go to bed, eat and spend their time. Staff remove barriers
 through creative and personalised approaches. We found examples of people taking holidays
 abroad, attending community events, enjoying social occasions and visiting pubs.

 People consistently describe care staff as kind, respectful and supportive. One person told us, “It’s
 a real family home here.” Another described the service as “brilliant” and said, “They don’t treat us
 like residents, they treat us like people, like it’s our own home.” People told us staff listen to them,
 respect their choices and encourage them to achieve their goals. Relatives also described excellent
 outcomes. One relative said, “They are amazing with my father, absolutely brilliant.” Another told
 us, “He’s improved a lot since he’s been there.” Another told us how their loved one has become
 much happier in presentation and mood since moving into The Hollies and said, “she’s quite the
  little adventurer now its lovely seeing photos of all the places she’s visiting and enjoying with staff”.
 We found people are valued, respected and supported to reach their potential.

 People maintain important relationships and emotional connections. Care staff support people to
 spend time with family, enjoy holidays with relatives and keep in regular contact through
 technology. Staff also provide meaningful support during bereavement. Care staff support people to
 remember loved ones and maintain important emotional connections following bereavement and to
 share this experience together as a group. These experiences help people maintain emotional well-
 being and support each other through loss.

 People develop meaningful friendships and participate actively in their communities. We found
 strong evidence that people were experiencing opportunities that enriched their lives and increased
 their confidence. Records, observations and feedback from relatives demonstrated improvements
 in confidence, emotional well-being and community participation. People enjoy holidays, sporting
 activities, social events and community groups. One person reconnected with groups linked to an
 important part of their life history and identity. Another person regularly enjoys activities that reflect
 their sensory preferences, with speedboat trips being one of them.

People benefit from highly personalised accommodation that reflects their interests and
preferences. Bedrooms contain meaningful possessions, photographs and memorabilia. One
person proudly showed us a bedroom personalised with items that reflected their interests and

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive excellent care and support because care staff know them exceptionally well and
design support around their wishes, strengths, risks and goals. People’s plans are highly
personalised. They clearly describe what matters to each person, how they communicate and how
staff support them to achieve positive outcomes. We found people’s preferences and individual
needs were evident throughout their care records and daily support. We read outstanding pen
picture profiles written by the manager from each person’s voice and perspective. They capture
people’s character, personality and life journey, and clearly describe what is important to them.
They also provide strong evidence of the progress people have made and show how they are now
thriving

People are treated with dignity and respect. Care staff involve people in decisions about their care
and support them to maintain control over their daily lives. We observed and read evidence of
people being given choices throughout the day. Care staff adapt support around people’s routines
rather than expecting people to fit into routines set by the service.

Healthcare monitoring is robust. Care staff understand people's health conditions and respond
quickly when needs change. They maintain accurate records and seek specialist advice when
required. This helps people remain healthy and safe while enjoying maximum freedom. The service
has very strong links with health, mental health and social care professionals. The manager works
closely with doctors, consultants, district nurses, occupational therapists, physiotherapists and
specialist services. This collaborative approach helps people access the right support at the right
time and achieve excellent outcomes. We found examples of the manger strongly advocating for
people, arranging specialist assessments and ensuring health concerns received prompt attention.

Care staff demonstrate extensive knowledge of how to support people safely while maximising
freedom and independence. They use positive risk management and avoid unnecessary
restrictions. Rather than limiting opportunities, staff find creative ways to help people participate in
activities, develop skills and enjoy meaningful experiences.

We found excellent examples of personalised support. Staff understood one person's sensory and
communication needs in detail. They used sensory cues to help them understand daily routines
and activities. Staff used highly personalised sensory approaches to help the person understand
routines and participate in activities they enjoy. Another person's progress demonstrated the impact
of consistent and skilled support. When they moved to the service, they previously experienced
significant distress, had limited communication and relied heavily on support from others. Care staff
supported the person to develop their communication skills, increase their independence and
reconnect with other peers. This helped restore their sense of identity and belonging. They now
take part in community activities, use technology to keep in touch with family, and complete some
daily living tasks independently.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a warm, welcoming and highly personalised home. The environment supports
people's well-being, independence and quality of life. We found the home was clean, comfortable
and well maintained throughout. The home creates a positive first impression. The property has a
personalised house name, well-maintained lawns and colourful displays, which give it a welcoming
and homely feel. Accessible ramps support people to enter and leave the home safely and
independently.


The manager told us they have successfully built positive relationships with the neighbours. When
the home first opened, the manager actively engaged with neighbours and invited them to visit the
home and meet people living there. This helped build understanding and positive relationships.
People are now well integrated into their local community. The manager uses creative ways to
celebrate people's experiences and achievements. Digital photograph frames display holidays,
activities and important life events. These displays help people reminisce, share experiences with
visitors and take pride in what they have achieved.


The design and layout of the home supports independence and accessibility. Several accessible
doors lead directly to outdoor areas, making it easy for people to move between the house and
garden. The accessible pathways and patios connect around the home and encourage people to
spend time outdoors. The garden provides a safe and attractive space for relaxation, activities and
social occasions. A gazebo provides sheltered seating, access to shade and allows people to enjoy
the garden throughout the year. During the inspection, we saw people and staff enjoying lunch
together in the garden. Mealtimes felt relaxed, social and inclusive. Staff sat and ate with people,
creating a warm family atmosphere. Throughout the home we observed many personalised
touches which reflected people's lives, interests and achievements. We found the home organised
and well maintained throughout. The service has achieved a five-star food hygiene rating.


People benefit from a well-maintained environment. The provider has invested in improvements,
including new flooring and ongoing refurbishment work. Health and safety checks are up to date
and completed regularly. We saw evidence that the provider responded promptly to
recommendations made by the fire safety service. Required improvements had been addressed
quickly and further planned work was underway at the time of inspection. The service provider
invests in the home and improving the environment through ongoing work and refurbishments.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from good leadership and management. Leaders create a positive, inclusive and
respectful culture. People, staff and professionals have high confidence in the service. The
manager is visible, approachable and knows people exceptionally well. The manager leads by
example and promotes compassionate, person-centred care. The manager promotes a clear vision
of a “home from home”, where people are valued and supported to achieve outcomes that matter to
them. We found this vision embedded throughout the service. Care and support is organised
around people’s preferences, strengths and goals.


People help shape the service. Some people take part in staff recruitment interviews and attend
team meetings. Their involvement helps shape how the service operates and develops. As a result,
people’s confidence and participation have increased. This gives people a genuine voice in
decisions that affect their lives. People are also involved in discussions about activities, plans, and
service development.


The manager promotes equality and inclusion, making reasonable adjustments for staff and
provides tailored support when needed. This includes accessible learning resources, additional
coaching and practical workplace adaptations. These actions help staff develop their skills and
succeed in their roles and promotes an inclusive culture.


People receive support from a knowledgeable and competent workforce. Staff receive regular
supervision, annual appraisals and training that reflects people’s needs. All staff are registered with
Social Care Wales and hold appropriate DBS (Disclosure and Barring Service) checks. The
manager ensures sufficient staffing arrangements are in place to meet people's needs. Flexible
staffing and contingency arrangements support continuity of care and enable staff to respond
quickly to changing circumstances. We have not always been informed of details about
safeguarding concerns and allegations of staff misconduct at the earliest opportunity. The provider
also needs to strengthen systems for recording, monitoring and analysing concerns, patterns and
trends. This will support transparency, learning, oversight and continuous improvement.


Leaders actively seek feedback from people, relatives, staff and professionals. They use this
information to review and improve the service. Professional feedback captured within the service
was consistently positive and highlighted the excellent outcomes people achieve. We spoke to two
professionals during our visit. They confirmed the home is managed effectively and told us the
manager enhances people’s quality of life. There is a strong culture of person-centred care,

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
