Summary:

 People experience good well-being and receive person-centred support that promotes choice,
 independence and positive outcomes. Care planning is detailed and people, relatives and
 professionals speak positively about the support provided. People maintain relationships, access
 activities and live in a welcoming environment. Staffing pressures have affected consistency at
 times, although recent recruitment is improving this.

 People receive good care and support that is tailored to their individual needs through detailed care
 planning, effective risk management and strong partnership working with professionals.
 Safeguarding arrangements and Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) processes support
 people's rights and safety. Medication management requires improvement due to recording errors;
 however, enhanced oversight has been implemented to minimise risk.

 The environment is good. People live in a homely, personalised and well-maintained environment
 that supports comfort, dignity and well-being. Ongoing refurbishment and environmental
 improvements enhance quality of life. Robust maintenance systems, regular servicing, fire safety
 arrangements and environmental audits help ensure facilities; equipment and the environment
 remain safe and suitable.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People receive support that is centred on their individual strengths, preferences and personal
 outcomes, and are empowered to make choices and have control over matters that affect them
 wherever possible. Care plans viewed are comprehensive, reviewed regularly and provide clear
 guidance about communication, health needs, risks and what matters to people. Feedback from
 people and professionals evidenced positive health and well-being outcomes, particularly for those
 with complex needs. One professional stated, “Cedar House provides person centred care and
 understand the needs of the individual.” People speak positively about life at the service, one said;
 “I really like it here, the staff are great, they help me to do things that I want to do. I’m very happy
 here.” However, staffing instability has at times affected continuity of support and opportunities for
 meaningful engagement, meaning experiences are not always consistently delivered to the high
 standard intended.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. People benefit from a service where
 safeguarding arrangements, risk management processes and staff training help promote their
 safety whilst respecting their rights and independence. Risk assessments are detailed and
 proportionate, enabling staff to support people safely whilst encouraging positive outcomes. Staff
 understand their safeguarding responsibilities and feedback any concerns they have about the
 people they support. We saw concerns are appropriately escalated when required. Most people,
 relatives and professionals’ express confidence in the care provided. However, we found some
 issues? with medication records during this inspection. We are assured, however, that these are
 being addressed, minimising any risk to people. For example, stronger oversight of medication
 practices has already been implemented.

 People are supported to maintain meaningful relationships, participate in their communities and
 engage in activities that matter to them. Staff interactions observed were warm, respectful and
 person-centred, with positive feedback received from relatives and professionals regarding the
 caring culture within the home. One relative commented, “It is clear that the team know the
 residents as individuals and genuinely want them to feel safe, comfortable and well cared for.”
 Another described their family member’s birthday celebration organised by the manager as “a
 special and magical occasion.” People usually have opportunities to access community activities,
 entertainment and social events, supporting connection and inclusion. In recent weeks, staffing
 pressures have sometimes reduced opportunities for outings and meaningful engagement;
 however, this is an improving situation as newly recruited care staff are completing their shadowing
 shifts in the service so activities will soon revert to former levels.

 People live in an environment that is welcoming, personalised and generally well maintained.
 Improvements since the last inspection include refurbishment, redecorating and environmental
 enhancements, helping to promote dignity, comfort and quality of life. The environment supports

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes. We
reviewed care records, risk assessments and daily records and found these are detailed, regularly
reviewed and provide staff with clear guidance on how to support people safely whilst promoting
independence and choice. Support plans contain information about communication, health needs,
behaviour support, emotional well-being and personal goals, reflecting a good understanding of
each person as an individual. We saw evidence of effective partnership working with health and
social care professionals to support people with complex physical and mental health needs.
Professionals speak positively about the service, with one community nurse noting, “The quality of
care provided is consistently very good, with good communication maintained between the District
Nursing team and the care home staff,” whilst another stated staff “really know and understand
people’s needs.” Feedback from relatives is largely positive recognising the commitment of staff to
supporting people.

People are protected from harm and abuse. We found appropriate systems in place to protect
people whilst ensuring their rights and freedoms are respected. Care records contain
comprehensive and individualised risk assessments relating to areas such as mobility, choking,
epilepsy, self-harm, exploitation, nutrition and personal safety. We reviewed DoLS documentation
and found authorisations in place where required, with further applications submitted and monitored
appropriately. Staff work alongside external professionals to ensure restrictions are proportionate
and in people's best interests. Professionals consistently described the care provided as safe and
responsive, with one reporting staff demonstrate “good care, showing best interests of the patient.”
The combination of detailed risk management, safeguarding awareness, effective partnership
working and adherence to legal safeguards provides assurance that people are protected from
harm whilst receiving care that respects their individual rights and circumstances.

The Manager has implemented further oversight to ensure medication is safely managed. During
our review of records we identified gaps in Medication Administration Records (MAR) charts and
stock-count discrepancies that had not always been identified through existing audit processes.
Whilst we found no evidence that people had experienced harm as a result of these recording
issues, improvements to strengthen oversight and ensure medication records consistently reflect
medicines administered have already been prioritised by the service manager and we are assured
that these improvements will minimise any further risk of errors going forward. We saw medicines
are stored securely within a dedicated medication room and found effective systems in place for
ordering, receiving and disposing of medicines. Staff responsible for administering medication
complete relevant training and competency assessments, and medication procedures are
discussed regularly through audits and staff meetings.

People’s risk of infection is minimised. We found effective infection prevention and control
arrangements embedded within the service. Training records show high compliance rates for
infection control training, and infection prevention audits consistently achieved positive outcomes.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a spacious and welcoming home that is designed to support their comfort,
independence and quality of life. During our inspection we observed a homely environment which
has benefited from further refurbishment and redecoration since the previous inspection. Bedrooms
are personalised to reflect individual preferences, and communal areas provide opportunities for
social interaction, activities and spending time with visitors. Outdoor areas are accessible and well
maintained, including the addition of a new garden shed, enabling people to enjoy outdoor space
safely. We found the environment supports the diverse and complex needs of people living at the
service, including those requiring specialist equipment and mobility aids. Feedback received
demonstrates people feel comfortable in their surroundings and can access facilities that matter to
them. The service also continues to promote opportunities for social engagement through regular
entertainment, community links and use of communal spaces. Overall, the accommodation
promotes people's well-being, dignity and independence and supports them to achieve positive
outcomes.

People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment to help
them achieve their well-being outcomes while identifying, mitigating and reducing health and safety
risks. Good arrangements are in place to ensure the environment remains safe and well
maintained. Records viewed demonstrate essential servicing and maintenance checks are
completed consistently, including electrical installation testing, gas safety certification, water
hygiene monitoring, asbestos management, lift servicing, emergency lighting, profiling beds, call
alarm systems and moving and handling equipment inspections. Fire safety arrangements are well
established, with weekly alarm testing, regular fire door checks, external fire risk assessments and
individual Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) available and regularly reviewed. We
saw evidence of a comprehensive programme of environmental, health and safety and infection
control audits which help identify and address issues promptly.


The provider's governance systems ensure maintenance concerns are discussed through staff
meetings and quality assurance processes, with clear oversight from both local and regional
management teams. The home was observed to be clean, tidy and free from significant hazards,
with equipment appropriately maintained and available to meet people's assessed needs. Ongoing
investment in the physical environment, together with effective monitoring arrangements,
demonstrates a proactive approach to reducing risks and maintaining a safe living environment.
The service is currently working towards improving their food hygiene score which is currently a 3,
the manager is aware of the improvements needed and is working towards this as well as recruiting
for a permanent cook’s position in the service.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Effective governance arrangements are in place to monitor the quality and safety of the service. We
saw a comprehensive programme of audits covering care planning, infection control, environmental
standards, finances and overall service performance and more. These are completed by both the
registered manager and regional managers, providing independent oversight and challenge.
Formal visits have been undertaken regularly by the Responsible Individual, who also engages
directly with people living at the service to gather feedback about their experiences. Quality reviews
demonstrate feedback from people, relatives, staff and professionals is considered as part of the
provider’s quality assurance processes to drive improvements. We saw evidence of action planning
and continuous review, particularly in relation to staffing pressures, medication governance and
workforce development. Audit outcomes and quality monitoring activity provide assurance that the
provider understands the service’s strengths and areas requiring development and is taking actions
to improve. Policies and procedures viewed are reviewed routinely and in line with the current
legislation.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. Training and workforce records evidence staff have access to a
wide range of mandatory and specialist training relevant to the complex needs of people living at
the service. Mandatory training compliance is almost completely up to date, with particularly strong
compliance in safeguarding, medication awareness, infection control, first aid, moving and
handling, equality and diversity, and Mental Capacity Act training. Staff supporting people with
specialist needs have also completed training in areas such as acquired brain injury, dementia,
epilepsy, mental health, positive behaviour support, autism and more. Recruitment records
demonstrate appropriate pre-employment checks are undertaken before staff commence work.
Staff spoken with are knowledgeable about people's needs and described receiving regular
support, supervision and development opportunities. One staff member told us, “I feel privileged to
work here, I think everything here is well organised,” whilst another stated, “We all work together as
a team really well.” These findings support the view that people are supported by a workforce with
the skills, knowledge and competence required to meet their assessed needs.

The inspection identified staffing stability as the most significant challenge facing the service.
Feedback from staff, relatives and previous quality reviews highlighted the impact of staff turnover
and the continued recruitment of new staff members on continuity of care and staff morale. Several
staff describe the pressures associated with supporting and mentoring new recruits while
continuing to meet the needs of people living at the service. One member of staff said, “The staff
turnover is exhausting, it feels like we’re always training new people,” whilst another stated, “The
new starters we’ve had this time are really good and I think once they know what they are doing we
may finally be a bit more stable.” Despite these challenges, we found evidence that recruitment
activity had been significant, with several new staff already appointed and progressing through
induction and compliance processes. The provider had recognised the impact workforce instability

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
