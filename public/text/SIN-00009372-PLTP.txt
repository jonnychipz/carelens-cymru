       Care & Support                       Good

Summary:

 We rated well-being as requires improvement because people do not always have meaningful
 opportunities to influence reviews, decisions and care planning. Records do not consistently
 demonstrate how people’s views, or those of their representatives, inform decisions. In addition,
 people have not always accessed the range of community and sensory activities described in the
 provider’s Statement of Purpose and assessed as important to them in their personal plans.

 People experience positive care and support from a staff team which knows them well and
 promotes their dignity, respect and individual choices. We observed warm, person-centred
 interactions and found detailed care planning, risk assessments and medication management
 arrangements which support people’s safety and well-being.

 We found the environment to be of good standard. People live in a comfortable, homely and well-
 maintained environment that supports their needs and independence. The home provides a range

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People maintain relationships with family members, friends and others who are important to them.
 Care staff understand the value of these relationships and support people to stay in contact.
 Planned social activities and positive interactions with care staff help promote people’s well-being.
 The management team arranges social events with people from other services to celebrate special
 occasions and maintain social connections. This supports people to develop and sustain
 relationships outside their immediate home environment.
 Outcomes for people require improvement because people and their representatives do not always
 have meaningful opportunities to share their views through reviews, meetings or advocacy. People
 do not consistently achieve the outcomes they identify for themselves. This is because care
 reviews do not always include people’s views or the views of their representatives. We also found
 gaps in record keeping. Records do not always demonstrate people’s representatives have been
 involved in best-interest decisions where appropriate. We identified gaps in recording and
 promoting people’s voice and the views of their representatives during incidents, periods of distress
 and changes in emotional well-being. The provider introduced a new distress monitoring tool and
 arranged support from its internal PBS (Positive Behaviour Support) trainer to strengthen staff
 knowledge and skills in recognising, recording and responding to distress.

 Outcomes for people require improvement because people are not always supported to maximise
 their independence and make choices about how they spend their time. Service pressures, staffing
 arrangements and a lack of available drivers have reduced opportunities for people to access
 community activities. The provider’s Statement of Purpose describes a range of sensory activities
 and meaningful opportunities in the community. However, records show people have not accessed
 these opportunities consistently for some time. We saw evidence people do access the community,
 but opportunities are infrequent and do not always reflect the person’s goals, interests or
 aspirations. The provider has started to address these issues, and we saw evidence of changes
 beginning to be implemented during the inspection.

 Challenges within the service have had an impact on outcomes for people. We expect the provider
 to take action and will follow up progress at the next inspection.

       Care & Support                       Good




Outcomes for people are good because care staff treat people with dignity and respect. We
observed positive, person-centred interactions throughout the inspection. Most care staff used
calm, gentle and empathetic approaches promoting people's well-being and independence. Care
staff offered people choices in their daily lives, including what to eat, how to spend their time and
activities they wished to take part in within the home.

Care staff know people well and understand their individual needs, preferences and routines. We
found positive relationships between people and care staff, creating a familiar and supportive
environment. Although the service has experienced some staff turnover, several staff have worked
at the service for a long time, which helps provide continuity of care. We saw an example of the
deputy manager advocating on behalf of a person to achieve positive health and well-being
outcomes, including supporting a person to access more flexible healthcare arrangements that
better reflected their preferences and daily routines.

People's personal plans focus on their strengths and describe how care staff should support them
to achieve their personal outcomes. Plans consistently reflect people's preferences, routines,
beliefs and support needs. We reviewed detailed personal profiles, assessments and risk
assessments which help guide care and support. The service has also introduced a new recording
system to evidence the choices offered to people, including meal options, clothing choices and
activities. We saw photographs and other records capturing meaningful experiences, including
holidays, social events and community activities.

People receive their medication as prescribed in accordance with national guidelines and the
service provider’s medication policy. Audits are completed to ensure consistency of practice and
good standards are maintained. Leaders made improvements after identifying medication had not
always been stored in line with requirements. They also identified a pattern of medication errors
and took action to address this, streamlining the organisation of stock, which is effective.

People are protected from abuse and avoidable harm as far as possible. Most care staff
demonstrate a good understanding of safeguarding and whistleblowing procedures and are
confident raising concerns. Leaders and managers respond to safeguarding concerns and
allegations of staff misconduct appropriately and make referrals to safeguarding teams where
required. Leaders continue to monitor staff practice and have plans to introduce additional coaching
and mentoring to strengthen values-based, person-centred practice across the service. The
registered manager has commenced reflective team meetings within the service to address any
concerns raised and to improve and promote person centred practices.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an accommodation which supports their needs, well-being and independence. The
home is homely, accessible and suited to the people who live there. We observed a calm
atmosphere throughout the inspection. Care staff responded appropriately to people’s sensory
needs and helped create an environment where people appeared comfortable and settled. The
home is situated in a quiet residential area close to local amenities.


The environment is clean, warm and well maintained. People have spacious bedrooms that are
personalised to reflect their interests, preferences and individual needs. People can spend time in a
range of communal and private areas. Shared living spaces encourage social interaction, while
bedrooms provide privacy and opportunities for relaxation.


People benefit from accessible outdoor spaces. The gardens and patio areas are attractive, well
maintained and provide opportunities for relaxation, recreation and social engagement. We saw a
‘grow your own’ garden area and observed people using the enclosed outdoor space. One person
had access to a separate outdoor area that could provide additional opportunities for sensory
activities.


Arrangements to promote people's safety are effective. Records showed regular fire drills,
personalised emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs), equipment servicing, water temperature
checks, asbestos management and legionella monitoring. These arrangements help keep people
safe while supporting their independence. People have individualised PEEPs which are reviewed
and updated regularly. We identified temperatures within the communal area used to store
medication can increase during periods of hot weather. The provider is considering how they can
address this.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management are good because leaders understand the service well and
demonstrate a commitment to improving outcomes for people. The Responsible Individual (RI)
regularly visits the service and maintains oversight through quality assurance activities. Senior
leaders identify patterns, trends and areas requiring improvement and work closely with the
management team to address them. We found leaders to be open, transparent and reflective
throughout the inspection.

Managers provide visible leadership and promote person-centred values throughout the service.
We saw positive examples of management supporting people and working alongside care staff.
The management team recognises further coaching and mentoring is needed to develop staff
confidence and skills in some areas of practice.

The provider has systems to monitor the quality and safety of the service. These include visits by
leaders to audit records, observe practices and gain feedback from people and staff. Leaders use
this information to identify areas for improvement. However, we found improvements identified
through quality assurance processes have not always been addressed in a timely way. Records
show some areas for improvement have remained unresolved for over a year. This is longer than
we would expect and has delayed progress in improving outcomes for people.

The RI and management team had already identified many of the issues affecting people’s well-
being which we found during inspection. Leaders acknowledged barriers affecting progress and
took action to address them, including changes in the registered manager. We saw evidence
leaders had implemented additional measures to address these issues.

The service employs appropriate recruitment, supervision and training arrangements. Care staff
receive training relevant to their roles and managers work proactively with health and social care
professionals when people's needs change. Staffing levels are sufficient to meet people’s care and
support needs within the service.

Most relatives gave positive feedback about the service, management team and care staff. They
told us they were kept informed about significant changes and felt their family members were safe.
Leaders are aware communication with some families need strengthening, along with recording of
concerns and complaints to ensure actions and outcomes are clearly evidenced.

Overall, leadership and management are good because leaders understand the service, identify
areas requiring improvement and are open and transparent about challenges. Leaders take action
when standards fall below expectations and have implemented additional measures to improve

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
