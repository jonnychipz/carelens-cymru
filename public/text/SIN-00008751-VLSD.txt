Summary:

 The Grange is a residential home, providing support with personal care for adults with learning
 disabilities, autistic spectrum disorders, mental health issues and acquired brain injury. It is situated
 in the town of St Asaph, with easy access to GP surgeries and local amenities such as local shops,
 a leisure centre and library. The home is comfortable and homely throughout providing a good
 quality environment which meets people’s needs. Support staff prioritise people’s wellbeing. They
 know people well and ensure they are supported to achieve good wellbeing outcomes. The care
 and support provided is good as support staff work closely with health and social care professionals
 to ensure people’s needs are met.

 The manager and responsible individual (RI) oversee the service, and are approachable and
 available to people, relatives, and staff. They value peoples’ voices and ensure their opinions are
 listened to and any concerns are always taken seriously. They seek people’s views on how they
 would like the service to run, through regular house meetings and an open and transparent
 approach.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People’s right to make their own choices and take informed personal risks is promoted by the
 positive culture in the service. People are supported to participate in a range of activities of their
 choosing, including social groups, volunteering, and paid work. People told us about the activities
 they have planned, and we could see they are allocated one to one time to ensure they are
 supported to do the things which are important to them. Those who prefer to maintain their own
 independence are supported to do so as far as possible. People are supported to take positive
 risks, such as managing their own medication. People are actively involved in the decisions that
 affect them, ensuring their voices are respected and acted upon. This strong approach enables
 people to identify and achieve their wellbeing outcomes. People told us they feel well supported at
 the service. One person told us they are grateful to have been placed at the service. A social care
 professional we spoke with told us “I feel that The Grange supports X extremely well, they take time
 to understand [this person’s] communication, preferences and triggers and support them in an
 empathetic, understanding, and professional way.”

 People are safeguarded from abuse and neglect. There is an open-door policy, and we saw people
 come and speak with management throughout the day. We saw when people raise issues or
 concerns, these are taken seriously and thoroughly investigated. There is a strong emphasis on
 listening to people and ensuring their voices are heard, which ensures their rights and dignity are
 consistently upheld. People are encouraged to access advocates and attend self-advocacy groups
 in the local community.

 People are supported to develop and sustain positive relationships within their community and the
 people they live with. Support staff hold weekly house meetings in which people are consulted for
 their views and consider their plans for the week ahead. People are involved in the running of the
 service where possible and are asked for their feedback when new start staff complete trial shifts
 The service provider has formed positive communication and relationships with people’s relatives.
 There are regular newsletters to keep them informed about what is happening in the service, and
 an annual Christmas party is held for people, their relatives, and staff members. One relative told
 us their family member was happy and settled at the service and they are well supported to stay in
 touch with family members with the use of technology. Whilst staff members do not speak fluent
 Welsh, they can communicate with some basic Welsh and have access to technology to assist with
 this. Welsh speaking people are encouraged to meet and spend time together so they can
 converse in Welsh.

       Care & Support                       Good



The provider gathers useful information from professionals and others who are already involved in
people’s care and support and speaks to people about their needs and preferences when possible.
This information fully informs the decision about whether they can provide a service to people.
People are given an opportunity to visit the service including overnight stays before they are offered
a place, to ensure their needs can be met and they are compatible with other people already
receiving a service.

People’s personal plans are strengths based and outline how staff should support people to
achieve their wellbeing outcomes. They consistently contain people’s preferences, routines, and
beliefs. Personal plans start with person centred background information, which gives a clear sense
of the person. They contain clear details about what is important to the person, and how to
communicate with them. There are detailed routines and active support protocols, which explain
how to support people to manage tasks as independently as possible. Support staff can follow
these plans to ensure they are delivering person centred care and support in line with people’s
wishes. People have clearly recorded outcomes, and weekly logs record their progress to achieving
these. Personal plans and risk assessments are reviewed regularly and updated in response to any
changes.

People are referred for appropriate care and treatment at the right time, and recommendations for
care and treatment are carried out as directed by professionals. People have clear and up to date
health care plans and hospital passports, which health professionals can follow to ensure person
centred care continues to be delivered if they are admitted to hospital. Support staff ensure people
are supported to attend routine health check-ups.

People are kept safe by a strong approach to safeguarding. There is a positive culture of safety in
the service, based on openness and honesty. People have clear positive behaviour support plans
in place, giving support staff clear instructions for supporting them to maintain a positive state of
wellbeing. Support staff keep clear records of any behaviours that challenge people present with,
which can be used to identify patterns and to refer for professional support when this is needed.
Any incidents are thoroughly recorded with learning logs, which consider why the incident occurred
and what could be done differently. We saw how these records and reflection exercises have been
used to improve the support people receive and have a positive impact on their wellbeing
outcomes.

       Environment                         Good



People have access to a variety of different communal and private spaces in which to spend time
alone, socialise, or entertain visitors. People are able to personalise their rooms, and we saw they
had made them comfortable to suit their personal tastes. People can request to move rooms, and
any requests are considered whenever possible. There are several communal lounges which
people can access, choosing to socialise with others, or to sit quietly and relax. The house is split
into two sides, with one side meeting the needs of people who are more independent, and the other
meeting needs of people who require a quieter, lower stimulus environment. There are gardens
with outdoor seating which can be accessed and enjoyed in nice weather. We saw some people
had planted vegetables in the raised planters, and there is a BBQ which is used on warmer days.


Regular servicing, maintenance, and immediate repairs of facilities ensure the safety and wellbeing
of people using the service. The service provider ensures fire safety equipment is well maintained
and serviced and regular fire drills take place. People have detailed and up to date personal
emergency evacuation plans in place, meaning it is clear what support they would need in the
event of an emergency evacuation. Gas, electrical and water safety checks are undertaken
regularly, and we saw evidence of gas and electrical safety certification. The service has a food
hygiene rating of five, which is the highest that can be achieved. The building is safe and secure.
We could not enter the premises without explaining who we were, and were required to sign in.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People achieve their personal outcomes because the service provider ensures there are enough
suitably qualified and trained staff to deliver care and support. The manager ensures recruitment
checks are thoroughly completed for all new staff. We saw evidence of reference and disclosure
and barring service (DBS) checks for all staff. New staff are required to enrol on qualifications and
register with Social Care Wales (the workforce regulator) once they have completed their induction.
Staff receive regular supervisions, and their training and development needs are considered as part
of an annual appraisal. Support staff receive regular training in core and specialist areas. The
manager is qualified to deliver staff training in positive behaviour support and management. As this
is delivered in house it is personalised to meet the needs of the service and for support staff to
reflect on the specific needs of the individuals they support. The deputy manager organises the rota
and ensures there are plenty of support staff to meet people’s needs, including meeting their one-
to-one hours. Support staff told us they enjoy working for the service.


The service provider’s oversight and governance arrangements foster a positive and
compassionate culture in the service. Medication audits are completed monthly to ensure safe and
effective processes are followed for the administration of medication. Clear records are kept of any
accidents, incidents, and safeguarding concerns. People feel confident raising concerns, knowing
their input is welcomed, as there is a highly positive culture of openness and honesty throughout
the service. We saw any concerns are thoroughly investigated with appropriate and prompt action
taken. The manager holds regular staff meetings, and we saw staff are consulted for their views on
how the service is running. The RI is present in the service couple of times a week and knows staff
and people well. They provide a three-monthly record of their visits, and this considers whether
people are meeting their wellbeing outcomes, as well as inspecting the premises and reviewing a
selection of records. A six-monthly quality of care report is completed, which considers the views of
people, relatives, staff, and professionals. This is a person-centred document, focusing on the
outcomes people have achieved, and considering what is working well alongside any areas for
improvement.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
