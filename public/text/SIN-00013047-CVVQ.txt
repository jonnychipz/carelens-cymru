Summary:

 52 Narberth Road provides a welcoming, comfortable environment which meets the needs of the
 people who live there.
 Wellbeing is rated ‘good’. People are treated with dignity and respect and are supported to make
 choices about their daily lives. Independence is encouraged, and we saw people being supported
 to maintain both their physical and emotional health.
 Care and Support is rated ‘good’. The service delivers compassionate, person-centred care. Staff
 know people extremely well and demonstrate warmth, skill and attentiveness in their interactions.
 Communication is effectively supported, health needs are proactively monitored, and people told us
 they enjoy living at the home.
 Environment is rated ‘good’. The home is warm, inviting and comfortable. Routine redecoration and
 furniture renewal is planned following some internal work on doorways. There are also plans to
 make layout changes to suit the needs of people.
 Leadership and Management is rated ‘good’. The provider demonstrates effective oversight and
 promotes a positive organisational culture. Good outcomes are consistently achieved due to the
 provider’s commitment to developing and retaining a skilled and competent workforce.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are treated with dignity and respect and benefit from warm, respectful and nurturing
 relationships with staff who know them well. People are actively involved in shaping daily life.
 Monthly house meetings, supported with picture communication, enable individuals to contribute to
 decisions about menus, activities and household tasks. Staff were consistently seen encouraging
 and supporting people in daily routines. We found records do not demonstrate people are involved
 in identification of their outcomes and goals, though we are told this is the case. The provider is
 taking action to ensure this is captured. One resident told us, “I love it here,” describing staff as
 “excellent.”

 People consistently told us they are happy living at the home, with comments such as “I like living
 here.” Throughout the inspection, we observed relaxed, positive interactions between people and
 staff, characterised by humour, encouragement and meaningful conversation. Keyworkers (primary
 workers), in particular show a strong understanding of people’s histories, preferences, talents and
 communication styles.

 People generally experience a good range of day-to-day activity and stimulation. We saw
 individuals participating in preferred activities including jigsaws, music, television, dominoes,
 shopping outings and celebrating birthdays. Staff encourage participation in household tasks and
 support people to maintain routines that promote independence. Although one person reported
 they were not encouraged to help, their keyworker provided several examples demonstrating this is
 not the case. However, staffing pressures have recently impacted community participation for some
 people. Rotas, supervision records and documentation we considered confirmed periods where
 staff sickness or resident ill-health reduced opportunities for community outings. While staffing
 levels meet the minimum daytime requirement, the impact on meaningful activity and community
 access has affected well-being for some individuals.

 Communication needs are understood and respected. One person has a detailed communication
 profile supported by flash cards and clear staff guidance. Accessible tools such as picture-based
 surveys and green/amber/red feedback symbols enable people to express their views and exercise
 choice.

 People feel safe living at the home and know who they can speak to if they have concerns. Staff
 are attentive to changes in people’s presentation, such as noticing smudged glasses or reduced
 appetite, and respond promptly and compassionately. During periods of ill-health, staff maintain
 close contact and visit people in hospital to offer reassurance and continuity. These actions
 contribute positively to people’s sense of belonging, security and emotional comfort.
 There is clear evidence that staff support people effectively through difficult emotional experiences.
 We saw an example where a person feels able to talk about the loss of a close relative following
 sensitive interaction and support of staff and the wider organisation. The service also arranges
 memorial plaques and planting in memory of relatives, creating shared opportunities.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive consistently good quality, person-centred care that reflects their individual needs
and preferences. We saw an example where the provider chose not to offer someone a residency
agreement as they considered how this would impact on residents currently living in the home. This
demonstrated sound professional judgement and helps maintain the safety and stability of people
already living at the home.

Personal plans are detailed, current, and promote independence, safety and well-being. Plans
contain meaningful and personalised information, including family birthdays, preferred routines,
hobbies, communication needs and people’s own words, giving authenticity and ownership to the
content. Care staff demonstrate an excellent understanding of what people can do for themselves
and the provider is considering how this can be better captured in personal plans. The daily records
reflect care staff’s knowledge of people and demonstrate what people have done and achieved.
Risk assessments are comprehensive, and behavioural support guidelines are well understood by
staff. Incident forms are completed thoroughly and include lessons learned, evidencing a reflective
and accountable culture.

People are supported to maintain good physical well-being through access to a wide range of
healthcare services, with records and discussions with staff confirming that individuals see GPs,
dentists, neurology and other specialists. When new or emerging needs are identified, timely
referrals are made to health professionals. Staff demonstrate detailed knowledge of individuals’
health conditions and the actions required to keep them well. This includes working closely with
healthcare professionals to manage specific risks, such as concerns about blood clotting, ensuring
appropriate treatment and clear communication to the team. During the inspection, responsive
practice was evident, including a visit from the podiatrist, staff arranging an optician appointment
after noticing a person’s loose glasses, and a beneficial change following concerns about
medication timing. Equipment, such as wheelchairs and pressure cushions, is well maintained, and
individuals with specialist dietary requirements, receive safe and appropriate meals prepared and
stored in line with guidance.

People are protected from harm. Medication systems are safe and well managed, and staff support
individuals to be involved in their medication where appropriate. Staff demonstrate strong
awareness of specific medication-related needs. Infection control arrangements are robust and
support people’s safety. Staff demonstrate a clear understanding of safeguarding responsibilities,
and a recent safeguarding incident related to access to alcohol was appropriately reported,
analysed and addressed. The Responsible Individual maintains regular contact with people and
uses highly skilled communication, with consultation notes clearly showing that individuals’ views
are sought and acted upon.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment which meets their needs, supporting and encouraging wellbeing. The
home feels warm and welcoming. Communal spaces are spacious and allow people to socialise,
dine, and take part in shared activities. The kitchen is used primarily by staff, but residents are
supported to participate in cooking or baking when they wish, promoting independence and life
skills. People’s bedrooms were observed to be warm and comfortable and decorated to individual
choice. Bedrooms were equipped with adequate storage and personalised items reflecting people’s
interests and identity. This level of personalisation supports dignity, autonomy, and emotional
wellbeing. People have access to private spaces to meet with family and professionals. The home
is showing signs of wear but there is a plan in place to redecorate and replace some of the
furniture.

Bathroom arrangements require attention. One resident with mobility issues is sharing an ensuite
bathroom with another resident, compromising their dignity. Cleaning systems are in place but not
effectively overseen, leading to a decline in standards. We saw clear plans for improvement.
Outdoor areas are well maintained and include a lawned garden, patio space with seating and
raised flower beds. Residents have recently planted daffodils, demonstrating opportunities for
engagement with the environment and personal involvement in outdoor activity.

The home provides a safe physical environment that demonstrates proactive risk management
whilst keeping people safe. Measures observed include radiator covers throughout the home to
prevent burns, window restrictors fitted on first-floor windows to reduce risk of falls and fire
extinguishers located throughout the building. The provider has recently installed new fire doors
and door closers following an external fire inspection. The building is well maintained, and we saw
evidence of relevant health and safety checks and audits carried being carried out. The service
maintains detailed and accessible Health & Safety, Fire Safety, and Legionella policies and risk
assessments. These reviews demonstrate a thorough understanding of potential risks and
appropriate mitigation measures. Compliance certificates are well organised and easy to access.
We saw evidence of regular safety checks including gas safety, electrical installation, PAT testing,
fire equipment and emergency lighting. The service provides suitable equipment to meet peoples’
needs.

Measures are in place to ensure visitors to the building cannot enter without having to be let in, and
identity checked. All care records and personnel files are stored securely in a locked office.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has effective quality monitoring systems in place. The service uses audits to ensure
care and support is provided as required. Feedback plays a significant role in shaping improvement
within the service. The provider gathers views from people, families and staff in multiple ways,
using this to make changes, address concerns and refine practice. While governance documents
require some strengthening to formalise this learning, the culture of responsiveness, together with
the Responsible Individual, (RI)’s active engagement, means feedback is regularly turned into
action. This is ensuring the service continues to evolve and improve. Policies, records and safety
measures are well established and kept up to date.

The service benefits from a highly visible and committed RI who demonstrates strong values-based
leadership. People told us they know the RI well, and observations showed he engages naturally
with individuals and staff, taking time to build meaningful relationships. The RI is frequently present
in the home, participates in activities, and visits people in hospital during periods of ill health. This
promotes a culture of trust, reassurance and emotional safety. Staff consistently described the RI
as supportive, approachable and responsive.

The provider has strict selection and vetting processes which ensure staff are competent and
trustworthy. The process is robust, incorporating competency testing, literacy and numeracy
checks, and clear expectations around safe practice.

A comprehensive training matrix demonstrates strong engagement with learning and development,
including specialist training to meet residents’ needs.

There is evidence of a supportive staff culture. Supervision records show reflective discussions,
competency checks and coverage of mandatory areas, and staff reported feeling encouraged and
valued. Team meetings are detailed and incorporate training.

The workforce is generally stable, and the service does not use agency staff, supporting continuity
of care. Staffing is described as flexible, with additional staff arranged to facilitate hospital
appointments or outings. However, staffing pressures in recent months have reduced opportunities
for some people to access the community as frequently as they wish.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
