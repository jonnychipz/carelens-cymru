Summary:

 Llys Gwyn House is a residential care home located in Pontarddulais, Swansea, providing
 residential care and support for older adults, including people living with dementia and age-related
 conditions, in a comfortable environment that promotes wellbeing and independence.

 Well-being is good because people are supported to make choices, maintain important
 relationships and take part in meaningful activities that matter to them. People told us they feel
 safe, respected and well cared for, while relatives and professionals described the service as,
 responsive and welcoming. The appointment of a well-being co-ordinator has increased
 opportunities for engagement and community involvement, helping people to enjoy positive
 experiences and achieve outcomes that are important to them.

 Care and support is good because people receive person-centred care from staff who understand
 their needs and preferences. Care plans are regularly reviewed, concerns are escalated promptly

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely and with control over their lives. People are supported to make
 choices about how they spend their time. The recent appointment of a well-being co-ordinator is
 having a positive impact on people and increasing opportunities for meaningful engagement.
 During the inspection, we observed people enjoying baking and singing sessions and saw a group
 preparing to attend a local knit and natter group in the community. The co-ordinator demonstrated
 enthusiasm, creativity and a commitment to inclusion, with potential plans to develop one-to-one
 opportunities for people who prefer individual support. Residents' meetings have been
 reintroduced, giving people opportunities to share their views and contribute to decisions about life
 in the home. Staff demonstrate a strong understanding of promoting choice, with one member of
 staff stating, “We do promote choice, we make sure residents are very involved and we give them
 the atmosphere to express themselves and tell us exactly what they want.” Staff also recognised
 the importance of dignity and respect, with one telling us, “The service provides a safe, caring and
 welcoming environment where residents are treated with dignity and respect.” This was reflected in
 feedback from an external professional who commented, “There is a lovely feeling when you enter
 the building,” and a relative who told us, “She’s well looked after and is treated with respect

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. People told us they feel safe living at the
 home. One person said, “I do feel safe here definitely. The night staff come and check on you’’.
 Appropriate safeguarding, whistleblowing and complaints procedures are in place. Relatives told us
 staff listen and respond to concerns raised. One relative commented, “We’ve brought a few little
 things up and they definitely listen and they definitely react.” This indicates people and their
 representatives are confident concerns will be taken seriously and acted upon.

 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. People benefit from positive
 relationships with staff who know them well and understand their individual needs. One person told
 us, “I’m happy here, they’re all lovely.” Relatives also spoke positively about the relationships
 between staff and residents, commenting, “All the regular staff they know her well.” The home
 promotes and maintains open communication with families, with one relative telling us, “Any little
 hiccup at all we will get a phone call, they’re very good I think.” The service promotes the Welsh
 language through an active offer approach, with Welsh-speaking managers and staff available,
 bilingual signage throughout the home and information available in Welsh on request.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes.
People receive person-centred care and support that reflects their individual needs, preferences
and life experiences. Personal plans contain detailed information about people’s backgrounds,
interests, relationships and communication needs, enabling staff to provide support that promotes
wellbeing and respects their identity. Plans are reviewed regularly to ensure they remain relevant to
people’s needs. People have access to healthcare support, including a weekly GP ward round, and
staff work effectively with external professionals to promote positive outcomes. We saw evidence of
staff promptly escalating concerns relating to skin integrity, mobility and changes in people’s health
needs. Feedback about the service is positive. One external professional told us, “The home works
well as a team. Whenever I ask for information gathering or tasks that need to be followed up and
speak to carers/management team, they act upon this as soon as they are able. They are always
professional, friendly and approachable.” Relatives also commented, “We are happy with the level
of care the staff offer our relative,” “The current staff look after mum extremely well and are very
helpful,” and “They keep me informed at all times and they have the residents’ interests as their
main concerns.” The service also seeks people’s views to improve their experience, including
introducing a new menu based on feedback.

People are protected from harm and abuse through effective safeguarding arrangements and risk
management processes. Safeguarding training is up to date for all staff, supporting a
knowledgeable and confident workforce. Staff spoken with demonstrated a good understanding of
their safeguarding responsibilities and how to escalate concerns appropriately. People benefit from
personalised risk assessments that are regularly reviewed and updated to reflect changing needs.

People’s medication is safely managed. Residents receive regular medication reviews to ensure
medicines remain appropriate to their needs. Robust medication management systems are
supported by monthly audits, controlled drug checks and spot checks. Staff check existing stock
before ordering medicines, helping to reduce waste, and unused medicines are returned to the
pharmacy at the end of each cycle. The service also receives regular pharmacy audits. PRN
medicines are clearly documented and reviewed, with records showing minimal use where
prescribed. Medication training compliance is high.

People’s risk of infection is minimised. Infection prevention and control practices are embedded
within the service. Infection control training compliance is high, and infection prevention and control
is discussed regularly during team meetings. Staff were observed following appropriate infection
control procedures and sufficient personal protective equipment was available.

       Environment                         Good




People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment that
supports their well-being and keeps them safe. The home is clean and welcoming, with a range of
facilities that meet people's needs and preferences. Accommodation is provided over three floors
and includes six lounges and two dining rooms, giving people opportunities to spend time in both
quiet and social environments. One lounge includes a kitchenette, supporting people to maintain
their independence and take part in everyday tasks safely where they wish. Bedrooms viewed
during the inspection are personalised with people's belongings and reflect their individuality,
helping to create a homely atmosphere. People also have access to adapted communal bathrooms
and shower facilities that promote privacy, dignity and accessibility.

The environment supports people living with dementia using dementia-friendly signage, visual
prompts and bilingual signage throughout the home. People can move around the service
confidently and safely, with clear walkways and unobstructed fire exits observed during the
inspection. Security arrangements are in place to promote safety while maintaining people's rights
and dignity. These include secure door entry systems, visitor signing-in procedures and CCTV, with
best-interest decisions in place where required.

People benefit from access to safe outdoor spaces. A secure internal courtyard allows people to
spend time outdoors independently and includes flowers, plants, garden ornaments and a seating
area. Residents have been involved in planting flowers, supporting meaningful engagement with
the environment. Additional outdoor areas are secure and accessible, providing further
opportunities for people to enjoy fresh air and outdoor space.

The provider demonstrates a commitment to maintaining and improving the environment. We
observed refurbishment work during the inspection, including new carpets, redecoration and the
introduction of new furnishings and artwork. While some areas appear dated, there is clear
evidence of ongoing investment and a planned programme of refurbishment.

People can be confident that facilities and equipment are appropriately maintained. We reviewed
health, safety and fire records, including evidence of regular equipment servicing, an up-to-date
gas safety certificate and current five-year electrical certification. A maintenance system is in place
to identify and address repairs. The kitchen is clean, organised and has achieved a food hygiene
rating of 5 which is the highest possible score. Kitchen staff demonstrate a good understanding of
people's dietary requirements, preferences and specialist needs, including modified diets and
Speech and Language Therapy (SALT) recommendations. Information about people's nutritional
needs is readily available, helping to ensure meals are prepared and served safely in line with
professional guidance and individual requirements.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance and oversight to ensure smooth operations and high-
quality care. Comprehensive governance systems are in place to monitor the quality and safety of
the service and support continuous improvement. We saw clear evidence of responsible individual
involvement through quality-of-care reviews and quarterly governance visits and reports covering
areas such as staffing, training, activities, safeguarding, complaints, operational challenges and
general governance. Feedback is gathered regularly from people, relatives and staff through
questionnaires and is used alongside a strong programme of audits to identify areas for
improvement and monitor progress.

Audits provide oversight of key areas including personal plans, care records, body maps, skin
integrity, food and fluid intake, weights, incidents, falls, activities, maintenance, complaints,
compliments, supervision and appraisal processes. Records demonstrate findings are reviewed,
actions identified and outcomes monitored. We saw evidence that lessons are learned and shared
with staff to strengthen practice and improve outcomes for people. Notifications are submitted to
CIW appropriately, demonstrating an understanding of regulatory responsibilities and a
commitment to openness and accountability. Staff feedback generally reflects a positive and
supportive culture. One member of staff told us, “It’s a good feeling anyway, very nice here, the
manager is very approachable, can always speak to her if you need to be supported.” Another said,
“Most of the staff communicate well, support one another during busy shifts, and share a strong
team spirit.” Staff consistently describe the service as supportive and focused on providing high-
quality care.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, skills and qualifications to meet
people's care and support needs. Staff benefit from regular supervision and appraisal processes
that support professional development and reflective practice. Records show supervisions focus on
strengths, areas for development, current practice issues and agreed goals, supporting staff and
managers to work together to improve practice. Staff told us they feel well supported and are
confident concerns will be addressed appropriately. High levels of training compliance are
maintained across mandatory and specialist training, helping to ensure staff have the knowledge
and skills required to meet people's needs. We saw records of regular team meetings which reflect
discussions around important topics such as infection prevention and control, dietary requirements
and personal plan updates. People can be confident staff are recruited safely, with appropriate pre-
employment checks completed. We saw staff are registered with Social Care Wales and are
supported to achieve and maintain qualifications relevant to their roles, helping to ensure people
receive care and support from a skilled and competent workforce.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
