Ratings:

        Well-being                           Good




       Care & Support                       Good



       Environment                         Good



        Leadership & Management               Good





Summary:

 Croft House is a residential service located in Llanelli supporting up to 13 individuals who have a
 range of complex needs and behaviours. There are seven self-contained flats, four shared flats,
 and two single occupancy flats.

 The rating awarded for Well-being is ‘Good’ as people are involved in decisions about them, are
 treated with dignity and respect and are protected from harm and abuse where this is possible. The
 rating awarded for Care and Support is ‘Good’ as the person, their representative and a range of
 multi-disciplinary professionals are involved in their care. The rating awarded for the Environment is
 ‘Good’ as the service is well maintained and safe but communal areas can be improved to make
 them more homely and welcoming. The rating awarded for Leadership and Management is ‘Good’
 as there is effective oversight of the service by the RI and the dedicated management team who
 lead a motivated, well trained team of staff who respect them.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have control over their day to day lives and are involved in making decisions which affect
 them. People’s views and suggestions are gathered and acted upon. Observations and records of
 minutes of meetings and discussions clearly demonstrate people are consulted about their care
 and support, aspirations, activities and meals. People offer their opinions and suggestions during
 the RIs visits and during their interactions with care workers. People spoken with confirmed they
 had the meals they preferred and there were always choices available to them. An observation of
 one person being reassured about the specific meals and food available for him supports this.
 Individuals are actively involved in their care planning and reviews. People attend activities and
 events which are important to them including hobbies and interests.

 People’s communication needs are suitably met. People are supported to communicate in a range
 of accessible and inclusive ways. These are clearly documented in care records, understood by
 staff and observed during the inspection.

 People are treated with dignity and respect. Throughout the period of the inspection staff interacted
 with people in a friendly, kind and respectful manner. People are afforded space when needed, are
 listened to and offered choices to support and inform them.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect where this is possible. People told us they
 feel safe and are able to raise concerns; they told us “I know I can discuss any concerns”, “I feel
 safe here”  I  will speak to staff  if  I am concerned about something”. This  is also echoed by
 professionals involved with the service. The manager has an open-door policy and some people
 choose to visit the office to speak directly to her with any concerns they have.

 People live in accommodation which meets their needs, however, additional work is required to
 improve the communal areas to make it feel more homely and welcoming.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes. Care
records are produced and reviewed  in conjunction with the person, professionals and  their
representative wherever  possible. Personal plans are  detailed and provide a sense  of the
individual, their care needs and how the person chooses to have their care provided. Personal
plans and associated risk assessments are reviewed and updated regularly. Care and support is
person centred and geared around  their needs, wishes and  aspirations. Care workers are
passionate about the support they provide, and the positive outcomes individuals achieve. We
observed a care worker celebrating the achievement of the person they were supporting with the
manager and their colleagues.

There is strong evidence to demonstrate a range of health and social care professionals are
involved in people’s care and support when required. This is well documented in care records. In
addition, professionals speak highly of the service; they told us “My client has tried a care home
prior to this, and they could not meet his needs. I am exceptionally pleased how Croft House can
manage his behaviours along with his health, family relationships and my client seems very happy.
I do not think my client would be better cared for anywhere else” and “I found strong evidence of
person-centred care, with individuals actively supported in daily living, community engagement, and
emotional wellbeing”.

Care workers demonstrate a good understanding of the people they provide care and support to
and speak very positively about working in the service. One care worker told us “I never feel I don’t
want to come to work,  it really  is a great place to work, the people we support make  it so
rewarding”.

People are protected from harm and abuse. The manager and staff attend a range of safeguarding
training and are clear on their roles and responsibilities in reporting any concerns and keeping
people safe. Care workers have a good understanding of the vulnerability of the people they
support and are aware of the visual, verbal and non-verbal communication used by individuals to
indicate how they are feeling. Staff practice is supported by policies and procedures which are
accessible and regularly reviewed. The service submits safeguarding referrals where necessary,
participating in and learning from safeguarding processes and outcomes to improve the service.

       Environment                         Good



People have access to a variety of different private and communal spaces and their needs and
preferences are taken into consideration. People choose the decoration of their flats and contribute
to decisions around décor in the communal areas. People’s flats are personalised according to their
preferences and requirements. One person showed us their flat and told us “It’s a really nice flat, I
like the posters I have put up as it makes it homely”. The communal areas are a little spartan and
this has also been identified by the Responsible Individual (RI) during their visits to the service. The
manager told us that there is a plan to improve the internal environment.

Regular servicing, maintenance and repairs to facilities ensures the safety and well-being of the
people living in the service. The manager and staff confirmed that any maintenance issues are
dealt with in a timely manner. There are a range of environmental audits and checks undertaken.
There are effective security and health and safety measures in place including all visitors having to
sign in and out of the service in addition to identifying themselves. Emergency exits are free from
obstructions and clearly signed. Cleaning fluids are safely locked away when not in use and
firefighting equipment are regularly serviced.

People have access to a safe space at the rear of the building which have been decorated and
improved to make the area more welcoming for people to use. One person told us “I like sitting out
here, especially when it’s sunny”. The staff office is located in the rear garden area which people
can freely access to meet with the manager are staff.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance and oversight of the service. The RI visits the service
regularly and completes detailed quarterly reports. Quality of Care reports are produced six
monthly and CIW has received copies of these. There are a range of quality audits undertaken by
the RI, the Area Operations Manager (AOM) and the manager. The information obtained from the
audits are used to help improve the service. The AOM works closely with the manager and is
readily available for support and direction whenever required. The manager speaks highly of the
AOM and the professional and supportive relationship they have developed. The service operates
within its Statement of Purpose and notifies CIW of reportable issues in a timely manner.

The management team are respected by care workers who feel well supported by them. Care
workers we spoke with told us “I thoroughly enjoy working for M&D and in Croft. I feel really
supported by the deputy and manager. The training and support is great. I love my job” and “I Love
the job and Croft. I am extremely well supported by staff team and [AOM], I can call him any time I
need him”

People living in the service speak positively about the management of the service. They told us;
“the staff are great, they really support me”, “I really like living here, the staff are good and kind”.
Health and social care professionals told us “staff demonstrated respectful and adaptive
communication, and residents expressed satisfaction with the support received. Health and safety
standards were met, and leadership was found to be stable and proactive” and “I have a very
positive professional relationship with the manager and staff of Croft”.

People are supported by staff with the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. Care workers undergo a thorough recruitment process with all
required checks and clearances in place prior to them commencing employment in the service.
Following their recruitment, care workers receive a good induction which is complimented by an
ongoing training and development programme. Care workers speak positively about the training
and the regular one-to-one supervision and support they receive. This is corroborated by the staff
records we have read. All staff spoken with are clear about their responsibilities in reporting any
concerns they have about the safety of the people living in the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
