Summary:



 Daniel House provides an exceptionally caring and person-centred service where individuals
 receive high-quality support from a dedicated care staff team. Care staff know individuals well and
 promote dignity, respect, independence and choice. Families spoke highly of the service and were
 very positive about the care and support provided. Individuals appeared happy, settled and
 comfortable in their individual flats and enjoyed warm, positive interactions with care staff. Care
 and support provision is excellent.

 Well-being outcomes are excellent. Individuals are supported to make choices, pursue their
 interests and participate in meaningful activities tailored to their preferences and abilities. Care staff
 demonstrate a detailed understanding of individual needs and support people to achieve positive
 outcomes and maintain fulfilling lives.

 The environment is good. The home and individual flats are clean, comfortable and homely,
 providing a settled and welcoming atmosphere. While some areas would benefit from
 modernisation and freshening up, maintenance records and plans evidenced ongoing and
 proposed improvement work, demonstrating ongoing investment within the service.

 Leadership and management is good. Managers are visible within the service and demonstrate
 commitment to supporting individuals and care staff. Governance arrangements are effective. Care
 staff spoke positively about the support they receive and described feeling valued by the
 management team.

Findings:


        Well-being                                Excellent




 People living in the home experience exceptionally positive outcomes and benefit from highly
 personalised care and support that promotes their well-being, independence and quality of life. We
 observed consistently warm, respectful and meaningful interactions between care staff and people
 living at the service. Care staff demonstrated a detailed understanding of individuals' needs,
 preferences and communication styles, responding sensitively and patiently to requests for support.
 There was a clear sense of trust and affection between people and care staff, with interactions
 appearing natural, relaxed and genuine. Individuals were confident in approaching care staff and
 seeking reassurance, demonstrating the strong and positive relationships that have been
 developed.

 People are supported to have choice and control over their daily lives through carefully planned
 and individualised support arrangements. Each individual has a personalised activity planner and
 schedule, developed collaboratively to reflect their interests, goals, abilities and communication
 needs. Care staff use a range of personalised communication tools, including visual prompts and
 schedules, enabling people to understand their routines, express their preferences and fully
 participate in decision-making where possible.

 We found a strong culture of promoting independence and celebrating individual achievements.
 Although formal feedback was not sought directly from people due to capacity considerations and
 unfamiliarity, observational evidence demonstrated that people were happy, settled and
 comfortable. Two individuals enthusiastically invited us into their flats, proudly showing personal
 possessions. These interactions reflected a strong sense of ownership, confidence and pride in
 their flats and daily lives.

 The service promotes social inclusion and meaningful engagement with family and the wider
 community. Feedback from relatives was very positive. One family member described the care staff
 team as "wonderful" and praised the professionalism and responsiveness of the management
 team. They told us care staff always listen, communicate openly and promptly raise any concerns.
 They expressed confidence in the Responsible Individual (RI) and management team, describing
 them as attentive, professional and approachable. Families reported being very happy with the
 quality of care provided and stated they would readily recommend the service to others.

 Personalised weekly menus are developed with each person and displayed within their flats to
 ensure care staff are aware of dietary preferences and requirements. Where appropriate, visual
 aids are used to support people to make informed choices about meals.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive exceptionally personalised care and support that is tailored to their individual needs,
preferences and aspirations. Care plans demonstrated a strong commitment to person-centred
practice, with detailed personal plans providing care staff with comprehensive information about
people's backgrounds, relationships, communication methods, health needs, interests, goals and
desired outcomes. Plans are reviewed regularly and updated at least every three months, or
sooner where people's needs change, helping to ensure care remains responsive and effective.
The service places a strong emphasis on achieving positive outcomes for people. We saw
evidence of meaningful progress in people's lives, including improved family relationships, an
increased ability to tolerate and engage in social interactions, engaging with community
opportunities and increased independence.

People are actively involved in planning and reviewing their care and support. Personal Centred
Planning (PCP) documentation included photographs, evidence of achievements and contributions
from individuals themselves. Care staff described how people are increasingly involved in attending
and contributing to their own review meetings, helping to ensure their wishes, views and aspirations
remain central to decision-making.

Positive Behaviour Support plans were detailed and reviewed monthly by the in-house behaviour
analyst and management team. Behavioural data is collected, analysed and presented through
monitoring tools and graphs, enabling patterns, triggers and outcomes to be identified and support
strategies adjusted accordingly. The SHINE programme further enhances practice through
multidisciplinary input from behaviour analysts and speech and language professionals, ensuring
people benefit from highly coordinated and specialist support. Risk management arrangements
were robust and promoted positive risk-taking while supporting people's safety and independence.
Individual risk assessments, including those relating to community access and daily living skills,
were comprehensive, regularly reviewed and aligned with personal plans and behaviour support
strategies.

People benefit from excellent access to healthcare services. Records evidenced regular
engagement with health professionals including GPs, dentists, opticians, specialist clinicians,
district nurses and behavioural specialists. Health needs were clearly documented, monitored and
reviewed, ensuring people receive timely support to maintain and improve their well-being.
Medication Administration Records (MAR) reviewed were accurately completed, providing a clear
audit trail of medication administration. Medication was securely stored within individual medication
cabinets located in people's flats. PRN medication protocols were accessible, giving care staff
clear guidance regarding the circumstances in which medicines should be administered.

Daily records, activity schedules and personalised planners demonstrated a consistent focus on
promoting engagement, routine, choice and independence. Information was presented in
accessible formats, including visual resources where required.

       Environment                         Good




People benefit from living in a safe, comfortable and well-maintained environment that promotes
their well-being, independence and quality of life. The service is arranged into individual flats,
providing people with their own personalised living spaces whilst also enabling access to
communal and outdoor areas. We found the environment to be clean, tidy and welcoming.

The service promotes a person-centred environment that reflects the needs, preferences and
personalities of the individuals living at the service. Each flat is decorated and furnished according
to the individual's tastes, with support from family members where appropriate. Bespoke décor,
themed personal spaces and personalised furnishings help create a sense of ownership, familiarity
and comfort. Bedrooms and communal areas were furnished to a good standard and provided a
homely environment where people could relax, engage in activities and receive support in a
dignified manner. Care staff bedrooms were appropriately furnished, clean and well maintained.

The layout of the service supports independence and the development of daily living skills. People
are encouraged and supported to participate in everyday tasks such as meal planning, shopping,
household activities and maintaining their living spaces. Weekly visual food menus are completed
between people and care staff, supporting choice and involvement in decisions about meals and
shopping. We also saw activity schedules and visual prompts used throughout the service to
promote understanding and participation.

The service has effective systems in place to maintain a safe environment. Restricted areas were
appropriately secured using locks, coded key cabinets and, where required, door alarm systems
tailored to individual needs and window restrictors were fitted where necessary. Cleaning products
were stored securely within locked COSHH cupboards, and cleaning and laundry rotas were
implemented within each flat and its care staff team. We observed the environment to be clean
and well organised, supporting good infection prevention and control practices.

People have access to the outdoor spaces that encourage meaningful engagement and well-being.
Individuals are involved in growing fruit and vegetables within garden areas and caring for potted
plants. Visual labels are displayed throughout the gardening areas, promoting accessibility and
supporting communication and learning opportunities.

We reviewed servicing and maintenance records, which were up to date. Individual flats would
benefit from further modernisation and redecoration to enhance their appearance and ensure they
remain comfortable and homely. We observed maintenance staff undertaking improvement works
during the inspection, with plans in place to continue refreshing and modernising areas of the
service.

       Leadership & Management               Good




The service benefits from strong and effective leadership that promotes a positive culture and
supports good outcomes for people living at Daniel House. Care staff spoke highly of the
management team and the Registered Individual (RI), describing them as approachable, supportive
and committed to driving improvements within the service. Feedback from care staff highlighted the
positive changes that have been implemented. Care staff told us the leadership team is visible,
responsive and focused on ensuring decisions are made in the best interests of individuals.

There was a clear culture of teamwork throughout the service. Care staff described good morale
within their teams and told us they work collaboratively to meet the needs of individuals. During
discussions, care staff demonstrated a good understanding of the people they support and spoke
passionately about their roles. Comments included that everything is done for the individual and
that management creates a positive culture which drives improved outcomes. Care staff told us
they feel valued and appreciated, with recognition schemes and monthly incentives helping to
promote care staff well-being and retention.

The provider has effective systems in place to monitor care staff performance and well-being.
Records reviewed demonstrated that supervision and appraisal arrangements were well managed
and care staff confirmed they receive regular supervision and feel supported in their professional
development.

Safe recruitment practices are followed. Care staff were appropriately registered with Social Care
Wales and training records demonstrated that care staff completed mandatory and specialist
training, including Safeguarding, Medication Administration, First Aid, Infection Prevention and
Control, Behaviour Support and Fire Safety. This helps ensure care staff have the skills and
knowledge necessary to support individuals safely and effectively.

The provider has established robust governance and quality assurance arrangements. Regular
audits and quality monitoring processes enable oversight of the service and support continuous
improvement. Care staff surveys, team meetings, management meetings and suggestion systems
provide opportunities for feedback and engagement. Records were well organised, accessible and
readily available during the inspection, demonstrating good management oversight.

People, relatives and care staff were complimentary about the service. We viewed positive
feedback from family members who described the care and support provided as "above and
beyond" and expressed appreciation for the dedication of care staff. Safeguarding records
demonstrated appropriate management and oversight of notifications and referrals. Leadership
and management arrangements provide effective oversight of the service, support care staff to
perform their roles well and promote positive outcomes for people living at Daniel House.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
