Summary:
 People living at Yr Hafan Nursing Home experience good outcomes across all four inspection
 areas. The service meets regulatory expectations and delivers consistent, person-centred care.

 People are supported to live safely and healthily, with choice and control over daily life. Care
 promotes dignity, independence and positive risk-taking. People feel happy and secure, and
 families express confidence in how wellbeing and safety are promoted. Safeguarding arrangements
 are effective. Care is compassionate, responsive and tailored to individual needs. Care plans are
 detailed, regularly reviewed and reflect people’s preferences. People are supported to engage in
 meaningful activities and social interaction. Medicines are managed safely, infection prevention
 measures are effective, and families report high satisfaction with care.

 People live in a clean, safe and welcoming environment that supports comfort and wellbeing.
 Bedrooms are personalised and communal areas are well maintained. Health and safety systems
 are effective, with plans in place to improve outdoor space.

 The service is well led, with effective governance and quality assurance. Staff are well supported
 through supervision, appraisal and training, ensuring they have the skills to deliver safe,
 high-quality care.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People living at Yr Hafan Nursing Home experience positive wellbeing outcomes and are supported
 to live healthily, safely and with a good level of control over their daily lives. Care and support
 arrangements are tailored to individual needs and promote positive risk-taking, enabling people to
 maintain independence while remaining protected from avoidable harm.

 People told us they felt happy and comfortable living at the home. One person said, “I like the
 people here,” while another shared, “I can do anything I want to do.” These comments reflect that
 people feel listened to and involved in decisions that affect their lives. Families also spoke
 positively about their relatives’ wellbeing, with feedback such as, “She seems happy and
 comfortable,” and “The staff make her feel safe.”

 People are protected from abuse and neglect through effective safeguarding arrangements. Staff
 understand their responsibilities and are confident in recognising and escalating concerns. People
 consistently reported feeling safe, and families expressed reassurance about how risks are
 managed. Staff described an open culture, with one stating, “We’re supported to raise concerns
 and know they’ll be acted on.”

 The service supports people to develop and maintain positive relationships and to engage in
 activities that matter to them. People have regular contact with family and friends and are
 encouraged to take part in social and recreational activities that promote emotional wellbeing.
 Residents described enjoying shared activities, telling us, “We often have parties and singalongs,”
 and “There’s always something going on.” Relatives highlighted the impact this has on wellbeing,
 saying, “They keep people social and happy,” and “My relative’s mental wellbeing has really
 improved.” Staff echoed this person-centred approach, commenting that residents are “treated like
 family.”

 Accommodation and facilities support people’s wellbeing outcomes. The environment is
 comfortable and adapted to meet mobility and care needs, promoting privacy and dignity. People’s
 language and cultural needs are recognised, with access to bilingual information and staff able to
 support communication preferences. One resident summed up their experience by saying, “It’s
 lovely here.”
 Overall, people at Yr Hafan Nursing Home experience good wellbeing outcomes. They feel safe,
 valued and included, with strong emotional and social support. Families and staff consistently
 describe a caring, inclusive culture where people’s wellbeing is central to how care is planned and
 delivered.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive consistent, person-centred care and support that meets their assessed needs and
helps them achieve positive wellbeing outcomes. Care plans are detailed, individualised and
regularly reviewed to reflect changes in people’s health, mobility and preferences. Records
demonstrate that people and, where appropriate, their families are involved in care planning and
decision-making.

People living at the service spoke positively about the care they receive and the relationships they
have with staff. Comments included, “It’s a good laugh, I like the people here,” and “, the staff are
so kind.” People described enjoying a range of activities and social interaction, such as “bingo and
singalongs,” which support emotional wellbeing and meaningful occupation.

Families also provided positive feedback and expressed confidence in the care provided. One
relative told inspectors, “Very satisfied, brilliant – they supported me as well,” while another said,
“They treat people as if they are family.” This reflects a caring and compassionate culture within the
home.

Safeguarding arrangements are robust. People are protected from harm and abuse through clear
policies, trained staff and effective management oversight. Safeguarding concerns are
appropriately recorded, escalated and managed in partnership with external agencies where
required. Families confirmed they felt reassured about safety, with comments such as, “The staff
make my mum feel safe,” and “Any concerns raised were dealt with appropriately.”

Medicines are managed in line with relevant policies and guidance. Storage, administration and
recording systems are in place, and staff responsible for medicines have received appropriate
training. Management oversight includes regular audits and review of practice. Minor issues
relating to documentation consistency were identified during inspection; these were recognised by
the service and corrective actions had been implemented to strengthen governance.

Infection prevention and control procedures are effective. The service follows public health
guidance, with appropriate use of PPE, good hand hygiene practices and consistently clean
environments. These measures help protect people’s health and reduce the risk of infection.

Overall, care and support at Yr Hafan Nursing Home are delivered to a good standard. People feel
cared for, safe and valued, and families express confidence in the service. Where areas for
improvement are identified, leaders respond appropriately to maintain and strengthen outcomes for
people.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is clean, safe and supportive of their wellbeing outcomes. The
premises are well maintained overall and provide suitable accommodation, facilities and equipment
to meet people’s assessed needs. Communal areas, including the lounge and dining room, were
observed to be clean, tidy and welcoming, contributing to a calm and homely atmosphere.

Bedrooms were found to be appropriately furnished, clean and personalised, supporting people’s
dignity, privacy and comfort. Equipment required to meet people’s care needs, including
pressure-relieving mattresses, call bells and moving and handling equipment, was available, in
good working order and aligned with care plans and risk assessments. People spoke positively
about the environment and how it made them feel: “The staff are so kind.” And “I like the staff and
the food is lovely.”

The kitchen has been refurbished and holds a five-star food hygiene rating, supporting safe and
effective meal provision. Laundry facilities, storage areas and arrangements for handling harmful
substances were appropriately managed, secure and organised.

Outdoor space is available for people to use; however, several people identified that this area could
be further developed to enhance wellbeing and quality of life. Feedback included:

       “I’d like the garden to be a bit more interesting.”

        “It would be nice if there was more space outside to sit and relax.”

        “If the garden was improved, I could get out to get some fresh air.”
     The provider has recognised this feedback and has plans in place to improve the outdoor
      environment.

Health and safety arrangements are robust. Risks to people’s safety are routinely identified,
assessed and managed. Fire safety systems, including alarms, emergency lighting and evacuation
arrangements, were in place and appropriately maintained. Essential equipment servicing and
maintenance records were available, and there was evidence of effective infection prevention and
control practices throughout the home.
Where minor environmental issues were identified, these were either addressed promptly or
accompanied by clear plans for improvement. Overall, people live in a safe, comfortable and
well-maintained environment, with continued development planned to further enhance their
experience, particularly in relation to outdoor space.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leaders and managers at Yr Hafan Nursing Home provide effective governance and oversight,
supporting people to achieve positive outcomes. There are clear organisational arrangements in
place, with a visible Registered Manager and an active Responsible Individual (RI) who work in
partnership to ensure the service is well managed and compliant with regulatory requirements.

Governance systems are embedded and operate effectively. Regular manager oversight, team
meetings, safeguarding reviews and provider monitoring ensure risks are identified, escalated and
addressed appropriately. There is evidence of learning from incidents, complaints and external
feedback, which is used to improve practice. Staff understand their roles and lines of accountability
and report confidence in leadership arrangements.

Quality assurance processes are comprehensive and include RI statutory visits, Quality of Care
Reviews and a programme of audits covering care planning, medication management, infection
prevention and the environment. These systems provide assurance about the quality and safety of
care and are supported by clear action plans where improvement is required. The Quality of Care
review confirms outcomes are positive and that development areas are proportionately identified
and monitored.

The service demonstrates financial stability and appropriate resourcing. There is no evidence of
financial risk impacting on care delivery. Ongoing investment is evident in staffing, training and
environmental improvements, with further planned developments to enhance people’s quality of
life.

Robust recruitment processes ensure staff are suitable and safely employed. Supervision and
appraisal systems are in place and consistently used to support performance, learning and
wellbeing. Training compliance is very strong, indicating effective oversight and forward planning.
Staff describe feeling valued, supported and motivated, and there is evidence of long staff tenure,
supporting continuity of care and positive relationships for people living at the home. Staff
consistently describe leadership as approachable and supportive, stating that managers are
responsive to concerns and open to feedback. They report a positive workplace culture, strong
teamwork and a shared commitment to person-centred care.

Leadership and management arrangements at Yr Hafan Nursing Home are effective and well
established, supporting a stable service that meets regulatory expectations and delivers good
outcomes for people.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
