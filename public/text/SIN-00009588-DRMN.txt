Summary
Bonymaen House is a homely and welcoming service that provides care to adults who
require a period of care and support to achieve their reablement needs. The service actively
promotes independence and encourages people and their families to be involved in their
care. There are up to date personal plans in place which are written in partnership with
people and reflect the person’s support needs well. The service employs a skilled staff team
who are appropriately trained to meet people’s needs. The Responsible Individual (RI) and
management team have a strong presence in the service and support staff through formal
and informal discussion. Staff feel confident in their roles. People can do the things that
matter to them. We saw staff contributing to the wellbeing of people through warm and
sensitive interactions.


There are plans in place to maintain the service and ensure there is ongoing refurbishment
to the property. The grounds are well maintained and provide a pleasant and peaceful area
for people to spend time and relax or exercise. There is good governance by the RI which
drives improvements and ensures people’s needs are met.

Well-being
People have a voice and are treated with dignity and respect. People told us they are fully
involved in their care, are supported to write their own personal plans and goals and are
involved in reviews of these. People’s individual circumstances are considered, and the
service actively seeks to accommodate these. ‘My Voice’ meetings take place monthly, and
these offer a forum for people to discuss the service and raise any concerns. Minutes of
these are available in different formats.

People’s physical, mental health, and emotional wellbeing is consistently promoted. The
service works alongside the nursing team who are led by an experienced clinical lead.
There is a qualified nurse on duty throughout the day to attend to any nursing needs. The
service actively works in partnership with other healthcare professionals such as GP’s,
occupational therapists, and physiotherapists. We saw records of people being supported
to attend healthcare appointments. Specialist equipment is available in the service to
promote people’s independence, reablement and overall wellbeing. We saw an Inclusive
technology station at the service which had a range of equipment and adaptations people
could “try before you buy”.

People live in a homely and safe environment that promotes their well-being. People told us
that they can rest and feel safe and comfortable in their surroundings. They told us “Staff
are fantastic. Food is terrific. My family can visit every day” and “staff are marvellous and
are helping me become more able”. The management team told us people’s needs are
assessed prior to moving into the service to ensure their needs can be effectively met. We
saw records of these assessments in people’s plans. When leaving Bonymaen house
people are referred to community-based services. This includes the Community
Reablement and Wellbeing team/ 3rd sector and care and support agencies. People can
access a safe and pleasant outdoor area unrestricted and can access the community as
they please.

People are protected from harm and neglect. Staff receive safeguarding training and those
spoken with have good knowledge of their responsibilities and how to report concerns they
may have about people they support. There is a safeguarding policy in place which is
reviewed as required. People are provided with good information about the service along
with a complaints procedure should they need it.

Care and Support
People are supported exceptionally well and in the way they prefer with personal plans and
risk assessments that fully reflect their needs. Personal plans are co-produced with the
person and their representatives. We saw people and their representatives are consulted
with about how they would like to receive care and support. Individual needs and
preferences are documented clearly in personal plans. Personal plans are written to a very
high standard and clearly inform staff how to provide care and how to best support people
to achieve their personal outcomes. People and their representatives told us they are fully
involved in the review of their personal plans. Daily care documentation is very thorough
and informative.

People receive excellent care and support and with few exceptions are heavily involved in
the planning of their care. People receive a service designed in consultation with the person
and considers their wishes and aspirations. People’s goals regarding their care and re-
ablement are discussed and recorded when first receiving the service, and these are
reviewed daily. We saw very warm and sensitive interactions between care staff and
people. People appeared relaxed in their surroundings whilst being supported to work
towards their re-ablement goals. People told us “staff are great, I’m always treated with
dignity and my decisions are respected” and “I’m fully involved in my care and I am well
looked after”. A professional told us “The team are very proactive, encouraging people to be
independent and work on their goals”.

People are empowered to make choices about their health and are supported to access
healthcare and other services to maintain their ongoing development and well-being. Staff
at Bonymaen House work in collaboration with other healthcare professionals, such as
Physiotherapists, Occupational Therapists and Discharge Liaison Nurses to provide a
holistic care package to people using the service. This supports people to achieve their
reablement goals. Records of these services are recorded in people’s personal plans and
progress logs maintained. A GP visits the service daily and reviews people’s health needs.
There are facilities within the service that can effectively aid people to achieve their desired
reablement outcomes. For example, there is an area where people can make drinks or
snacks for their visitors, and a breakfast station. There is also a gym at the service which
people can attend with a physiotherapist to help make progress with their mobility goals.
The service also offers innovations such as an “inclusive technology station” which features
aids and adaptations that people can try out if they feel they may benefit them.

The service actively promotes the Welsh language. There are bilingual signs throughout the
service and Welsh language classes are offered to people who would like to learn or
develop their existing language skills. We saw a “Welsh word of the day” displayed in
communal areas.

 Environment

--- PAGE 6 ---
Care and support is provided in a location and an environment that promotes and enables
people to achieve their personal outcomes. We found all areas of the service to be nicely
decorated, clean and clutter free. There are communal spaces which are bright and
welcoming where people can relax and socialise. We saw a number of bedrooms which
overall are nicely decorated, and people can bring their personal belongings to the service if
they wish. There is a well-maintained, pleasant, secure and accessible outdoor area which
people can access unrestricted. There are areas of the service people can use to achieve
their re-ablement goals. This includes a dedicated area with a kitchen people use to make
meals and drinks for themselves or their visitors. Adaptations and equipment are available
where needed. We saw manual handling equipment is available and regularly maintained
and serviced. There are ongoing plans for the continued maintenance of the service along
with future refurbishment plans.

The environment is safe, secure and there are robust processes in place to ensure health
and safety checks are completed and documented. The service has a secure entry system
in place and a visitors’ book. This is to ensure the safety of people is maintained and to
comply with fire regulations. We saw mandatory fire safety checks take place routinely and
certificates for gas, fire detectors, fire extinguishers, electricity and electrical equipment are
all up to date. A fire drill had taken place recently and all staff receive fire warden training.
Water temperature checks are taken routinely and documented. Personal emergency
evacuation plans (PEEP’s) are in place for people and they each receive a copy of this.
Laundry facilities are kept in a separate locked room and away from food preparation areas.
The home has a current food hygiene rating of 5 (very good). We saw appropriate storage
and control of substances hazardous to health (COSHH). These were kept in a designated
locked area and risk assessed. We saw staff wearing appropriate personal protective
equipment (PPE) and they told us there were sufficient supplies of these. Cleaning
schedules are in place and completed by a dedicated team of staff.





Leadership and Management

--- PAGE 7 ---
The provider has consistent and strong governance arrangements in place to ensure the
smooth operation of the service. There are systems and processes in place to monitor,
review and improve the quality of care and support. The RI visits the service regularly and
speaks to people, their representatives, staff and other healthcare professionals to gather
feedback about the quality of care and support. This is used to monitor existing support
arrangements and identify any improvements needed to the service. The management
team complete regular audits of the service which ensures high quality care is provided and
any shortfalls identified and swiftly addressed. We saw feedback questionnaires on display
in communal areas, and people, visitors and staff are encouraged to complete these.

People receive care and support from an experienced and highly competent staff team who
have appropriate knowledge and skills. We saw a selection of staff files and saw
recruitment processes are safe and robust, with necessary pre-employment checks
completed before employment starts. All staff receive an induction which is in line with the
All Wales Induction Framework set by Social Care Wales (SCW). Staff receive mandatory
and service specific training relevant to their role, with ongoing refresher courses delivered
as required. Staff told us they were able to ask for additional training if they felt it was
needed. All staff are supported to achieve nationally recognised qualifications such as
Qualifications and Credit Framework (QCF).

The service has a very committed staff team who feel supported in their roles. Staff receive
regular supervision and appraisal, and team meetings are frequently held. Minutes from
team meetings show that conversations are two way and there is an open and honest
culture within the team. Staff wellbeing is promoted within the service, and we saw
wellbeing discussions taking place within supervision sessions. Additionally, there is a staff
wellbeing board on display along with an “open-door” policy from the management team.
Staff spoken with told us “everything about this service is fantastic. I’m unbelievably proud
to work here” and “best management I’ve worked with, any queries, anything I want to try,
any training I need, they’re fully supportive”.

People and their representatives have access to very good information about the service.
People receive a welcome pack which is available in easy to read format and Welsh. A
written guide is available to people which provides information about the service, including
how to make a complaint if needed.
