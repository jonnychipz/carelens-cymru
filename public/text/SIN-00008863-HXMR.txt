Summary:

 Wellbeing is rated good because people felt safe, respected and supported to make choices about
 their lives. Residents, relatives and professionals provided consistently positive feedback about
 care, relationships and wellbeing. People maintained family and community connections and
 experienced kind, person-centred support.

 Care and support are rated good because people received safe, person-centred care that met their
 assessed needs. Care records evidenced effective risk management, healthcare involvement,
 medication management and monitoring of nutrition, hydration and skin integrity. Positive
 interactions were observed throughout the inspection. Improvements were required to strengthen
 outcome-focused care planning and meaningful activity provision.

 Environment requires improvement because inconsistent health and safety monitoring, gaps in fire
 safety checks and an unresolved patio trip hazard reduced assurance that environmental risks
 were being effectively managed. However, people lived in a clean, comfortable and well-maintained
 environment. Bedrooms, communal areas and facilities supported wellbeing outcomes.

 Leadership and management require improvement because governance systems were not

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive wellbeing outcomes and tell us they felt safe, supported and treated
 with dignity and respect. Residents described the home as “nice and homely,” stated staff were
 helpful and reported they felt safe living at the service. Relatives consistently expressed high levels
 of satisfaction and described staff as caring, approachable and responsive to people's needs.

 People are supported to maintain control over their daily lives and make choices about routines,
 care and activities. Staff questionnaires indicated people choose when they get up and go to bed,
 and relatives confirmed the service promotes independence and respects personal preferences.

 People are kept safe and protected from abuse through established safeguarding arrangements.
 Staff had access to safeguarding policies, training and whistleblowing processes and demonstrated
 an understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities. Records evidenced safeguarding referrals
 were monitored and appropriate action was taken when concerns arose.

 Positive relationships are promoted. Feedback from relatives and professionals highlighted warm,
 respectful interactions between staff and people using the service. People were supported to
 maintain family relationships and had access to social opportunities, activities, visiting entertainers
 and community involvement. Residents' meetings were held to gather views and inform service
 development.

 We observed a calm and relaxed atmosphere throughout the inspection. People appeared
 comfortable in the company of staff and interactions were caring, respectful and person-centred.
 Mealtime observations demonstrated good engagement, sensitive support and positive
 communication between staff and residents.

 Some areas for improvement were identified. Opportunities for meaningful activity and stimulation
 could be further strengthened and some relatives commented that parts of the environment
 appeared dated. The service is currently working towards providing the Welsh language active
 offer, there was evidence of some bilingual signage, Welsh language resources and Welsh-
 speaking staff.

 Overall, evidence demonstrated that people are safe, treated with kindness and respect, supported
 to maintain relationships and enabled to make choices about their lives. Feedback from people,
 relatives and professionals was overwhelmingly positive and provided assurance that wellbeing
 outcomes are being achieved.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that meets their assessed needs and promotes positive
outcomes. Residents told us they felt safe, were supported by kind staff and received care in the
way they preferred. Relatives were highly satisfied with the service, describing staff as caring,
family-orientated and responsive to changes in people's needs. Visiting professionals confirmed
staff knew people well and delivered person-centred care.

Care records evidenced comprehensive assessments, risk management plans, regular reviews and
effective monitoring of health needs. People at risk of skin damage, dehydration, weight loss or falls
were appropriately assessed and monitored. Records demonstrated timely referrals to healthcare
professionals and ongoing clinical oversight. Medication management arrangements were safe,
with secure storage, appropriate auditing and accurate administration records.

We observed positive and respectful interactions between staff and people using the service.
During mealtimes, staff provided sensitive support, promoted dignity and encouraged
independence. People appeared relaxed and comfortable and received support in a timely manner.
Nutritional and hydration needs were monitored effectively and specialist advice was sought when
required.

People had access to activities and opportunities for social engagement through resident meetings,
group activities and community involvement. However, the service did not employ a dedicated
activities coordinator and activity provision relied on care staff. The provider had recognised this
and planned to recruit an activities coordinator to strengthen meaningful occupation opportunities.

Some improvements were required. Care plans did not consistently identify clear personal
outcomes, reducing assurance that care planning was fully outcome focused. Staff training relating
to outcome-based care planning and meaningful engagement would further strengthen practice.
Despite this, the evidence demonstrated that people received safe, compassionate and responsive
care which met their health, wellbeing and support needs.

       Environment               Requires Improvement



People live in a clean, comfortable and well-maintained environment that supports their wellbeing.
Residents told us they felt safe living at the home, and relatives consistently described the
environment as clean, comfortable and supportive of wellbeing. People benefit from accessible
communal areas, personalised bedrooms, suitable equipment and pleasant outdoor spaces
including seating, planting and garden features.

The home was observed to be clean and orderly, with good infection prevention and control
practices, secure medication storage, appropriate COSHH arrangements, safe laundry facilities
and sufficient equipment to meet people's needs. Bedrooms reviewed were personalised, clean
and free from hazards, with equipment matching assessed needs. Environmental health standards
were positive, with a five-star kitchen rating and appropriate clinical waste disposal arrangements.

There was evidence of ongoing investment in the premises, including replacement carpets,
improvements to shower room panels and planned future refurbishment works. Relatives described
the home as welcoming and homely, although some commented that parts of the environment
appeared dated and would benefit from modernisation and additional space.

Despite these strengths, improvement is required regarding the provider's management of health
and safety risks. Records showed inconsistent monitoring of smoke detector testing, hot water
checks and legionella controls. Monthly emergency lighting checks, escape route checks and
routine fire extinguisher inspections were either absent or not consistently recorded. The fire
logbook required improvement and evidence of ongoing compliance could only be partially
demonstrated. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the provider to make improvements
before the next inspection.

A significant concern related to the front patio area. Uneven paving and trip hazards had been
identified previously and remained unresolved at the time of inspection. The provider had not
addressed this known environmental risk despite previous feedback. While the outdoor area was
attractive and supported wellbeing outcomes, the unsafe patio created an avoidable risk of falls and
injury for people using the service.

Overall, people benefit from a comfortable, clean and suitable environment that promotes
wellbeing. However, improvements are required to strengthen health and safety monitoring
arrangements and ensure identified environmental risks are addressed promptly to maintain a safe
environment for all.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



People are supported by an established management team that is visible and approachable. Most
staff reported that managers listen to concerns, provide support on the floor and are available when
needed. Staff described leaders as approachable and responsive, and relatives consistently
praised communication and management responsiveness. Outcomes for people were positive, with
relatives reporting satisfaction with care and feeling informed about changes to their family
members’ wellbeing.

The provider has governance arrangements in place including RI statutory visits, Quality of Care
Reviews, staff meetings, audits and oversight by the Responsible Individual. Evidence
demonstrated ongoing review of service quality, investment in environmental improvements and
monitoring of care practices.

There was evidence of a positive culture and willingness to improve. Team meeting records
demonstrated that management identified concerns regarding communication, documentation
quality, activities provision, equipment monitoring and training, and had implemented actions to
address these issues. Staff questionnaires indicated staff felt valued, supported and able to access
training opportunities.

However, quality assurance arrangements were not consistently effective. The provider was unable
to provide the requested audit schedule and audit reports during inspection. Health and safety
monitoring records were incomplete, and identified environmental risks, including the uneven patio
area, had not been fully addressed despite being raised previously. Evidence also identified
recruitment file deficiencies and gaps in supervision and appraisal arrangements for some staff
members. These issues indicate that management oversight had not consistently identified or
resolved compliance concerns in a timely manner.

Improvement is required with ensuring full and satisfactory recruitment information is available for
all staff. Sampled staff files contained missing references, incomplete employment histories and
absent recruitment documentation. Consequently, the provider could not demonstrate compliance
with legislative requirements or assure that all staff had been appropriately vetted prior to
employment. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the provider to make improvements
before the next inspection.

Improvement is also required with consistent supervision, appraisal and development opportunities.
Sampled staff files contained missing appraisals and supervisions, staff reported infrequent
supervision, and training gaps were identified. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the
provider to make improvements before the next inspection.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
