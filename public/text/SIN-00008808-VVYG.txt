Summary:


 Since the last inspection the service has implemented several improvements, these consider well-
 being, care and support, the environment and leadership and management. The service has
 listened to feedback and focused on people’s well-being and experience. They have implemented
 new activities and places for people to socialise. A new activities staff member provides regular
 opportunity for people to access the community. Goals and aspirations are now more person-
 centred, the service continue to work on this and drive improvement. One relative said, “Could not
 be more grateful for the service they provide, and the way in which it is delivered.” Well-being is
 ‘Good’.

 Care and Support is ‘Good’. Plans are detailed and informative. Risk assessments are in place
 which identify clear ways to reduce risks. Medication is appropriately administered in a safe way.
 Infection control measures are in place, and the service are developing improved systems to

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People benefit from a provider who listens and acts. We found that regular resident meetings mean
 people can share their views and ideas, following a suggestion from residents a pub has been set
 up in an unused lounge area. The area has a traditional pub feel, with tables and chairs, drinks and
 snacks in addition to a juke box machine and dart board. One member of staff told us “Residents
 appear happier and have more activities to do since the pub being put in.” There is an activities
 planner in place which includes art, bingo, pub games, music and movement, one to one activities,
 Holy communion, baking class and karaoke. One lounge has been turned into an ‘Activities room’
 there are board games and activities available and accessible. Relatives praised the service and
 care staff saying, “Carers provide excellent one to one with X,” and “X is very well cared for and
 treated more like a member of the family,” and “They send me videos of her dancing.”


 People are treated with dignity and respect by care staff who know them well, one relative said,
 “The staff know my mother and me, and we know them.” We saw choices are offered and staff
 approach people with kindness and understanding. People appear to hold good relationships with
 care staff, and several have worked at the service for many years providing consistency and
 continuity. One relative told us, “The very low turnover of staff is wonderful.” Relationships are
 promoted through activities and events, bringing people together. We saw relatives visiting on the
 day of inspection. Relatives describe their loved ones as ‘happy’ and settled’. The service has
 appointed a dedicated staff member for activities ensuring people have the opportunity to regularly
 access the community and attend events. One relative told us, “I know she’s in the best place
 possible”.


 People told us they like their rooms and some are very personalised. We saw efforts to improve
 people’s sense of belonging by offering choices in paint colours so rooms can be individually
 decorated and personalised. There are a range of areas people can relax in with others or use their
 bedrooms to have privacy. There are plans in place to ensure the environment considers dignity
 and infection control, such as new bathrooms being fitted. The service continues to drive
 improvement at the service considering people’s well-being outcomes. There is evidence to show
 the service is committed to supporting people to develop individual goals and aspirations alongside
 their required health outcomes. There is some Welsh signage in the home, and the manager told
 us the Statement of Purpose is available in Welsh. The Statement of Purpose has been reviewed
 and updated. We discussed with the provider ways to enhance the Welsh Active offer.

       Care & Support                       Good





People and their relatives praised the service. Feedback viewed continuously describes care and
support as ‘Excellent’. Relatives told us, “X is very settled and happy,” “It’s very good care and
personalised,” “I cannot fault the care my mum receives”, and “We receive a phone call whenever
there is an issue with health, medication or other issues.” Personal plans are much improved; there
is an electronic system in place to help record information. One staff member said, “The residents
are a priority to everyone.” Some documents we viewed such as a ‘Life history’ is extremely
detailed and person centred; this helps care staff understand the person and their interests. We
discussed with the service utilising this information to aid in supporting people identify achievable
and person-centred outcomes relating to hobbies, activities and interests. Documents are regularly
reviewed to ensure they are up to date.

Daily records are good and evidence care and support is mostly carried out in line with the person’s
plan. One relative told us, “They do take really good care, X is always clean and tidy, smartly
dressed.” Care staff record accidents and incidents, and this information is reviewed by
management, who identify any patterns and addressing potential improvements. We discussed
with the manager to ensure they are clearly recording their oversight of records to show actions
taken. Relatives told us in the event of an incident, communication is excellent and managers act
“Swiftly, efficiently and with care.” Risk assessments are much improved with specific details on
measures on how to reduce potential risks.

Medication is well managed. There are organised systems in place which aid staff administering
medication. Documents are clear and include important information about the person including their
picture, date of birth and any allergies. Care staff receive the required competency checks, to
ensure their skills and knowledge remain up to date. The service has sought additional training for
senior staff to enable timely assessments to be carried out. We saw health professionals are
involved in the service, and managers told us they are quick to seek support if they need to. The
GP visits weekly and we saw the involvement of Dieticians, Speech and Language Therapy and
Dentists. Commissioners gave positive feedback regarding the home’s improvement.

Infection control has been considered and action taken following our previous inspection. The
service has recruited more domestic staff to aid in cleaning. The service has been inspected by the
Food Standard agency and received the score of 5, meaning the service has ‘very good’ hygiene
standards. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is available at stations throughout the home,
providing easy access for care staff. We saw care staff using PPE appropriately. We noted daily
checks and audits on the environment are not always accurate. We discussed with the manager
delegating responsibilities to enable layers of oversight of infection control.

       Environment               Requires Improvement



The home is welcoming and appears clean and is inviting. One relative told us, “Every member of
staff greets me as a friend, make me feel so welcome.” The home is close to local amenities, and
we saw some people enjoy going out to local shops and parks independently or with support.
Several rooms have ensuite facilities which are clean and some rooms are very personalised.
There are several places for people to sit and relax around the home, including lounges and dining
areas. There is a small outdoor space available at the rear of the property. The service has made
several improvements to the service since our last inspection. Areas of significant wear and tear
have been addressed; there is ongoing painting and decorating being carried out. One staff
member told us, “The colour and decor on the walls brightens the place up and improves moods.”
There is a new staff room available ensuring staff have a place to safely store their belongings and
can enjoy their break. The maintenance person attends the home weekly, ensuring jobs can be
completed in a timely manner. A planned maintenance schedule helps the service identify works in
advance; we discussed ways to improve this to ensure priorities can be identified and actioned
swiftly.


The home is comprised of several buildings, with separate staircases and corridors, leading to
different communal areas of the home and people’s bedrooms. The provider is investing in the
service to drive improvement and ensure it is safe. There are new bathrooms ordered, ongoing
decorating, new dryers and kitchen work surfaces purchased. In addition, the fire panel system is
being updated. Health and safety systems are in place. Fire wardens have received further training
to ensure they know their responsibilities. We found personal emergency evacuation plans have
been improved and include more detail. We discussed with the provider considering areas of
potential risk identified, such as steps at the back of fire exits and uneven ground in the garden.
Fire drills are carried out and managers told us how they use role play to imagine real life
emergencies ensuring their equipment and plan is effective. There is firefighting equipment
throughout the building which is checked by external agencies.

The home receives regular health and safety checks and servicing on equipment to ensure it is
safe to use. Oversight has improved and systems in place to aid governance. However, the service
needs to ensure that thorough oversight is maintained of the environment. We found some audits
completed not to be accurate and areas of potential risk which had not been identified by the
service. The fire risk assessment in place identifies several areas requiring action. Whilst we note
the RI is taking action; we discussed proactive ways they could identify issues themselves. Whilst
the home is clean, we found some areas to be missed and require a deep clean. This remains an
area for improvement, and we expect the provider to make the improvements.

       Leadership & Management               Good





We saw several improvements have been made across the service. Managers have listened to
feedback and taken action. The Deputy manager told us, “It has been positive, we’ve all pulled
together,” and the manager told us they are working hard to drive improvement. The service
continues to work on embedding systems and oversight processes to ensure these identify
improvements. The training matrix in place provides a clear overview for managers to identify any
gaps or improvements. We saw that areas of improvement in relation to training have been
addressed. Care staff receive their supervisions and staff meetings. Most staff told us they feel
supported by managers, saying “They make sure the staff are being taken care,” “Always make
time for us,” and “We have meetings to make sure everyone is okay.” Staff told us that the manager
and RI are visible at the service, listening to feedback and investing in the service, they have both
worked at the service for several decades. One staff member said, “The leadership, from the
managers to the owners, genuinely care about the people here.” The RI completes their regularity
duties, gaining feedback from people and staff as well identifying improvements required. We
discussed with the management team ways to improve their auditing systems to make these more
thorough, robust and to ensure quality over quantity.


People and their relatives describe care staff as “Amazing,” “Marvellous,” and “Wonderful.” Most
care staff enjoy their roles and said, “I love working for Ty Melin,” “I can honestly say this is one of
the best companies I have ever worked for” and “I wouldn’t go to another company.” Staff are
appropriately registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator and complete a Disclosure
and Barring service check. Staff members are supported to progress and develop, completing new
qualifications. The manager is booked onto training to develop knowledge, and we discussed
resources available to promote continuous development and learning. One relative said, “I cannot
speak highly enough of the staff.” Rotas show that staffing levels are reflective of the services
statement of purpose and that managers cover when required to ensure staffing levels are
maintained. Some staff told us they sometimes feel ‘short staffed’, especially during times of annual
leave and sickness. We discussed with the provider considering the deployment of staff during
busy times and the need to ensure staffing levels are reviewed and remain sufficient.


The service has considered the leadership structure and employed an additional administrative
team member, as well as developed key worker roles. This aids in managing the day-to-day
running of the home. The service is in the process of embedding layers of oversight to help ensure
governance systems are accurate, effective and robust.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
