Summary:

 Hillbury House Care Home is situated in a quiet residential area of Wrexham and surrounded by
 large, well kept gardens. The environment is clean, well maintained, and people can personalise
 their rooms. The equipment and facilities help keep people safe, comfortable and support their well-
 being.

 People are treated with dignity, respect and are supported to make choices about their daily lives.
 Care and support are delivered in line with personal plans, and people benefit from a stable,
 familiar staff team.

 The manager ensures enough care staff are always on duty so people receive timely care and
 attention. Staff receive supervision and training to maintain the skills needed to meet people’s
 needs. The Responsible Individual (RI) is regularly present in the home, speaks with staff, people
 and relatives, and oversees governance and quality assurance which support safe service delivery.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are treated with dignity and respect and experience positive well-being in the home. During
 our visit, we saw people relaxing and socialising in communal areas with their visitors and each
 other. Care staff respond promptly and sensitively to people’s needs, including using their preferred
 names, maintaining eye contact, and recognising non-verbal signs of discomfort or a need for
 support with personal care. We saw interactions between people and care staff were calm,
 respectful and reassuring. People describe staff as kind and supportive, and relatives also gave
 positive feedback about how staff support people’s well-being. We saw staff care for people’s
 personal belongings and clothing respectfully. Bedrooms contain personalised soft furnishings,
 photographs, pictures and ornaments, all of which are kept clean and treated with respect while
 staff work in people’s rooms.

 People are supported to make choices in their daily lives, including their routines, meals and how
 they spend their time. We saw people choosing meals and being offered alternatives if they
 changed their minds. Care staff respected people’s decisions while encouraging choice in a
 supportive way.

 A wide range of group and individual activities is available to help people take part in things they
 enjoy and find meaningful. Activity records show regular art sessions, music and singing,
 reminiscence, games, gentle exercise and one-to-one engagement. Records also explain why
 people sometimes chose not to take part, for example if they preferred to stay in their rooms, spend
 time with visitors or rest when feeling unwell. This shows respect for individual preferences and
 choices. The manager also ensures people maintain links with the local community through
 religious services in the home and visits from local schools and children’s groups, who read with
 residents and join in activities and gardening.

 People’s communication needs are recognised and responded to appropriately. We saw staff using
 clear language, an appropriate tone and reassuring gestures where needed. People’s Welsh
 language preferences are recorded in their plans and supported by staff where possible. Children
 from a local Welsh-speaking school visit the home to chat with residents in Welsh and take part in
 intergenerational activities. These interactions support people’s emotional well-being and sense of
 belonging.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive personalised care and support that responds to their needs. Pre-admission
assessments are completed by the manager or experienced senior care staff and draw on
information from professionals, the person themselves, and their relatives. Initial care plans reflect
people’s diagnoses, health risks, routines, preferences and support needs. They highlight what
people can still do for themselves, give clear guidance for staff, and are reviewed regularly and
updated as required to reflect changes in people’s needs and preferences.

Care staff know the people they support well and provide individualised care that meets their
needs. During our visit, care staff described people’s individual needs, preferences and health risks
clearly and explained the individualised support they provide. Records in the home and our
observations show care is delivered in line with personal plans, including personal care, nutrition,
hydration, mobility and emotional support.

People are supported to maintain good health and well-being through timely involvement from
healthcare professionals. Records show referrals are made promptly for support from GPs, speech
and language therapists, physiotherapists and other professionals. Their recommendations are
recorded in personal plans and followed by staff, helping to promote continuity of care and positive
health outcomes for people.

People are kept safe from harm and abuse, and concerns about safety from people, staff or
relatives are listened to. One relative we spoke to about their experiences of the home told us their
loved one is “safe here, and that makes it easier for me.” Records show concerns are recognised,
recorded and escalated appropriately, with timely referrals and appropriate action following
incidents. Feedback from commissioners confirms improvements in the quality and timeliness of
safeguarding referrals and information sharing.

Medication is managed safely in the service. We saw care staff follow appropriate procedures
during medication rounds, and medication administration records are completed in full and reflect
people’s plans and prescriptions. Care staff who administer medication receive training and regular
competency checks to ensure their practice remains safe. The manager is working with
commissioners and the local health board to strengthen joint working and support safe medication
management in the home.

Good hygiene practices are in place across the service. Staff receive relevant training, follow clear
policies and cleaning schedules, and we saw sufficient domestic staff on duty during our visit.
People’s rooms, communal areas and facilities are clean, tidy, and staff have access to appropriate
personal protective equipment. The home was awarded a level 5 food hygiene rating at its last
environmental health inspection.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that supports their safety, comfort and well-being. The home is clean,
warm, well maintained and decorated in a homely and welcoming style. Corridors are kept clear of
clutter to ensure people can move about the home safely. Communal areas are bright, spacious
and airy, with plenty of comfortable seating and dining space for people to socialise and eat
together if they choose. The communal lounges on the ground floor provide kitchenette facilities
where families and visitors can source drinks and refreshments for themselves and people with
support from staff. They also provide various areas of seating and dining spaces for people to
receive visitors if they do not want to use their rooms. During our visit, we saw the upstairs lounge
was being redecorated and improved with a new kitchenette and picture windows. New soft
furnishings and furniture are planned for the redesigned space. These changes will make the space
brighter and more welcoming and create a more home-like living space. The refurbishment work
was planned and managed to minimise disruption to people. Where the upstairs lounge was
temporarily unavailable, alternative arrangements were made so people continued to receive care,
take part in activities and access support safely.

Bedrooms are pleasantly decorated and contain the furniture people need, with larger items
secured safely to the wall. Many rooms are personalised with people’s belongings, photographs
and soft furnishings. We also saw equipment in place where needed, such as sensor mats, call
bells and pressure-relieving mattresses. Facilities and equipment across the home meet people’s
needs and are maintained to ensure they are safe. Records show health and safety servicing and
safety checks are up to date, including fire safety, routine environmental checks and equipment
servicing. These are carried out by the provider’s qualified maintenance team and external
contractors. There are good systems in place to report repairs or maintenance issues and these
are monitored through monthly meetings and reports overseen by the RI.

Outdoor areas include patio seating, gardens and pathways, which are secure and accessible, and
people are supported to use them safely. These areas and the wider grounds are very well
maintained and planted with shrubs, flowers and trees, providing pleasant and varied views all year
round from bedrooms and communal areas.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Governance and oversight arrangements support safe and effective service delivery and good
outcomes for people. These arrangements include regular quality audits, monthly management
reports to the provider, regular management meetings with the RI and senior managers for the
provider, and regular quality of care reviews. Day-to-day service delivery audits cover key areas in
the home including safeguarding, medication, incidents and accidents, pressure area care,
infection prevention and control, and nutrition and hydration. Issues identified through the audits
are addressed by the manager through action planning and then escalated to the RI and relevant
departments where necessary. The provider maintains detailed policies covering all aspects of care
and support and the service delivered, which are reviewed and updated in line with regulations and
changes in practice or legislation. Records show the manager engages well with external agencies,
including commissioners and the regulator. The RI is regularly present in the home and remains
well informed about quality improvements and emerging issues. Their oversight is maintained
through regular discussions with the manager, monthly management reports, and routine visits to
the service. During these visits, the RI reviews a selection of records for themselves, and speaks
with people using the service, relatives and staff to obtain feedback on their experiences of the
service.

The manager ensures staffing levels, skills mix and deployment meet people’s needs. Rotas show
appropriate cover across shifts, and during our visit we saw good numbers of staff were available,
attentive and responsive. The service benefits from a stable and experienced staff team, with no
reliance on agency staff. New staff are recruited safely, with appropriate vetting, Social Care Wales
registration, and a supportive induction for each new staff member. Ongoing vetting and
registration checks are carried out for all staff. Staff training and supervision arrangements support
safe practice and staff development. Records show staff receive regular supervision focused on
care quality, safeguarding and record keeping, alongside annual appraisals to discuss and support
professional development. This helps promote a positive culture and supports staff retention and
therefore supports continuity of good care for people. Staff receive a range of mandatory and role-
specific training, with regular updates to maintain required skills and knowledge. Spot checks and
observations by the manager are used to identify any need for refresher or additional training,
which is then followed up through one-to-one supervision.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
