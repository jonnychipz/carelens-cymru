Summary:

 Woffington Care Home is a service in Tredegar providing care for adults without nursing needs.
 Since our last inspection, the service has appointed a new service provider, responsible individual
 (RI) and manager. Although these changes are recent, we found significant improvements across
 the service. Two areas for improvement (AFI) identified at the previous inspection have been
 addressed and are no longer required. Both related to oversight by the RI, which has strengthened
 considerably. The AFI relating to medication management remains in place. Whilst medication
 systems have improved, further action is required to ensure medicines are managed consistently
 and safely.

 Many people experience positive outcomes because their well-being is promoted and they receive
 good quality care and support. People are encouraged to make choices, maintain relationships and
 take part in meaningful activities. The environment is comfortable, personalised and supports
 people's needs. Leaders have strengthened governance and oversight arrangements, which
 support the continued development of the service. Planned environmental improvements and
 ongoing work to enhance care planning demonstrate a commitment to continuous improvement.
 We have rated all four inspection themes as ‘Good’.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Many people live healthily and safely with control over their daily lives. Leaders have introduced
 regular house meetings which provide opportunities for people to share their views about the
 service, including menus, activities and daily routines. The service is redeveloping personal plans
 to strengthen person-centred practice and better reflect people's wishes, preferences and personal
 outcomes. People and their representatives can also provide feedback through conversations with
 staff, management and a suggestion box. This helps leaders identify opportunities to improve
 outcomes for people.

 People are supported to maintain their independence and remain connected to their surroundings.
 We saw dementia-friendly resources throughout the service, including dementia clocks, orientation
 boards, sensory equipment and seasonal displays. These help people understand the time, place
 and events around them. People make choices throughout the day, including meal options and
 access to snacks. Communication needs are met through a range of approaches, including visual
 communication aids. The service is also making positive progress in promoting the Active Offer.
 Welsh language signage is displayed throughout the home and speaking Welsh is encouraged.

 Many people experience positive physical, emotional and social well-being because they take part
 in meaningful activities. Leaders have strengthened activity provision through the appointment of
 an activities coordinator. People participate in regular activities including arts and crafts, sports
 activities and pamper sessions. Visiting groups and entertainers also support engagement,
 including pet therapy sessions and choir visits. People told us there is always something to do and
 spoke positively about the activities available. These opportunities help people maintain interests,
 regular stimulation, and social connections.

 People are supported to maintain important relationships. Visitors can attend the service without
 unnecessary restrictions and people are encouraged to sustain relationships with family and
 friends. Leaders are also developing stronger links with the local community to further enhance
 opportunities for people. Feedback from relatives was mixed but mostly positive. Whilst a minority
 raised concerns about staffing levels, many told us their relatives receive good care and described
 care staff as dedicated, kind and hardworking. Relatives also described positive changes since the
 new leadership team took over the service. The RI assured us all feedback received would be
 reviewed and used to support further improvement of the service.

       Care & Support                       Good




Most people receive good quality care and support that helps them achieve their personal
outcomes. Improvements to personal planning have strengthened people's involvement in how
their care is delivered. Personal plans increasingly reflect people's life histories, preferences,
strengths and outcomes, together with guidance for care staff on how best to support them. Plans
include information about specialist needs and identified risks. Further work remains ongoing and
should strengthen person-centred practice further. Leaders work with health and social care
professionals to support people's health and well-being and are actively improving arrangements
for escalating concerns when required.

Many people are treated with dignity and respect by care staff who know them well. During the
inspection we observed warm, positive and respectful interactions throughout the service. Care
staff provided reassurance, conversation, drinks, snacks and activities in a responsive and person-
centred way. Staff used humour and tactile support appropriately to support people's emotional
well-being. People spoke positively about care staff and described them as friendly, caring and
hardworking.

People are mostly protected from harm through effective safeguarding arrangements. Leaders
have strengthened safeguarding practices and demonstrated a commitment to developing a
positive safeguarding culture. Safeguarding concerns are referred appropriately to the Local
Authority, and leaders analyse incidents to identify patterns, trends and learning opportunities. Care
staff receive safeguarding training and understand their responsibilities. Improved infection
prevention and control arrangements, including enhanced cleaning practices and planned
environmental improvements, help reduce the risk of infection for people.

Medication management has improved since our last inspection, but outcomes in this area still
require improvement. Leaders had organised the medication room, returned excess stock and
strengthened medicines processes. Care staff receive medication training and competency
assessments. However, we identified inconsistencies in medicines administration. Medication
administration records contained gaps where staff had not signed to confirm medicines had been
administered. In some instances, records indicated medicines may have been missed. Records
relating to the effectiveness of 'as required' medicines were not consistently completed. These
issues had not been identified through existing quality assurance arrangements. We were pleased
to see leaders have introduced a more robust medication audit process, which should strengthen
oversight once fully embedded. This AFI remains from the previous inspection because sufficient
progress has not yet been made. Outcomes for people require improvement because medicines
are not always managed consistently and safely, and we expect the provider to make further
improvements.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that meets their needs and supports their well-being. The layout of
the service promotes privacy, dignity and independence. People have access to communal and
private spaces where they can socialise, spend time alone or meet visitors. Dedicated activity
areas contain a range of equipment supporting different interests and preferences. Smaller rooms
provide opportunities for private visits with family and friends when people wish.

People benefit from a warm, comfortable and welcoming environment. Suitable equipment and
facilities support a variety of care and support needs and contribute positively to people's physical
and emotional well-being. Bedrooms are personalised with photographs, decorations and personal
belongings that help create a homely atmosphere. Communal areas also contain personalised
displays and artwork. A sheltered outdoor seating area provides a pleasant space for people to
spend time outdoors.

Whilst some areas would benefit from refurbishment and modernisation, leaders have clear plans
to address this. We reviewed a detailed environmental improvement programme which identifies
areas requiring renovation over the next twelve months. These planned improvements should
further enhance people's comfort, dignity and overall experience of living at the service.

The provider has effective systems to maintain health and safety compliance. A dedicated health
and safety technician completes routine checks and testing, whilst external specialists support
compliance with regulatory inspections and servicing requirements. These arrangements help
ensure the premises remain safe and comply with relevant legislation and guidance.

Security measures help protect people whilst maintaining their rights, privacy and dignity. Visitors
sign in and provide identification when required. Some areas of the service remain secure to
support people's safety.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from improved leadership, governance and oversight arrangements. Since the
previous inspection, changes to the leadership structure have strengthened quality assurance and
accountability. The new RI demonstrates a clear understanding of their regulatory responsibilities
and undertakes visits to the service in line with regulatory requirements. RI reports include
observations, discussions with people and staff, review of records and analysis of service
information. Leaders use this information to identify improvements and support positive outcomes
for people. This represents a significant improvement since the previous inspection. Although some
systems remain relatively new, early evidence indicates they are having a positive impact on
service quality.

The manager has established a positive culture focused on continuous improvement. New quality
assurance processes are helping leaders monitor service delivery more effectively and identify
areas requiring action. People, relatives and staff spoke positively about both the manager and
service provider. Leaders acknowledged previous shortcomings regarding communication with the
regulator and provided assurance that future notifications would be submitted promptly where
required. We also found evidence of a more open and transparent culture, which encourages
learning when things go wrong.

Leaders have strengthened opportunities for people, relatives and staff to contribute to the ongoing
development of the service. Regular engagement takes place through meetings, surveys, day-to-
day discussions and feedback mechanisms. Leaders consider feedback and use it to inform
service improvements. This developing culture of openness and collaboration supports continuous
improvement and helps ensure decisions remain focused on achieving positive outcomes for
people. The service provider has also shown a commitment to learning from incidents, complaints
and feedback to improve practice and maintain the quality of care provided.

People are mostly supported by care staff with the skills, knowledge and qualifications required to
meet their needs. Recruitment processes are generally robust and leaders provided assurance that
these arrangements will continue to be strengthened. Care staff receive regular supervision, and
leaders are introducing a consistent appraisal programme. Most mandatory training is up to date.
However, some staff require additional service-specific training to strengthen their understanding of
specialist needs of people living within the service. Leaders have introduced a new training platform
and have committed to improving overall training compliance. These actions should help ensure
staff continue developing the skills needed to support people effectively. This is something we will
review at our next inspection.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
