Summary:


 Springfield is a residential service supporting up to six people. It is located within the Rhymney
 Valley area of Gwent. It is set in a picturesque area whilst also being close to a range of local
 facilities. The service is able to support people with a variety of needs including autism spectrum
 disorder, complex needs including behaviours that challenge as well as people with physical or
 learning disabilities.

 Springfield has been rated as excellent in the well-being theme because people are truly at the
 heart of the service. They are supported to work towards individual goals and care workers support
 people to participate in things they enjoy. Care and support is also rated as excellent because
 personal plans are strengths focussed, and care workers encourage people to complete things they
 are able to do and support when necessary. The environment theme is rated as good. This is
 because the service is suitable for people’s needs and there are appropriate processes to maintain
 good health and safety within the service. Leadership and management is rated as excellent. There
 is outstanding oversight and governance of the service to ensure it runs smoothly and care workers
 feel valued and appreciated for their work.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent





 People have choice and control over their daily lives as much as they are able. This includes
 people choosing which care workers they wish to support them, as well as what activities they want
 to do each day. People are offered a variety of opportunities to visit the local area, and wider
 community visits are planned regularly. People are encouraged to share ideas of things they would
 like to do and care workers support people to facilitate this. During the inspection we saw care
 workers respect people’s choices when offers of activities were declined or refused. Care workers
 support people with care and compassion, and we observed lots of warm interactions throughout
 the inspection. People are supported to work towards well-being goals. Progress towards these
 goals is reviewed regularly and when goals are met, people are supported to identify new goals to
 work towards. Care workers are afforded plenty of time to support people, and people’s well-being
 and happiness is very important to all care workers.

 People are kept safe from harm and abuse. There are robust policies and procedures in place in
 this service, care workers receive regular training and know how to use their training to ensure
 people’s safety. People’s physical health and well-being are supported. Referrals are made to
 external agencies to organise routine and urgent health care when needed.

 People are supported to communicate in their own way and care workers support people with their
 individual communication needs. Care workers have taken time to learn different methods to
 support people’s communication, for example learning braille. Care workers are highly skilled and
 adapt their approach to each person they support. People are empowered to communicate their
 feelings and wishes without being rushed by care workers. This creates a safe and empowering
 environment for people to freely express themselves.

 People live in a homely environment which meets their needs. Families and visitors can visit at any
 time and are welcomed by the service. There are private spaces for people to meet with visitors if
 they wish. The service is working towards providing the Active Offer of the Welsh language. There
 is bi-lingual signage around the service and ideas from people to promote and learn Welsh are
 acted upon by the registered manager.

       Care & Support                           Excellent




People are supported by a dedicated and very high quality team of care workers and receive the
care they require in a way that is meaningful to them. A thorough assessment is completed before
people move into the service to ensure people’s needs can be met. This pre-assessment leads to
the development of bespoke personal plans. These plans are strengths focussed and identify
people’s abilities, and the things people can do independently or with minimal support. The level of
detail and care taken to ensure personal plans are meaningful to each person is outstanding.
Personal plans also set out how care workers can support people in areas they need assistance as
well as people’s preferences around how they receive care. People’s routines and individuality are
respected by care workers. People’s history is captured in detail in their personal plans, this
includes important milestones through their lives, important people, family and dates to remember
as well as things to avoid and dislikes. There is a clear collaboration between people, their family
members and external professionals in the creation and development of personal plans. Risk
assessments are in place which identify possible risks to people within the service and in the
community. These assessments set out what actions are taken to minimise risk to keep people
safe while promoting people’s independence. Personal plans and risk assessments are reviewed
regularly to ensure they remain up to date and relevant for people’s current needs. As part of the
review process, a nominated key worker reports on the previous month. This includes considering
people’s well-being, activities, mood, physical and mental health and other observations.

There is a detailed medication policy, and robust procedures are in place to ensure safe medication
administration. The medication storage is orderly and organised, with highly effective oversight and
review by the registered manager. There are good procedures around infection prevention and
control, and the service has plenty of supplies of personal protective equipment (PPE).

During the inspection we saw plenty of warm interactions between people and care workers. It is
clear care workers know people well and work hard to support people to have enjoyable
experiences every day. Families told us “Staff are lovely” and that their relatives have been well
supported. A visiting professional told us “There has been positive communication” from care
workers and the registered manager to support someone moving into the service.

       Environment                         Good





The service is spacious and welcoming and accessible for people at the service. Communal areas
are homely and nicely decorated, with trinkets and photographs on display. There is a choice of
communal spaces for people to access during the day including the lounge room, the kitchen and
sunroom downstairs and there is a multipurpose activity room available upstairs. There is a lift
which provides access upstairs, meaning all areas of the service are accessible to everyone.
People’s bedrooms are personalised and decorated in line with people’s tastes and interests. This
personalisation includes décor, bedding, accessories and furniture items. Bilingual signage is
located around the service identifying all areas. There is a large notice board displaying available
activities and games, which also includes photographs and bilingual descriptions to ensure
accessibility for people at the service.


There is a large, safe garden with an outside patio area which has a large pergola to provide
shade. Most of the garden space is lawned with mature trees and bushes around the perimeter.
There are chairs and tables as well as outdoor decorations. The garden is private and undisturbed
and wild rabbits and birds are often seen in the garden which people enjoy.


Appropriate safety checks are completed to ensure serviceable equipment is safely maintained.
Regular health and safety checks are completed including regular audits by the registered
manager. There is a robust cleaning schedule to maintain high standards of cleanliness.
Appropriate fire safety precautions have been including a robust fire risk assessment and fire drills
take place routinely. There are Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEP’s) in place for all
people at the service to ensure their safety in the event of a fire. These are reviewed routinely and
updated as required.


The service has a Food Standards Agency rating of five (very good), so people can be assured
there are appropriate food handling practices taking place within the service.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



There are robust processes in place to ensure safe recruitment. All care workers must provide
appropriate identification documents as well as complete a Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS)
check. Care workers are supported to complete a robust induction to support them into their caring
role. This includes completing mandatory training to ensure care workers have the required
knowledge and skills to support people at the service. Refresher training is completed by all staff
when required. All care workers are compliant with all training at the time of inspection.


There are good processes in place to ensure accidents and incidents are recorded and any
required actions are taken. There have been no complaints received by the service since their last
inspection; however, many compliments have been shared from family members and visiting
professionals. These are celebrated and shared among the staff team. There are regular team
meetings which share any service updates with care workers. They also provide the opportunity for
care workers to share their views of the service to the registered manager. All care workers receive
a regular supervision sessions with their manger. Supervision sessions follow a template to ensure
consistency for all care workers and includes care worker welfare, training and targets as well as
feedback from the manager. All care workers receive an annual appraisal. All care workers have
completed or working towards completing formal qualifications and are all registered with Social
Care Wales, the workforce regulator.


The Responsible Individual (RI) visits the service regularly to meet with people to hear their views
and wishes. It is clear people at the service know the RI well and we observed the warm
interactions between the RI and people during the inspection. The RI also completes a six monthly
Quality of Care report. This report is very robust and outlines people’s views and comments, as
well as goals for the service manager to complete with target dates for expected completion. Care
is delivered as set out in the Statement of Purpose, and an easy read Service User Guide sets is
available for people to understand the standards of care they can expect to receive.


Care workers told us they are “Very well supported.” And “The company appreciate staff”. They
also told us there were opportunities for career progression within the company and the registered
manager was “Open to suggestions” of ways to improve the service. It is clear care workers are
very passionate about supporting people at the service to live happy and as independent lives as
possible.

 Areas identified for improvement
 Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
 immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
 action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


 Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
 people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
 Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
 improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
 and outcomes for people have improved.


 CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
