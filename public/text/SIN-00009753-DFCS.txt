Summary:

 People are well supported in Plas Isaf and told us they feel safe in the home. They have a voice
 and have daily choices. Varied activities are offered to ensure people are stimulated and have
 opportunity to socialise with others. People are supported to keep in touch with friends and family
 and remain a part of the local community. The rating for well-being is good as people live as
 healthily and safely as able with choices regarding their daily lives and care.

 People are cared for by a stable staff group who provide continuity in care. People said they are
 treated with dignity and respect and have good relationships with care staff. There are good
 systems in place to ensure people receive their prescribed medication. Senior staff audit quality of
 care markers such as hygiene, infection control, accidents, falls and incidents to ensure good
 standards of care. The rating for care and support is good as people receive the care and support,
 they need to achieve their personal outcomes.

 People are happy with their rooms and can personalise them to make them homely. There are
 appropriate facilities in the home to support people’s desired outcomes. There is a maintenance

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People feel they have a voice regarding their care and daily lives. People can choose what to do
 with their day and have varied meal choices. People told us they are happy with the service in the
 home, and the food is good. A person said, “It’s good here, they’re very flexible, you have choices.”
 People are offered a selection of activities according to their personal preferences. We saw a
 programme of events had been organised for the Christmas period. We saw people chatting in the
 lounge or having visits from family and friends. Some people chose to spend time in their rooms
 and said they were comfortable watching TV and reading. People have visitors as they want and
 we witnessed good rapport between care staff and visitors. People’s personal plans include details
 of their personal history, their likes and dislikes and individual care objectives.

 People’s personal plans evidenced they receive personalised care from care staff who are trained
 and supported to enable good care standards. We saw people are encouraged to make personal
 choices. Care staff anticipate people’s needs, and we witnessed friendly conversations between
 people and care staff. People told us they are happy with the care, and care staff are “Kind”, and
 treat them with dignity and respect. People praise the meals provided and said they can access
 drinks and snacks as they want them.



 The service has procedures in place to safeguard the people living there. Care staff receive
 safeguarding training and are supported with updated policies and protocols to follow in the event
 of a concern. Care staff spoken with are aware of local safeguarding procedures and how to
 access local authority teams to ensure people’s safety. Care staff said they can approach senior
 staff if they have any concerns regarding people’s care.

       Care & Support                       Good



People’s plans are written according to their individual needs and personal objectives. Their
preferences, history and people and dates of importance are also recorded to enable person
centred care and to provide a measure for personal aims. Daily and nightly records are kept in a
clear, easy to read way and the records are organised and easy to navigate on a electronic system.
Care staff can input into care records as they go along to ensure records of care are contemporary.
The electronic system flags up when personal plans and risk assessments are due to ensure they
remain appropriate for people’s care needs. Health care visits and appointments are recorded and
any changes to the person’s care. People access health care advice in a timely way as care staff
know people well and can anticipate their needs.



People’s health needs are monitored to keep them as healthy as possible. People are consulted
regarding their care and are encouraged to make decisions and influence their care. Care staff
offer sensitive care and demonstrate a respectful attitude towards people. People told us they are
treated with respect. People’s nutritional needs and weight is monitored, and they can access
nutritional food and drinks. People have varied activities to stimulate their memory and encourage
socialisation. They can have optician; dental and chiropody appointments as required and can
access hairdressing services.

Medicine administration and storage practices are good. There is an audit system for medicines
entering and leaving the home. Medicines are stored in a secure, and the temperature of the room
and medicines fridge is monitored daily to ensure they are stored in an optimal way. People are
given their medicines individually and as prescribed. Care staff receive medicine administration
refresher training to ensure their knowledge is up to date and to enable good practice. The
manager said they can access medication for people in a timely manner.


There are good safeguarding practices in the home to keep people as safe as possible. Care plans
are regularly audited to ensure good care standards. Care staff receive safeguarding training and
told us they know how to report any incidents or practices of concern. Senior staff report
appropriate concerns to Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) and other relevant agencies. Accidents
and incidents are recorded, and appropriate actions are taken.

       Environment                         Good





People live in a home which is well maintained and careful of their health and safety. The home
presents as clean and tidy and has spacious lounges for people to be sociable or quiet in.
Walkways and fire exits are kept clear of obstacles. People can choose to spend time in their room
or in the lounge areas to socialise, engage in activities, eat meals and receive visitors. People
confirmed they have daily choices regarding where to spend their day. The home has a programme
of redecoration and maintenance in place and people have a say in the décor of the home and their
rooms. There are appropriate facilities in the home and people are happy with the laundry service
provided. The hygiene rating for the home is five, this is the highest rating possible. People can
access the equipment needed for their safe care; this is well maintained and serviced to ensure
their safety.


Health and safety checks and assessments are up to date to enable people’s safety. Utility checks
and certificates are in place for water, gas and electricity. Weekly fire alarm checks are conducted,
and staff are trained regarding evacuating people in the event of a fire. People have personalised
personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs), to ensure their safe evacuation in the event of an
emergency. Housekeeping staff are employed to ensure the home is clean and has good infection
control. Entrances to the home are locked and people sign in and out for accountability and a
record in the event of a fire or emergency of any extra people within the home.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has good governance arrangements in place to ensure the smooth running of the
service. The RI visits regularly and considers the views of residents, family and care staff regarding
the service. The RI provides a quality report regarding the home in line with the requirements of the
regulation, the RI considers people’s well-being, the care and support provided, environmental
needs and maintenance and managerial issues such as staffing requirements, training and
supervision. The manager feels well supported by the RI and can access training required by their
role. Service audits conducted by senior staff are discussed with the RI and any actions to be taken
to maintain good care standards. People have an assessment prior to accessing the service to
ensure the home can meet their needs. People have information regarding the service prior to
using the service, this information is frequently reviewed to ensure its accuracy.

Care staff are safely recruited. Care staff records evidence that appropriate employment checks
are in place and in date to ensure they are appropriate to work with vulnerable adults. Checks are
made to ensure care staff are qualified and registered with Social Care Wales. Care staff receive
regular training and supervision suitable for their role. There is a system in place for care staff to
catch up if they miss training. Training is an ongoing process throughout the year, and good audit
records are kept. Care staff said they are happy working in the home and that senior staff are
available and approachable, a care staff member said, “No complaints at all”. The service retains
good numbers of care staff to ensure continuity of care for people.


The provider invests in the home; the building is maintained to a good standard to provide dignified
surroundings for people. People are offered meals made with fresh ingredients and can access
snacks and drinks as required. Care staff are safely recruited, and investment is made in
appropriate training. Equipment is replaced as required as are soft furnishings, towels and bedding.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
