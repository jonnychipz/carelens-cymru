Summary:


 Parklands Care Home is situated in a residential area of Malpas. It provides both long-term
 accommodation and short-term placements for up to 15 people. Short-term care is available for
 individuals with reablement goals who benefit from a structured and supportive programme within a
 residential setting, enabling them to regain independence and confidence.

 The wellbeing of people living at Parklands is good. People are supported to maintain their
 physical, emotional, and social wellbeing, with staff promoting independence, choice, and dignity in
 day-to-day life. There is a warm and inclusive atmosphere where individuals feel safe, comfortable,
 and valued.

The care and support people receive is good. Care is person-centred and tailored to meet
individual needs, preferences, and goals. Staff know people well and provide compassionate,
consistent support which promotes positive outcomes and enhances quality of life.

The environment is good. The home is clean, well maintained, and designed to meet the needs of
the people living there. Communal and private spaces are comfortable and accessible, helping to
create a homely and safe setting.

Leadership and management at Parklands is good. There are effective governance systems in
place, and leaders provide clear direction and oversight. This ensures the service is well run, with a
focus on continuous improvement and positive outcomes for people.

Findings:


        Well-being                           Good




 People living at Parklands generally experience positive wellbeing outcomes and are supported in
 a calm, respectful, and person-centred environment. During the visit, people appeared relaxed and
 comfortable within their surroundings, and the overall atmosphere of the home was peaceful and
 welcoming. Interactions between staff and residents were consistently warm, kind, and respectful,
 with staff demonstrating a good understanding of people’s individual needs, preferences, and
 routines. There was clear evidence people were supported to maintain their independence
 wherever possible, particularly through the reablement approach embedded within the service,
 which focuses on enabling people to regain skills and confidence following a period of illness or
 hospital admission.

 Feedback from residents and families was largely positive, with many commenting on the caring
 nature of staff and the supportive atmosphere within the home. One family member told me, “Mum
 is so much better off in here; she is making progress every day.” People reported feeling safe and
 well supported in their day-to-day care, and there was a sense of trust in the staff team.

 Families spoke positively about their ability to visit freely and maintain regular contact with their
 loved ones, which is an important aspect of maintaining emotional wellbeing and social
 connections. Some individuals expressed a desire for more information and communication,
 particularly in relation to their future plans and returning home. This indicates people want to know
 more about what will happen next, despite care being supportive,

 Records reviewed indicate people are involved in their personal plans and goal setting, reflecting a
 person-centred approach to care delivery. Reablement outcomes are often achieved, with evidence
 of increased independence in areas such as mobility, personal care, and daily living skills. Families
 reported feeling included in care planning processes and valued as part of decision-making. People
 spoke positively about spending time in the community with their families and expressed
 satisfaction in returning to the home afterwards.

 Opportunities for meaningful engagement are present; however, these are not consistently
 structured across the service. While people are supported during care activities and mealtimes,
 there is limited evidence of a consistent programme of activities outside of these times. This can
 lead to periods where people are less engaged or stimulated, which may impact their overall
 wellbeing over time.

       Care & Support                       Good



People at Parklands receive care and support which is generally safe, effective, and centred on
individual needs. Staff demonstrate a good understanding of residents’ requirements and provide
care in a responsive, respectful, and compassionate manner. A reablement-focused approach is
embedded for those who require it. People who are living at the home on a short-term basis are
supported to develop daily routines, where appropriate, and are encouraged to regain skills,
confidence, and independence, often with a focus on returning home. This approach supports
individuals to achieve their personal outcomes and maintain as much independence as possible.

People who live at the service on a longer-term basis are also supported to maintain their
independence. Observations during the visit confirmed staff support people with mobility, personal
care, and daily routines in an appropriate manner. Staff interactions were positive and respectful,
promoting dignity and choice, and people were supported at their own pace.

Feedback from residents and families indicates care provision is kind, consistent, and supportive,
with most people reporting they feel well cared for. Staff demonstrated a clear awareness of
individual risks and needs, including managing more complex conditions such as epilepsy, reduced
mobility, and allergies. This includes proactive monitoring and appropriate responses to changes in
people’s health and wellbeing.

Medication management processes are in place, and staff demonstrate an understanding of safe
administration practices. Previous audits had identified a number of errors in medication processes;
whilst there is no evidence these resulted in harm, they highlighted the need for improved oversight
and assurance of staff competency. The service has responded by introducing an electronic
medication management system, which was being embedded at the time of the inspection. This
system is intended to reduce the risk of error, improve accuracy in recording, and strengthen
overall governance of medication practices.

Where appropriate, people are encouraged to manage their own medication as part of their
reablement goals. This was evident for some individuals within the service and supports
independence and confidence. For those self-administering, medication is stored securely in locked
safes within people’s personal living spaces, with appropriate risk assessments in place to ensure
safe practice.

Risk assessments are generally comprehensive and reflect people’s individual needs and risks.
Staff demonstrate an ability to respond appropriately to changes in condition or circumstance,
ensuring care remains safe and responsive.

       Environment                         Good




Parklands provides an environment which is safe, clean, and suitable for the needs of the people
living there. During the visit, the home was observed to be tidy, well maintained, and welcoming,
contributing positively to people’s comfort and overall wellbeing. The atmosphere within the home
was calm and organised, with staff ensuring communal areas were safe and accessible. Communal
spaces were appropriately furnished, offering a homely feel, and individuals’ bedrooms were
personalised with belongings, supporting identity and a sense of belonging.

The service demonstrates compliance with key health and safety requirements. Records we
reviewed confirm essential checks are up to date, including fire safety systems and drills, legionella
monitoring, gas safety certification, electrical testing, and equipment servicing such as hoists and
lifts. These checks provide assurance the environment is maintained safely and risks are
minimised.

The home holds a Food Hygiene Rating of 5, reflecting high standards in food storage, preparation,
and kitchen cleanliness. Infection prevention and control measures are established, and the service
has demonstrated the ability to manage infectious outbreaks effectively, ensuring people’s health
and safety are protected.

The outdoor environment is generally well maintained and provides a pleasant and accessible
space for residents to enjoy fresh air. The outdoor space was tidy and inviting, supporting
opportunities for social interaction and planned activities.

Planned improvements to the building, including investment in infrastructure and energy efficiency
measures such as the installation of an air source heat pump, were evident. This demonstrates a
proactive approach by the provider in maintaining and enhancing the physical environment.

Assistive technology is available within the service, and there has been a clear effort to display
equipment and provide information to support residents preparing to return home. This reflects the
service’s reablement focus and helps people understand options available to promote
independence. While these resources are beneficial, there are opportunities to further improve
accessibility and ensure all residents can engage easily with the information provided.

       Leadership & Management               Good




The leadership and management arrangements at Parklands support people to achieve positive
outcomes, promoting their overall wellbeing. The service benefits from effective organisational
structures, governance systems, and robust oversight, contributing to a stable, consistent, and
person-centred environment. These arrangements ensure people receive high-quality care which is
responsive to their individual needs and preferences.

During the inspection, feedback described a positive and supportive staff culture, contributing to
staff retention and continuity of care. Staff spoke positively about their working environment, with
one member of staff stating, “The staff here are nice… really good team morale.” This
demonstrates staff feel valued, respected, and part of a cohesive team. Another staff member
stated, “The culture enables staff to work together confidently and provide effective care.”

Staff confirmed they receive regular supervision, training, and opportunities for professional
development. One staff member told us, “I have loads of training… my line manager is keen to see
me progress,” indicating a clear focus on developing staff skills and knowledge. This ensures staff
are equipped with the expertise and confidence required to meet people’s needs.

Quality assurance processes are well established, including regular audits, monitoring systems,
and structured documentation to support oversight and accountability. The Responsible Individual
undertakes regular reviews and engages with people using the service at least every three months.

There is clear evidence feedback from people, and their families is actively sought, valued, and
used to inform service development. Where improvements are identified, clear action plans are in
place, demonstrating a commitment to continuous improvement.

The provider maintains appropriate policies and procedures, which are understood and
implemented by staff and regularly reviewed to ensure compliance with current legislation and
national guidance.


Robust recruitment and vetting procedures are in place to ensure all staff and volunteers are
suitable and competent, with agency staff expected to meet the same standards. This helps ensure
people are supported by a safe, skilled, and trustworthy workforce.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
