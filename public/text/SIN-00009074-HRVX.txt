        Well-being                           Good

        Leadership & Management               Good


Summary:

 People are well supported in Bryn Marl and told us they feel safe in the home. People have a voice
 and have daily choices. Activities are offered to ensure people are stimulated and have opportunity
 to socialise with others; work is in progress to offer a full programme of events. People are
 supported to keep in touch with friends and family. People are happy with the menu offered and
 can access snacks and drinks. The environment is well maintained, and people have a choice
 regarding the décor of their bedroom. The rating for well-being is good as people live as healthily
 and safely as able with choices regarding their daily lives and care.

 People are cared for by a stable staff group who provide continuity in care. We observed people
 are treated with dignity and respect and have good relationships with care staff. There are good
 systems in place to ensure people receive their prescribed medication. Senior staff audit quality of
 care markers such as hygiene, infection control, accidents, falls and incidents to ensure good
 standards of care. The rating for care and support is good as people receive the care and support;
 they need to achieve their personal outcomes.

 People are happy with the upgrades made to the environment which are an ongoing process.
 There are appropriate facilities in the home to support people’s desired outcomes. There is a
 maintenance plan in place to ensure the home is in good repair and appropriate to provide people’s
 care. The home has updated risk assessments in place to ensure people’s health and safety. The

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People feel they mostly have choices regarding their care and daily lives. People can choose
 where to spend their day and what meals to have. People told us they are happy with the service in
 the home, and the food is good. People are offered activities and support according to their
 personal preferences. We saw people had been doing some crafts, a person was assisted to do a
 jigsaw, other people in the lounge were enjoying afternoon tea. Some people chose to spend time
 in their rooms and said they were comfortable watching TV. People’s personal plans include details
 of their personal history, their likes and dislikes and their individual care objectives. People’s first
 language choices and any communication needs are recorded for care staff to offer appropriate
 support.


 People’s personal plans evidence they receive personalised care from care staff who are supported
 to enable good care standards. We saw people are encouraged to make personal choices. Care
 staff can anticipate people’s needs, and we witnessed good rapport between people and care staff.
 People told us they are happy with the care and are treated with dignity and respect. One person
 told us care staff are “like friends and make a difference in the home.” People praised the meals
 provided and said they can access drinks and snacks as they want them.


 The service can safeguard the people living there if required. Care staff receive updated
 safeguarding training and are supported with policies and protocols to follow in the event of a
 concern. Care staff spoken with are aware of local safeguarding procedures and how to access
 local authority teams to ensure people’s safety. Care staff said they can approach senior staff if
 they have any concerns regarding people’s care.

       Care & Support                       Good



People’s plans are written according to their individual objectives and needs. People’s preferences
and personal history including important people and dates are documented to enable person
centred care and to provide a measure for personal aims. Daily and nightly records are recorded in
a clear, easy to read way and are organised and easy to navigate on a computerised system. Care
staff can input into care records as they work to ensure records of care are contemporary and
accurate. Care staff said they like the system as it is efficient and easy to use. The computer flags
up when personal plans and risk assessments are due for review to ensure they remain
appropriate for people’s care needs. Health care professionals’ visits are carefully recorded and
any resulting changes to the person’s care. Senior staff said people can contribute to their personal
plans if they are able, further documented evidence of this would be of benefit to demonstrate
choice and people’s influence on their care. People’s health needs are monitored to keep them as
healthy as possible. People access health care advice in a timely way as care staff know people
well and can anticipate their needs. People can have optician; dental and chiropody appointments
as required and can access hairdressing services.


Care staff offer sensitive care and demonstrate a respectful attitude towards people to enable
dignity in their care. People’s nutritional needs and weight is regularly monitored, and people can
access nutritional food and drinks. People have a choice of where to spend their day and can
access some activities.

Medicine administration and storage practices are good in the home. There is a good record of
medicines entering and leaving the home. Medicines are stored in a secure way, and the
temperature of the room and medicines fridge is monitored daily to ensure they are stored in an
optimal way. People are given their medicines individually and as prescribed. Care staff receive
medicine administration refresher training to ensure their knowledge is up to date and to enable
good practice. Senior staff said they can access medication prescriptions for people in a timely
manner.


There are good safeguarding practices in the home to protect people from harm. Care plans are
regularly audited to ensure good standards. Care staff receive safeguarding training and told us
they know how to report any incidents or practices of concern. Senior staff report appropriate
concerns to Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) and other relevant authorities. Accidents and incidents
are recorded, and appropriate actions are taken.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a home which is well maintained and careful of their health and safety. The home
presents as clean and tidy and has spacious lounges for people to be sociable or quiet in.
Walkways and fire exits are kept clear of obstacles. People can choose to spend time in their room
or in the lounge areas to socialise, engage in activities, eat meals in and receive visitors. People
confirmed they have daily choices regarding where to spend their day. The home has an ongoing
programme of redecoration and maintenance to provide people with a pleasant environment.
People and care staff said they are happy with the new lounge area on the first floor and said it is
“lovely”. This is used for various activities and is decorated in a fresh and modern style. People can
have a say in the décor of their room and can personalise them according to personal taste and
preference. There are appropriate facilities in the home and people are happy with the laundry
service provided. The hygiene rating for the home is five, this is the highest rating possible. People
can access the equipment needed for their safe care; this is well maintained and serviced to ensure
their safety. There are spacious grounds which are well maintained, people enjoy sitting out in good
weather. There are parking facilities for visitors and sufficient space for an ambulance as required.

Health and safety checks and assessments are audited and are up to date to enable people’s
safety. Utility checks and certificates are in place for water, gas and electricity. Weekly fire alarm
checks are conducted, and staff are trained regarding evacuating people in the event of a fire.
People have personalised personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs), to ensure their safe
evacuation in the event of an emergency. Housekeeping staff are employed to ensure the home is
clean and has good infection control. Entrances to the home are locked and people sign in and out
for accountability and a record in the event of a fire or emergency of any extra people within the
home.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has good governance arrangements in place to ensure the smooth running of the
service. The RI visits regularly and considers the views of residents, family and staff regarding the
service. The RI provides a quality report regarding the home in line with the requirements of the
legislation. This considers people’s well-being, the care and support provided, environmental
needs, maintenance and managerial issues such as staffing requirements, training and
supervision. The manager feels well supported by the RI and can access training required for their
role. Service audits conducted by senior staff are discussed with the RI and any actions to be taken
to maintain good care standards. People have an assessment prior to accessing the service to
ensure the home can meet their needs. People have information regarding the service prior to
using the service, this information is frequently reviewed to ensure its accuracy.


Care staff are safely recruited. We saw from staff records that appropriate employment checks are
in place and in date to ensure they are appropriate to work with adults at risk. Checks are made to
ensure care staff and nurses are qualified and registered with the relevant governing body. Care
staff receive regular training and supervision suitable for their role. On-line training is used; this
shows a high attendance rate from care staff and nurses. Training is also provided in person for
certain subjects, a care staff member said, “the training provided is very good.” Training is an
ongoing process throughout the year, and good audit records are kept. Care staff and nurses said
senior staff are approachable and supportive. A member of staff said they are happy in their work
and, “It’s a good home, we do our best for people.” The levels of staff employed match that which is
stated in the Statement of Purpose document for the service enabling continuity of care for people.


The provider invests in the home; the building is maintained on an ongoing basis to provide
dignified surroundings for people. People are offered meals made with fresh ingredients and can
access snacks and drinks as required. Care staff are safely recruited, and investment is made in
appropriate training. Equipment is replaced as required as are soft furnishings, towels and bedding.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
