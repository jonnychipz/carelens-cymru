Summary:

 People who have made Langton Hall their home receive a good standard of care. Everyone we
 spoke with have high levels of confidence in the service.

 Wellbeing is rated as good because of the relationships people, and those important to them, have
 with the staff team. This, alongside the opportunities to do things that are important to them and
 that they enjoy, mean they are treated with dignity and respect. People are safe and supported by a
 team of workers who are appropriately trained and effectively led by an experienced and dedicated
 manager.

 Care and support is good because the team have up to date care personal plans to follow together
 with a good knowledge of each individual. Some improvements are needed to documentation
 which the manager is addressing.

 The environment is good because although some parts of the service are dated, there is a plan to
 enhance the physical environment. Standards of cleanliness are good but additional cleaning time

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely. They are treated with dignity and respect. Relatives have high
 levels of confidence in the service describing the staff as Lovely.. fantastic”, “Really really friendly”
 and “Amazing”. The atmosphere is calm and well organised with care workers knowing their roles
 and responsibilities. Interactions between people and those caring for them are friendly and relaxed
 with some good-humoured banter. Care workers know people and can recognise if an individual is
 unwell or would benefit from a medical review or other interventions. Relatives are welcome at the
 service and feel involved in people’s care.

 There are some opportunities for people to do things that matter to them but no dedicated activities
 worker is employed meaning care workers offer activities when they are able to.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. Visitors are required to sign in so that staff
 know who is on the premises at all times. The service is clean, comfortable and generally homely.
 Policies and training make sure care workers know their responsibilities in respect of safeguarding
 and workers find the manager approachable and responsive.

 There are limited opportunities for people to receive a service in Welsh. Some staff are Welsh
 speaking but personal plans, menus and signage are not available bilingually.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care from a team of enthusiastic and motivated staff. Care workers
know people well, knowing what and who is important to them. Personal plans contain information
about the individual, their personal background as well as how they want their care to be offered.
Support workers find the records helpful. There are plans for a range of areas including mobility,
nutrition and communication. Some people have been assessed as needing repositioning and
whilst no one receiving care has any pressure damage, records do not always show repositioning
is carried out in line with the support plans. The manager is confident this is down to staff not
recording interventions and addressing this with the team.

There are some opportunities for people to do things that are important to them. Singers and other
entertainers visit the service. Care workers, when possible, offer more informal activities such as
ball games, quizzes and music. Some people think there is enough going on in the service while
others say they sometimes feel “Bored”.

Care and support is offered in a person-centred way with workers feeling they are not rushed and
their colleagues understand that each individual is given the time they need. Care staff feel they
work well as a team and can rely on each other to make sure people’s needs are met in an
individualised and person-centred way.

People are protected from harm and abuse because all staff we spoke with know their
responsibilities in relation to safeguarding. They are confident about reporting any concerns and
are certain their manager would take the actions needed to make sure people are appropriately
safeguarded. An up-to-date policy supports the training staff have in safeguarding.

People’s medication is safely managed. A senior member of the team is responsible for ordering
and receiving medication. A recent audit shows a high level of compliance. A check of Controlled
Drugs showed the right quantity of medication is available and a policy is available for staff to
follow. Care workers are trained in the safe administration of medication.

There is an understanding of the importance of good nutrition. People are offered a choice of meal
and alternatives are available also. Special diets and special events are catered for, the most
recent being an afternoon for Royal Ascot week. The kitchen has been awarded the maximum
score of five by the Food Standards Agency which means they found standards to be very good.
Staff consider they have the equipment they need. There is a good emphasis on people remaining
hydrated with drinks offered regularly throughout the day. People are encouraged to use the dining
room but if people prefer to eat elsewhere this is respected and accommodated.

The risk of infection is minimised because the provider promotes hygienic practices. There are
adequate supplies of personal protective equipment (PPE). Regular checks and audits show a high
level of compliance with the standards set.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that meets their needs. There are several lounge areas as well as a
dining room for people to spend together if they choose to. Many bedrooms have some ensuite
facilities and there are additional bathrooms throughout the service. People have personalised their
bedrooms with items of furniture, photographs and soft furnishings.

Whilst clean, parts of the service are in need of redecoration and refurbishment. The provider has
already recognised improvements are needed to the environment and there is an ongoing
programme of work. Some flooring has been replaced and plans for remaining carpets to be taken
up and replaced with vinyl flooring. Some toilets have been refurbished. Parts of the service retain
some original and attractive original features.

People are happy with the standards of cleanliness throughout but some workers think additional
housekeeping staff are needed.

The grounds are extensive and generally in good order but some maintenance would enhance the
areas further. A large wall surrounds the service making it safe for people to spend time outside
when the weather permits.

The provider ensures people’s care and support is provided in a location and environment
with facilities that promote people’s well-being and safety. The environment meets the
needs of the people living there. Within the home there are numerous communal areas including a
large communal lounge, a smaller, quieter sitting area and spacious dining room. We saw people’s
bedrooms are personalised with items of personal choice. We saw people moving freely within the
area in which they live.

Confidentiality is maintained throughout the service. People are safe from unauthorised
visitors entering the building, as all visitors have to ring the front doorbell before gaining
entry and are asked to record their visits in the visitors book when entering and leaving.
Care records are held electronically, and staff personnel records are securely stored in the
administration office.

Health and Safety measures are in place to keep people as safe as possible. Equipment
and facilities including hoists and slings are tested regularly. Fire safety equipment is tested
both internally and by external contractors and all staff have fire safety training. People
living at the service have a Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan (PEEP) in place, which
guides care staff on how to evacuate people in the event of an emergency.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has some effective and robust governance arrangements in place to monitor quality.
Reports written by the RI are comprehensive, reflective and focus on both quality and
improvement.

A range of quality audits and checks are completed including the environment; mattresses; moving
& handling equipment and fire safety. These show a high level of compliance.

There is a good focus on developing a skilled and knowledgeable workforce. The training matrix
shows most training is up to date with staff completing training in a range of areas including
communication, dementia awareness and first aid. Care workers are appropriately supervised and
get constructive feedback on their work to help them in their professional development.

Care workers find the manager approachable and responsive and are able to raise any ideas or
concerns they have with her. They are confident they will get a helpful and constructive response.

There is a safe and robust recruitment process and staff files contain the information needed,
including references, an employment history and the required safety checks. Files are easy to
navigate and appropriately stored securely.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
