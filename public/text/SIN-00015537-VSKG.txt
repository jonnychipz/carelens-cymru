Summary:

 Ty Mawr Care Home provides good-quality care and support for up to 54 people and is situated in
 the village of Abercrave.

 People at Ty Mawr consistently express satisfaction with the care and support they receive. Staff
 demonstrate a good understanding of people’s needs and preferences, delivering person-centred
 care with compassion and kindness. Care documentation is person centred addressing individual
 support requirements while considering health and safety risks.

 The service is well-managed, with suitable governance and quality assurance systems in place to
 ensure the smooth and effective operation of the home. Staff receive training relevant to the needs
 of those they support and report feeling valued and well-supported in their roles.

 The living environment is clean, comfortable supporting people’s well-being. A programme of

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to make choices about their daily lives. They have freedom to decide how
 they spend their time and are encouraged to maintain contact with family and friends. Activities are
 available for those who wish to take part, and the service employs two activities coordinators who
 work with people to develop an activities timetable. Plans are in place to establish a residents’
 committee, giving people the opportunity to contribute to decisions about life in the home, including
 activities and menu options.

 There are arrangements in place to safeguard people from harm and abuse. Staff are recruited in
 line with regulatory requirements to ensure they are suitable to work with vulnerable people. They
 receive training relevant to the needs of those they support, including safeguarding, medication
 management, and moving and handling. Risks to people’s health and safety are documented in
 their personal plans. Medication management systems ensure medicines are stored securely and
 administered safely.

 People live in an environment that promotes their well-being. The service is undergoing a
 comprehensive refurbishment, which includes improvements to communal areas, personal rooms,
 bathing facilities, and outdoor spaces. There are enough communal areas which are clean,
 comfortable, and conducive to social interaction. People’s rooms are personalised to individual
 preferences, fostering a sense of ownership and familiarity. The home is well maintained, with
 appropriate systems in place for regular checks and servicing of utilities and safety features,
 ensuring a secure and pleasant living environment.

 People are treated with dignity and respect and report having positive relationships with staff.
 Feedback from people indicates high levels of satisfaction, with comments such as, “The staff are
 lovely. Very nice people,” and “The staff are wonderful; I get on with all of them. My wife died
 recently so I came here. They turned sorrow into happiness.” During the inspection of Ty Mawr, we
 observed warm and respectful interactions between staff and people, demonstrating that staff value
 people and uphold their dignity. Family members also provided positive feedback, with one stating,
 “My dad loves it here; he has really thrived. He’s close to the staff and the other people who live
 here. He’s healthier now than he has been for years.”

       Care & Support                       Good



People living at Ty Mawr benefit from a good standard of care and support. Assessments are
completed prior to admission to ensure the service can meet people’s needs. Following the initial
assessment, a personal plan is developed, outlining each person’s care and support requirements
as well as any identified health and safety risks. We reviewed several personal plans and noted
some require further development to ensure they accurately reflect people’s current needs.
Personal plans are reviewed regularly; however, we found no clear evidence people or their
representatives are actively involved in the review process. These matters were discussed with the
provider, who assured us steps will be taken to address the issues identified.

People’s health needs are well supported at Ty Mawr Care Home. Each person’s health
requirements are documented within their personal plans. Nursing and care staff demonstrate a
good understanding of these needs and are proactive in identifying changes in presentation,
promptly seeking advice or intervention from relevant healthcare professionals when required.
During our inspection, a visiting healthcare professional commented: “I think the care is really good.
We receive referrals about health needs, and the staff here follow our advice.” All healthcare
appointments are recorded, and medical correspondence is securely stored. Support with
medication is available, with medicines stored securely and administered in line with prescriber
instructions. Nurses and care staff receive appropriate training and have their competency
assessed. Monthly medication audits are undertaken to maintain safe and effective practice.
However, we identified some areas of medication management that require further strengthening.
This was discussed with the provider, who assured us corrective action will be taken.

People’s rights and freedoms are protected in line with legal requirements. Where people are
unable to make decisions about their care, the service ensures appropriate authorisations are in
place under the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS). Staff receive training to understand their
responsibilities and uphold these safeguards. We identified personal plans need to include more
detail about the conditions attached to DoLS authorisations. The provider has confirmed
improvements will be made.

Staff are trained to implement effective infection prevention and control measures. Infection control
training forms a core component of the service’s mandatory training programme, supported by a
comprehensive policy that promotes best practice. During our inspection, we conducted a visual
review of the environment and observed staff wearing appropriate levels of personal protective
equipment (PPE) while delivering care and support. There is a plentiful supply of PPE on-site, and
it is readily accessible for staff use.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a clean, comfortable, and homely environment. The home is undergoing a major
refurbishment, with communal areas, bedrooms, and bathroom facilities being systematically
upgraded. Admissions have been temporarily suspended while these works are completed. Our
visual inspection confirmed there are sufficient communal spaces for socialising and activities.
People can personalise their rooms with their own belongings to create a sense of home. The
kitchen holds a Food Standards Agency rating of five, reflecting very good hygiene standards.
Domestic staff are on-site daily to maintain cleanliness. Outdoor areas include a large garden and
an accessible patio with seating, both of which are being improved as part of the refurbishment.
There are adequate bathroom facilities and specialist moving and handling equipment for those
who need it. A lift provides access to upper floors for people with mobility needs. Laundry facilities
are appropriate for the size of the home and include an in/out system to minimise cross-
contamination.

The service takes appropriate measures to maintain safety and security. Regular servicing,
maintenance, and repairs are carried out to ensure the well-being of people living at the home.
Health and safety audits are completed by the manager, and utilities such as electricity and gas are
serviced by accredited contractors. Specialist equipment is maintained in line with manufacturers’
guidelines. A fire risk assessment is in place, supported by routine fire safety checks and servicing
by qualified professionals. Actions identified during a recent fire service inspection are currently
being addressed.

Security measures are in place to safeguard people. The premises are secured to prevent
unauthorised access, and all visitors are required to sign in and out under staff supervision.
Confidential information is stored securely and accessed only by authorised personnel.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider is committed to maintaining a skilled and knowledgeable workforce. During our visit,
we observed appropriate staffing levels, and staff confirmed that current arrangements meet the
needs of the service. Staff receive training tailored to the needs of the people they support, and
records show most mandatory training is up to date. Staff told us they feel supported in their roles
and benefit from regular supervision. However, we found some staff had not received supervision
at the required three-month intervals. The provider has assured us that this will be addressed.

The service follows a robust recruitment process to ensure staff are suitably qualified and
trustworthy. All required pre-employment checks are completed, including references, employment
history verification, and Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks. Nurses are registered with
the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC), and care staff are registered with Social Care Wales
(SCW). New staff complete a structured induction programme to familiarise them with their roles
and the specific needs of people living at the service. We noted that some DBS certificates had
recently expired; however, the provider has assured us this will be addressed promptly.

The provider has appropriate governance and quality assurance systems in place to ensure the
service runs smoothly. The Responsible Individual (RI) maintains oversight of operations and
engages with people using the service and staff during visits. The manager completes regular
audits covering key areas such as health and safety, infection control, and medication
management, helping to identify issues and take timely action. We noted some auditing processes,
such as medication audits, need strengthening to clearly record actions taken when discrepancies
are identified. The provider has assured us this will be addressed. In addition, an area manager
provides an extra layer of quality assurance through regular visits, reviewing the environment,
staffing, and the experiences of people living at the service. The provider has policies and
procedures in place promoting safe practice within the service. Staff are aware of how to access
these documents when required. We noted some policies require some minor adjustments to
ensure they are suitably robust and aligned with best practice.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
