Summary:

 Parklands is situated in the village of Bedwas, near Caerphilly. It is a purpose-built care home
 offering both general residential and nursing care. The service aims to provide a family
 atmosphere, encouraging people, staff, and relatives to plan and celebrate special occasions
 together.

 The wellbeing of people living at Parklands is good. People reported feeling safe, settled, and
 respected. Relationships, including those with family members and partners, are supported, and
 people are encouraged to remain active and take part in meaningful activities.

 The care and support people receive is good. People are supported through person-centred care
 that reflects their individual needs, preferences, and desired outcomes. Care plans and risk
 assessments are reviewed regularly, with involvement from people and their families.

 The environment is also good. The home is clean, well maintained, and welcoming. Indoor and
 outdoor spaces promote comfort, privacy, and social interaction. The home has a range of facilities

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 The wellbeing of people living at Parklands Care Home is good. People spoken with described
 feeling safe, secure, and at home. People have access to several communal areas where they can
 spend time with others and their loved ones. Relationships are respected and supported, including
 enabling a married couple to continue living together despite having differing care needs.

 People are supported to maintain meaningful activities, community connections, and personal
 interests, such as walking in the community, gardening, and creative activities. A wellbeing
 coordinator supports people to remain active and continue with hobbies. A self-employed
 hairdresser visits the home twice a week, enabling people to maintain regular haircuts and styles,
 including colouring and perms.

 Relatives spoke positively about staff knowing people well and responding appropriately to
 changing needs. One relative commented, “They know my mum very well. We are here all the time,
 so we know exactly what they do, and they are really good.” Feedback from relatives indicated that
 people are treated with kindness and respect, and that staff make efforts to promote independence
 within individuals’ abilities.

 Examples of staff going above and beyond were shared, including a member of staff using their
 own time to support a person to access the community on a weekly basis. This was reported to
 have had a significant positive impact on the person’s wellbeing. One person said, “I absolutely
 love going to bingo, and without her I wouldn’t be able to go.”

 Observations during the inspection showed people appearing relaxed, appropriately dressed, and
 comfortable. Interactions between staff and people using the service were caring and respectful.
 People are supported to take appropriate risks where possible, and risk assessments are in place
 to support this. People are treated with dignity and respect and are encouraged to identify their
 wellbeing outcomes and build on their strengths.

 People are offered choice in their daily lives and have opportunities to reach their potential and
 engage in meaningful activities, such as choosing meals, taking part in hobbies, engaging in
 community activities where appropriate. Communication needs are suitably met to ensure people
 and staff can understand each other. This includes meeting people’s language preferences.
 Parklands provides reading materials in Welsh, English, and Arabic. Planned activities and
 spontaneous interactions with staff and others help people maintain a positive sense of wellbeing.

       Care & Support                       Good



The care and support people receive at Parklands Care Home is good. Case tracking
demonstrated that personal plans generally reflect people’s individual needs, life histories, risks,
and desired outcomes. Records showed evidence of regular reviews of care plans, risk
assessments, and monitoring documentation, which supports continuity of care and
responsiveness to people’s changing needs. People and their families are involved in reviewing
personal plans where appropriate and understand the importance of this in ensuring care remains
relevant and person centred.

Where people had complex or changing needs, there was clear evidence of multi-disciplinary
involvement, including timely referrals to health professionals such as GPs, district nurses, and
specialist services. This ensured people received the right care at the right time and helped to
prevent unnecessary deterioration. Care is delivered in a way that promotes routine and structure,
supporting emotional wellbeing while respecting individual preferences and choices.

Medication management at the service was found to be safe and effective. Medication records
reviewed were accurate, complete, and up to date. Medicines were stored securely, administered
as prescribed, and subject to regular audit. Controlled drugs were managed appropriately, with
clear and accurate records maintained. Room and fridge temperatures were monitored and
recorded consistently to ensure medicines were stored within safe temperature ranges. People
receive their medication in a person-centred way, taking into account their preferences,
independence, and level of support required.

The provider ensures people have access to a nutritious and balanced diet. Menus offer choice,
and people are supported to make decisions about what they eat. Specialist dietary requirements,
including those related to medical need, culture, ethics, and religion, are routinely catered for.

People feel secure and protected, and there is evidence that staff understand and act in
accordance with safeguarding responsibilities. Staff spoken with demonstrated awareness of
safeguarding procedures and were clear about how to raise concerns appropriately. People are
supported by skilled and competent staff to achieve their personal outcomes using the least
restrictive approaches possible, promoting dignity, independence, and wellbeing.

       Environment                         Good



The environment people live in at Parklands Care Home is good and supports people’s safety,
comfort, and wellbeing. The premises are well maintained to a good standard, and there is a
dedicated member of staff responsible for maintenance and repairs who takes clear pride in the
appearance and upkeep of the home. This contributes positively to the overall atmosphere and
helps ensure the environment remains safe, comfortable, and welcoming.

Communal spaces meet the diverse needs of people living at the service, promoting independence,
choice, and social interaction. There are several communal areas available, enabling people to
spend time with others, take part in activities, or enjoy quiet time as they wish. These spaces also
provide opportunities for private meetings with family members and loved ones, and are arranged
to be accessible, comfortable, and welcoming for people with varying levels of mobility and
individual need.

The home was observed to be clean, tidy, and well organised throughout the inspection. Bedrooms
were suitably furnished and personalised with people’s own belongings, helping to create a sense
of familiarity and comfort. This personalisation supports people’s identity and emotional wellbeing.
There are multiple communal areas that cater for both small group and larger group activities,
allowing people to choose how and where they spend their time.

The dining area is arranged to support social interaction, allowing people to sit together and
engage in conversation during mealtimes. Staff are encouraged to sit and eat with people, which
helps promote a relaxed and inclusive atmosphere. In addition, there is a small coffee-shop-style
room that provides a more informal and private space. This is used by people who may not feel
comfortable leaving the home but wish to socialise with family and friends in a quieter setting.

Outdoor spaces are safe, attractive, and accessible, supporting people with physical, sensory, and
cognitive impairments. The home benefits from a well-kept garden with seating areas and a potting
bench, offering opportunities for relaxation and meaningful activity. These outdoor areas support
people’s wellbeing and enable them to enjoy fresh air and nature safely.

Safety checks, servicing records, and certificates were available and up to date, including those
relating to fire safety, equipment servicing, and environmental safety. Infection prevention and
control measures were in place and effectively implemented.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management at Parklands Care Home is good. The registered manager was
visible throughout the inspection, approachable, and knowledgeable about the people living at the
service. People, relatives, and staff knew the manager well and spoke positively about her
leadership style. One family member commented, “If we ever have a problem, we know we can go
straight to the manager – not a problem.” This reflects an open and accessible management
approach that supports trust and confidence in the service.

Staff across a range of roles described feeling supported and valued in their work. They reported
being able to raise concerns and discuss issues openly, without fear of negative consequences.
Staff confirmed they receive regular supervision, appropriate training, and opportunities for learning
and development. Professional development was evident, with some staff having progressed into
more senior roles during their time at Parklands, demonstrating a commitment to developing skills
and retaining experienced staff.

The service provider’s governance and oversight arrangements promote a positive and
compassionate culture. There is a clear focus on supporting people’s wellbeing while also ensuring
staff feel supported in their roles. Staff spoken with demonstrated a good understanding of their
responsibilities and the values of the service.

People and relatives reported feeling confident that any concerns or complaints would be taken
seriously, investigated thoroughly, and responded to promptly. They described feeling comfortable
raising issues and were reassured that they would not experience any negative repercussions for
doing so. This contributes to people feeling safe and listened to.

Effective quality monitoring systems are in place to support continuous improvement. These
include regular audits of care and support, monitoring incidents and accidents. Senior leaders
regularly visit the service and collect feedback from people using the service, relatives, staff, and
visiting professionals. Information gathered through these systems is reviewed and used to identify
areas for improvement and maintain standards of care.

Governance arrangements include oversight by the Responsible Individual, ensuring accountability
at provider level. Recruitment records sampled during the inspection were complete and
demonstrated safe recruitment practices, including appropriate checks. Staff demonstrated a clear
understanding of safeguarding procedures and were able to explain who to report concerns to and
how these would be managed.

Leadership arrangements at Parklands support consistency, accountability, and a shared
commitment to delivering safe, high-quality, and person-centred care.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
