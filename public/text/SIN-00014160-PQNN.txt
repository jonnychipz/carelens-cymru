Summary
Young people are supported to be independent and achieve their goals and aspirations.
Assessments are carried out to ensure their needs can be met prior to offering a placement
and is kept under ongoing review. Young people are supported with their learning and
development and attend an education provision at Coleg Plas Dwbl. They have
opportunities to engage in a range of activities which are outcome focused and promote
their independent living skills. Young people are supported with their health needs and have
access to specialist services such as occupational health and speech and language
therapists.

The manager provides supervision to a core care staff team who are committed to their role
in supporting the needs of young people. The Responsible Individual (RI) provides support
to the manager and visits the service regularly. Quality assurance systems are in place
which monitor the quality of care being delivered.

The service has two houses on site and are decorated to a high quality. The homes provide
a holistic space for young people which is suitable to meet their needs. Externally there are
large grounds for young people to spend time on nice days and areas where they can
engage in outdoor activities.

--- PAGE 4 ---
Well-being
Young people are consulted about their care and support, and their voices are being heard.
Weekly meetings take place which enable them to express their views and wishes and
make decisions about their day to day lives. Weekly planners are developed with
assistance from care staff and provide choices for meaningful activities they enjoy within
the home and out in the community.

Young people are supported to grow their own fruit and vegetables at home and in college.
They make choices about what meals they would like to eat and develop menus which
reflect their likes and dislikes. Homegrown produce is used when cooking meals and they
are encouraged to live healthy lifestyles.

Young people attend Coleg Plas Dwbl for their education and have opportunities to develop
independent living skills which include horticulture, crafting, and woodworking. Suitable
arrangements are in place to support young people to attend the college and positive lines
of communication are held between the college and the service.

Young people are supported with their physical health needs and are registered with local
health services. They are supported by care staff to attend regular appointments with GP’s,
opticians, dentists and specialist health services.

Medication is stored appropriately, and effective recording and monitoring systems are in
place. Care staff are suitably trained in the handling and administration of medication.

Safeguarding recording and monitoring systems are in place to safeguard young people. A
weekly safeguarding meeting is attended by college and care managers to monitor and
discuss student’s welfare and any possible concerns. Young people have access to
guidance on how to raise concerns and are encouraged to voice their worries with care staff
when they need to.

Young people are enabled to maintain positive relationships with people who are important
to them and is supportive of their emotional well-being. They socialise with friends at
college, youth clubs and discos and have a sense of community presence. Family members
are actively involved in the care and support needs of young people and contact is
promoted within the home.

--- PAGE 5 ---
Care and Support


Young people’s care and support needs are suitably assessed prior to offering a placement.
A transition system provides young people an opportunity to visit the service and build
positive relations with peers and care staff before moving to the home. Risks and
compatibility are considered, and a rationale on how the service can be delivered which
keeps young people safe and live in a positive environment.

Young people have access to a guide which is suitable for the needs and understanding of
each person. Information to the provision of the service is included, local amenities and
leisure activities. A complaints procedure outlines how young people can raise concerns
and contact information is available.

Personal plans are developed alongside young people and provide guidance to care staff
on how they would achieve personal outcomes. We saw a range of different documents
outlining young people’s goals and aspirations which inform how they would like their care
and support to be delivered. Information relating to health needs, cultural beliefs and people
involved in their care is available. It is unclear from documents reviewed the extent to which
parents and social workers are involved in the development of personal plans.

Positive behaviour plans outline young people’s likes and dislikes and provide guidance on
how they would like to maintain a positive sense of well-being. Restrictive intervention
information is available however, further developments and clarity is needed within
documentation.

Young people are encouraged to take positive risks when engaging in tasks or activities.
Assessments are undertaken which provide guidance to care staff on how to keep them
safe.

Care staff present as kind, caring and respectful. We saw positive interactions with young
people and communication methods used to suit their needs.

--- PAGE 6 ---
 Environment
The service consists of two homes in large rural grounds overlooking the countryside. The
houses present as cosy, warm and well furnished. Both properties are homely and provide
an environment which supports young people’s overall well-being. Young people have
access to various living areas where they can enjoy some quiet time or play games with
peers or care staff.

Bedrooms are warm, spacious and decorated to the wishes of young people. Photographs
of people who are important to them and artwork are located on the walls. Suitable storage
has been provided for young people for clothing items. Bedrooms do not have en-suites
however, designated bathrooms to carry out personal care are available.

Kitchen areas are clean, and cupboards and fridges are well stocked. Suitable equipment is
available for young people to make meals. All temperatures on fridges were recorded
consistently and a daily task sheet had been completed by care staff.

Outside provides opportunities for young people to engage with outdoor activities. Large
grounds where they can access walk paths, grow vegetables and engage in woodworking.
Care staff encourage young people to be outside and lead healthy lifestyles.

Young people have Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEP’s) and provide guidance
to care staff on how to support them during an emergency.

Health and safety is monitored and maintenance carried out when necessary. COSHH
items were secure and locked away.

The service provider has a visitor’s book, and we were asked to sign in and our
identification verified on arrival.

Leadership and Management
The statement of purpose (SOP) provides detailed information in relation to the provision of
the service and education provided by the college. The service is working towards the
Welsh active offer and provides information to support young people to learn the Welsh
language and its culture.

The service provider has robust quality of care systems. Processes are in place which
monitor, review, improve the quality of care, and focus on driving improvement for young
people.

The RI carries out regular visits and provides oversight and governance of the service.
Analysis is carried out in relation to staffing, standards of care and support and any
concerns raised. Consultation with young people and care staff are carried out and any
actions considered to support their views.

The manager and care staff receive regular supervision discussions include personal
development and training needs. From records reviewed a suitable probationary review
system is in place and a focus on providing care staff with skills relevant to carry out their
role effectively was evident.

Care staff are suitably qualified or are working towards their health and social care
qualification. They undertake mandatory training, and most are up to date. Care staff told us
they felt the training provided allows them to carry out their role effectively.

An effective rota system is in place which reflects suitable staffing levels for the care needs
of young people. Shortfalls are covered by care staff and the manager which provides
consistency and continuity for young people.

Team meetings are carried out regularly and are suitably attended by care staff. They
provide a good insight into recent developments of young people and provide actions care
staff need to take to improve the delivery of care.
