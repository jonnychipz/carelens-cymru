Summary:

 Ty Eirin is a purpose-built care home in Tonyrefail providing nursing and residential care for up to
 90 people.

 People experience excellent well-being outcomes. Staff know people extremely well and provide
 compassionate, respectful support that promotes dignity, choice, independence and rights. People
 maintain relationships with family and friends and enjoy a wide range of meaningful activities.

 People receive excellent care and support from a dedicated and highly skilled staff team.
 Comprehensive assessments and detailed personal plans help ensure support is personalised and
 reflects what matters most to people. Staff respond promptly to people's needs and work closely
 with healthcare professionals to promote positive outcomes.

 The environment is excellent. The home is welcoming, safe and accessible, with a variety of
 attractive communal and outdoor areas that support social interaction, independence and well-
 being. Bedrooms are comfortable, personalised and homely, helping people feel settled and
 secure.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being outcomes because they are treated with dignity and respect
 and have meaningful control over their daily lives. People are encouraged to make choices where
 they could and told us staff are kind and respectful. They can choose when to get up, go to bed,
 receive visitors and participate in activities. Regular resident/relative meetings take place, whereby
 people can have a say in the running of the home. People are supported to maintain good nutrition
 and hydration. We observed a positive lunchtime experience where people were supported in a
 calm and respectful manner. Meals are varied, nutritious and presented attractively, including
 modified meals where required.

 People benefit from extensive opportunities to take part in meaningful activities and maintain social
 connections. The service employs three activity coordinators and provides a varied programme of
 activities, including reminiscence sessions, exercise classes, community outings and one-to-one
 support. Innovative initiatives such as the Dementia Café, resident advocates and the “Well of
 Wishes” project help people remain engaged, valued and connected to those who matter to them.
 Family members are actively encouraged to engage with the service through regular events and a
 family support group led by current and former relatives.

 We observed warm, respectful and meaningful interactions and people appeared relaxed and
 comfortable in the presence of staff. Relatives and professionals consistently speak positively
 about the quality of care and support provided. People living in Ty Eirin told us, “The staff are
 lovely”, “Everything about the place is great, you can’t find a fault here” and “Everyone has been a
 high 5, amazing!” Staff respond promptly to requests for assistance and there are sufficient staff
 available to provide support in a calm and unhurried manner. People feel safe and know who to
 speak to if they have concerns. Information about advocacy services and how to raise concerns is
 readily available throughout the home.

 Robust safeguarding and risk management arrangements help ensure people remain safe while
 promoting independence. Care plans and risk assessments viewed were highly effective and help
 staff support people safely while promoting their independence. Specialist equipment such as
 sensor mats and call bells are available to enable people to receive the care that they need in a
 timely manner. Incidents and accidents are recorded and, where appropriate, reported to relevant
 agencies.

 The service makes a positive effort to promote Welsh language and culture through Welsh-themed
 displays, Welsh language activities and staff who can communicate in basic Welsh.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



Staff know people exceptionally well and provide highly personalised support that reflects their
individual needs and preferences. The service undertakes detailed pre-admission assessments to
ensure it can fully meet people's needs. Prospective residents and their families are encouraged to
visit the service prior to admission.

Personal plans are person centred and provide staff with clear information about how to support
people effectively. Personal plans are reviewed monthly and people and their representatives are
actively involved where possible. Risk assessments are detailed, enabling people to make informed
choices while ensuring appropriate measures are in place to keep them safe. We spoke to people’s
relatives who were visiting on the day of our inspection, and the feedback was excellent. One told
us, “On the ground staff - first class care! The love and the care they show is amazing!”

People experience excellent continuity of care because the service has a stable staff team with low
staff turnover. We saw appointments with professionals including GPs and district nurses are
recorded, with any advice given, documented in people’s personal plans. People’s rights to liberty
are protected and safeguarded. The provider reports any concerns about people’s safety to local
safeguarding teams and makes Deprivation of Liberty Safeguarding (DoLS) referrals when there is
a risk that care arrangements may deprive them of their liberty.

People receive their medicines safely and as prescribed through a well-managed electronic
medication system. Robust arrangements are in place for the ordering, storage, administration and
monitoring of medicines. Controlled drugs are stored and administered appropriately, and clear
guidance was available for the administration of medicines prescribed on an 'as required' (PRN)
basis. Staff receive regular medication training and competency assessments to ensure they have
the knowledge and skills required to administer medicines safely. Routine audits and effective
managerial oversight promote excellent practice and help ensure medication management remains
safe, effective and person-centred. Staff use innovative pain assessment technology to support
people who may be unable to communicate when they are experiencing pain. This enables staff to
identify concerns promptly, respond effectively, and improve people's comfort and well-being.

Infection prevention and control measures are in place to protect people from the risk of cross
contamination. Highly effective daily cleaning schedules are in place, and the home is clean and
tidy throughout. Care staff receive relevant training and demonstrate a good understanding and use
personal protective equipment (PPE) which is readily available throughout the service. The service
is supported by an infection control policy, and regular infection control audits are completed to
support safe practice.

       Environment                              Excellent



People benefit from an exceptional environment that is thoughtfully designed. Providing a range of
welcoming communal areas where people can relax, socialise and participate in meaningful
activities. Themed lounges, activity areas, quiet spaces and secure outdoor areas give people
choice about how and where they spend their time. We observed people throughout the home
appearing comfortable, relaxed and engaged in their surroundings. The environment reflects a
strong understanding of supporting people living with dementia. People have access to spacious
bedrooms and are encouraged to personalise them with their own furniture, photographs and
possessions. Communal areas are attractively furnished and well maintained, while specialist
bathing facilities and equipment help ensure people's individual needs can be met safely.

People enjoy living in a clean, welcoming, and comfortable home. Dedicated housekeeping staff
help keep the environment tidy, hygienic, and well cared for. The kitchen has achieved a Food
Hygiene Rating of five, demonstrating very good standards of food hygiene and safety. The
provider demonstrates a strong commitment to maintaining a safe environment. Effective systems
are in place to monitor and maintain the premises, equipment and grounds. A rolling programme of
servicing and maintenance ensures equipment remains safe and fit for purpose. Up-to-date
certification is available for gas and electrical installations, while water systems are routinely
monitored and managed. Fire safety arrangements are robust, with regular testing of alarms,
emergency lighting and fire equipment. Each person has a Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan
(PEEP) tailored to their individual needs, and staff participate in regular fire drills to ensure they can
respond effectively in an emergency. Substances hazardous to health (COSHH) were stored
securely and appropriate laundry facilities support the safe management of linen

Measures are in place to maintain the security of the home. Visitors are required to sign in and out
electronically, helping to protect people from unauthorised access. The environment is safe,
accessible and welcoming, providing people with an excellent place to live that promotes positive
outcomes and enhances their daily lives.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from excellent leadership and management that is focused on achieving the best
possible outcomes for people. The service provider has created a strong, positive culture where
people are at the heart of the service and staff are encouraged to provide compassionate, person-
centred care. The home is led by an experienced and dedicated management team who are
visible, approachable and committed to continuous improvement. The provider demonstrates a
strong commitment to ensuring there are enough skilled, competent and knowledgeable staff
available to meet people's needs. On the day of our inspection, staffing levels were appropriate and
sufficient to meet people’s needs. Robust recruitment processes are followed to ensure staff are
suitable for their roles. We sampled staff personnel files and found the required pre-employment
checks were completed, including appropriate references and Disclosure and Barring Service
(DBS) checks. Nurses are registered with the nursing and midwifery council (NMC), which
evidences their suitability for practice. Care staff are registered with Social Care Wales (SCW) the
work force regulator.

People benefit from a staff team that is exceptionally well trained, well supported and highly
motivated. Staff told us they undertake a comprehensive programme of mandatory and specialist
training through a combination of face-to-face and e-learning opportunities. This includes
safeguarding, infection prevention and control, dementia care, stoma care and falls prevention.
Training compliance levels are excellent. Staff told us they feel valued and supported by the
management team and records confirmed they receive regular supervision, guidance and
opportunities for professional development. Staff feedback was positive, including comments such
as, “Staff enjoy working here and have really good relationships with people " and “The manager is
lovely”. Stable staffing arrangements help promote continuity of care and enable staff to develop
meaningful relationships with people.

Robust governance and quality assurance systems provide excellent oversight of the service.
Comprehensive audits are completed across all areas of service provision and are analysed in
detail to identify trends, drive improvement and maintain high standards. Feedback from people,
relatives, professionals and staff is actively sought and used to inform service development. People
and their representatives have a range of formal and informal opportunities to share their views and
contribute to the ongoing development of the home. The Responsible Individual maintains effective
oversight through regular monitoring and quality reviews. The service works openly with Care
Inspectorate Wales (CIW), local authority social services, and commissioning teams. It maintains
positive working relationships with partner agencies and meets its statutory notification
responsibilities in a timely manner.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
