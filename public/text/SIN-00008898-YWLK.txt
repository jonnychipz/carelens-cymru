Summary:

 Ashdale Nursing Home provides residential, respite and nursing care for adults. It is situated near
 the harbour town of Pembroke on the picturesque Pembrokeshire coastline.

 People experience good well-being. We found people to be safe and happy. Care staff have good
 relationships with people, relatives and professionals visiting the service. People experience good
 well-being outcomes and maintain control over many aspects of their daily lives. Care staff treat
 people with dignity and respect, encouraging them to make choices about their care and support.

 People receive good care are support. People are protected from harm and abuse. Personal plans
 are good. They provide clear guidance for staff on the care and support needs of people. People’s
 medication is safely managed.

 The environment is good. People have access to quality indoor and outdoor space which impacts
 positively on their health and wellbeing. There is clear ongoing investment into the service.

 The leadership and management are excellent. People are placed at the heart of the service by
 leaders who are visible role models. The service provider’s oversight and governance
 arrangements foster a strong positive culture which is supportive, inclusive and respectful.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. They appeared relaxed and content
 throughout the inspection. People told us, “It’s great, I like it”. We observed care staff interacting in
 a calm, sensitive and reassuring way, adapting their approach when people were confused or
 unsettled. Care staff consistently use kind and respectful language, promoting dignity and
 emotional well-being through positive, person-centred engagement. We saw people seeking
 reassurance and social interaction from care staff, reflecting positive and trusting relationships. This
 contributes to a stable and emotionally supportive environment.

 People decide when to get up, retire to bed and how they spend their day. The home provides a
 variety of spaces where people can socialise or spend time alone if they prefer.

 People’s health and well-being needs are met. They benefit from regular involvement from health
 professionals and the care staff demonstrate a clear understanding of people’s needs. People are
 protected from harm. Care staff have received training in safeguarding vulnerable people and are
 aware of their safeguarding responsibilities. Risks are understood and managed appropriately,
 enabling people to be supported safely while maintaining as much independence as possible.

 Care staff support people to remain active and engaged. The service employs an activities
 organiser who organises a range of meaningful activities. These include singing, quizzes, history
 presentations, therapy dogs, a spa and well-being day. These opportunities contribute positively to
 the well-being of the people, improving their quality of life and helps them to achieve their well-
 being outcomes.

 People maintain relationships with those important to them. Family members and friends can visit
 freely and care staff create a welcoming atmosphere for them. During the inspection, we observed
 positive interactions between care staff and people living at the home. People socialise comfortably
 with care staff and each other throughout the day.

 The environment makes a significant contribution to people’s well-being. Bedrooms are
 personalised and reflect individual preferences. The service remains exceptionally clean, homely
 and well maintained.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are protected from harm and abuse. People are supported by highly skilled staff with an
excellent understanding of their individual needs, preferences and their responsibilities around
safeguarding. We saw a motivated, experienced team who were keen to make a significant
difference to people’s lives. A relative told us, “Staff engage well with patients [people] and their
families to identify needs and deliver individual care.” There are thorough risk assessments in
place. The service provides effective safeguarding training. Policy and procedures are in place and
these are followed by the care staff.

Care records are comprehensive, person-centre and accurately reflect people’s current care and
support needs. However, we did not see much evidence supporting the achievement of well-being
outcomes. The provider told us that they would review this. This reassured us.

People told us they are happy with the care and support they receive. This was supported by
feedback obtained from visiting professionals. One professional said, “Every time I have visited the
home I've consciously noted, the overall standard of the public areas, which is always improving,
the staff are always helpful and courteous. When I've had to wait in a public area, I've witnessed
the staff supporting residents.”

People receive medication in a safe, person-centred way considering choice, individuality and best
practice guidelines. There are good clear medication management policies and procedures.
Medication management arrangements are effective. Care staff administer medicines safely and
confidently, in accordance with current guidance and best practice. There is thorough and regular
medication training for care staff. The service stores medicines securely in a designated medication
room and monitors storage conditions appropriately.

People’s risk of infection is minimised. The service has a dedicated domestic team which maintain
the home to a good standard, keeping the environment immaculately clean. There are consistent
infection, prevention and control measures in place. There are good supplies of personal protective
equipment (PPE) made available throughout the service. These are used by care staff. Care staff
are trained and there is an effective policy in place which care staff were aware of. We saw a good
supply of cleaning products.

People receive a nutritious and varied diet. Catering staff prepare most meals using fresh
ingredients and offer choices at mealtimes. The service accommodates specialist dietary
requirements. “Its good [the food], nice.” Care staff understand the importance of promoting
hydration.

       Environment                         Good



The home is a purpose-built building, with accommodation and office space across two floors.
People can choose from a range of communal areas. The home includes several lounges and a
well-appointed dining room. There is a secure garden and patio area to the rear of the premises.
High-quality furnishings and attention to detail create a comfortable and homely atmosphere
throughout the service.

The communal spaces meet the needs of people, promoting independence and providing
opportunities for private meetings, activities, and recreation. There is a vibrant and inclusive
atmosphere at the home where visitors are always made to feel welcome.

We saw a clean and well-maintained kitchen which holds a five-star food hygiene rating. The
kitchen was well stocked with fresh local produce. We were told. “Food is good here. I get to
choose what I want.”

The environment reflects people’s needs and preferences and supports their independence.
Outdoor areas include seating and a patio, providing opportunities for relaxation and meaningful
activity.

Specialist equipment for people is checked and routinely serviced. Comprehensive health and
safety audits are completed to help identify and action any issues with the environment. There is
routine servicing of utilities such as electricity and gas which is carried out by external contractors.
There are strong fire safety measures including a recent fire risk assessment carried out by suitably
qualified person. Any remedial actions are being addressed. All people living at the service have a
personal emergency evacuation plan (PEEPs) and routine fire drills are completed to make sure
people can be evacuated safely, if needed.

The provider implements an effective maintenance programme. Contractors service equipment,
including hoists and slings, within required timescales. Fire safety arrangements are well managed.
Care staff complete regular fire alarm tests and fire drills, maintain fire-fighting equipment
appropriately and follow a current fire risk assessment.

People benefit from a warm, clean, comfortable, welcoming environment with continuing
investment by the provider to improve their well-being. The provider invests consistently into the
service. We saw new furniture throughout the building and the decoration in communal and private
spaces was good.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



The leadership and management are a significant strength of the service. People are supported to
achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective organisational arrangements,
governance and oversight to ensure smooth operations and high-quality care. The leadership and
management team are very accessible and are routinely assisting care staff. People achieve good
outcomes because the provider has a very strong commitment to ensuring high numbers of
extremely skilled and knowledgeable staff are always in the service. There are very consistent
staffing levels across the service at all times of day and night.

People benefit from a positive culture which is supportive, inclusive, and respectful. Leaders are
visible role models as they guide the strategic direction of the service. The responsible individual
(RI) is accessible and well known to people, care staff, visiting relatives and professionals. They
have effective governance arrangements and maintain oversight of the service.

People are supported by care staff with the necessary expertise, skills and qualifications to meet
people’s care and support needs. People are empowered through the support of trained, skilled
and motivated care staff to achieve their self-directed outcomes. Care staff have access to good
quality training and regular supervision to ensure they continue to be equipped to undertake their
roles.

There is a very stable staff team with low turnover ensuring people have consistency and can
develop trusting relationships. The provider regularly checks and adjusts the staffing levels and
skills mix to keep up with the changing needs of the people they support.

The service provider has strict selection and vetting processes for recruiting care staff to ensure all
their people are qualified and trustworthy. We saw all care staff undergo routine and regular checks
to ensure they remain suitably fit to work in the service and are appropriately registered with
professional bodies. We found care staff files to be a very high standard and well organised with all
the appropriate information made easily available for us to view.

Managers provide care staff with regular supervision and annual appraisals, helping them to reflect
on practice and develop their skills.

Overall, Leadership and management is excellent because people have high levels of confidence
in the service leadership. There is a strong positive culture. Leaders are visible, promote that
culture and initiate comprehensive audits of all aspects of the service to monitor practice and
ensure excellent outcomes.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
