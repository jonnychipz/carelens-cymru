Summary:

 People experience good well-being at Plas Y Don. Care staff create a warm, welcoming
 atmosphere and develop positive, respectful relationships with people. Individuals feel listened to
 and valued, and they contribute to decisions through meetings and everyday choices. People take
 part in meaningful activities and maintain connections with family, friends and the wider community.


 People receive good care and support that is person centred and responsive. Care staff use clear,
 detailed care plans and review them regularly. They involve health professionals to support
 physical and mental health. Care staff assess and manage risks effectively and follow safeguarding
 and medication procedures that promote safe, consistent practice.


 People live in an environment that is clean, comfortable and homely. Communal areas are
 welcoming, well maintained and support social interaction. Facilities meet people’s needs and
 enable independence in daily routines. Indoor and outdoor spaces provide pleasant surroundings
 where people can relax, spend time with others and enjoy a sense of familiarity and security.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People living at Plas Y Don experience good well-being. The service provides a warm, friendly and
 welcoming environment where people feel safe, valued and respected. Care staff interact with
 people in a kind, patient and considerate manner, creating a calm and positive atmosphere
 throughout the home. Residents appear comfortable and at ease, and visitors receive a warm
 welcome, supporting people to maintain important relationships with family and friends.

 The service actively promotes the Welsh language and meets the requirements of the Active Offer.
 Most care staff speak Welsh and support people to communicate in their preferred language. This
 strengthens people’s sense of identity, belonging and cultural connection, ensuring they feel
 understood and respected in daily interactions.

 People build trusting relationships with care staff who understand their needs and preferences.
 Care staff provide reassurance, encouragement and companionship throughout the day. People
 feel confident approaching care staff for support or conversation, which strengthens their sense of
 security and promotes emotional well-being. People have opportunities to express their views and
 influence decisions about their daily lives. Residents’ meetings take place regularly and provide a
 forum for discussion about food, activities and routines. Care staff listen carefully and act on
 feedback, ensuring people remain involved in shaping the service.

 People take part in a wide range of enjoyable and purposeful activities, including live music,
 singing, crafts and group events. Cultural and religious celebrations are recognised and valued.
 People also access their local community through visits and outings, which support social
 interaction and maintain links with familiar places. A mobile shop visits the home, supporting
 independence and enabling people to choose items for themselves.

 The service supports personal choice and dignity through access to hairdressing. A hairdresser
 visits regularly, and some people choose to arrange their own appointments and use the on-site
 salon. This supports individuality and promotes well-being. The service continues to expand
 opportunities that promote inclusion and emotional well-being. People stay involved in daily life and
 develop a sense of purpose through positive interactions and shared experiences within the home
 and wider community. Care Staff promote choice and independence in everyday living, enabling
 people to maintain personal routines and preferences. Managers take a proactive approach to
 enhancing well-being. They seek feedback, strengthen community links and introduce new
 opportunities for engagement. Care staff encourage participation, nurture relationships and
 celebrate achievements, supporting people to enjoy fulfilling, active and enriching lives.

       Care & Support                       Good




People living at Plas Y Don receive good care and support that meets their needs and promotes
their safety, dignity and independence. The service completes thorough pre-placement
assessments to ensure it can meet people’s needs before admission. These assessments consider
physical health, mobility, emotional well-being and personal preferences, supporting informed and
appropriate decisions about placement. Care staff deliver person-centred care based on detailed
care plans that reflect individual preferences, life histories and “what matters” information. Care
plans remain current and are reviewed regularly to reflect changing needs. Care staff monitor
people’s physical and mental health and work closely with health professionals, including GPs,
community nurses and occupational therapists, to ensure co-ordinated and responsive care.

The service uses appropriate legal frameworks, including Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards, to
support people safely. Care staff complete risk assessments and manage identified risks
effectively, enabling people to maintain independence where possible. Care staff use equipment
confidently and safely, supported by relevant training and guidance. Care staff monitor weight,
hydration and overall well-being and respond promptly to any changes. They support people in a
consistent and respectful manner, working with professionals to understand individual needs and
promote positive outcomes.

Medication systems support safe and effective practice. Care staff complete training and
competency assessments, and regular checks ensure medicines are administered correctly.
Safeguarding procedures are well understood, and care staff act promptly to protect people from
harm.

Managers take a proactive approach to maintaining care quality. They review care records, support
care staff and work closely with partner agencies to ensure care remains responsive and effective.
This approach promotes consistency and continuous improvement. One relative told us, “The staff
know my relative well and always take the time to support them properly.” Care staff understand
people as individuals and provide care that promotes comfort, respect and reassurance. Effective
communication between care staff supports continuity of care, and structured handovers ensure
important information is shared clearly, enabling care staff to respond consistently and safely to
people’s needs each day. They observe changes in presentation and respond promptly, seeking
additional support when required. Care staff maintain clear records and use this information to plan
and deliver care effectively. They encourage independence and dignity while providing appropriate
support, ensuring people feel secure and confident in their daily lives within the service every day
and continue to promote positive outcomes for everyone receiving care.

       Environment                         Good




People living at Plas Y Don benefit from an environment that is clean, safe and comfortable,
supporting their well-being and daily routines. The home provides a warm, welcoming and homely
atmosphere, with communal areas arranged to promote relaxation and social interaction. Seating
areas are comfortable and positioned to encourage conversation, enabling people to spend time
together or enjoy quieter spaces according to their preferences. The conservatory offers a bright
and pleasant environment where people can sit, relax and enjoy views of the garden. Décor reflects
a strong sense of identity and place, with local photographs, familiar items and bilingual materials
supporting connection to the community and culture.

Recently completed painting and decorating throughout the home enhances the overall
appearance and maintains a fresh, well-presented environment. Displays of activities and events
show an active setting and reflect how people remain engaged in daily life. Facilities meet people’s
needs effectively. The kitchen is clean, well-organised and maintained to a high standard,
supported by a food standards agency rating of five (very good). The hairdressing salon provides
an authentic and positive experience, promoting dignity, independence and personal choice. A
mobile shop is available within the home, enabling people to browse and select personal items,
supporting independence and enhancing daily living.

Outdoor areas are tidy and accessible, with seating available for people to enjoy fresh air and
spend time outside. Garden spaces, including raised beds and a potting shed, provide
opportunities for participation in gardening and outdoor activity.

Health and safety arrangements are strong and well managed. Regular checks of equipment,
including electrical testing, ensure safety for people and staff. Fire safety systems are in place and
maintained, including routine testing of alarms and emergency lighting, supporting a safe
environment.

Care staff maintain high standards of cleanliness and ensure spaces remain uncluttered and easy
to navigate, supporting independence and safety. Bedrooms provide private areas that people
personalise, promoting comfort, familiarity and ownership. Managers take a proactive approach to
maintaining and improving the environment. They identify areas for development and plan
enhancements to maintain high standards. People move freely around the home and access
shared spaces confidently, supporting independence and participation in daily life.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management are good. The service operates in an open, transparent and proactive
way. Managers are visible within the home and create a positive culture where care staff feel
supported and valued. Governance systems are effective and support the delivery of safe and
consistent care. Regular staff meetings take place and focus on improving practice, sharing
learning and strengthening teamwork across the service. This approach supports clear
communication and ensures staff understand expectations and responsibilities.

The Responsible Individual provides effective oversight through regular visits and engagement with
the service. Reports cover key areas including care, staffing, safeguarding and health and safety,
supporting accountability and ongoing improvement.

Managers take a proactive approach to identifying opportunities for development and improvement.
They respond to feedback from people, relatives and care staff and use this information to
strengthen the service and improve outcomes. They promote reflective practice and support
learning to maintain high standards of care. Care staff receive structured induction and ongoing
training that supports their roles and responsibilities. Training is planned and monitored to ensure
care staff maintain competence and confidence in their work. Supervision provides opportunities for
discussion, guidance and development, supporting consistent practice.

Staffing levels are managed effectively to meet people’s needs and maintain continuity of care.
Managers respond to changing needs and ensure sufficient care staff are available to deliver safe
and reliable care each day. Recruitment processes support the development of a stable workforce.
Monitoring systems are in place to review the quality of the service. Audits and checks provide
assurance that standards are maintained and support continuous improvement. Findings inform
action and contribute to service development.

Managers communicate clearly with care staff and provide direction that supports safe and
effective practice. One member of the care staff told us, “There has been a lot of hard work, and
standards have improved across all areas.” The service demonstrates a strong commitment to
maintaining quality and supporting care staff to deliver positive outcomes for people. This approach
supports stability, promotes confidence and ensures the service continues to develop and improve
over time while providing safe and effective care each day for all people living at Plas Y Don.
People benefit from a service that values improvement, accountability and clear communication.
Care staff work together effectively and support one another to maintain standards. Managers
remain responsive and ensure systems operate reliably and consistently across the service to meet
standards consistently.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
