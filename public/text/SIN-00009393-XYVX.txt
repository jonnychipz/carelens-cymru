Summary:


 People living at Ty Clyd achieve good positive well-being outcomes through person-centred care,
 meaningful activities, strong healthcare support and respectful staff relationships. People receive
 good, person-centred care that promotes health, dignity and independence. Strong safeguarding
 and healthcare support contribute to positive outcomes.

 The environment is good. Promoting independence, well-being and social engagement. People
 benefit from suitable facilities, personalised living spaces and effective safety measures.
 Leadership and management arrangements support good quality care through stable leadership,
 effective monitoring and a positive staff culture.

 Greater oversight of records, training, medication competency assessments and personal plans is
 needed. Due to this, three areas for improvement were identified and issued during inspection

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People living at Ty Clyd experience good outcomes in relation to their well-being. Information
 gathered during the inspection demonstrates that staff treat people with dignity and respect and
 support them to make meaningful day-to-day choices. People participate in menu planning, choose
 where they eat their meals and influence aspects of service delivery through resident meetings and
 informal discussions. Supporting positive wellbeing for people living at the service.

 The service adopts a person-centred approach to health and well-being. Staff seek advice when
 they identify changes in an individual's presentation and make referrals to appropriate health
 professionals. Staff continue to support people appropriately whilst awaiting professional input. The
 service respond well to people's nutritional and hydration needs by providing modified diets and
 support with eating where required. People and relatives provided very positive feedback about the
 quality of food and the care provided by staff.

 The service supports people to maintain meaningful relationships and remain socially engaged.
 The service provider communicates regularly with families through newsletters, family events,
 functions and a relatives' social media communication channel. People participate in a varied
 programme of activities, including arts and crafts, exercise sessions, gardening, themed
 celebrations, music events, baking and community outings. The service encourages relatives to
 attend events and celebrations, helping people maintain important relationships. People and
 relatives consistently describe the home as welcoming and speak positively about staff interactions
 and the atmosphere within the service.

 The promotion of Welsh language and culture was observed. Staff incorporate Welsh words and
 phrases into daily routines and create visual displays that encourage people and staff to learn and
 use Welsh. This supports cultural identity, inclusion and awareness of the Welsh language.

 The service generally protects people from harm through effective safeguarding processes and
 staff awareness. Safeguarding training forms part of the induction process and ongoing supervision
 arrangements. Staff demonstrate a clear understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities and
 reporting procedures. The environment promotes an appropriate balance between safety and
 independence. Suitable measures are in place to monitor access to the service whilst enabling
 people to leave independently where appropriate.
 Further development is required to strengthen evidence of how feedback directly influences service
 improvement. Whilst the service offers a broad range of activities, some individual activity records
 remain limited and do not consistently demonstrate people's levels of engagement or outcomes
 achieved.

       Care & Support                       Good



People at Ty Clyd receive good, person-centred care and support that promotes their health,
dignity and independence. The service completes pre-admission assessments and gathers detailed
information about people's histories, preferences, routines and support needs before they move
into the home. Personal plans contain detailed assessments and one-page profiles that help staff
understand what matters to each person and how they wish to receive support.

Personal plans generally contain detailed information; however, records reviewed during the
inspection do not always reflect people's current needs and circumstances. Inconsistencies exist
between assessments, reviews and care plans in relation to allergies, behavioural changes, activity
preferences and changing health conditions. In some cases, reviews identify changes in need, but
associated personal plans are not updated to reflect these changes. As a result an area for
improvement was issued around personal plans.

Staff monitor people's health needs and arrange timely access to healthcare professionals,
including GPs, district nurses, social workers, podiatrists, community psychiatric nurses and other
specialist practitioners. Staff recognise changes in people's health, behaviour and well-being and
make appropriate referrals when concerns arise. Consistent staffing and established relationships
with healthcare professionals promote continuity of care and support positive outcomes for people.

Safeguarding arrangements help protect people from harm whilst respecting their rights and
independence. Staff identify risks, work proactively with external agencies and take appropriate
action to support people's safety and well-being. Staff complete safeguarding training and
demonstrate confidence in recognising and reporting concerns.

Medication management systems are generally effective. Staff store and record controlled drugs
appropriately, receive medication training and complete competency assessments before
administering medicines. However, inspection findings identify weaknesses in stock control
arrangements for other medication. Discrepancies in medication quantities reduce assurance that
the service maintains full accountabilities for all medicines. As a result of this an area for
improvement was issued around medication. However, the service has begun implementing
practices to improve this area.

People receive compassionate support from staff who know them well. People and relatives
describe staff as “kind, attentive and responsive”. Daily records generally reflect the care and
support staff provide. Staff demonstrate a good understanding of people's preferences, routines
and support needs, helping to ensure care is delivered in a personalised and respectful manner.

       Environment                         Good



The environment at Ty Clyd supports people's safety, comfort and well-being. The home provides a
clean, well maintained and welcoming environment that meets the assessed needs of people living
there. People and visitors describe the service as “comfortable and homely.”

The layout and design of the home promote independence, choice and social interaction. People
have access to a range of communal lounges, dining areas and outdoor spaces that encourage
participation in daily life and social engagement. People personalise their bedrooms with
photographs, furniture and personal belongings, helping to create familiar surroundings and
maintain a sense of ownership and identity. Outdoor areas, including gardens and walkways with
seating, provide opportunities for relaxation and supports people's well-being.

People have access to equipment that supports their safety, mobility and independence. The
service provides appropriate aids, including hoists, stand aids, mobility equipment, call systems,
bed sensors and evacuation equipment. Staff maintain Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans
(PEEPs) and review them regularly to ensure they reflect people's current needs. Secure
arrangements are in place for the storage of medicines, hazardous substances and confidential
information, helping to protect people and maintain safety within the home.

The service demonstrates positive health and safety practices. Fire safety equipment receives
regular servicing and staff carry out fire drills to support emergency preparedness. Ongoing
maintenance helps ensure the premises remains safe and suitable. People and their families report
feeling safe within the environment and spoke positively about the quality of accommodation and
facilities. Recent investment in improvements, including new carpeting and refurbishment of
communal areas, further enhances the living environment.

The service continues to improve. There are plans to further develop outdoor facilities, including a
more permanent sheltered outdoor area. Managers complete routine environmental checks, but
records do not always clearly evidence what has been inspected, what actions have been identified
and whether these actions have been completed.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management arrangements at Ty Clyd support the delivery of good quality care
and promote regulatory compliance. The service benefits from a stable management structure.
Staff spoke positively about leaders, describing them as “approachable, supportive and
responsive.” This creates a positive culture and provides staff with clear direction and support in
their roles.

Managers use a range of systems to monitor service delivery, including training, supervision,
incidents, accidents, complaints and medication management. They review accidents and incidents
regularly, identify trends and take action to reduce risks and improve outcomes for people. Staff
attend regular team meetings, including care and catering meetings, which support communication
and service development. Structured supervision arrangements are in place, with records
maintained for probationary and ongoing supervision. Recruitment practices generally meet
regulatory requirements and include identity verification, references, Disclosure and Barring
Service (DBS) checks and induction processes.

During the inspection, managers demonstrated a clear commitment to learning and improvement.
Following inspection discussions, they introduced enhanced monitoring tools to strengthen
oversight of personal plans and medication management. These actions reflect a proactive
approach to addressing areas for development.

The providers policies and procedures are appropriate to the needs of the people supported by the
service.

The provider seeks to gather information from a range of sources and continue to develop methods
of gathering this information. Such as coffee mornings and activities.

Governance arrangements do not always provide sufficient assurance that systems operate
effectively across all areas of the service. Inspection findings identified gaps within some staff files.
Training records do not consistently highlight refresher requirements, reducing management
oversight of compliance. In addition, some medication competency assessments require renewal.
Further inaccuracies were found within people’s personal plans. Due to this an area for
improvement was issued around oversight.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
