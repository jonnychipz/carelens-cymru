Summary:

 Zoar is a residential home in Tonypandy, providing care and support for up to 30 people.


 People experience good well-being outcomes and are supported by care staff who know them well
 and promote their dignity, choice and independence. Feedback from people and their
 representatives is positive, with many describing the service as caring, welcoming and supportive.

 Care and support is good. People receive personalised care from care staff who understand their
 needs, preferences and routines. The service benefits from detailed care planning systems,
 effective safeguarding arrangements and good systems for managing medication and infection
 prevention and control.

 The environment is good. People live in a clean, comfortable and personalised home with access

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People achieve positive well-being outcomes because they are supported by staff who know them
 well and respect their choices, preferences and routines. People have control over many aspects of
 their daily lives, including when they get up and go to bed, where they spend their time, how they
 structure their day and the support they receive. People told us they are happy living at the service
 and feel “well looked after”. Relatives expressed confidence in the quality of support provided and
 told us that “everything works well”. We observed warm and respectful interactions throughout the
 inspection. Care staff responded promptly to requests and supported people to make day-to-day
 decisions, helping them maintain their independence, exercise choice and remain in control of their
 lives.


 People are supported to maintain their interests and take part in meaningful activities. We saw
 evidence of a varied programme of activities and community outings in the service's magazine and
 social media. People spoke positively about previous trips and looked forward to future
 opportunities. A professional told us activities “appear to have a very positive impact on resident
 well-being and quality of life”. Community links are maintained and we observed family and friends
 visiting during the inspection. We were told staff “always have time to talk to the family”, supporting
 positive relationships between people and those important to them. Families told us they feel their
 relatives are safe living at the home. There are effective systems to protect people from abuse and
 neglect, concerns are appropriately reported to relevant agencies and care staff demonstrated an
 understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities and confidence in raising concerns with
 managers.


 People were comfortable, settled and received support when needed. We observed staff treating
 people with kindness and responding appropriately to requests for assistance. Whilst outcomes for
 people are good overall, there are opportunities to further enhance day-to-day experiences. During
 parts of the inspection, some staff interactions were focused on completing care tasks rather than
 maximising opportunities for meaningful engagement. We observed limited staff-initiated interaction
 in communal areas at times, and mealtimes did not always maximise opportunities for
 conversation, social engagement and encouragement. The provider recognised these findings and
 acknowledged that further developing a consistent social and relationship-centred approach
 throughout the day would enhance people's well-being and experience of living at the home.


 The accommodation is suitable to meet people's needs and everyone is encouraged to personalise
 their bedrooms. The home provides suitable communal and private spaces where people can

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive good quality care and support that helps them achieve their personal outcomes.
Families told us they are happy with the care provided and described support as “personalised care
that understands individual needs”. The service benefits from a well-organised electronic care
planning system. Personal plans contain detailed information about people's needs, preferences,
routines and desired outcomes and link effectively to associated assessments and risk
management plans. There is a robust pre-admission process that helps ensure the service can
meet people's needs before they move in. Records include information about what matters to
people, helping staff provide consistent and person-centred support. We found care records are
regularly reviewed and updated, ensuring care remains responsive when people's needs change.
Risk assessments provide staff with clear guidance on how to support people safely whilst
promoting their independence.


People receive support to maintain their health and well-being. Records demonstrate appropriate
involvement from a range of healthcare professionals, including GPs, district nurses and specialist
services where required. We saw evidence of timely referrals when people's needs change and
healthcare recommendations being incorporated into care planning arrangements. Care staff
monitor changes in people's health and well-being and seek professional advice when required.
Effective communication between staff, families and professionals helps ensure people receive
coordinated care that promotes positive outcomes.


People are protected by appropriate safeguarding arrangements. Care staff demonstrate a good
understanding of their safeguarding responsibilities and relatives told us they would feel confident
raising concerns if required. Records show the service works with partner agencies when concerns
arise and takes appropriate action to help keep people safe. People's rights are promoted through
the use of legal safeguards. We saw evidence of Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards applications
and authorisations being monitored to ensure any restrictions are lawful, proportionate and
regularly reviewed.


Medication management arrangements are good overall. Effective systems are in place for the
ordering, storage, administration and auditing  of medicines.  Infection prevention and control
arrangements are well managed and help reduce the risk of infection. These arrangements help
protect people and minimise the risk of healthcare-associated infections.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that supports their safety, comfort and well-being. The home is clean,
comfortable and well maintained overall. Bedrooms are personalised with people's belongings and
reflect their individual preferences, helping to create a homely atmosphere. The accommodation
provides suitable facilities to meet people's needs. People have access to communal areas
throughout the home, as well as an accessible garden area where they can spend time outdoors.
Toilet, bathing and laundry facilities are sufficient and support people's day-to-day needs.


The provider maintains effective systems to ensure the environment remains safe. We saw
evidence of regular servicing and maintenance of equipment, alongside good oversight of fire
safety arrangements. Safety measures, including window restrictors, alarm systems and up-to-date
Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs), help reduce risks and support people's safety.
The service has achieved a Food Standards Agency rating of 5, reflecting very good food hygiene
standards.


Regular health and safety checks are completed and recorded electronically, supporting the timely
identification and management of environmental risks. Hazardous substances are securely stored
and infection prevention and control arrangements help maintain a clean environment and reduce
the risk of infection. These arrangements support people to live in an environment that is safe and
suitable for their assessed needs.


The provider has recognised opportunities to further enhance the physical environment through
planned refurbishment. Whilst some areas demonstrate good attention to detail and create a
welcoming atmosphere, parts of the home appear worn and would benefit from refreshing. Some
communal areas are underused and could be developed further to maximise their potential for
people living at the service. Plans are in place to address these areas and further enhance the
environment.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from positive leadership and management arrangements that support good
outcomes. Care staff spoke positively about working at Zoar and described a supportive
environment where teamwork is valued. Care staff told us they feel supported by managers,
listened to and able to raise concerns. We observed positive relationships between managers and
staff and found leaders are visible and involved in the day-to-day operation of the service. This
contributes to a stable culture that benefits both people and staff. Care staff told us “the team works
together effectively, communicates well and always puts the people we support at the centre of
everything we do”.


Governance and quality assurance arrangements are effective. The Responsible Individual (RI)
exceeds the minimum regulatory requirements for oversight and demonstrates a strong
commitment to monitoring the quality of the service. The RI maintains regular contact with the
management team, providing support, challenge and oversight to help drive continuous
improvement. Quarterly reports provide detailed analysis of service performance, identify areas for
improvement and evidence actions taken in response to findings. The biannual Quality of Care
Review is a particularly good example of reflective practice, drawing on feedback from people,
relatives, staff and professionals to evaluate the quality of care provided and support ongoing
service development. Where areas for improvement are identified, action plans are developed,
monitored and reviewed to assess their effectiveness.


The provider has suitable arrangements in place to recruit, support, develop and retain staff.
Training compliance is good across a range of core subjects and staff told us training helps them
carry out their roles effectively. The induction process is comprehensive and provides staff with the
skills and knowledge required to support people safely and consistently. Supervision and appraisal
arrangements are established and provide opportunities for staff to reflect on their practice, discuss
their wellbeing and identify development needs.


Staffing arrangements support continuity of care and help ensure people are cared for by staff who
know them well. Leaders maintain good oversight of staffing levels, workforce development and
staff performance. Recruitment processes help ensure suitable staff are employed and systems are
in place to monitor mandatory checks and training requirements. We found staff had up-to-date
Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks and care staff were registered with Social Care
Wales. The service also recognises staff contributions through reward and recognition initiatives,
helping to promote staff morale, retention and a positive workplace culture.

Managers demonstrate a reflective approach and are committed to continuous improvement.
Information gathered through audits, surveys, observations and quality assurance processes is
used to identify strengths and areas for development. Leaders show a good understanding of the

leadership arrangements support the delivery of safe, person-centred care and positive outcomes
for people.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
