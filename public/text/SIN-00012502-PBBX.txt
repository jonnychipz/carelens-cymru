Summary:

 Glencourt is a small care service without nursing in Caerphilly supporting people with a range of
 needs and abilities. At our previous inspection, we identified significant concerns relating to the
 quality of care and the effectiveness of oversight by the Responsible Individual (RI), resulting in the
 issuance of two Priority Action Notices (PANs). At this inspection, we found significant
 improvements across all areas of the service and acknowledge the considerable commitment,
 dedication and hard work demonstrated by the provider, leaders and staff in achieving these
 changes.

 Of particular note has been the comprehensive redevelopment of people's personal plans, which
 are now more person-centred and outcome focused. The provider has also strengthened quality
 assurance arrangements, resulting in improved monitoring, oversight and accountability. A newly
 appointed RI has introduced more meaningful systems for regulatory visits and reporting, providing
 greater scrutiny and improved alignment with regulatory expectations.

 As a result of the progress made, we are satisfied that the concerns which led to the PANs have
 been addressed and both have been removed. The service has achieved ratings of ‘Good’ across
 all four inspection themes.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to have choice and control over their daily lives and are encouraged to
 maintain their independence wherever possible. Since the previous inspection, significant
 improvements have been made to care planning arrangements, resulting in personal plans that are
 more person-centred, detailed and outcome focused. Plans now provide clear information
 regarding people's communication needs, health requirements, preferences, aspirations and
 desired outcomes. Where possible, people have been involved in developing and reviewing their
 plans, ensuring their views, wishes and life experiences are reflected throughout.

 Staff demonstrate a good understanding of individuals' needs and communicate with people in
 ways that promote participation, choice and informed decision-making. We observed warm and
 positive interactions between people and staff. Staff used appropriate tactile reassurance and
 support where this was welcomed, which had a positive impact on people's mood and
 engagement. The provider has focused on embedding a more positive, person-centred culture
 within the home, and this was evident in the relaxed atmosphere and positive experiences
 observed.

 People are supported to live healthy, safe and fulfilling lives. People’s health needs are clearly
 identified and monitored, and personal plans are regularly reviewed and updated to reflect their
 changing needs and circumstances. The complete renovation of care planning documentation has
 strengthened how people's wellbeing outcomes are identified, monitored and evaluated, providing
 staff with clearer guidance regarding the support required to promote positive outcomes. Risk
 assessments take a proportionate approach to positive risk management, enabling people to
 maintain as much independence and control as possible whilst ensuring their safety and wellbeing
 remain protected.

 The service supports people to maintain meaningful relationships and participate in activities and
 experiences that are important to them. Improvements to people’s accessibility, including the
 installation of a wheelchair-accessible ramp and investment in more suitable transport, have
 increased opportunities for people to participate in community life. These developments have
 enabled people to access a wider range of social, recreational and community-based opportunities
 that support their wellbeing and quality of life. Further work is planned to enhance activity provision,
 ensuring people are supported to participate in a wide range of meaningful opportunities that reflect
 their interests, preferences and aspirations, whilst contributing positively to their wellbeing and
 quality of life. The service also continues to promote the Welsh Active Offer, with people's language
 preferences clearly documented and Welsh language resources available throughout the home.

       Care & Support                       Good



Significant progress has been made since the previous inspection through a comprehensive review
and redevelopment of personal planning documentation. Personal plans are now more detailed,
consistent and reflective of individuals' strengths, support needs, preferences and aspirations.
People, and where appropriate those important to them, have been involved in the development
and review of their plans, helping to ensure support remains personalised and responsive.
Improved review processes have strengthened the service's ability to monitor progress towards
outcomes, evaluate the effectiveness of support and respond promptly to changing needs.

People receive care and support that is focused on achieving outcomes that are important to them.
Staff demonstrate a good understanding of people's individual needs, preferences and routines and
were seen supporting people in line with the preferred support styles. Support is delivered in a way
that promotes choice, dignity and participation, helping people remain involved in decisions about
matters that affect them. Improvements have contributed to a more person-centred approach to
supporting people and achieving positive outcomes.

People are protected through effective safeguarding arrangements and robust risk management
processes. Risk assessments provide clear guidance for staff and support a positive approach to
risk-taking that promotes independence whilst maintaining safety. The service has established
systems for identifying, reporting and responding to safeguarding concerns, and staff are routinely
reminded of their safeguarding responsibilities. Individuals are encouraged and supported to
express their views and participate in decisions regarding their care and support, helping to ensure
their rights are respected and promoted.

People benefit from safe medication management practices and effective infection prevention and
control arrangements. Medication systems were found to be robust, with appropriate arrangements
in place for the storage, administration and monitoring of medicines. Records evidenced a
continued focus on positive behaviour support approaches and reducing reliance on as required
mood stabilising medication. The home was observed to be clean, tidy and well maintained, with
sufficient stocks of personal protective equipment available and colour-coded cleaning equipment
in use. These measures help minimise the risk of infection and support people to live in a safe and
healthy environment.

       Environment                         Good



People live in accommodation that is safe, comfortable and suitable to meet their needs. The
provider has invested in improvements to the environment which have enhanced the overall
presentation of the home and created a more welcoming and homely atmosphere. Refurbishment
works have improved communal living spaces and contributed to an environment that supports
people's comfort, wellbeing and quality of life. Communal areas are clean, well maintained and
provide opportunities for both social interaction and relaxation. People's bedrooms are
personalised and reflect their interests, preferences and personalities. This supports people to feel
comfortable within their home and promotes a sense of ownership and identity.

The provider has demonstrated a commitment to improving accessibility and ensuring the
environment remains responsive to people's changing needs. Specialist furniture has been
purchased to support individuals more effectively, and accessibility improvements, including the
installation of a wheelchair-accessible ramp and the purchase of a more suitable vehicle, have
significantly enhanced opportunities for people to access their local communities. These
developments have supported greater inclusion, independence and participation in activities that
matter to people.

Appropriate security arrangements help ensure people live in a safe environment without imposing
unnecessary restrictions on their freedom. At the time of the inspection, we were asked to provide
identification and record our attendance in the visitors' book, demonstrating suitable controls over
access to the home. Appropriate health and safety arrangements are in place to help ensure
people live in a safe environment. Regular servicing, maintenance and safety checks are
undertaken for the premises and equipment, with records providing assurance that key systems are
monitored and maintained. Fire safety procedures are well established, with routine drills and
testing completed to help ensure staff are prepared to respond effectively in an emergency. The
provider gave assurance that routine fire drills will continue to be undertaken, enabling all staff to
participate over the coming months. We welcome this commitment and look forward to reviewing
progress at the next inspection. Relevant health and safety certification provide assurance that
appropriate testing, servicing and maintenance arrangements are in place to ensure compliance
with applicable health and safety legislation and requirements.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service has made significant progress in strengthening leadership arrangements and
embedding a culture focused on continuous improvement. Management demonstrated a good
understanding of the service's strengths and areas requiring further development and were able to
evidence actions taken in response to previous inspection findings. There is a clear commitment to
improving outcomes for people, and the provider has invested considerable time and effort in
driving positive change across the service. We recognise the hard work and commitment shown by
leaders and staff in implementing substantial improvements since the previous inspection. The
progress made within a relatively short timescale demonstrates a clear focus on continuous
improvement and achieving better outcomes for people.

The provider has developed and strengthened quality assurance systems which support effective
monitoring and continuous improvement. A range of audits, quality reviews and monitoring
activities are undertaken across the service to assess performance, identify areas for development
and evaluate the effectiveness of improvements. The service promotes openness and transparency
and actively seeks feedback from people, their representatives, staff and professionals. Feedback
is used to inform service development and improvement planning. The service provider maintains
positive and constructive relationships with commissioners, healthcare professionals and the
regulator (Care Inspectorate Wales), demonstrating a willingness to work collaboratively and
respond appropriately to recommendations and feedback.

A newly appointed RI has introduced revised arrangements for completing regulatory visits and
associated reports. These provide a more structured and meaningful evaluation of service quality
and are more closely aligned with regulatory expectations. Recent RI visits demonstrate increased
oversight of service delivery and a greater presence within the home, enabling the RI to develop a
better understanding of people's experiences. The manager continues to maintain effective
oversight of the day-to-day operation of the service and has demonstrated a strong commitment to
driving improvement.

People are supported by a staff team that is increasingly skilled, knowledgeable and well
supported. Improvements have been made to the quality and consistency of staff supervision since
the previous inspection, with records evidencing more reflective discussions regarding practice,
safeguarding, wellbeing and professional development. Training compliance has improved
considerably and provides assurance that staff have access to the knowledge and skills required to
support people safely and effectively.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
