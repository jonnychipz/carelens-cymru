Summary:

 Bryn Edwin Hall Residential Home is a care home providing accommodation and personal care for
 people living with dementia. Well-being is good at the home because people are supported by staff
 who know them well and provide respectful, person-centred care. Positive interactions were
 observed throughout the home, with staff promoting dignity, inclusion and engagement. People are
 encouraged to maintain relationships, participate in activities and contribute to decisions about their
 daily lives. Monitoring of wellbeing and health helps ensure people receive appropriate support.

 People have good care and support through personalised care based on assessments and
 regularly reviewed care plans. Staff understand individual needs and provide support that promotes
 health, wellbeing and independence. Effective arrangements are in place to manage risks and
 respond to changing needs.

 The Environment is good because people live in a clean, comfortable and welcoming home.
 Bedrooms and communal areas support social interaction, relaxation and meaningful activity.
 Effective processes are in place to maintain cleanliness, safety and the suitability of the
 environment, including outdoor spaces that enhance wellbeing.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People at Bryn Edwin Residential Home are supported by staff who know them well and
 understand their individual preferences, routines and support needs. During the inspection, we
 observed numerous positive and respectful interactions throughout the home. Staff spoke with
 people in a warm and familiar manner and took time to engage people in conversation and
 everyday activities. We observed staff supporting people during mealtimes whilst seated alongside
 them, promoting dignity and inclusion. In communal areas, staff were visible, available and
 interacting with people.

 People have opportunities to participate in social, recreational and meaningful activities. Staff
 explained how activities are matched to people's interests and preferences and described the
 home's "Daily Sparkle" approach, which ensures people receive dedicated one-to-one
 engagement. We saw records of individual sessions tailored to people's interests. The home
 engages with a digital Cognitive Stimulation Therapy programme to evaluate outcomes relating to
 wellbeing, mood, cognition and social engagement. The service has also recently introduced a
 Behaviour Support and Engagement Lead role to further develop meaningful engagement and
 person-centred support for people living in the home. People can participate in activities at the
 home and join trips into the community.

 Staff and managers support people to maintain relationships and participate in community life. We
 saw evidence that the home liaises closely with relatives and friends of people and engages with
 them about changes in care needs. Relatives told us staff make considerable efforts to encourage
 social opportunities whilst respecting people's wishes and preferences.

 People are involved in decisions affecting their lives and support. We spoke with an advocate who
 described improvements to the home’s engagement with people around decisions that affect them.
 We observed strength-based and proportionate documentation that clearly considered people’s
 ability to make decisions. People are given opportunities to provide feedback on the service
 through regular resident meetings, which consider activities, food, care and support, environment
 and safety. Records demonstrate the provider responds to issues raised and provides feedback on
 progress and actions taken.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support that is informed by good assessment and care planning
arrangements that support consistency of care and promote person-centred practice. We reviewed
care records and found detailed information to guide staff in meeting people's individual needs and
preferences. Care plans consider a range of areas including health, wellbeing, communication,
nutrition, mobility and personal outcomes. Records are regularly reviewed and updated to reflect
people's changing circumstances. Daily records show staff monitor people's wellbeing and provide
ongoing support throughout the day.

Staff have effective arrangements in place to promote and monitor people's health and wellbeing.
We reviewed evidence of regular weight monitoring, with records demonstrating consistent
oversight of nutritional wellbeing. Kitchen staff have access to information relating to food
preferences, allergies, intolerances and specialist dietary requirements. Specialist eating and
drinking guidance is available to support the safe preparation and provision of modified diets, and
staff have access to additional information regarding the equipment and utensils required to
support people’s eating and drinking needs. Dietary information is routinely monitored and
communicated to care staff and kitchen staff, supporting safe and effective care. People's health
needs are reviewed on an ongoing basis, and records demonstrate a proactive approach to
monitoring changes and identifying potential concerns. Staff work proactively with professionals to
support people's changing needs. Records show routine liaison with professionals, including
reviews of medicines and health needs to help ensure care remains appropriate and responsive.
Good standards of cleanliness are maintained throughout the service and infection prevention and
control arrangements are evident. Laundry cleaning schedules demonstrated routine oversight of
cleaning processes and equipment

People benefit from risk management arrangements that seek to balance safety with independence
and choice. We reviewed individual risk assessments, including those relating to mobility, falls and
other safety risks. The provider completes individual falls reviews to analyse patterns, levels of
concern and contributing factors when people experience multiple falls. These reviews consider
actions required to reduce risk and promote people's safety and wellbeing. Records show ongoing
monitoring and review of incidents, helping staff to identify emerging risks and respond
appropriately to changes in people's needs. Incident information is used as part of wider review
processes to support learning and continuous improvement.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment that is comfortable, homely and supports their wellbeing. We saw
people can personalise their living environments and maintain belongings that are important to
them. Bedrooms viewed during the inspection reflect people's individuality and personal
preferences, with personal possessions and refreshments visible within rooms. Communal areas
are clean, welcoming and provided space for people to socialise, participate in activities or spend
time quietly according to their preferences. During the inspection, we saw people moving freely
around communal areas and appearing comfortable within their surroundings. Outdoor areas are
well maintained and provided additional space for people to spend time outside with opportunities
to participate in gardening activities and grow vegetables. Information was displayed throughout the
home to support people to make choices and remain informed about daily life within the service.
This included information about menu options and activities taking place within the home.

Established maintenance and safety arrangements support the ongoing suitability of the premises.
The manager and Responsible Individual maintain oversight through routine checks, servicing and
monitoring processes, helping to ensure the environment remains safe and suitable for people's
needs. Information relating to people's privacy and how personal information is managed is
available within the home, helping to promote transparency and awareness amongst people,
visitors and representatives.

Effective systems support the monitoring and maintenance of the environment. Staff use an
electronic maintenance reporting system to record, track and monitor maintenance concerns
across the home. Staff demonstrated how repairs are identified, logged and reviewed to ensure
issues are progressed appropriately, and we saw examples of maintenance works and ongoing
investment in the environment. We also saw evidence that concerns identified through routine
monitoring are acted upon and progressed. During the inspection, we identified some areas
requiring maintenance, particularly in parts of the premises not routinely accessed by people living
in the home. Management responded positively to the issues raised and demonstrated that
appropriate action has been taken.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The home benefits from established leadership and management arrangements that support
positive outcomes for people. Staff spoke positively about the management team and described
managers as approachable, supportive and responsive. We observed sufficient staffing throughout
the inspection and received positive feedback about the quality of care provided. A positive
organisational culture is promoted within the service, and staff contributions are recognised and
valued. We saw examples of staff recognition through an appreciation board and initiatives
involving both people and staff.

Staff are supported through recruitment, training and performance management processes. We
reviewed workforce information, recruitment records and personnel files, which demonstrated
appropriate employment checks and oversight of Disclosure and Barring Service requirements.
Staff receive training across a broad range of topics relevant to their roles. The provider had
undertaken a detailed analysis of workforce skills, identifying areas of strength and opportunities for
further development. An action plan was in place to support ongoing workforce development.

The manager and Responsible Individual maintain oversight of the quality and safety of the service
through a range of governance arrangements. We reviewed Responsible Individual reports,
incident monitoring, falls reviews, medication reviews, resident feedback and quality assurance
documentation. Resident meetings provide opportunities for people to share their views on the
service and records demonstrate feedback is considered and responded to. We saw evidence of
individual falls and medication reviews being used to identify patterns, monitor risks and inform
actions to improve outcomes for people. The Quality of Care Review reporting process considers
performance in line with CIW inspection themes and identifies both strengths and areas for further
development.

We found evidence that management oversight has strengthened since the previous inspection,
including improved analysis of service information, stronger quality assurance arrangements and
greater scrutiny of key areas of service delivery. Documentation demonstrated improved
consideration of decision-making processes and individual rights, supported by more robust
governance and review arrangements. As a result, a Priority Action Notice issued at the previous
inspection has been addressed. Whilst progress has been made, opportunities remain to further
strengthen some governance processes, particularly in relation to privacy, data management and
evidencing the effectiveness of systems used within the service. The Manager and Responsible
Individual acknowledged these areas for development and committed to introducing measures to
address them.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
