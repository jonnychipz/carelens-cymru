Summary:

 Ty Machen is a small residential care home for adults, set in a quiet residential area near local
 shops and amenities. The service is provided in a single level property with level access to the front
 and rear gardens.

 Well-being, care and support, environment and leadership and management are rated as excellent.
 We found evidence of an outstanding commitment to delivering an exceptional standard of care
 and support to people, to enable them to improve their independence and control over their lives
 and achieve highly personalised wellbeing goals and outcomes. The environment is monitored and
 updated in line with people’s preferences, needs and enables them to achieve what matters to
 them. We found the highly effective leadership and management drives continual learning and
 development across all areas of the service, embedding a culture of reflection and empowerment.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 Well-being is excellent because people experience positive outcomes and are supported to have
 as much control over their day-to-day lives as possible. We saw people taking part in activities and
 routines that reflected their individual preferences and interests. People are treated with dignity and
 respect, and staff actively promote choice, independence and the achievement of personal goals.
 We saw support being provided to people with warmth and care, and interactions between people
 and care staff appeared friendly and relaxed.


 People benefit from support which is responsive to their changing needs and enables them to make
 progress towards outcomes which are important to them. Regular reviews and monitoring help care
 staff recognise achievements and adjust support when required. Our observations and relative
 feedback demonstrated people have positive, respectful relationships with staff. These
 relationships support people to communicate their preferences, participate in daily life and receive
 support in ways meaningful to them.


 People maintain important relationships and connections with family members, friends and their
 wider communities. Relatives described strong partnership working and told us they are kept
 informed and involved in decisions about their family member's care and support. Feedback
 highlighted the positive relationships which have developed between families and the staff team,
 and the efforts made to support people to participate in activities and family experiences which are
 important to them.


 People are supported to remain well and to take an active role in making decisions regarding their
 health and well-being. Care staff support healthy lifestyles, access to appropriate healthcare and
 participation in activities which promote physical and emotional wellbeing.


 People are supported by care staff who understand their safeguarding responsibilities and take
 action to protect them from harm. Leaders promote a culture of openness and learning and there
 was evidence of risks, restrictions and support arrangements being reviewed regularly to promote
 people’s autonomy. Records demonstrated appropriate arrangements were in place where
 restrictions were required, helping to ensure people receive support in the least restrictive way
 whilst maintaining their safety and wellbeing.

       Care & Support                           Excellent


Care and support is excellent because people receive exceptionally personalised support which
reflects their assessed needs, preferences, what matters to them and their desired outcomes. We
found care workers have a very strong understanding of people's individual needs, preferences and
personal outcomes and can anticipate people’s needs on their behalf. We reviewed a sample of
care records and found personal plans clearly describe people's strengths, needs, routines,
interests, needs and personal outcomes. Where people require support to identify personal goals,
care staff who know them well identify meaningful outcomes on their behalf. This process is
supported by the service's quality of life assessment tool, helping to ensure goals are individualised
and reflect what is important to the person. Personal outcomes are monitored through monthly
reviews, key-worker meetings and outcome-tracking systems to ensure they remain in line with
what matters to the person.


Continuity is promoted by a highly consistent staff team and effective information-sharing with
health and social care professionals and family members. Feedback from relatives confirmed staff
understand people's individual needs, preferences and triggers and use this knowledge to tailor
support to people. We read care records which evidenced proactive work with health and social
care professionals and showed appropriate referrals are made when required, and professional
guidance was incorporated into support plans. Relatives gave examples of staff helping people
access health interventions they had previously found difficult or distressing and attributed this to
the skills of the care staff and the service as a whole, along with a high level of consistency in how
people are supported.



People's rights and freedoms are promoted through a person-centred and least restrictive
approach to care. Records demonstrated appropriate arrangements where restrictions were
required, and support plans reflected relevant legal safeguards. Staff demonstrated they
understood how to balance safety with independence, and restrictions were reviewed and reduced
where safe to do so.



People receive their medication in a safe and person-centred way. Medication records are
accurate, regularly audited and reflect people's individual needs. Leaders demonstrate a proactive
approach to continuous improvement by identifying opportunities to strengthen medication
practices and implement well-considered changes to systems and oversight arrangements.

       Environment                              Excellent



The environment is excellent because people live in accommodation which significantly enhances
their wellbeing, promotes independence and supports them to achieve positive outcomes. We
observed a homely, welcoming environment which was clean, well maintained and carefully
adapted to support people's comfort, dignity and wellbeing. The accommodation is arranged over a
single floor with level access available to both the front and rear of the home, providing easily
accessible outdoor space directly from the living areas. Bedrooms are highly personalised and
reflect people's individual tastes and preferences, helping create a strong sense of ownership and
belonging.


The provider has made considerable investment in the environment to ensure it continues to meet
people's changing needs and preferences. Recent improvements include redecorating bedrooms,
enhancing outdoor areas and creating additional opportunities for people to access and enjoy the
grounds. The provider also demonstrates a strong commitment to continuous improvement, with
further environmental enhancements planned to maximise independence, strengthen opportunities
for participation and further support people to achieve positive well-being outcomes. People benefit
from an environment which successfully balances safety with independence. We saw evidence that
restrictions had been reviewed and reduced where possible. Leaders demonstrate a thoughtful and
person-centred approach to risk management, ensuring environmental safety measures are
proportionate and do not unnecessarily restrict people's freedom, choice or quality of life.


Robust systems are in place to maintain the premises and equipment to a high standard. Records
demonstrate regular health and safety checks, servicing arrangements and environmental
monitoring. Leaders respond promptly when issues are identified and use findings to inform
continuous improvement. As a result, people benefit from living in an environment which is safe,
well maintained and highly suited to their individual needs and preferences.


People benefit from highly effective arrangements to support their safety in the event of an
emergency. Each person has a personalised emergency evacuation plan which reflects their
individual needs and the support they require. Regular evacuation drills are undertaken at varying
times and are carefully reviewed to identify any learning opportunities. Feedback from these
exercises is shared with the manager and staff team, supporting continuous improvement and
helping to ensure emergency procedures remain effective, well-coordinated and responsive to
people's needs.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



 Leadership and management is excellent because people achieve excellent outcomes as a result
of highly effective leadership and governance arrangements. Leaders promote a very strong,
inclusive and person-centred culture, with a clear focus on quality of life, independence and
personal goals. Staff spoke extremely positively about the direction of the service and described
significant improvements to systems, working practices and the environment. Relatives also
expressed a high level of confidence in the leadership team and praised the organisation,
communication and oversight within the service.


People benefit from a stable, committed and well-supported workforce. Staff described a positive
team culture in which roles are clear, colleagues work effectively together and leaders are
approachable for support, guidance and advice. Formal one-to-one supervision takes place at least
every three months and includes reflective discussion about people's outcomes, staff wellbeing,
performance and development. Annual appraisals provide a fuller review of achievements, skills
and future objectives.


The provider demonstrates a very strong commitment to workforce learning and development.
Training records show a comprehensive programme covering mandatory, specialist and service-
specific subjects, with very high levels of compliance. Effective systems enable leaders to monitor
training and respond promptly to development needs. Staff can also access leadership
development opportunities, including programmes designed to support career progression and
prepare staff for future leadership responsibilities.


People can be assured the provider follows robust recruitment procedures designed to promote
their safety and well-being. We reviewed a sample of personnel records and found evidence
appropriate pre-employment checks are completed, including obtaining references, verifying
employment history and undertaking criminal records checks before staff commence employment.
These arrangements help ensure people are supported by staff who have been assessed as
suitable to work with vulnerable adults and can meet their needs safely.


Effective quality assurance arrangements enable leaders to understand performance, identify areas
for development and take timely action. Audits, quality reviews and management oversight show
improvements are implemented and monitored, helping the service remain responsive, well led and
focused on positive outcomes.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
