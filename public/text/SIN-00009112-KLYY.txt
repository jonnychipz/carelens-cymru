Summary:


 Three Cliffs Care Home is in a rural location along the Gower coastline. It overlooks Three Cliffs
 Bay, and people find the view to be both stunning and peaceful. People require transport to access
 community facilities. The service offers transport to support visits and help people stay connected.

 People are content in their home. Care staff provide the physical and emotional support people
 need to promote their health and well-being. People are positively occupied and stimulated through
 their social interactions and meaningful activities.

 The service works effectively with medical and specialist services to help people stay healthy.
 Managers closely monitor practice and drive improvements where necessary. People receive the
 right level of support with their medication, and good hygiene practices help reduce infection risks.

People live in a safe environment. Private and communal rooms are spacious, personalised and
well maintained. There is ample space for people to relax and spend time with others. Regular
enhancements to the décor help keep the environment stimulating.

The service has good leadership and management. The team is open and responsive to feedback,
which helps things run smoothly. Staff are motivated and content in their roles. The Responsible
Individual (RI) monitors standards carefully and looks to make continuous improvements.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People receive good quality care and support which promotes their health and well-being. Personal
 plans are detailed and explain clearly how care staff should meet people’s physical and emotional
 needs. We saw care staff assisting people in a gentle, dignified and respectful way. Families told us
 they find the staff team to be friendly and accommodating. One relative said, “I think they’re doing
 an excellent job”. People enjoy various activities, which are facilitated by two full-time well-being
 leads and care staff. We observed people to be happy and engaged during a group game. The
 well-being leads created an inclusive atmosphere by offering encouragement and using physical
 prompts to help people participate and feel involved. People also appeared energised and uplifted
 during a performance from a ukulele band. One relative commented, “Residents come alive with
 musical entertainment”.

 The service has systems in place to help keep people safe. Staff receive safeguarding training and
 understand how to respond to concerns or incidents. Managers work openly with other agencies
 and professionals to implement appropriate safeguards when people’s safety or welfare is at risk.
 We found that recommendations are acted upon swiftly following serious incidents, with learning
 shared across the team to improve practice. One staff member said, “The manager always relays
 feedback and recommendations”. The RI routinely reviews safeguarding concerns and other
 incidents during quality assurance checks, ensuring appropriate actions are taken.

 People have regular, meaningful interactions with care staff, which have a positive impact on their
 well-being. Care staff communicate with people effectively using verbal prompts and visual cues.
 The support and reassurance people receive helps to put them at ease and gives them a sense of
 purpose and belonging. Very few staff speak Welsh, although the manager told us they enjoy
 learning Welsh words and phrases from residents. The Welsh culture is also celebrated through
 various activities. Relatives told us they feel welcome at the home and part of its community. The
 home accommodated a relative’s request for a counselling support group to be set up, so relatives
 can share their experiences and take comfort from the support provided.

 The accommodation promotes people’s well-being. There are measures in place to help keep
 people safe as they go about their daily routines. Communal rooms and bedrooms are suitably
 furnished and decorated and include facilities to meet people’s needs. Relatives told us they would
 welcome an area where they can sit closer to their loved ones, and the service is exploring how this
 can be provided safely. The décor is continually being refreshed to help people recognise and
 engage with their surroundings.

       Care & Support                       Good





People’s care and support needs are consistently met. Care staff monitor people closely, and we
observed them to be well-kempt and comfortable in their surroundings. People enjoyed their
lunchtime meals, which were provided according to their dietary needs and preferences. The home
has recently introduced a new menu, and staff are committed to offering choice and flexibility at
mealtimes. We saw care staff assisting people to eat and drink in a patient, considerate manner.
People and/or their representatives are involved in planning and reviewing care to ensure it
continues to meet individual needs and goals. The service is also introducing an improved system
to capture people’s views during regular care reviews.

Managers act upon concerns to help protect people from harm. Due to inconsistencies in practice,
the management team is closely monitoring moving and handling procedures to ensure they are
being carried out correctly. Staff have also received additional refresher training to build on their
skills and confidence. We saw care staff assisting people to mobilise calmly and safely, taking time
to promote their comfort when using equipment. The management team is also carrying out spot
checks during the night to monitor care practices and routines. Care staff who regularly work night
shifts are being given opportunities to work during the day, allowing them to gain new experiences
and develop their skills while working under direct supervision of leaders and managers.

People receive the right level of support with their medication. An electronic system tracks the
administration of medication and shows that people consistently receive their medicines at the right
time. We found that correct legal processes are followed where medicines are administered
covertly. Regular reviews with medical professionals allow for timely adjustments to medication and
help prevent any overuse. We saw that medication is stored securely and at suitable temperatures.

People receive good continuity of care and have access to specialist services when needed. Health
professionals spoke highly about the team’s collaborative approach, which helps them manage
complex needs and behaviours while improving people’s experiences. For example, people have
been supported to settle into their environment, adopt healthier routines and gain confidence with
daily activities. The service has a low staff turnover, helping people to develop close relationships
with care staff based on a sound understanding of their needs and preferences. Relatives told us,
“They always phone to update us whenever there is a change” and “Communication is excellent”.

The service maintains a high standard of hygiene and infection control. Staff complete regular
training in relation to infection control and domestic staff work diligently to ensure the home and
equipment remain clean and hygienic. Relatives told us the home is always clean when they visit,
which we also observed. One relative said, “It’s spotlessly clean and smells very fresh”. The service
was awarded a food hygiene rating of 4 (good) in March 2026. We were told of plans to restructure

       Environment                         Good





Many bedrooms and communal areas have views of the sea, which contribute to the home’s calm
atmosphere. There are some outdoor seating areas overlooking the bay, providing space for
people to relax and enjoy the view. The home also has an enclosed garden where people can
safely spend time outdoors. A relative told us people had enjoyed being outside over the summer.
Due to its rural location, the service offers transport to support visits and help people maintain
important relationships with family and friends.

The service has various lounge and dining areas which offer social and leisure opportunities. We
observed people to be content as they spent time interacting with care staff, watching television,
listening to music, dining together, taking part in activities and watching entertainment. A pub with
traditional features has been created next to the main building, providing extra space for people to
enjoy private functions and other social events. Bedrooms have been personalised and we saw
people taking comfort from the familiar items around them.

People benefit from an environment that is regularly improved and updated. Lounge and bathroom
facilities have recently been renovated, and new furniture has been ordered. Further developments
include upgrading the office and reception area and creating a small dining area in ‘The Pier’.
There are also plans to add colour contrasts and decorative items to all corridors, to give them a
homelier feel and help people orientate to their surroundings more easily. Relatives told us, “The
décor is refreshed all the time” and “They’re always making improvements; it’s getting better and
better”.

There are measures in place to reduce risks to people’s health and safety. The building is secure
from unauthorised access and closed-circuit television (CCTV) is used externally to enhance
security. CCTV has also been introduced to communal areas internally to assist managers with
investigating incidents. Keypads are fitted to exits and some internal doors to prevent people
accessing hazardous areas. We observed equipment being stored safely within designated storage
rooms and found that people have access to the specialist equipment they need, as outlined within
personal plans. Records confirm that equipment and utilities are operating safely. The service
employs two full-time maintenance officers who carry out routine health and safety checks plus
general repairs. Staff complete fire safety training and take part in regular fire drills. These are
carried out more frequently when improvements in practice are needed. We found that personal
emergency evacuation plans are easy to access and accurately reflect people’s needs.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The service benefits from effective leadership and management. Relatives, professionals and staff
consistently told us they have confidence in how the service is run and find the management team
to be open and transparent. Staff described managers as supportive and understanding, easy to
talk to and responsive to requests and concerns. The manager provides visible accountability and
works alongside staff when needed to deliver care. One relative commented that the manager is
“absolutely responsive, goes on walk arounds, is always out and about and very involved”.

The quality of the service is closely monitored. Managers respond to complaints in line with
company policy, using these as opportunities to learn and improve practice. Staff told us they are
always informed about significant events, and they showed good awareness of the measures in
place to help keep people safe. The RI uses feedback from people using the service and an
analysis of key data to identify areas for improvement. Actions are set and progress is monitored
during the RI’s formal visits and six-monthly quality reviews.

People are cared for by appropriate numbers of staff who have been through a robust recruitment
process. Records confirm that staff are vetted by the Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) prior to
employment and at three-yearly intervals thereafter. Staff are encouraged to complete recognised
care qualifications and managers support them to maintain their registration with Social Care Wales
or the Nursing and Midwifery Council. Staffing levels are routinely reviewed with the senior
management team as the occupancy of the home increases and dependency levels change.
Worked staffing rotas show that day and night shifts are consistently staffed to the levels planned.

The service ensures staff have the knowledge and skills to meet people’s needs. Staff complete a
programme of mandatory and specialist training, and professionals spoke highly about the qualities
and skills of the team. Training compliance is regularly monitored through supervision meetings
and quality assurance checks undertaken by the RI. Records show that any practice issues are
addressed through supervision meetings and disciplinary procedures, with staff undergoing further
training and competency assessments when required. We found staff morale to be good, with
many reflecting on the strong sense of teamwork and shared commitment to providing people with
the best possible care. Many relatives commented on the positive atmosphere within the team. One
relative said, “There’s a really good team spirit here as well as a high standard of professionalism”.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
