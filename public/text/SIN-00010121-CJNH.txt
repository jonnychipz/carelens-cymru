Summary:


 Brookside Care Home is a care home based in Powys, providing care and nursing support to
 people with a range of needs.

 We identified improvement in most areas since the previous inspection. At the last inspection, we
 issued two Priority Action Notices (PANs) and five Areas for Improvement (AFIs) relating to health
 and safety, personal planning, dignity and respect, Responsible Individual (RI) oversight, and
 staffing arrangements. During this inspection, we found sufficient progress had been made to
 remove one PAN and three AFIs. The remaining PAN relating to health and safety has been
 downgraded to an AFI because improvements have reduced the level of risk to people. Whilst
 some residual health and safety concerns remain, we no longer consider people to be at significant
 or imminent risk.

 We found continued improvement in staff training arrangements. However, whilst training
 compliance has improved it remains below the standard expected. Further work is required to
 ensure personal plans are consistently person-centred, outcome focused and accurately reflect

Overall, leaders have demonstrated commitment to improvement and the changes made since the

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good




 People experience positive well-being outcomes because care staff know them well and support
 them in a kind, respectful and unhurried manner. We observed many positive interactions
 throughout the inspection. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of people’s histories,
 preferences and daily routines, helping people maintain control over their day-to-day lives. People
 appear comfortable in the service. Sufficient staff are available to provide support without staff
 appearing rushed or task focused. We saw people receive reassurance and encouragement when
 mobilising and noted a calm and relaxed atmosphere throughout the home. These positive
 interactions support people’s dignity, independence and emotional well-being.

 People are increasingly involved in decisions about their care and support. We found personal
 reviews are more meaningful and place greater emphasis on people’s views and involvement.
 People and their representatives contribute to reviews and there is evidence the service is
 strengthening a culture where people’s voices are heard. Staff recognise people’s capacity and
 encourage independence whenever possible. Language preferences are recorded and the service
 is making efforts to promote the Welsh Active Offer. We observed examples of Welsh language
 promotion, including telephone calls being answered in Welsh. This demonstrates the service is
 working towards identifying and meeting people’s language and cultural needs.

 People maintain important relationships with family and friends. Visitors are welcomed at the
 service and care staff understand the importance of maintaining connections with those closest to
 people. Staff described opportunities for people to access community activities, including local
 groups, church attendance and shopping trips. It is encouraging that the service is continuing to
 strengthen links within the local community and expand opportunities available to people.

 People benefit from an improving activities programme. We observed positive engagement during
 a live musical performance and saw people enjoying the experience. Leaders have recognised this
 area requires further development and have taken action by recruiting additional activities support.

 Outcomes for people require improvement because personal plans do not consistently demonstrate
 a strengths-based and outcome-focused approach. Although reviews have improved, personal
 outcomes are not always reflected throughout personal plans, and some records lack sufficient
 personalisation. This means staff do not always have access to consistent information about what
 matters most to people and how they wish to achieve their individual outcomes. Leaders offered us
 assurance that this issue would be rectified and we look forward to testing this at our next
 inspection.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement




Many people receive caring and respectful support from staff who understand their needs and
preferences. People benefit from continuity of care, and we observed positive interactions
throughout the inspection. Care staff are familiar with people's routines and demonstrate
commitment to supporting their health and well-being. We found evidence of multi-disciplinary
working, with specialist involvement from health and social care professionals where required.
Case tracking identified examples of input from speech and language therapists, district nurses and
other partner agencies to help support people’s care needs. Recent admissions demonstrated
improved assessment processes, and personal plans had been completed within required
timescales following admission.

Medication management is largely effective. Medication administration records are generally
completed well, stock balances reconcile appropriately, and controlled drug records are accurate.
Staff demonstrate a good understanding of medication systems, and storage arrangements are
organised. We found evidence of regular medication audits, providing management with oversight
of medication practices.

People are generally protected from infection. Staff have access to personal protective equipment,
and domestic staff undertake regular cleaning duties. Most communal areas and bedrooms appear
visibly clean and environmental cleaning schedules are completed. Management responded
positively when concerns around cleanliness were raised during inspection and took prompt action
to address identified issues.

People receive care from staff who take safeguarding responsibilities seriously. Safeguarding
incidents are recorded and managed appropriately. Management and care staff were open and
transparent during the inspection and acted quickly to mitigate risks when matters were brought to
their attention.

Outcomes for people require improvement because personal plans do not consistently provide
accurate, person-centred and outcome-focused information to direct care. Case tracking identified
plans that contained inconsistent, contradictory or outdated information. We found monitoring
records relating to nutrition, fluid intake, repositioning and health monitoring were not always
consistent with assessed needs. Whilst people's needs are generally identified, records do not
always accurately reflect the care people receive or the outcomes they wish to achieve. In addition,
personal plans do not consistently contain meaningful information about people's histories and
what is important to them. Greater emphasis is required on ensuring plans remain accurate,
personalised and outcome focused. People may not always achieve positive outcomes because
personal plans do not consistently provide sufficient information to direct staff in the care they
provide. This area requires improvement and we expect the service provider to take timely action.

       Environment               Requires Improvement




People live in an environment that generally meets their needs and supports their day-to-day lives.
The service provides a range of communal spaces. Bedrooms are spacious, comfortable and
personalised with photographs, artwork and personal belongings. People have access to
equipment that supports a range of care and mobility needs, helping them maintain independence
and safety. We found facilities were largely accessible.

The provider has made progress since the previous inspection and demonstrates a realistic
understanding of the improvements still required. It was positive to see prompt action taken when
issues were identified during inspection. Equipment no longer required was removed and areas
highlighted for additional cleaning received immediate attention. We particularly welcomed the
comprehensive environmental improvement plan provided during the inspection, which
demonstrated awareness of current priorities and future investment requirements.

Health and safety arrangements are somewhat effective. The maintenance technician
demonstrates good oversight of health and safety matters. We found evidence that routine
servicing, testing and safety checks are completed appropriately. Management also demonstrated
openness regarding environmental challenges and provided clear evidence of planned
improvements. People will benefit from ongoing investment in the environment. Since the previous
inspection, work has been undertaken to improve the premises and leaders have developed a
structured plan for further renovation. This demonstrates commitment to enhancing the quality of
the environment over time.

People’s outcomes require improvement because parts of the environment and associated
maintenance arrangements do not consistently support people's well-being and safety. Inspectors
identified uneven flooring, worn furnishings, unstable furniture and damaged fixtures within several
bedrooms. Whilst these issues do not currently have a significant impact on people's physical
health, they reduce assurance that all environmental risks have been effectively addressed.

Parts of the home appear tired and in need of refurbishment. Continued investment in décor,
furnishings, equipment replacement and general maintenance is needed to ensure the environment
consistently reflects the standards people deserve. We recognise the significant commitment made
by the service provider to improving the premises and appreciate that substantial investment will be
required. People may not achieve the best possible outcomes because the environment and
facilities are not consistently maintained to the standard expected. This area requires improvement
and we expect the service provider to take timely action. Progress against the environmental action
plan will be reviewed at the next inspection.

       Leadership & Management               Good




The service benefits from committed leaders who are open, transparent and receptive to
improvement. Since the previous inspection, management has introduced a number of positive
changes, including revised pre-admission assessments, improved handover arrangements and
strengthened review processes. Throughout the inspection, leaders engaged positively with
inspectors, responded appropriately to feedback and demonstrated a willingness to address
concerns promptly. We received positive feedback regarding leadership and management within
the service.

The RI maintains a visible presence within the home and demonstrates a good understanding of
the service and the people living there. We found evidence of increased engagement with people
and staff through questionnaires, consultations and regular quality assurance activity. RI reports
have improved considerably and now include the range of information expected under the
regulations. It was positive to note that several areas for improvement identified by the RI aligned
with our own findings, demonstrating insight and commitment to service development.

Governance records, safeguarding information and statutory notifications are organised and readily
accessible. The Statement of Purpose (SoP) is comprehensive and reflects regulatory
requirements. Likewise, the Service User Guide contains useful information for people and
representatives and reflects the service delivered.

People are supported by caring staff. Staffing levels observed during inspection appeared sufficient
to meet people's needs and were consistent with the SoP. Safe recruitment practices have
improved since the previous inspection and most staff are appropriately registered with Social Care
Wales, the workforce regulator. Supervision practices have also become more consistent, and
records demonstrate meaningful discussion regarding performance, well-being and development.

People may not achieve consistently positive outcomes because some staff are not sufficiently
trained. Staff training compliance, whilst improved, is not yet consistently meeting expected
standards across mandatory and service-specific subjects. In addition, induction arrangements
require strengthening to ensure new staff receive sufficient preparation for working in a large and
complex care home environment. This remains an area for improvement, and we expect the
service provider to take timely action.

Whilst governance arrangements are developing positively, further work is required to ensure
audits focus on quality, accuracy and outcomes rather than completion alone. Targeted audits of
care planning and workforce compliance are required to strengthen oversight. We look forward to
reviewing these continued improvements at our next inspection.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
