        Leadership & Management               Good


Summary:

 Maes Llewelyn Residential Care Home, in Newcastle Emlyn, provides care and accommodation for
 up to 40 people across five units.

 Well-being is “Good” as people have control over daily life and receive respectful support to
 maintain their health, relationships and interests.

 Care and Support is “Good” as care staff know people well and provide consistent, person-centred
 care. Effective safeguarding and risk-management systems help people feel safe and protect their
 rights.

 The Environment is “Good” as people live in comfortable, personalised accommodation that
 promotes privacy, independence and social contact.

 Leadership and Management is “Good” as Leaders promote an open culture, support care staff
 effectively and use strong governance and feedback systems to improve the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People maintain and improve their physical, mental and emotional health because care staff
 provide good-quality care and support. We saw people enjoying chair exercises, ball games, music
 therapy and garden walks. Care staff encourage people to share hobbies, spend time together and
 build friendships, creating a strong sense of community. They helped a person who had recently
 moved into the service to settle and develop a friendship. The visible manager promotes an open,
 positive culture. People, representatives, care staff and visiting professionals spoke positively about
 the service. Well-trained care staff know people well and use detailed personal plans and risk
 assessments to guide person-centred support. The manager and dementia champions support
 good dementia care. The service also works effectively with health and social care professionals.

 Care staff treat people with dignity and respect and support them to enjoy living at Maes Llewelyn.
 They encourage friendships, shared hobbies and mutual support. Managers remain accessible,
 while care staff listen, provide clear information and help people make informed choices. The
 provider makes key information, including its complaints process, available in Welsh. People and
 their representatives told us they take part in decisions that affect them, and we saw care staff act
 on their views. We heard care staff and people speaking Welsh. Care staff enter the reality of
 people living with dementia and respond without judgement. These practices help people feel
 valued and included.

 The provider’s systems help care staff identify and address risks promptly. Safeguarding and
 whistleblowing policies guide care staff in protecting people from abuse and neglect. The
 management team audits the environment, care practices and medication, with oversight from the
 Responsible Individual (RI). The maintenance team completes regular fire safety, infection control
 and water temperature checks. Individual and environmental risk assessments identify hazards and
 appropriate controls. These arrangements help care staff keep people safe.

 People live in accommodation that meets their needs and enhances their quality of life. They
 personalise their bedrooms, while welcoming communal spaces support social contact and privacy.
 Dementia-friendly features, including memory boxes and colour-coded tableware, help people
 orientate themselves and take part in meaningful activity. People said they are happy living at the
 service. Relatives and visiting professionals also praised the environment and support. The
 provider uses comprehensive health and safety systems to maintain a safe, comfortable home.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care staff know people well and consistently provide good-quality care and support. A core team
has worked at the service for many years. We saw skilled care staff use their knowledge of
people’s individual needs and preferences to provide person-centred support. The experienced and
caring management team supports care staff well and promotes a positive culture. We found care
staff placing great importance on ensuring people recently moved into the home are put at ease
and encouraged to develop friendships with others. This people told us had made a significant
difference to their lives.

Care staff support people to follow their preferred routines and daily structure. We saw care staff
respond flexibly to people’s needs and choices about how they wished to spend their day. The
service is calm and welcoming, and we saw many positive interactions between people and care
staff. People and their representatives spoke positively about managers and care staff.
Representatives said, “the staff are warm and friendly” and “I feel this home is an extension of my
family”. People said, “the staff are wonderful and so kind” and “the manager will do anything for us”.

People’s personal plans tell care staff how to support them to live well at the service. The provider
gathers useful information from professionals and others involved in people’s care and support.
The plans record people’s preferences, routines, interests, life histories and important relationships.
They use positive language and give care staff clear guidance about how each person wishes to
receive support. The provider reviews personal plans regularly. However, the reviews do not
always show people’s progress towards their outcomes or how people and their representatives
contribute to the process.

The provider protects people from harm and abuse. Open communication promotes a strong
safeguarding culture. Care staff receive appropriate training and follow clear policies and
procedures. We found the provider protects people’s liberty in line with legislation and current
practice. Detailed individual and environmental risk assessments help care staff keep people safe.

Care staff manage people’s medicines safely and understand each person’s prescribed treatment.
Relevant training, regular practice audits and a clear medication policy support staff. Recent
internal and external audits found that care staff follow safe and consistent practices. Care staff
organise and store medicines in line with requirements, which protects their safety and
effectiveness.

       Environment                         Good



People can choose from a range of communal and private spaces where they can spend time
alone, socialise or entertain visitors. The service comprises five smaller units, each with
comfortable lounge and dining areas. One unit specialises in supporting people living with
dementia. Where needed, people had personalised memory boxes outside their bedrooms to help
them find their rooms. Bedroom doors also displayed people’s names clearly. Communal spaces
support people’s independence and provide opportunities for private meetings, activities and
recreation. An additional sun and garden room offers a pleasant space for activities and family
gatherings.

We saw people enjoying the pleasant, enclosed and accessible gardens. One person said, “I love
sitting in the garden listening to the birds”. Artwork and photographs of the local area displayed in
communal corridors may help prompt people’s memories. We saw clean, well-presented and
personalised bedrooms. The service provider supplies suitable furnishings and equipment to meet
people’s needs, including specialist beds, call systems, moving and handling equipment, and
access to communication facilities. The design of bathrooms, showers and toilets promotes privacy,
dignity, safety and accessibility. These features help people feel comfortable and at home.

The service provider identifies and manages risks to people. We found detailed individual and
environmental risk assessments, supported by regular audits and reviews of records. Regular
servicing and maintenance, together with prompt repairs, help keep people safe and well. A
dedicated, experienced on-site maintenance officer keeps the premises in good condition and
maintains well-organised records.

The service provider ensures the premises meet current health and safety and environmental
health legislation, national guidance, and relevant Food Standards Agency standards. We saw
evidence that the Fire Service and external fire safety consultants had inspected the premises. The
provider has completed most actions and has plans in place to address the few that remain. Most
personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) were up to date, and the manager was updating
the small number that remained outstanding.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People achieve consistently good outcomes because the provider ensures care staff have the
skills, knowledge and support to meet their needs. The provider has effective recruitment and
induction processes in place. An experienced, long-standing core staff team mentors and guides
less experienced care staff. We saw care staff go the extra mile to cover current vacancies and
staff absences. Line managers support staff through one-to-one supervision sessions. These
sessions take place at least quarterly, alongside an annual review. Care staff receive relevant
training that reflects the care needs of the people they support.

People have high levels of confidence in the provider because leaders promote a positive,
supportive, inclusive and respectful culture. We found the manager approachable and accessible
and saw them regularly supporting people and care staff on the floor. The manager operates an
open-door policy, which helps people, representatives, visitors and professionals feel comfortable
raising concerns and confident that leaders will address them appropriately. The manager told us
the RI supports them well and visits the service regularly. People, care staff, relatives and visiting
professionals gave very positive feedback. Relatives told us, “Staff here are all lovely” and “it’s
amazing here”. People said, “It doesn’t matter what you want, they get it for you” and “the manager
is so caring”.

The provider uses effective quality-monitoring systems to maintain high standards of care. We saw
leaders act as visible role models and involve people, care staff and visiting professionals in quality
assurance processes. Leaders value feedback and use it to drive continuous improvement. The
provider has robust, well-embedded governance arrangements. The RI completes comprehensive
quarterly and six-monthly reports with support from the internal quality assurance team. The
provider has a clear, relevant complaints policy that people can access easily. People,
representatives and professionals told us they feel confident giving feedback or raising concerns
because leaders welcome and respond to them in a spirit of partnership.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
