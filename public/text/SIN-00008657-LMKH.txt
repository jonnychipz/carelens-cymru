Summary:

 People’s well-being requires improvement as they are living in a home which does not consistently
 promote their well-being. Choices are not always given as there is not always enough care staff on
 duty. Shortcomings in the quality and maintenance of the living environment, are having a negative
 impact on people’s comfort, dignity and overall quality of life. There is little to occupy peoples time
 and no outings into the community due to staff shortages. Staff morale is low as they do not feel
 valued.

 Care and support require significant improvement as people are not consistently supported to
 achieve positive outcomes due to shortcomings in the dining experience, nutrition, activities
 provision, staffing arrangements, care planning, risk management and Welsh language provision.
 Insufficient staffing arrangements limit opportunities for meaningful engagement, supervision and
 the delivery of care that reflects people's individual needs, preferences and rights.

The environment requires improvement. The premises are not being maintained to a suitable
standard and do not currently support the delivery of safe, dignified care. The service provider has
failed to ensure environmental risks are consistently identified, assessed and managed. This
includes risks relating to access to hazardous areas, external exits, boiler rooms and trip hazards.
The provider must ensure people live in a safe, clean and well-maintained environment where risks
are effectively controlled, and infection prevention and control standards are consistently
maintained.

Leadership and management require significant improvement. The Responsible Individual (RI) has
not ensured effective governance, oversight or organisational arrangements to support the safe
and effective operation of the service or the delivery of consistently high-quality care. Staff
recruitment is not robust and there is not enough staff employed in the home.

Findings:

        Well-being                 Requires Improvement



 People do not consistently have control over their day-to-day lives. The provider does not use a
 dependency assessment tool to determine whether staffing levels are sufficient to meet people’s
 needs. People told us they experience delays in receiving care and support. Opportunities to
 participate in meaningful activities are limited due to staffing pressures, reducing people’s choice
 and control over how they spend their time. Meal choices are recorded after meals are served, and
 refreshments are not routinely offered throughout the day. We are not assured people’s views are
 meaningfully sought or acted upon. The RI’s discussions with people are generalised and do not
 demonstrate effective engagement or oversight of people’s experiences.

 People do not always receive care and support that is responsive to their changing needs. Care
 records do not consistently reflect people’s current needs and there is no effective ongoing
 assessment process to identify and respond to changes. People told us care staff are kind and
 work hard to support them. Referrals and appointments are arranged to promote people’s health
 and well-being. However, there is not enough care staff deployed to ensure people are monitored
 to ensure their safety and well-being. Medication management practices also require improvement.
 As a result, people cannot be assured they consistently receive care and treatment that meets their
 assessed needs.

 Systems to protect people from harm require improvement. Governance and oversight
 arrangements have failed to identify a range of significant concerns found during this inspection,
 exposing people and staff to avoidable risks. Management are not involved in recruitment of new
 staff and pre-employment checks are not always carried out by the provider to ensure staff are
 suitable and fit to work with adults at risk. The provider has not demonstrated effective systems to
 consistently identify, assess and mitigate potential harm. Whilst care staff have received
 safeguarding training and appropriate policies are in place, these arrangements are undermined by
 weaknesses in recruitment, care planning, risk management, staffing levels and management
 oversight. The registered manager is routinely included in staffing numbers, reducing their capacity
 to effectively monitor practice and drive improvement.

 People do not always live in accommodation that consistently meets their needs or promotes their
 well-being. There are insufficient domestic staffing hours to maintain the home to a consistently
 clean and hygienic standard. Security access in and out the home requires improvement and,
 whilst routine safety checks are completed, these do not provide assurance that the environment is
 safe, suitable and supports positive outcomes for people. Although some improvements have been
 made since the last inspection, including new carpeting and limited redecoration, the environment
 requires further investment to ensure it is comfortable. A comprehensive environmental audit is
 needed to identify and address areas where standards fall short. Infection prevention and control

       Care & Support
                                       Improvement


People are assessed before moving into the home and personal plans are developed using
information from people, their representatives and relevant professionals. However, care records
are not always reviewed and updated to reflect people's current needs. We found inaccuracies and
inconsistencies within care records. For example, one person's records referred to dentures and
associated oral care support despite them no longer wearing dentures. A mobility care plan had not
been updated to reflect changes in need, and a nutritional personal plan contained conflicting
instructions regarding the use of fluid thickener. Risk assessments are not consistently in place to
support the safe management of identified risks. We found limited evidence of assessments
relating to bed rails, floor sensors, relationships between people living in the service, and health
conditions requiring specialist support. As a result, care staff may not have sufficient guidance to
provide safe and consistent care.

The lunchtime experience is not consistently person-centred. Menus are not displayed, meal
choices are not always recorded before meals are served, and drink options are limited. Tables are
not fully prepared, with no napkins available. Limited staffing reduces opportunities for engagement
and supervision during mealtimes. People have previously raised concerns about the lack of
dessert variety in a residents meeting, which has not been addressed.

The provider does not ensure people have sufficient opportunities to take part in meaningful
activities or maintain community links. No organised activities took place during the inspection
despite being scheduled. People tell us outings have not occurred for some time and activities
within the home do not meet their needs. These concerns had previously been raised at residents'
meetings, but there is no evidence of effective action being taken. The lack of consistency in care
documentation, risk assessments, mealtime experience, activities and lack of staff engagement is
placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority action notice.
The provider must take immediate action to address this issue.

Improvements are required in medication management. Staff complete appropriate training and
competency assessments, and people generally received their prescribed medicines. However,
refrigerator temperatures for stored medicines are not monitored, some prescribed creams have
illegible labels or opening dates from January 2026, and creams are accessible within bedrooms.
Medication records contain gaps, medicines appear to be checked by only one member of staff on
receipt, and there is limited evidence of follow-up action where medicines have been refused for
prolonged periods. These findings do not demonstrate sufficiently robust medicines management
arrangements. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the provider to take action.

The home does not consistently maintain appropriate standards of hygiene and infection control.
Staff complete infection control training, one domestic staff member is employed and works three
mornings per week, with cleaning responsibilities otherwise undertaken by care staff.

We found a number of environmental concerns, including a bathroom without suitable hand-drying
facilities and a visibly unclean laundry room. Dirty laundry was stored in a bath and had to be
transported through the dining room to access the laundry area, increasing the risk of cross-
contamination. Cleaning equipment such as mops are significantly dirty and not used in
accordance with colour-coding procedures and sinks and a call bell requires cleaning. The laundry
room is visibly unclean with no washable paintwork on the walls. The service received a Food
Hygiene Rating of 1 (Major Improvement Necessary) in April 2026. The provider has since made
improvements and requested a re-inspection.

       Environment               Requires Improvement




The home has benefited from a number of environmental improvements; however, further work is
required. People told us they are generally happy with their surroundings, but we found the
provider's approach to maintenance is largely reactive, with action often taken only after concerns
have been raised by others. This raises questions about the effectiveness of the provider's
oversight and monitoring of the service. Since the previous inspection, new carpets have been
installed in some areas of the home and furniture and beds in the poorest condition have been
replaced. However, concerns remain regarding the condition and suitability of some bedroom
furnishings, including damaged bed frames. These issues had previously been raised with
management by family members and professionals. We also found a recently decorated bedroom
had not been finished to an acceptable standard, with patchwork paintwork that did not match the
existing décor.

The provider does not consistently identify, assess and manage health and safety risks within the
service. Appropriate systems are in place to monitor visitors entering and leaving the home, routine
fire safety checks are generally completed, servicing and maintenance records for gas and
electrical systems are up to date, and people have individual Personal Emergency Evacuation
Plans. However, we identified several health and safety concerns during the inspection. A
homemade wooden step leading from the dining room to the courtyard was damaged, with broken
timber along the edge, and lacked both a graduated ramp and handrails. Warning signs are not
consistently displayed when cleaning activities create slip hazards. The external door from the
dining room remained unalarmed for much of the morning, providing unrestricted access to an
external area and the route used for transporting soiled laundry. This meant people could
potentially access/leave the area unsupervised. We also found weaknesses in access control
arrangements. Although visitor checks are undertaken on arrival, the inspector and another visitor
were admitted to the home by a visitor rather than a member of staff. In addition, the inspection
identified an out-of-date fire extinguisher safety check in the laundry room, with records showing
the last inspection had been completed in 2024. These have been identified as areas for
improvement, and we expect the provider to take action. We will review these areas in the next
inspection.

                                    Requires Significant
       Leadership & Management
                                       Improvement


Arrangements for the oversight and governance of the service require significant strengthening to
ensure effective quality assurance, accountability and continuous improvement. The manager and
deputy manager are present within the home on most days and provide direct support to people
when required, this has impacted on their ability to fulfil key management responsibilities. The
manager told us they do not have sufficient time to complete essential management duties,
including audits, quality assurance activities, oversight of care records and monitoring of service
performance. Governance systems are not operating effectively to identify, monitor and address
areas of concern. The Statement of Purpose does not accurately reflect the current management
and staffing arrangements within the service. In addition, the provider has failed to publish the
service's annual return and quality ratings as required. The provider must ensure the Statement of
Purpose is updated to reflect the service as delivered and that all required information, including
the annual return and service ratings, are published and made available in accordance with
regulatory requirements. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have
therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to address this
issue.

We found a significant number of care records, risk assessments and staff records containing
sensitive personal information stored in an unlocked garden shed. The shed also contained
gardening equipment and decorating materials. Confidential information was not stored securely
and was accessible to unauthorised individuals, placing personal data at risk. A number of issues
identified during this inspection, including staffing pressures, shortcomings in care planning,
storage of confidential information, medication management, activities provision, infection
prevention and control, and environmental risks. Staff morale appears low and staff told us they do
not feel valued and raised concerns regarding employment practices, including the absence of
enhanced payments for bank holidays and perceived inequalities in the allocation of contracted
hours. Some staff reported hours are prioritised for overseas workers with sponsored contracts,
which had contributed to dissatisfaction within the workforce.

The RI completes three-monthly visits and produces reports detailing their findings. However, these
reports do not evidence meaningful consultation with people living in the home, their
representatives or staff. The reports contain generalised statements regarding satisfaction with the
service but do not record who was consulted or the views expressed. This limits the provider's
ability to demonstrate that people's experiences are informing service development and
improvement. The RI also produces six-monthly quality of care review reports. Whilst these reports
contain information relating to the service, they do not demonstrate robust analysis of complaints,
audits, incidents, safeguarding concerns, staffing issues or statutory notifications. As a result, there
is limited evidence that trends are being identified, lessons are being learned, or clear actions are
being implemented to drive improvement. This is placing people’s health and well-being at risk, and

we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The provider must take immediate action to
address this issue.

People are not consistently supported by a sufficient number of suitably recruited and vetted staff.
Staff receive training and regular supervision. The manager told us they have no responsibility for
recruiting staff. New staff are deployed to the service by the provider through one of their other
companies. We found the provider does not always obtain satisfactory references and completed
application forms before staff commence employment. In addition, Disclosure and Barring Service
checks are not consistently obtained in accordance with safe recruitment requirements.

Care staff are required to undertake multiple roles during their shifts, including providing personal
care, completing domestic and laundry duties, preparing and serving evening meals after the cook
had finished work, supervising people in communal areas and facilitating activities where time
permitted. These competing demands limited opportunities for staff to provide meaningful
engagement, social interaction and person-centred care. People and staff told us there are
insufficient staff available within the home. Staff described difficulties balancing care tasks with
domestic and catering responsibilities, whilst people told us they often had to wait for assistance
and had no opportunities to participate in activities or access the local community. This is placing
people’s health and well-being at risk, and we have therefore issued a priority action notice. The
provider must take immediate action to address this issue.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
