Summary


Children are making good progress at Ty Banwen. They attend school and see people who
are important in their lives. Children’s health needs are known and understood, and action
is taken to ensure they receive the support they need and have opportunities to engage in
activities of their choosing.

The home has a team of care staff who work across two services and are led by a manager
who oversees two homes operated by the service provider. The manager ensures they are
available for children and care staff and care staff told us they were accessible and
responsive to support the service to operate effectively. The environment is clean, warm,
welcoming and well maintained.

The Responsible Individual (RI) visits regularly to complete quality assurance checks and
considers the quality of service provided. A review of the quality of care ensures the
ongoing development and improvement of the service to support children achieve the best
possible outcomes.

--- PAGE 4 ---
Well-being


Children are consulted about their care and can voice their opinions, preferences, likes and
dislikes. Their views are requested and considered through a number of ways including
monthly online surveys, keyworking sessions and house meetings. Children were observed
to have positive, warm relationships with care staff who genuinely care for them and are
committed to improving their lives. Children told us they participate in activities of their
choice and spend time doing what they enjoy and care staff encourage them to attend clubs
and try new sports. Children have been active in raising money for charity by holding bake
sales supported by care staff.

Children are registered with health services including GP, opticians, dentists and attend
routine appointments. Care staff ensure they receive the care and support they need to
promote their physical and emotional health and well-being. Children are supported to
attend mainstream school and have education plans in place.

The environment supports children’s overall well-being. The home is clean, tidy, and
suitable for children. The building is safe from unauthorised access and well maintained.
Visitors are requested to sign in and out of the service.

Care staff report they work in a positive environment, with a strong staff team and feel
supported and listened to by seniors and management. There is a children’s guide to living
in the home, this gives a detailed guide for young people about the rules of the home, their
entitlements, and how to make a complaint. Children can be assured they are cared for by
care staff who understand their safeguarding responsibilities, all care staff have completed
their safeguarding training and there is an up-to-date safeguarding policy. Children have
access to an advocate, should they wish to use this service.

--- PAGE 5 ---
Care and Support


Children are well presented and observed to be comfortable in their surroundings. They are
supported to spend time with their families and those that are important to them and care
staff told us children’s friends come to the home. Children are supported to attend clubs
including cadets and they take part in activities including building models with care staff,
baking, fishing, mountain biking, skateboarding and swimming with friends. Children are
supported to have pets and encouraged to take responsibility for feeding and cleaning fish
tanks.

Trauma informed positive behaviour plans and impact risk assessments are developed to
provide comprehensive information to support children’s behaviour, these are updated
regularly and signed by children’s social workers. Child friendly personal plans are
completed, and copies are provided to children. Provider assessments are in place, they
detail children’s personal outcomes and individual risks, these are reviewed in line with
regulations. Keyworking sessions take place regularly and topics discussed cover areas to
improve outcomes and support children to meet identified targets.

Feedback from social workers is positive, ‘They demonstrate a sound ability to follow the
children's respective care plans and understand their needs. Ty Banwen demonstrate
flexibility to adapt to any presenting behaviours.’ Incidents and physical interventions are
minimal, where these have occurred, they have been low level, debriefs with children and
care staff take place and records of these events are sufficiently detailed and have
management oversight.

Staff understand their duty to report safeguarding matters and children told us they feel safe
and are clear about the systems in place to achieve this. Records were seen where children
were requested to provide receipts for purchases and money spent on activities, the RI
gave assurances that they had recently become aware of this and had already taken action
to ensure this practice does not continue.

--- PAGE 6 ---
 Environment


Children benefit from an environment which is suitable for their needs. The home is clean,
light, warm and welcoming with a cozy feel. The home is well decorated and maintained
and furniture and fittings are of good quality.

There is sufficient communal space for staff to play and eat together, and the living room is
decorated in colours linked to children’s interests. Children can personalise their bedrooms
and are given choices on the colours, their bedrooms reflect their interests and are
decorated with photographs and personal items. Care staff told us ‘We try our best to run
like a normal family house for the young people rather than it being a work environment, by
doing this we have kept the house warm and welcoming with choice from the young people
of colours, pictures and even down to picking their own furniture.’ The kitchen was clean
and fit for purpose, it contained all the necessary appliances. The fridge was well stocked,
and food was appropriately labelled. The service has been awarded a food hygiene score
of four (good). The rear garden is an enclosed area, there is currently no outside storage,
but care staff told us that this has been actioned and they are awaiting maintenance to
complete this.

All necessary health and safety checks are in place and there is a clear filing system in
place. Gas and electricity safety testing is up to date. The fire risk assessment is on file and
records indicate regular checks are undertaken of fire systems. Fire drills take place with
staff and children to ensure they understand what action to take in an emergency and
recommendations from a fire service visit have been actioned. Checks are routinely
undertaken of visitors to the home and their identification checked.

--- PAGE 7 ---
Leadership and Management


The statement of purpose for the home accurately describes how the service is delivered.
Care staff work between two services located close to each other, they told us they enjoy
this and feel working between two services supports their personal development. Care staff
told us they have a strong team, they feel listened to, supported by management, training
provided helps them to meet the needs of the children they provide care for and that ‘Ty
Banwen is an amazing place to work with lots of opportunities to progress within the
company’.

Children can be assured they are cared for by staff who have been subject to safe
recruitment checks. Recruitment files we viewed were well organised and included all the
required information and checks. Care staff complete a range of mandatory and additional
training to support their skills development. Newly employed care staff complete the
service’s induction programme and the Social Care Wales (SCW) induction framework and
are encouraged and supported to gain relevant qualifications. Care staff remain in probation
until the SCW induction framework is completed.

Team meetings take place monthly; records of these meetings are detailed and actions
from the last meeting are followed up. Care staff receive regular supervision. The
complaints process is clear, the records are robust and the outcomes are recorded. The
office and files are well organised and there are clear systems in place. Files are not
separately locked away in cupboards, however, the office door is kept locked. Systems are
in place to protect children and keep them safe and care staff have attended safeguarding
training.

There is strong and effective leadership and oversight of the service. The RI visits the
service and completes quality assurance reports in line with regulations and there are
appropriate arrangements in place to support the operation of the service. RI reports are
comprehensive and detailed, they consult with children as well as seeking feedback from
care staff and other professionals. Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) receive notifications in
line with regulations.
