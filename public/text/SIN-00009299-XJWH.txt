Summary:

 Bryngwy Residential Home provides residential care for older adults in Rhayader in Powys. It is
 situated in a rural setting in Powys.

 Well-being is good. People are supported to maintain their physical, mental health, and emotional
 well-being. People speak positively about the service and the care they receive as they have
 effective working relationships with care staff.

 Care and support are good. Care staff develop and maintain personalised care plans and risk
 assessments which provide clear information about people’s needs, preferences and outcomes.

 Leadership and management are good. The service benefits from effective leadership and
 management arrangements which support positive outcomes for people. Leaders have a good
 understanding of the service and have established systems to review quality, monitor performance
 and identify areas for improvement.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. People direct their care and support
 and can voice their opinions on the service. Care staff engage with people making sure they have
 choice and control in any decisions. Risk assessments are detailed and provide clear guidance for
 care staff to follow.

 People are supported to maintain their physical, mental health, and emotional well-being. Personal
 plans are strength based and individualised, focussing on people’s physical and mental health, and
 emotional well-being.

 We found care staff to be respectful and helpful. Relationships are trusted, respectful and with good
 humour. They receive good training in areas such as falls prevention, medication and maintaining
 good hydration and nutrition. We saw the service has established strong links with health and
 social care professionals, drawing on their expertise when needed.


 People speak positively about the service and the care they receive as they have effective working
 relationships with care staff. We saw care staff following personal plans, explaining what they were
 doing and checking if it was okay to do so.

 Care staff are recruited safely and robust background checks are completed prior to employment
 starting. All new care staff engage in a period of induction and shadowing with experienced
 colleagues and the service provider has comprehensive policies and procedures in place to support
 this.

 People are supported to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. People are encouraged to attend
 events and occasions in the community. Relatives and representatives are encouraged to visit the
 service and to be involved in their care and support.

 People live in accommodation which supports their well-being outcomes. Care staff maintain a
 warm, welcoming and well-maintained environment. People have access to spacious communal
 areas, private bedrooms and secure gardens where they can relax and socialise.

 There are thorough safeguarding procedures in place which staff understand. Risk assessments
 provide clarity and consistent guidance and information to care staff. These adapt and are updated
 around the needs of people being supported, with care staff having access to ongoing internal and
 external specialist support. We found care staff to be caring and respectful.

 We were told that the service was recruiting an activities coordinator to arrange trips, activities and
 events for the people at the service. The advert was posted while we were carrying out our

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve good outcomes. Care staff
develop and maintain personalised care plans and risk assessments which provide clear
information about people’s support needs, preferences and outcomes. Personal plans focus on
physical health, mental health and emotional well-being. Care staff involve people and, where
appropriate, their relatives or representatives in care planning.

Personal plans are easy to read and provide care staff with clear information to enable them to
consistently provide good quality care. Care planning information is easily available to care staff
and includes peoples’ history, preferences, important relationships, relevant professionals, routines
and beliefs.

People and their relatives told us they are involved in assessments and review meetings. The
service actively encourages families and representatives to be involved in reviews where
appropriate, helping to ensure support remains relevant and responsive to people's needs.
However, we did not see any evidence of records of well-being goals and outcomes and who is
involved in review meetings. We did see evidence of involvement from healthcare professionals,
including GPs, district nurses and specialist services, when additional support or advice was
required.

People experience care and support which is dignified and respectful and we saw numerous
meaningful interactions with care staff. We saw people being skilfully supported by well-trained
care staff. People benefit from a consistent care staff team with a detailed understanding of their
individual needs and preferences. Overall people are positive on the care they receive telling us
“It’s very clean, I like it here. I’ve got nothing to complain about.” and “Yes, I couldn’t be in a better
place,” Relatives are also very positive about the service being provided.

People are protected from harm and abuse. People are supported by highly skilled care staff with
an excellent understanding of their individual needs and preferences. We saw a motivated,
experienced team who were keen to make a significant difference to people’s lives. There are
thorough risk assessments in place. The service provides effective safeguarding training. Policy
and procedures are in place and these are followed by the care staff. One member of staff told us,
“……. The overall level of care is good. People are happy.”

People’s medication is safely managed. There are good medication management protocols in
place. There are secure arrangements for storing, ordering, administering and disposing of
medication. Care staff have training relating to medication management and their competency to
administer medication is regularly assessed. There is no evidence of any recent mistakes or people
missing their medication.

People’s risk of infection is minimised. There are consistent infection, prevention and control
measures in place. There are good supplies of personal protective equipment (PPE) made

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment which is comfortable, safe and suitable for their needs. The home
provides a welcoming, warm, homely atmosphere. The service is a converted country house
situated in its own grounds with pleasant gardens including views over the surrounding countryside.
The service has accommodation set over two floors. There is a lift which enables access to the
upper floor.

There is a conservatory overlooking the rear gardens. It is currently being used as a storeroom. We
were told there are plans to further develop the conservatory into a communal area and we look
forward to seeing what changes will be made in forthcoming inspections. The provider constantly
looks to improve the environment to enhance the well-being of the people who live there.

People can spend time independently or socially, according to their wishes. The environment
promotes people's well-being and enables them to maintain their independence whilst receiving the
support they need.

During the inspection, we found communal areas were clean, tidy and well maintained. Bedrooms
are personalised with photographs, ornaments and personal possessions, helping people maintain
a sense of identity and belonging. People had access to a variety of communal spaces where they
could relax, socialise, meet visitors or participate in activities. The layout of the home supported
people to move around safely and independently wherever possible.

People told us they are happy with their surroundings and felt comfortable living at the service. The
atmosphere within the home is calm, relaxed and welcoming. We saw people choosing where they
wished to spend their time throughout the day and moving freely between communal areas. People
appeared familiar with their surroundings and comfortable accessing different parts of the home.

The service has a small outdoor area which is well maintained. This space provides opportunities
for people to spend time outdoors, socialise, eat with relatives and enjoy fresh air when they
choose. Appropriate measures are in place to promote people's comfort and safety whilst outdoors.

Overall, the environment is good. People have access to a variety of different communal and
private spaces in which to spend time alone, socialise or entertain their visitors. The providers are
investing in the service and improvements are being made to improve the environment.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from effective leadership and management arrangements which support
positive outcomes for people. Leaders have a good understanding of the service and have
established systems to review quality, monitor performance and identify areas for improvement.
There is a positive culture within the home, with a clear focus on supporting people to achieve the
best possible outcomes. An experienced manager leads the service and receives support from a
skilled team of care staff.

The service provider has strict selection and vetting processes ensuring all care staff are qualified
and trustworthy. Staff personnel files contain the appropriate information ensuring they are safe
and fit to work at the service. Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks are in place and
current. Care staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. We found staff
human resource files to be a good standard and well organised with all the appropriate information
made easily available for us to view.

Staff undergo routine and regular checks ensuring they remain suitably fit to work in the service
and are registered with professional bodies. New staff complete an effective induction programme
which includes training and shadowing experienced colleagues. Managers complete checks to
ensure new staff are competent in undertaking their roles. Care staff are appropriately trained and
supervised by the manager.

The provider seeks and values the views of people, their relatives and care staff. Quality assurance
reports demonstrate consultation and engagement with representatives. People told us they feel
involved in the running of the home and believe care staff listen to their views.

The provider fosters a supportive and inclusive culture where staff are respected and well
supported to develop in their career. Care workers are well supported through regular
communication with managers, ongoing supervision and continuous training.


Care staff spoke positively about the management team and consistently described managers as
approachable, supportive and visible within the service. Care staff told us they feel able to raise
concerns, seek advice and discuss ideas openly. Many spoke positively about the culture within the
home and described feeling valued, respected and supported in their roles. We found care staff
worked well together and demonstrated a shared commitment to providing good quality care. Care
staff consistently told us they have sufficient time to engage in meaningful conversations and
activities with people, helping to promote positive relationships and person-centred care rather than
focusing solely on tasks.

Overall, quality monitoring systems operate effectively. Internal and external audits demonstrate
high levels of compliance and satisfaction. Care staff and people living at the service feel confident

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
