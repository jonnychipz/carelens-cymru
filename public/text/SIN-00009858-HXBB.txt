Summary:

 Ty Mynydd is located in Neath and provides personal care and support to people. It is close to a
 number of community facilities which can be easily accessed from the service. The service has
 made significant progress since the previous inspection and people are now benefiting from
 improved outcomes across the service. There are notable improvements in care planning, staffing
 and staff support, governance systems and environmental safety.

 Well-being at the service is good and people receive personalised care and support that reflects
 their individual needs, preferences and aspirations. People have access to a wider range of
 meaningful opportunities and are supported by care staff who understand their communication and
 support needs.

 Improvements have been made to care and support and personal plans are detailed, regularly
 reviewed and provide staff with clear guidance to support people safely and effectively. Further
 improvements are required to ensure all records are completed consistently.

 The environment is good. Improvements identified at the previous inspection relating to health and
 safety monitoring and environmental checks have been addressed. People live in accommodation
 that supports their comfort, safety and well-being.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and safely with control over their lives. Since the last inspection, Ty Mynydd
 has made significant improvements in supporting people to achieve positive well-being outcomes.
 People are actively involved in making choices about their daily lives, including meals, activities
 and how they spend their time. A varied programme of meaningful activities is now in place, with
 people regularly accessing their local community and enjoying opportunities tailored to their
 interests. People’s communication needs are understood and supported well. Information is
 available in accessible formats, and the service demonstrates a positive commitment to ensuring
 people receive information in ways they can understand. We saw examples of personalised
 approaches to support people with specific communication needs. The service is working towards
 the Welsh active offer. A number of care staff employed are Welsh speakers, and the service will
 provide documentation in Welsh if requested.

 There are systems in place to ensure people are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. Care
 staff receive safeguarding training and those spoken with have a clear understanding of
 safeguarding procedures and are confident in raising concerns. Notifications and incidents have
 been appropriately reported, investigated and reviewed, with evidence of lessons learned and
 improvements implemented.

 Safe and healthy relationships are promoted, and people are encouraged to maintain meaningful
 relationships with those who are important to them. Records evidenced regular contact with family
 members and relatives provided positive feedback about the quality of care and communication
 from the service. A relative told us “staff are kind, knowledgeable and caring”. Care staff know
 people well and demonstrate a good understanding of their individual communication styles,
 preferences and support needs. Personal plans reflect people's social networks, interests and
 aspirations, helping staff provide personalised support. Records seen show people spending time
 in their local community and we saw people accessing the community throughout the day of the
 inspection. This demonstrates a commitment by the provider to ensuring people lead fulfilling lives
 and remain connected to the people and places that matter to them.

 The accommodation supports people to achieve their well-being outcomes. The environment at Ty
 Mynydd has improved considerably since the previous inspection and now provides a safe,
 comfortable and welcoming place for people to live. Improvement works are underway, including
 bathroom upgrades and enhancements to external areas, demonstrating the provider's
 commitment to continuous development. Environmental audits, maintenance checks and safety
 monitoring systems are in place and being completed consistently.

       Care & Support            Requires Improvement




People receive the quality of care and support they need to achieve their personal outcomes.
Improvements have been made since the previous inspection and people's personal plans are now
more detailed, outcome-focused and supported by individual risk assessments. Records
demonstrate a better understanding of people's communication needs, specific support
requirements and health conditions. However, improvements have not yet been embedded
consistently across the service. While some care records are comprehensive and evidenced good-
quality support, we found gaps in daily recordings, nutritional monitoring and personal care
documentation for one person. Activity records also show periods where engagement was limited
or not clearly evidenced. The inconsistencies in recording and evidence of care delivery mean we
cannot be fully assured that good outcomes are achieved consistently for everyone living at the
service. Outcomes for people require improvement because records do not consistently evidence
care and support is provided in line with individuals’ assessed needs, personal outcomes and
preferences, and we expect the provider to make improvements. We have discussed this with the
provider who is committed to making continued improvements.

People are protected from harm and abuse. Safeguarding systems are in place and staff
demonstrate an understanding of their responsibilities to recognise and report concerns. Staff
described management as "supportive" and told us they feel confident raising concerns and
managers would take appropriate action. Care staff training records show they receive
safeguarding training which is updated routinely. Some areas of practice indicate that safeguarding
and risk management arrangements require further strengthening. We noted that the front gate was
unsecured on arrival to the service, despite people living at the home requiring support to remain
safe. The provider has assured us measures will be taken to prevent the re-occurrence of this.

There are systems in place to ensure medication is safely managed. Medication management has
improved since the last inspection. Medication audits are undertaken, staff are trained and
competency assessed, and previous concerns regarding medication administration have largely
been addressed. However, on the day of inspection we saw a medication administration
authorisation record was out of date and there were no review dates seen. The provider has since
obtained an updated authorisation. We also found gaps in medication temperature monitoring
records. Outcomes for people may be compromised because medication management systems
require strengthening and we expect the provider to make improvements. The provider
acknowledges these concerns and demonstrates a commitment to address them.

People’s risk of infection is minimised. The service is clean, tidy and generally well maintained.
Improvements identified at the previous inspection regarding environmental monitoring have been
addressed, reducing identified infection control risks. Staff have access to personal protective
equipment and training compliance has improved.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment with appropriate and well-maintained facilities and equipment to help
them achieve their well-being outcomes while identifying, mitigating and reducing health and safety
risks. People live in a comfortable, spacious and homely environment that is well suited to their
individual needs. The service provides a range of communal and private spaces that promote
choice, independence and well-being. Bedrooms are personalised and reflect people's interests
and preferences. Communal areas are welcoming, clean and accessible, enabling people to spend
time together or independently according to their wishes. The service continues to invest in
improving the environment, with refurbishment works underway, including upgrades to a bathroom
and enhancements to outdoor areas. Improvements to the garden and boundary areas are
supporting safer access to outdoor space whilst maintaining opportunities for recreation and
relaxation.

The service is clean, clutter free and well-presented. The atmosphere throughout the home is calm
and homely, enabling people to spend time in ways that suit their individual preferences and
routines. There is a clear commitment to providing people with a pleasant place to live that
supports both their comfort and well-being outcomes. Photographs, personal belongings and
decorative features help create surroundings that reflect the interests of people who live there.
Equipment and furnishings are suitable for purpose and support people to remain comfortable and
independent.

Effective arrangements are in place to ensure the environment remains safe, well maintained and
fit for purpose. Since the previous inspection, significant improvements have been made in relation
to health and safety monitoring and environmental oversight. The provider has addressed previous
concerns regarding inconsistent health and safety checks, with evidence of routine maintenance
and monitoring systems now being completed regularly and effectively. Environmental audits,
maintenance checks and servicing arrangements provide assurance that facilities and equipment
are maintained in good working order. Emergency procedures are well established, and care staff
have clear guidance on supporting people safely in the event of an incident or emergency.

The home benefits from dedicated maintenance support, enabling repairs and improvements to be
addressed promptly. We spoke to a member of the maintenance team who has a good
understanding of the service and feels supported by the leadership team in addressing repairs and
planned improvements. Records show checks relating to environmental safety, infection prevention
and equipment maintenance are being completed and monitored. We found health and safety risks
are appropriately identified and managed, contributing to a safer environment for people and care
staff.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People are supported to achieve their outcomes because the service provider has effective
organisational arrangements, governance and oversight to ensure smooth operations and high-
quality care. Since the previous inspection, Ty Mynydd has undergone a significant period of
positive transformation, driven by strengthened leadership, improved oversight and a clear
commitment to achieving sustainable improvements for people. The service has responded well to
Priority Action Notices issued following the previous inspection, with leaders demonstrating a
strong understanding of the areas requiring improvement and implementing effective action plans
to address them.

Governance systems are focused on monitoring quality, identifying trends and driving service
development. Responsible Individual visits are thorough and reflective, evidencing clear analysis of
practice and meaningful actions to improve outcomes for people. Quality of Care Reviews are
completed within required timescales and increasingly focus on evaluating the lived experience of
people using the service, with feedback from relatives helping to shape ongoing improvements.
The introduction of robust monitoring systems has strengthened oversight of care records, health
and safety checks, staff performance and service delivery. The management team demonstrate a
clear vision for the service and discussed with us achievements and progress made as well as
areas still requiring development.

Care staff have the necessary expertise, skills, and qualifications to meet people’s care and
support needs. Safe recruitment practices are in place, with robust pre-employment and
background checks completed prior to staff starting work at Ty Mynydd. Ongoing checks are also
undertaken to ensure staff remain suitable to work within the service. The service has worked
proactively to strengthen workforce stability and reduce dependence on agency staff. Recruitment
initiatives have resulted in new staff joining the service, helping to provide people with greater
continuity and consistency of care. We saw there are regular team meetings held, and staff have
opportunities to discuss practice and service developments. Significant improvements have been
made in relation to staff training, supervision and appraisal processes, addressing concerns
identified at the previous inspection.

Staff receive consistent support, guidance and performance oversight, enabling them to develop
their knowledge and confidence to meet people's needs effectively. Care staff spoke positively
about working at Ty Mynydd and consistently described a supportive and inclusive culture. One
member of staff told us, “The managers are supportive and if I have any complaints, I have a
response,” whilst another described the service as “fantastic… a good place to work.”

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
