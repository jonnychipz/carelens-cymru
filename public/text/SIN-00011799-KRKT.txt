Summary:

 Ty Gwynno is a service providing nursing and residential care to older adults, including those who
 live with dementia. The service is located in a residential area to the north of Pontypridd.


 Well-being is excellent because people have exceptional support to achieve personal goals and
 maintain important relationships. A strong culture of inclusion, advocacy and positive risk-taking
 helps people enjoy meaningful experiences and achieve excellent well-being outcomes. Care and
 support is excellent because people receive highly personalised, outcome-focused care which is
 consistently reviewed and  delivered by  skilled care  staff. Robust  safeguarding, medication
 management and infection prevention systems help ensure people achieve positive outcomes
 safely. The environment is excellent because the service is thoughtfully designed to enhance well-
 being, independence and quality of life. Innovative facilities, ongoing investment and involvement
 from people help create a warm and homely environment. Leadership and management  is
 excellent because strong and  visible leaders promote a culture of continuous improvement,
 learning and innovation. Effective governance arrangements, strong staff support and a highly
 skilled workforce help ensure people consistently receive high-quality care and support.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 Well-being is excellent because people experience exceptional choice, control and involvement in
 their lives. They benefit from highly personalised support and outstanding opportunities to pursue
 meaningful interests and ambitions. People achieve excellent outcomes because care staff know
 them very well and support them to lead  lives which  reflect  their wishes, preferences and
 aspirations. Highly detailed, person-centred plans focus on individual outcomes and are reviewed
 regularly with people, relatives and professionals. People make choices about their daily routines,
 meals, activities and visitors and are actively involved in decisions about their care and support.
 Information is available in formats which meet their communication needs. Innovative advocacy
 arrangements help people’s voices to be heard, including measures such as resident advocates
 and relative advocates. The service provides exceptional opportunities through initiatives such as
 “Wishes and Dreams”, helping people achieve important personal goals and experiences. People
 enjoy opportunities through, for example, the service’s choir, woodwork workshop, lifestyle room,
 community café, walking groups and community outings. Welsh language and culture are strongly
 embedded throughout the service, with the service able to provide the Active Offer if required.


 Robust safeguarding arrangements and proactive oversight help people remain as safe as
 possible. Care staff understand their safeguarding responsibilities and act promptly when concerns
 arise. Risks are identified through regular assessments, reviews and monitoring, with people and
 relatives involved in risk management discussions. Strong partnership working with safeguarding
 and healthcare agencies, alongside extremely strong learning from incidents, promotes a culture of
 openness, safety and accountability.


 People are valued as individuals and supported to maintain important relationships, interests and
 identities. An inclusive culture promotes equality, respect and belonging. Strong community links
 provide opportunities for social inclusion and meaningful participation. Initiatives such as regular
 intergenerational activities with a visiting nursery, the resident advocate scheme and a wide range
 of leisure and learning opportunities help people maintain purpose and develop friendships.
 Positive risk-taking supports community involvement. People are supported to safely manage their
 finances.


 People live in accommodation which significantly enhances their well-being and quality of life. The
 home is warm, welcoming and personalised, with people encouraged to influence environmental
 developments and improvements. Appropriate equipment, adaptations and accessible design
 support people’s needs while promoting independence. Attractive outdoor spaces, access to

       Care & Support                           Excellent



Care and support is excellent because people receive highly personalised care which consistently
reflects their needs, preferences and aspirations. Care planning is highly detailed, person-centred
and outcome focused. Electronic care records provide clear guidance to help care staff support
people consistently. People, relatives and professionals are actively involved in planning and
reviewing care, with monthly reviews ensuring plans remain up to date. Personal plans reflect
people’s preferences,  strengths, goals and communication needs, while comprehensive  risk
assessments support informed decision making. Daily records demonstrate care is delivered in line
with assessed needs. We saw warm,  dignified and responsive interactions from care  staff.
Feedback about the quality of care is overwhelmingly positive. We were told "I’m more than happy
with the service. They brought my loved one back to life. They never used to open their eyes or get
up. Since coming here, they do things we never thought we'd see again" and "My loved one is
treated as an individual; the team have found out what they enjoy and is important to them in order
to feel valued and understood."


People are very well protected from harm and abuse because safeguarding arrangements are
highly effective and embedded throughout the service. Care staff are confident in recognising and
reporting concerns. Records demonstrate prompt action  is taken when issues arise. Detailed
incident analysis  identifies trends and supports preventative  action, with lessons learned  to
strengthen practice. Individual risk management arrangements help protect people while promoting
independence. Appropriate Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisations (DoLS) are in place.
Mental capacity and best-interest decision-making processes are well understood and involve
relatives, advocates and  professionals where  appropriate.  Strong  partnership  working  with
safeguarding agencies and healthcare professionals helps people to get the right protection and
support at the right time.


People receive  their medicines safely and as prescribed. Effective partnership working with
healthcare professionals supports regular medication reviews and timely responses to changing
needs. Records accurately reflect prescribed medicines and audits identify and address any issues
promptly. Medication  is stored securely and treatment rooms are clean, organised and well
managed. Competency assessments help ensure care staff administer medicines safely, while
lessons learned processes are used effectively following any errors or near misses. Controlled
drugs procedures, stock checks and monitoring arrangements are robust.


People are protected from the risk of infection through high standards of cleanliness and effective
infection prevention practices. The service is visibly clean and well maintained, with suitable hand
hygiene facilities, appropriate laundry arrangements and safe management of clinical waste. Care

       Environment                              Excellent



The environment is excellent because it is thoughtfully designed, continually developed and used
creatively to enhance people’s well-being. The service is maintained to an exceptionally high
standard. Significant investment and ongoing refurbishment are evident throughout the service,
demonstrating a  strong commitment  to  continual  improvement. The home  is  welcoming,
comfortable and homely, and reflects the Statement of Purpose. People are at the heart of
environmental design and development decisions, with  their views influencing environmental
themes and improvements across the home. Bedrooms are personalised and reflect individual
preferences, while creatively themed corridors help promote interest, orientation and well-being.


The environment  is accessible  for  varying  mobility needs. Large communal areas provide
opportunities for relaxation, social interaction and activities, while private spaces support visits and
confidential conversations. The service makes innovative use of available space through facilities
including a cinema room, woodwork workshop, lifestyle room, hair salon, café-style areas, and a
space to help meet people’s religious needs. These facilities are integrated into daily life and help
people maintain independence, build relationships, pursue personal interests and continue doing
activities which are important to them.


Attractive, accessible and secure outdoor spaces further enhance people’s quality of life. These
include innovative features such as a café area and adapted caravan experience. Garden areas
and flower beds are extremely attractive and well-presented. They provide opportunities for social
interaction, reminiscence, relaxation and meaningful occupation, enhancing people's quality of life
and overall well-being. The environment is clean, well ventilated, appropriately  lit and free from
clutter. Robust systems are in place to manage maintenance, servicing and health and safety
requirements, with all required certification up to date. We saw an extremely responsive approach
to maintenance. Appropriate security measures help keep people safe while supporting their rights
and independence, and restricted areas are managed appropriately. Overall, the environment
actively enhances people’s well-being.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management  is excellent because leaders have created a strong culture of
accountability, continuous improvement and innovation. People benefit from exceptional leadership
and  governance  arrangements  which  promote  positive  outcomes.  Leaders  are  visible,
approachable and provide strong direction across the service. The Responsible Individual (RI)
undertakes three-monthly visits to meet with people and care staff and completes detailed quality of
care reviews every six months. Daily management walkarounds provide comprehensive oversight
of areas such as care, staffing, medication, cleanliness and health and safety. A wide-ranging audit
programme is supported by strong clinical governance processes which analyse performance,
incidents, trends and required actions. Leaders actively seek and respond to feedback from people,
relatives and care staff through meetings, surveys and the “You Said, We Did” approach. This
demonstrates a genuine commitment to listening and improvement. Accident and incident reviews
focus on identifying causes, trends and preventative action. Learning is shared within the service
and across the wider service provider group, creating an exceptionally strong lessons learnt
culture. Policies and procedures are comprehensive and regularly reviewed, business continuity
arrangements are robust, and notifications submitted to CIW are of a consistently high standard.
Leadership promotes openness, transparency and innovation, with ambitious plans to further
enhance the environment.


People are supported by a highly skilled and motivated workforce. Leaders place significant
importance on recruitment, training, development and well-being. Recruitment processes are
robust and care staff files contain all required checks and documentation. Training compliance
rates are extremely high, and the comprehensive training programme reflects the needs of people
living at the service. Care staff champions provide leadership in key areas including safeguarding,
oral health, care planning and end-of-life care. Supervision, appraisal, induction and probation
processes are well established and consistently implemented, while learning from incidents is
translated into staff development and training. Staffing levels are regularly reviewed according to
people's needs and adjusted when required. Staff, people and relatives consistently told us staffing
levels are appropriate. We found care to be calm and relaxed, with call bells responded to and care
needs met promptly. Care staff spoke very positively about the support they receive from leaders,
telling us "Management are always approachable. Absolutely we are always listened to and our
views are always considered. I feel very valued", "I have the best job in the world!" and "The
managers are amazing, they try and support the staff and residents through everything." Staff
morale is very positive, and we found a strong culture of teamwork throughout the service. Well-
being initiatives, recognition schemes, career development opportunities and low staff turnover help
promote continuity of care and support people to achieve excellent outcomes.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
