Summary:

 Llys Gwyn is a well-established care home set in extensive private grounds. It is a 10-minute drive
 to the M4 motorway and is within easy reach of Swansea, Bridgend, Porthcawl and Cardiff. The
 service provides accommodation and personal care for older adults, including people living with
 dementia.

 People experience excellent well-being outcomes and are supported to maintain their
 independence and make choices about their daily lives. They maintain meaningful relationships
 with family and friends and participate in a wide range of activities that promote their well-being and
 social inclusion.

 People receive excellent personalised care and support from a dedicated and highly skilled staff
 team. Personal plans are comprehensive, regularly reviewed and reflect people's wishes,
 preferences and outcomes. Staff work effectively with health and social care professionals to

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes because they are supported by a caring and
 attentive staff team that places people at the centre of daily life. People told us they can choose
 when to get up and go to bed, receive visitors and how they spend their time. Regular resident
 meetings take place, whereby people can have a say in the running of the home. We observed a
 relaxed and sociable lunchtime experience where people were supported to enjoy their meals at
 their own pace. Nutritional well-being is promoted through varied menus and regular monitoring.
 The service actively seeks feedback from people about the food provided, and their views influence
 menu planning. This includes a popular weekly "residents' choice" meal, where people's favourite
 dishes are featured on the menu. Other initiatives, such as chip shop Friday and a weekly
 takeaway evening, help promote choice and make mealtimes enjoyable social occasions.

 People benefit from opportunities to take part in meaningful activities and maintain important
 relationships. The service benefits from a dedicated activities co-ordinator who engages people in a
 varied programme of activities including crafts, exercise, music, reminiscence, singers, community
 outings via community transport links and seasonal celebrations. The service celebrates people’s
 birthdays and special occasions with homemade cake. Families are encouraged to remain involved
 in people's lives and receive photographs and updates through the service's communication
 system. This helps people remain connected to those important to them.

 We observed positive, respectful and meaningful interactions throughout the inspection. Staff
 spoke to people with warmth and kindness and demonstrated a detailed understanding of their
 individual needs, preferences and routines. People appeared relaxed and comfortable in the
 company of staff. People living at Llys Gwyn shared positive feedback about the service.
 Comments included, “All staff here are lovely, I can’t praise them enough” and “It’s brilliant here, I’m
 happy”. Staff respond promptly to requests for assistance and there are sufficient staff available to
 provide support in a calm and unhurried manner. People feel safe and know who to speak to if they
 have concerns.

 Robust safeguarding and risk management arrangements help ensure people remain safe while
 maintaining their independence. Care plans and risk assessments reviewed were comprehensive,
 providing staff with clear guidance to support people safely and in a person-centred way. Specialist
 equipment, including sensor mats and call bells, are available to help ensure people receive timely
 support when needed. Incidents and accidents are appropriately recorded, monitored and reported
 to relevant agencies where required.

 The service makes a positive effort to promote Welsh language and culture using bilingual signage

       Care & Support                           Excellent




People receive exceptionally personalised care and support from staff that know them well. The
service completes detailed pre-admission assessments to determine whether people's needs can
be met safely and effectively before offering a placement. Personal plans are comprehensive, well
organised and person-centred. We reviewed several care files and found they reflected people's
preferences, personal histories, communication needs, strengths, risks and desired outcomes.
Plans provided clear guidance for staff and included detailed information about mobility, nutrition,
communication, medication, personal care, emotional well-being, activities and end-of-life wishes.
People's cultural preferences, routines and interests were also clearly recorded.

Reviews of personal plans are thorough and responsive. Records demonstrated plans are
reviewed at least monthly, or sooner when people's needs change. Reviews involve people, where
they wish to participate, alongside relatives, advocates and relevant professionals. Detailed risk
assessments enable people to make informed choices while promoting their safety and
independence. We spoke to people’s relatives who were visiting on the day of our inspection. One
relative told us “I think it’s great here! Really good, it’s lovely”.

People benefit from excellent continuity of care because the service has a stable staff team with
low staff turnover. We saw evidence of strong partnership working with healthcare and social care
professionals. Records evidenced regular involvement from district nurses, speech and language
therapists, falls teams, social workers, dentists, opticians and community mental health services. A
healthcare professional described care provided at the home as being of a very high standard and
told us staff know people well and follow professional advice appropriately. They said, “I’ve got no
concerns at all”. The provider reports any concerns about people’s safety to local safeguarding
teams and makes Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) referrals when there is a risk that care
arrangements may deprive them of their liberty.

People receive their medicines safely through a robust electronic medication management system.
Arrangements are in place for the ordering, storage, administration and monitoring of medicines.
Controlled drugs are stored and administered appropriately, and clear guidance was available for
the administration of medicines prescribed on an 'as required' (PRN) basis. Staff receive regular
medication training and competency assessments. Effective audits and management oversight
support safe, person-centred practice.

Effective infection prevention and control measures help protect people from the risk of cross-
contamination. Effective cleaning schedules are in place, and the home is clean and tidy. Staff
receive relevant training and use personal protective equipment (PPE) which is readily available.
The service is supported by an infection control policy, and regular infection control audits are
completed to support safe practice.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a comfortable, welcoming and homely environment that supports their well-being and
promotes independence. The service provides a range of communal and private spaces where
people can spend time socialising, participating in activities or relaxing quietly. People benefit from
a large accessible outdoor garden area that is often enjoyed during the summer months.

Communal areas are bright, attractive and suitably furnished. Recent decorative improvements
have enhanced the appearance of the home and help create a pleasant environment for people.
Creative design features, including themed decorative areas, contribute positively to the home's
character and provide visual interest for people living there. The date, time and daily menu choices
are clearly displayed to help people remain orientated. Bedrooms are personalised and reflect
individual preferences, supporting people's sense of identity and belonging. The provider has taken
steps to ensure the environment supports people with physical, sensory and cognitive needs.
Appropriate equipment is available, including stairlifts, hoists and sensor equipment, helping to
ensure people's needs can be met safely. We saw evidence the service had responded positively
to recommendations from external monitoring by installing contrasting red toilet frames to better
support people living with dementia.

People benefit from a clean environment as dedicated housekeeping staff maintain high standards
of cleanliness and hygiene throughout the service. Substances hazardous to health (COSHH) were
stored securely and appropriate laundry facilities support the safe management of linen. The
kitchen has achieved a Food Hygiene Rating of 5, demonstrating very good standards of food
safety and hygiene.

Robust arrangements are in place to maintain the safety of the home. A rolling programme of
servicing and maintenance ensures equipment remains safe and fit for purpose. Servicing records
found fire safety systems and utilities were regularly inspected and maintained. Fire safety
arrangements are robust, with regular testing of the alarms, emergency lighting and fire equipment.
Each person has a Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan (PEEP), and staff participate in regular
fire drills to ensure they can respond effectively in an emergency. The provider demonstrates a
positive approach to maintaining and improving the environment, with ongoing programmes of
refurbishment and replacement of furnishings.

Although the environment supports positive outcomes for people, some bathroom and shower
facilities appear dated and show signs of wear. While these facilities remain suitable for use and
appropriate equipment is available, refurbishment would further enhance the quality of the
environment. Measures are in place to maintain the security of the home. Visitors are required to
sign in and out, helping to protect people from unauthorised access. CCTV is in operation and a

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from excellent leadership and management. The service is led by an experienced
and dedicated management team who are visible, approachable and committed to continuous
improvement. The management team promote a positive, open and supportive culture and are
knowledgeable about both the people living at the home and the day-to-day operation of the
service. The provider demonstrates a strong commitment to ensuring there are enough skilled,
competent and knowledgeable staff available to meet people's needs. On the day of our inspection,
staffing levels were appropriate and sufficient to meet people’s needs.

Robust recruitment processes are followed to ensure staff are suitable for their roles. We sampled
staff personnel files and found the required pre-employment checks were completed, including
appropriate references and Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks. Care staff are registered
with Social Care Wales (SCW), the workforce regulator. The service benefits from a stable and
experienced workforce. The service has maintained strong staff retention and does not rely on
agency workers, which promotes continuity of care and enables staff to build meaningful
relationships with people.

People benefit from a staff team that is exceptionally well trained, well supported and highly
motivated. Training compliance is excellent and includes both mandatory and specialist training
relevant to the needs of people living at the home. Staff told us they complete a combination of
face-to-face and eLearning training. This includes safeguarding, infection prevention and control,
dementia care, stoma care, end of life, sepsis awareness and challenging behaviour. Staff
demonstrated a good understanding of safeguarding responsibilities. Staff told us they feel valued
and supported by the management team and records confirmed they receive regular supervision
and guidance. Staff feedback was positive, including comments such as, “Morale is great, we all
get on well, it’s like your little family” and “It’s lovely here, it’s so homely. I love it, I really do”.

Robust governance and quality assurance systems provide excellent oversight of the service. A
comprehensive programme of audits monitors key aspects of service delivery, including medication
management, infection prevention and control, care planning, accidents and incidents, environment
and health and safety. Audit findings are used to identify improvements and support the ongoing
development of the service. Quality of care reviews are completed by the Responsible Individual, in
line with regulatory requirements. Opportunities for people, relatives and staff to influence the
development of the service are embedded through meetings, surveys and regular engagement.

The service works openly with Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW), local authority social services, and
commissioning teams. It maintains positive working relationships with partner agencies and meets
its statutory notification responsibilities in a timely manner.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
