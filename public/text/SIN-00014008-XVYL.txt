Summary:


 Primrose Cottage is a small care home situated in the Vale of Glamorgan. People living at the
 service have easy access to good transport links and local amenities, which they frequently use.
 People experience excellent well-being outcomes because they live healthily and safely with
 control over their lives. They experience highly positive outcomes because they feel valued,
 respected and included. They receive support with meaningful activities, communication needs and
 health needs to achieve their personal aspirations and goals. Care and support outcomes are
 good, people experience successful health, well-being and safety outcomes because they receive
 person-centred support from care staff who know them well.

 The service environment is good, and supports people’s daily routines in clean, well-maintained
 and homely premises. The accommodation supports people's well-being outcomes by providing an
 environment where they experience independence, accessibility, choice and control.

 Leadership and management are good. We found there to be mostly effective quality assurance,
 with strong engagement, and clear accountability resulting in a provider committed to service
 improvement. Governance arrangements are generally effective and have contributed to improved
 outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent





 Without exception, all people are treated with dignity and respect and achieve excellent well-being
 outcomes. Whenever possible, people play an active part towards their daily routines. The support
 they receive ensures they maintain independence as much as possible during day-to-day tasks and
 decision making. We saw care staff interacting with people in ways which enables their
 participation. There is a clear commitment from the provider and all care staff to ensure people are
 listened to. We saw excellent examples where people are encouraged to build on their strengths.
 This included being more independent with their daily health needs, experiencing work type
 activities and increasing their confidence in the community. All people have advanced exceptionally
 well in their personal development since moving into the service. The provider is working towards
 delivering a service in Welsh. One person told us how they are answering the telephone and using
 basic Welsh words to greet people. People enjoy multiple opportunities to socialise and celebrate
 key events together.



 People are greatly supported to live well and achieve self-directed outcomes and told us of the
 positive and transformative impact this has on their well-being. The service offers people choice in
 their daily activities, which follows their personal plans, hobbies, interests and aspirations. People
 are experiencing new opportunities, such as being involved in the interview process for new care
 staff, going on holidays, taking part in weekly swimming sessions and taking a more active role in
 their daily outcomes. The service is providing people with enriching experiences, giving them
 opportunities to socially interact and develop and learn new skills. Regular resident meetings,
 activity planning, menu planning and social events ensure people have meaningful opportunities to
 exercise choice and influence daily life within the service. People told us they are happy and they
 feel valued and listened to.



 People are protected and safe from harm and abuse. People's comments and feedback to us
 consistently indicate they feel safe, respected, supported and able to express their views. Where
 people require additional safeguards, appropriate authorisations are applied for and embedded in
 everyday practice at the service. The responsible individual (RI) is available to people on a weekly
 basis and values their feedback which informs service and practice development. Where people
 have raised concerns, the provider takes appropriate action to ensure people always feel safe and
 comfortable at the service.

       Care & Support                       Good




The service provides a good standard of care and support. Care staff and service leaders know
and understand people well, which supports consistent and reliable daily outcomes. The service
produces a personal plan which informs care staff of a person’s care and support needs. We found
the plans contain good detail about the person, such as their personality, their likes, hobbies, and
the wider network of those important to them. People are fully involved, whenever possible, in
reviewing their personal plans. We found risk assessments to be insufficient to fully inform and
guide care staff. Although care staff know people very well, we discussed the potential risk with the
RI. They have taken prompt action to review and update the documents.



People receive support to manage their physical health. We observed several documents which
demonstrate people receive timely, appropriate support to meet their primary health care needs,
but appointment outcomes were not always evident. People told us of their experiences about how
well supported they are to attend important health appointments. Records show prompt action is
taken following concerns about a person’s well-being. The service encourages people to be
independent in managing their daily health outcomes, such as being more independent with some
medications, or managing their incoming mail from health professionals. We found the service
follows advice from health services such as GP’s and clinical professionals.



People’s medication is safely managed and administered by trained and competent care staff. Daily
medication records are complete. People access other medical professionals to review medication,
and the service acts on their professional advice when there are changes. We observed people to
look well because they receive consistent daily support to maintain their hygiene and personal
grooming standards in-line with their preferences. People access home cooked meals made with
fresh produce, and drinks and snacks are readily available. People told us the food is very good,
they enjoy planning the menu using cookbooks and some like to shop for the ingredients. The
service meets the needs of people with additional support to maintain a good nutritional in-take in-
line with clinical advice. Care staff receive additional training and awareness to provide support to
those with specific dietary needs.


People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because the premises and
equipment are kept clean and hygienic. Care staff complete relevant training and understand their
responsibilities; we observed infection control practice to be good throughout the home.

       Environment                         Good





People experience positive outcomes because they live in a safe, clean, comfortable and
personalised environment where health and safety arrangements and environmental oversight
supports their well-being. The provider has established systems to minimise infection risks,
maintain the premises and equipment, and monitor safety, enabling people to achieve their
outcomes in a homely and welcoming environment. Care staff keep people safe and check the
identity of visitors and keep a record.


The home was observed to be clean and well maintained throughout. We found a structured and
consistent approach to maintaining environmental standards. Care staff receive appropriate health
and safety, and food hygiene training, with good compliance levels. Sufficient Personal Protective
Equipment and cleaning supplies are readily available. We found effective environmental oversight,
ensuring standards are maintained and improvements identified where necessary. People are
actively involved in decisions about their home, and the service demonstrates a person-centred
approach by learning from feedback and adapting the environment to reflect people’s preferences.



People live in accommodation which promotes dignity, independence and quality of life. Bedrooms
are highly personalised and reflect individual interests, preferences and identities. Communal areas
are comfortable, welcoming and well used. People have access to suitable indoor and outdoor
spaces, they can choose where they spend their time, and benefit from a relaxed atmosphere
where visitors are welcomed. There is a rear accessible garden where people socialise. People told
us they enjoy the garden and how they have been included in discussions around what they want
in it, such as raised beds for plants, or activities to keep them occupied. People are highly satisfied
with the environment and how it positively impacts their well-being. We saw people sitting together
in the dining area, with several cookbooks open whilst planning their menu. It was a social and
collaborative activity; people were supportive of each other and enjoyed chatting and exchanging
food ideas.



People's records, medication and hazardous substances are securely stored, and security
arrangements balance safety with people's rights and privacy. The provider has considered the
Welsh active offer, and alongside notice boards in English, there is signage throughout the home in
Welsh.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The service benefits from clear governance arrangements which supports the delivery of safe and
effective care. There have been recent changes to management, and we discussed with the RI the
importance of embedding consistency in key roles at the service for people and care staff. There
are mostly effective quality monitoring systems in place, and feedback from stakeholders informs
service development. The RI completes formal visits to the service to speak with people and care
staff. These visits focus on evaluating the impact of care on people's lives. The RI acts when
needed should people raise any concerns or worries and this is a strength of the service. We found
missed opportunities in quality assurance activities to evidence they consider the consistency and
quality of health records and risk assessments. We discussed this with the RI who is taking
immediate action to review and strengthen their processes. The RI completes a quality-of-care
review which largely meets the regulatory requirement. The review informs the provider the service
is working effectively to meet people’s needs.



The service provider ensures timely notifications are sent to the relevant authorities, such as the
regulators or local authority safeguarding teams. The provider monitors concerns, complaints,
compliments and safeguarding matters. Safeguarding matters and concerns are thoroughly
investigated, and the provider operates with a duty of candour and a willingness to act, make
changes to practice and develop the service in response to matters raised.



Recruitment processes are well-managed, ensuring people receive support from suitably vetted
care staff. Disclosure and barring certificates are in place. Eligible care staff are registered with
Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator. Records of mentoring and shadowing for new staff
were not available; however, care staff receive timely supervision. We found the quality of recent
supervision records to be highly detailed. Training compliance is generally good, with identified
gaps monitored through supervision. Care staff receive training in core topics, and we found most
specialist topics are completed to meet specific care needs. The provider is actively working to
embed a positive, open and transparent culture. Ongoing communication with care staff reinforces
safeguarding responsibilities, whistleblowing arrangements, duty of candour and the Social Care
Wales Codes of Professional Practice. Overall, feedback from care staff told us they value
teamwork and are somewhat supported in their role. We shared with the provider how care staff
would like further consideration to working hours and learning and development opportunities.
Whilst we did not see impact on people at inspection the RI understands their responsibility to
engage with care staff and continue to develop a positive work culture.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
