Summary:

 3 Cwlach Road provides specialist support to people with a physical disability, challenging
 behaviour, mental health and autism. The service is registered to provide support to up to a
 maximum of five people. It is located in the coastal town of Llandudno, Conwy.

 Well-being is good as people are comfortable, relaxed and settled. Care and support is good
 because people receive person-centred care and support that reflects their individual needs,
 preferences and outcomes. Environment is good because it promotes people’s comfort, security
 and well-being. Leadership and management is good because governance systems and workforce
 support helped promote consistency and oversight within the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have good control over their daily lives and are supported to make choices that matter to
 them. People told us they are happy living at the service. One person said, “Yeah – I like it here,”
 and another spoke positively about managing their money, saying, “I’m saving money now.” People
 have choice over everyday decisions including what they eat, when they get up, where they go and
 the activities and holidays they choose to take part in. We saw evidence of staff working alongside
 people and relevant professionals to agree outcomes that promote safety and mental well-being.
 We also saw evidence of advocacy being used when required, including support arranged for one
 person who felt they were not being heard in relation to an important decision.

 People experience positive support that promotes their safety, rights and well-being. Staff told us
 people feel able to raise concerns and approach them for support. One member of staff explained,
 “We are open people – they know they can come to us and say what they wouldn’t say to others.”
 Throughout the inspection, we observed warm and respectful interactions between people and
 staff. Staff know people well and adapt their approach according to individual needs and changing
 mental health presentations. We found a strong emphasis on promoting people’s rights while
 balancing safety and positive risk-taking. Discussions with the manager demonstrate thoughtful
 consideration of least restrictive practice, including ongoing work with professionals to ensure
 restrictions remain proportionate and are regularly reviewed.

 People are supported to maintain relationships and participate in activities that are meaningful to
 them. Staff encourage people to stay connected with family and friends, and support visits from
 them. We saw good evidence of people taking part in a wide range of activities based on their
 individual interests and preferences. Examples include volunteering, mindfulness workshops,
 holidays, spa breaks, coach trips, home visits and overnight stays. Records show people are
 supported to access opportunities both locally and further afield, including visits to Delamere
 Forest, Bodnant Food Centre and Blackpool. Staff describe activities as being planned around the
 individual rather than delivered through a group programme, enabling people to pursue interests
 and achieve personal goals.

 People live in an environment that supports their comfort, independence and quality of life. We
 found the atmosphere throughout the service calm and welcoming. People live in personalised
 accommodation and are encouraged to make their living spaces their own. One person who had
 recently moved to the service told us, “I like my room, I’ll be getting some things I like to make it
 mine.” Whiteboards and calendars are used to help people plan their week and keep track of
 important appointments and visits. People choose how they spent their time, including relaxing in
 their own spaces and enjoying the outdoor areas. Staff respect people’s privacy and respond
 appropriately when individuals want time on their own.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive person-centred care and support that reflects their individual needs, preferences
and outcomes. People told us they are supported to make choices about their daily lives. One
person said, “Staff are supporting me, I get to go and do the things I want to do.” Staff demonstrate
a good understanding of the people they support and told us detailed care records helped them get
to know people well. One staff member said, “Care files have been great to get to know people,
they are really detailed.” Comprehensive personal plans, risk assessments and support plans are in
place. Plans contain clear goals and outcomes, and monthly evaluations demonstrated regular
review of people’s progress and changing needs. Care records also included guidance to help staff
respond appropriately when people experience low mood, distress or irritability, and reflect
individual preferences and important relationships.

People are protected from abuse and neglect because staff understand their safeguarding
responsibilities and there are systems in place to respond to concerns. Staff told us they have
completed safeguarding training and are confident concerns are acted upon. One staff member
said, “They deal with things really quickly.” Records show safeguarding concerns, incidents and
accidents are documented and considered appropriately. Throughout inspection, we observed
positive and respectful interactions between staff and people living at the service. Staff
demonstrate awareness of individual risks and vulnerabilities and understand the importance of
balancing protection with people's independence and choice.

People’s medicines are managed safely. Medication management systems included daily
medication counts, weekly stock audits and regular management oversight arrangements. We
reviewed medication administration records and saw evidence of monitoring and accountability.
Staff responsible for administering medicines complete refresher training and regular competency
assessments, and newly trained staff members undertake competency checks before working
independently.

People benefit from support that promotes their health and reduces the risk of infection. Staff told
us there was sufficient personal protective equipment available, which we observed throughout the
service. People appear well presented and their personal care needs are being met. Records show
people are supported to access healthcare services, including dental care. Weekly infection
prevention and control audits and walk-throughs are completed, covering areas such as waste
management, recycling and hygiene. Communal areas are clean and well maintained, and people
are supported to maintain their own living environments, helping to promote their health, safety and
well-being.

       Environment                         Good



People live in accommodation that promotes their independence, comfort and well-being. People
have their own flats and are supported to personalise their living spaces according to their
preferences. One person told us, “I like my room, I’ll be getting some things I like to make it mine.”
During our walk around, we saw clear evidence of personalisation throughout people's homes.
Individuals have access to their own shower facilities and can also choose to use a bath if they
wish. The service balances promoting independence with maintaining a comfortable living
environment. People are encouraged to take responsibility for their own homes and are supported
to keep them clean and organised in ways that work for them. We saw that personal living spaces
reflect people's lifestyles and preferences. Outdoor areas are accessible and well maintained.
During the inspection, people were seen spending time outside and enjoying views across
Llandudno Bay. The premises is secure, well maintained and provides a homely environment for
people to live in. Visitors are required to sign in on arrival, which supports the security of the home.

The provider has effective arrangements in place to maintain the safety and suitability of the
environment. We reviewed a range of health and safety records and found systems were
established to monitor the premises and equipment. Fire safety checks are completed regularly,
including weekly fire alarm testing, emergency lighting checks and servicing of fire safety systems.
Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are available and stored in multiple locations to
support emergency responses. The service maintains health and safety oversight and regularly
services and maintains facilities and equipment to ensure the safety and well-being of people using
the service.

There are good systems in place to monitor maintenance and environmental improvements. There
is a structured electronic maintenance system which allows repairs and environmental issues to be
reported, actioned, monitored and reviewed in a timely way. Weekly estates meetings provide
opportunities to follow up recommendations and outstanding works. We saw evidence of oversight
arrangements for refurbishment and future maintenance planning. The manager told us about
processes for prioritising essential works and requesting further improvements through the
provider's budgeting arrangements.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from effective management arrangements and positive leadership. Staff speak
positively about the culture and the support they receive. One member of staff told us, “This is the
longest I have stayed in a care job, that tells you I like it.” Staff described managers and senior
leaders as approachable and supportive. The manager spoke positively about the involvement of
the Responsible Individual (RI) and Area Manager, stating that the RI was “very good and
responsive” and that there were “consistent and regular visits.” There are positive and respectful
professional relationships between managers and staff. Noticeboards provide staff with information
about safeguarding, whistleblowing and professional registration requirements. Systems are
designed to promote an open culture where concerns can be raised and discussed. Staff meetings
take place regularly, and staff confirm they have opportunities to share their views.

The provider has governance and quality assurance arrangements in place to monitor the quality
and safety of the service. Regular audits covering medication, health and safety, environmental
standards and care planning take place. Managers and senior staff complete ongoing checks to
monitor whether key tasks and reviews have been completed. Weekly health and safety walk-
throughs and environmental audits are undertaken, alongside quarterly health and safety audits.
The provider completes quality of care reviews which consider areas including choice and control,
health and well-being, safeguarding and the suitability of the environment. These reviews identify
areas for development and proposed actions for improvement. RI visit reports demonstrate ongoing
provider oversight through discussions with people living at the service, staff and managers,
together with reviews of staffing, training, support plans, maintenance issues and significant events
affecting people. Annual returns have been submitted as required by the regulations.

People are supported by staff who receive training, supervision and oversight. Staffing levels on the
day of inspection were in line with the statement of purpose, and no concerns were identified
regarding workforce deployment. Staff say there are enough staff available to meet people's needs
and reported no concerns regarding access to training. One member of staff told us, they received
both induction and shadowing opportunities when they started work. Staff files show the required
recruitment checks have been completed, and Social Care Wales registrations are in place.
Supervision records demonstrate one-to-one support at the required frequency and reflective
discussions between managers and staff. A broad range of mandatory and specialist training are
completed, and the provider has plans in place to strengthen oversight of refresher training that is
required going forward.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
