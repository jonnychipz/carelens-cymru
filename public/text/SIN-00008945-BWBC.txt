Summary:

 Kington House provides a high standard of care and support for adults, specialising in supporting
 those on a journey of recovery following brain injury, or history of substance misuse.

 ‘Wellbeing’ is rated as ‘excellent,’ as people receive highly personalised care and support which
 promotes independence, recovery, health and positive outcomes. People feel safe, valued,
 respected and listened to. People are actively involved in decisions about their lives and are
 supported to pursue meaningful goals and maintain important relationships.

 ‘Care and support’ are rated ‘excellent,’ because people are involved in planning and reviewing
 their care. Documentation around this is strong, relevant and informative, ensuring support workers
 can deliver care and support to meet people’s needs.

 ‘Environment’ is rated ‘good,’ as there are systems in place to monitor and improve the building.
 The environment meets people’s needs, providing them with their own secure and safe space,
 while offering opportunities to engage with others in communal areas.

 ‘Leadership and management’’ are rated ‘excellent,’ because there is effective oversight by

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People living at Kington House experience excellent wellbeing outcomes because they are
 supported to have choice, control and independence in all aspects of their daily lives. Throughout
 the inspection we saw people making decisions about where they spent their time, what they ate,
 how they dressed, who they socialised with and how they accessed their community. We also saw
 people with financial control or suitable support mechanisms in place to assist with this. Support
 staff promote people's right to make decisions and provide encouragement to help them achieve
 positive outcomes while maintaining their independence. When people are considered to be at risk
 in the community, decision making is supported by professional advocacy services. This is
 supported through referrals to the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguarding (DoLS) services to ensure
 people’s rights are upheld and least possible restrictions are in place to help promote freedom
 where possible.

 The service supports people to pursue goals and aspirations which are meaningful to them. We
 found strong evidence of the service supporting individuals to make significant progress in their
 recovery and personal development. One person recovering from a brain injury described the
 service as instrumental in helping them regain independence and confidence, highlighting the
 positive impact support staff have had on their life. Another person had achieved improved
 continence through consistent support, encouragement and environmental adaptations designed to
 promote independence. These examples demonstrate the service's commitment to helping people
 achieve their desired outcomes.

 People are supported to participate in activities which enhance their wellbeing and reduce social
 isolation. We saw evidence of community access, cooking, family contact, quizzes and social
 opportunities within the home. People and staff spoke positively about the monthly music therapy
 sessions, which several people describe as “Enjoyable.” Where individuals have withdrawn
 following difficult life events, support staff use sensitive and patient approaches to re-engage them
 in meaningful activities. Small achievements, such as attending a local shop or visiting a café, are
 recognised as important milestones in a person’s recovery journey while others may be making
 more visible significant improvements, such as attending education.

 Communication is promoted effectively throughout the service. Information is available in a range of
 formats and people are supported according to their individual communication needs. We found
 evidence of Welsh language provision, sign language support and accessible communication tools.
 Support staff communicate with people in a way which is respectful, honest and tailored to
 individual needs. One person told us, “They have helped to build my confidence. They value who I
 am as a person.” Another person commented, “You can't get better than this. The staff are lovely

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive care and support which is exceptionally person-centred, responsive and focused on
achieving positive outcomes. We found people, their families, advocates and relevant professionals
are actively involved in developing and reviewing personal plans. Consultation takes place daily
and through regular formal reviews, ensuring support arrangements continue to reflect people's
wishes, aspirations and changing needs.

The service has robust assessment and care planning processes. Pre-admission assessments
consider people's needs alongside compatibility within the home, ensuring both new and existing
residents are supported appropriately. Care plans provide detailed information about individuals'
histories, preferences, strengths and support needs. Goal attainment plans clearly identify what
people can do independently, barriers to progress and the support required to achieve agreed
outcomes. Risk assessments are relevant, proportionate and reviewed following changes in
circumstances.

During the inspection we observed warm, respectful and professional interactions between all staff
and people living at the home. Support staff demonstrate detailed knowledge of people's needs,
preferences and communication styles. We saw support staff encouraging people to stay hydrated,
maintain healthy lifestyles, participate in activities and recognise their own achievements. There is
a consistent focus on building people’s confidence and promoting positive self-esteem. Support
staff respect people's wishes when they prefer privacy, while also gently encouraging social
interaction where beneficial.

People's physical and mental health needs are supported effectively through strong partnership
working with healthcare professionals. Individuals have access to doctors, dentists, nurses,
opticians and specialist services when required. The service actively promotes healthy living, with
support available for exercise, healthy eating, weight management and recovery from substance
misuse. We saw evidence of effective monitoring of weight, nutrition and health conditions, with
prompt referrals made when concerns arise.

Medication management arrangements are safe and well organised. Records are accurate, stock
control systems are robust, and support staff follow appropriate procedures for administration and
recording. We observed support staff administering medication sensitively and in accordance with
individual preferences. People told us they valued the support provided and told us their experience
is medicines are administered correctly and on time.

Overall, people receive high-quality care and support from knowledgeable staff who understand
their needs and are committed to helping them maintain their health and achieve positive
outcomes.

       Environment                         Good



People live in an environment which generally supports their safety, comfort and wellbeing. Kington
House provides accommodation within a community setting, enabling people to access local
facilities, maintain important relationships and participate in activities which are meaningful to them.


The layout of the home supports both independence and social interaction, allowing people to
spend time together while maintaining privacy when desired. Communal areas are welcoming with
a good standard of décor and equipment. During the inspection we observed people moving freely
around the home and making use of shared facilities. We saw people choosing to spend time in
different areas, some participating in cooking, while others spent time in the garden or lounges.
People’s bedrooms can be personalised, and we saw some which are highly reflective of the
person’s personality and interests. People have their own front door key and have access to
storage which can also be locked, providing them with peace of mind around security of their
personal possessions. We spoke to one person who explained the service provider had listened to
them, installing a shower in their en suite, replacing a bath which they found difficult to access. This
has improved their independence. People can develop confidence, maintain routines and
participate in daily living activities, including meal preparation, laundry and social events. This helps
people work towards independent living if this is their desired outcome.


The provider has systems in place for monitoring environmental safety and maintenance. All
services and equipment requiring external inspection and testing are complete. Management
undertake routine checks of the premises and maintain oversight of health and safety
requirements. Environmental audits are completed, identifying improvements needed, and these
are reviewed through governance processes. We identified several areas where standards could
be improved and discussed these with management, such as broken locks on cupboards.
Immediate action was taken to address this. The management are aware of environmental matters
which require improvement and have plans already in place to address them. Plans include the
replacement of kitchen worktops and refurbishment of some en-suite facilities, demonstrating a
commitment to continuous improvement.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management arrangements at Kington House are highly effective and make a
significant contribution to the quality of care people receive. The home benefits from a strong
management structure, effective governance systems and a culture which promotes openness,
accountability and continuous improvement. Support staff, management and people living at the
home spoke positively about the service and the support available to them. One staff member told
us, “The company is good,” while another said, “This feels like a family. I never dread coming to
work.”


The Statement of Purpose is detailed and available in alternative formats, including Welsh and
audio versions, ensuring information is accessible to people using the service. The service has
established systems for consulting with people, staff and stakeholders through meetings, reviews
and quality assurance processes. Feedback is actively sought and considered when planning
service developments and improvements.


Governance arrangements are comprehensive and provide effective oversight of service quality
and safety. Responsible individual (RI) visits exceed regulatory requirements and include
consultation with people and staff alongside reviews of records and the environment. Quality of
Care reports demonstrate thoughtful analysis of service performance and identify opportunities for
continued development. We found management use information gathered through audits and
monitoring systems to drive improvement and maintain oversight of key areas such as training,
medication management, documentation and health and safety.


The provider demonstrates a positive approach to learning and improvement. Following
medication-related concerns identified previously, the service strengthened arrangements for stock
control, administration procedures, audits and spot checks. Management reported these measures
have significantly reduced medication errors and we found evidence of robust systems now being
embedded in practice.


Support staff are recruited safely and receive extensive training, supervision and professional
development opportunities. Training is particularly strong and reflects the needs of people living at
the service. All support staff receive specialist learning in areas including mental health, acquired
brain injury, behaviours which may challenge and substance misuse support. Supervision
arrangements are well established and staff report feeling supported by managers and the wider
organisation.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
