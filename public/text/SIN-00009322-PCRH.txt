Summary:

 Plas Gwyn Residential Home is a Georgian mansion set within its own grounds overlooking
 Cardigan Bay. The home provides residential care for up to 16 adults in a warm, welcoming and
 homely environment where dignity, independence and well-being are at the centre of everyday
 life. People’s well-being is excellent because they are supported by an exceptionally skilled and
 compassionate staff team who consistently adapt care to help people achieve meaningful
 outcomes that significantly improve their quality of life. Care and support is exemplary, staff know
 people exceptionally well and deliver highly personalised care that promotes
 independence, confidence and wellbeing. The environment has been thoughtfully designed and
 continually adapted to meet people’s changing needs whilst maintaining a warm, homely
 atmosphere where people clearly feel a strong sense of belonging. Leadership and management
 have embedded a positive culture of continuous improvement, ensuring people consistently receive
 high-quality care from a confident, well-supported workforce.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People maintain important relationships with family and friends and are encouraged to remain
 active within their local community through meaningful activities, beach visits,
 countryside walks and community outings. Residents spoke warmly about living at Plas Gwyn, with
 comments including “We are lucky here” and “The food is 10/10.” People are treated with dignity,
 compassion and genuine warmth by staff who know them exceptionally well. Throughout the
 inspection people appeared relaxed, confident and comfortable in their surroundings, interacting
 naturally with each other, staff and visitors.
 People are empowered to make decisions about their daily lives and are supported to achieve
 outcomes that are meaningful to them. Staff demonstrated an excellent understanding of people’s
 interests, aspirations and communication preferences, enabling them to participate in activities
 which promoted both independence and emotional wellbeing.
 People experience significant improvements in their health and wellbeing because staff continually
 review care and adapt support and have achieved numerous life adapting outcomes.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive exceptionally personalised care from a staff team who consistently place individual
outcomes at the centre of care planning and delivery.
Care planning is dynamic and responsive, with people, relatives and professionals actively involved
in reviewing support whenever needs change. Staff demonstrate an excellent understanding of
people’s personalities, communication styles and aspirations, allowing them to tailor support
naturally throughout the day rather than relying solely on documented care plans. 
Activities are therapeutic. A dedicated activities coordinator delivers meaningful experiences based
on individual interests, helping people improve communication, mobility, confidence and emotional
wellbeing. Staff embed therapeutic techniques into everyday interactions, ensuring opportunities for
rehabilitation and personal development occur naturally throughout daily life. 
The provider works proactively with health professionals to ensure people receive timely healthcare
interventions. Care plans and risk assessments are reviewed promptly following changes in health
or mobility, while managers remain actively involved during hospital admissions and discharge
planning. This collaborative approach promotes continuity of care and contributes to consistently
positive health outcomes. 
Medication management is robust and embedded into everyday practice. Competency
assessments, regular audits, safe storage arrangements and effective oversight ensure medicines
are administered safely while maintaining people’s choice and dignity. Infection prevention and
safeguarding practices were equally well embedded throughout the service, with staff
demonstrating confidence in recognising risks and responding appropriately to keep people safe.

       Environment                              Excellent



People live in an exceptionally welcoming environment which has been thoughtfully designed to
promote independence, dignity and comfort.
The home is presented to an exceptionally high standard. It was spotless, well maintained and
decorated in a subtle, homely manner which immediately created a calm and welcoming
atmosphere. There was a clear sense that people were proud of their home. 
People’s bedrooms reflected their personalities, interests and preferences. Each room is highly
individualised, creating the impression of entering a completely different home from one room to
the next. This demonstrated the provider’s commitment to supporting people to maintain their
identity and personal choice within the service. People clearly valued both their own personal
spaces and the communal areas, making excellent use of lounges, dining rooms and gardens
throughout the inspection. 
Leaders continually invest in the environment to respond to people’s changing needs. Recent
improvements included installation of a new lift, improved ramp access, removal of environmental
barriers and development of a sensory room was taking place. These adaptations promoted
accessibility, independence and wellbeing whilst ensuring the home retained its warm, family
atmosphere. People proudly described the gardens and outdoor spaces, with one person referring
to the pathway as “my country walk,” demonstrating genuine ownership of their home.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership throughout Plas Gwyn is visible, compassionate and strongly focused on improving
outcomes for people.
Leaders have established effective governance arrangements which extend beyond regulatory
compliance and actively drove service improvement. Regular audits, Responsible Individual visits,
quality assurance systems, staff meetings and feedback from people, relatives and professionals
are used to evaluate practice and identify meaningful improvements. This was evidenced through
continued investment in both the workforce and environment, including accessibility improvements
and development of therapeutic spaces. 
The culture within the service is positive, open and supportive. A positive approach
was demonstrated towards the Welsh Active Offer. People were able to express themselves in the
language of their choice. Staff consistently described leaders as approachable, responsive and
encouraging, with communication flowing effectively through meetings, handovers and daily
contact. Staff achievements and long service is recognised, contributing to high morale and
workforce stability. 
The provider has strict selection and vetting processes for hiring staff to ensure staff are qualified
and trustworthy. Care workers undergo routine and regular checks to support them to remain
suitably fit to work in the service. Care workers are appropriately registered with professional
bodies.
The provider demonstrates a strong commitment to workforce development through
comprehensive induction, regular supervision, competency assessments and an extensive
programme of mandatory and specialist training. Staff are confident in their roles
and demonstrated the knowledge and skills required to deliver consistently high-quality, person-
centred care. Investment in staff development translated directly into positive experiences and
outcomes for people living at the home.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
