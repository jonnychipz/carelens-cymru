Summary:

 Cildewi is a care home located in Johnstown, a suburb of Carmarthen in Carmarthenshire,
 supporting adults with mental health needs. The home can support up to 7 people with 5 en-suite
 bedrooms and two separate studio flats for people moving towards semi-independent living.

 The wellbeing outcomes for people are excellent. People are empowered to make decisions about
 their daily lives and develop independence. They consistently experience a culture of respect,
 inclusion and choice, and told us they feel safe, valued and supported to achieve what matters to
 them.

 The care and support people receive is good as care staff know people well and provide
 compassionate, person-centred support that promotes health, wellbeing and independence.

 The environment is good, it is safe, comfortable and designed to promote independence. People’s
 bedrooms are personalised, communal areas are welcoming, and the environment supports people
 to develop daily living skills within a homely setting.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People consistently experience excellent wellbeing outcomes because they are respected
 and remain in control of decisions affecting their daily lives. We found a strengths-based culture
 where care staff focused on what people can do, encouraging them to develop life skills and
 increase their independence. People have bedrooms which are personalised and decorated to their
 own tastes and are appropriate to meet the unique needs of people. A sense of community is
 created in communal areas between people and care staff, as people are encouraged to use the
 communal space for activities they enjoy.

 People consistently develop their confidence, skills and resilience needed to move on to more
 independent living arrangements. The availability of two self-contained flats enables people to test
 and strengthen their independence in a safe and supportive environment. The self-contained flats
 offer people with the opportunity to live as independently as possible whilst knowing that support is
 available when required.

 People engage in a variety of meaningful activities and opportunities including independently
 accessing the local community, volunteering, partake in pod casts, attend clubs and activities, care
 for vegetables at a local allotment. These opportunities support people to remain active members
 of their community whilst developing confidence, social connections and a sense of purpose.

 People consistently benefit from a strong culture of openness where emotional wellbeing is
 actively promoted. Throughout the inspection we observed warm, respectful and meaningful
 interactions. One person told us, “The people here are great, I feel safe here, I never thought I’d be
 this lucky.” People benefit from strong partnerships between the service and external professionals,
 ensuring a joined-up approach to meeting individual needs. Feedback from people demonstrated
 they value the support they receive and reflected the positive relationships that have been
 established with care staff. The stable and nurturing environment enables people to build
 confidence, develop independence and achieve outcomes that are important to them. As a result,
 people experience a strong sense of belonging, security and purpose, and are supported to live
 fulfilling lives in accordance with their preferences and aspirations.

 Safeguarding arrangements are embedded within everyday practice and promote people's rights
 whilst maintaining their independence. Care staff support people to take positive risks and make
 informed decisions, ensuring risks are managed proportionately rather than restricting
 opportunities. This enables people to exercise choice, develop confidence and maintain control
 over their lives in a safe and supportive environment.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are supported by care staff who are kind, reliable, compassionate, know them well and treat
them with dignity and respect. Warm, reassuring and respectful interactions were seen throughout
the service.

People and professionals spoke positively about the care and support. Professionals shared
confidence in the care provided. We were told by professionals involved “they know the people
well, there is a cohort of regular staff who are knowledgeable”, “They always centre support and
decision making in a person-centred way” and “They are always looking at ways to promote further
engagement and activities within the home and wider community”. People living at the home told us
“Staff are incredible at their job. They help me and are really amazing” and “All the staff are really
friendly day and night and are very approachable”.

Health promotion is actively encouraged and personalised. The service provider uses strong,
established links with local health, and social care services. We saw people are referred for
appropriate care and treatment when required. People are supported to attend and take part in
regular checks and activities related to health promotion. We found medication is consistently
stored and administered safely. Care staff have good medication training and there is a
comprehensive medication policy in place.

The service has care planning and risk assessment arrangements in place to ensure it can safely
and appropriately meet people’s needs. People are involved in decisions about their care
and support. However, opportunities were identified to strengthen recording to ensure information
are routinely reviewed and updated to accurately reflect people's current needs, risks and
outcomes. More robust oversight of care planning documentation would provide greater assurance
that support remains responsive to changing needs.

A team of motivated and committed care staff are employed at the service, which allows people to
develop strong relationships with care staff. They are knowledgeable about the people in their care.
People are supported towards increasing independence through exercising choice and working on
individual and purposeful goals. People have freedom to choose what to do with their days and are
supported in creating and accessing a variety of activities, experiences and trips of their choosing
on a weekly basis. Care staff support people to access voluntary work supporting people’s
progress towards independence.

Systems are  in place  to protect people from harm and abuse. Care  staff have completed
safeguarding training and have a clear understanding of their responsibilities for recognising and
reporting safeguarding concerns.

       Environment                         Good



The Environment is good. People live in an environment that is safe, comfortable and supports
them to achieve their personal outcomes. Communal spaces are clean and arranged to create a
welcoming atmosphere. We saw people relaxed and comfortable in their surroundings and
spending their time as they choose.

People are encouraged to be actively involved with decisions in relation to the decoration of the
service. It was clear it was their home of which they were proud. People’s bedrooms are
personalised and reflect individual preferences, helping to create a sense of ownership and
belonging. Furnishings and décor are generally in good condition, and maintenance systems
ensure that any repairs or improvements are addressed.

There is a strong emphasis on developing independence and living skills within the service and
the accessibility of the environment and its facilities promote this. People are strongly encouraged
to complete daily tasks for themselves, such as meal planning, cooking and domestic care
activities, with support from care staff where needed.

The service provider ensures risks to health and safety are identified, mitigated and reduced.
Fire safety checks, drills, and equipment servicing are carried out regularly, and records
confirm compliance with statutory requirements. Storage areas for hazardous substances are
secure, and medication cabinets are locked to maintain safety. Security measures, including
external surveillance, are implemented appropriately and respect privacy. There is secure entry to
the service and a visitor’s log is maintained ensuring that visitors sign in and out of the service.

The kitchen was clean and organised, with appropriate checks in place. The service has achieved
a food hygiene rating of 5 (Very Good), demonstrating strong infection control and food safety
practices.

A secure garden can be accessed directly from the lounge, providing an outdoor space where
people can relax, socialise and spend time safely. We found people had been actively involved in
making improvements to the garden and influencing how the space is used and developed. This
helps promote a sense of ownership, belonging and pride in their home whilst ensuring the
environment reflects people's preferences and interests.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management are excellent and have a clear, positive impact on the quality of care
delivered. People have high levels of confidence in the service provider because leaders promote
an empowering and inclusive culture where people and care staff feel valued, respected and
listened to. The service is led by a highly visible, approachable and values-driven management
team, including the Responsible Individual (RI) and registered manager. They promote a clear
vision centred on respect, dignity and person-centred care, and this is fully embedded across the
service.

Governance systems are robust, well embedded and highly effective. Regular audits, quality
assurance processes and RI visits ensure that standards are continuously monitored and
improved. Feedback from people, care staff and stakeholders is actively sought through
questionnaires and engagement.

Professionals spoken with provided positive feedback about communication within the service. We
were told, “they work well with professionals, providing updates as required”, and “The manager is
particularly excellent at communicating”. This demonstrates effective partnership working and a
commitment to ensuring people's needs are met.

Care staff told us they can approach the manager as required, contributing to an open and
supportive culture. Care staff feel supported in their roles because of highly effective, present and
supportive leadership. Care staff spoken with told us, ‘I feel very valued’, ‘we are a good team’ and
“It’s very rewarding”.  It is evident that the continuous support provided to managers and care staff
creates a culture where everyone is committed to achieving high-quality outcomes for people. This
leadership approach fosters trust and reassurance, which contributes to a positive, open
environment where people feel listened to, respected, and valued.

Leaders actively recognise and celebrate the achievements of both people and care staff through
inclusive events, such as house awards. This helps to promote confidence, self-esteem and a
sense of belonging, whilst reinforcing a culture where success and personal progress are valued
and celebrated.

Robust recruitment and vetting processes help ensure people receive support from suitable and
appropriately skilled care staff. People receive care and support from a care staff team who are
appropriately supported. Care staff receive a comprehensive induction, ongoing training and
regular supervision, enabling them to maintain and develop their skills. There is a strong focus on
continuous learning and progression, with opportunities for care staff to develop leadership skills
and advance within the organisation. The strong leadership culture creates a positive, open and

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
