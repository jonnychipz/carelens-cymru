Summary:

 Bryn Awelon is located in an elevated position on the outskirts of Criccieth. The home benefits from
 panoramic views of the countryside, sea and Criccieth Castle. The home is owned by Bryn Awelon
 Nursing Home Ltd which is a family run business. The home can accommodate up to twenty-eight
 people who require nursing and personal care.

 People experience good wellbeing as there is a team of motivated staff who support people to
 enjoy a good quality of life. People are listened to and respected. Care staff embrace their
 individuality and give them as much control over their day-to-day lives as possible.

 Care and support is good as personal plans provide a good insight into who people are and how
 they can be supported to achieve their goals. They are regularly reviewed and updated. They
 actively promote people’s health and independence. People feel content doing things they enjoy in
 the home.

People live in an environment which good and improving. The home is clean, well maintained and
suitably equipped. There is ongoing refurbishment of the home.

Leadership and management are good as managers lead by example, work in partnership with
people, staff and professionals to provide a safe, reliable service that enhances people’s lives. Staff
work well as a team and receive a high level of support from managers, who motivate them to
continually learn and develop in their roles. There are robust systems of audit to help the
Responsible Individual (RI) monitor the quality of the service and drive improvement.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People receive support which enhances their physical and mental well-being. Care staff know what
 is important to people and how to support them to achieve their personal goals. People experience
 good continuity of care from a stable team of staff. Care staff encourage people to eat and drink
 healthily and to keep active. Their medication is managed in a safe way. People have things to look
 forward to and are encouraged to follow their interests. Care staff closely monitor people’s mental
 health and follow professional advice to ensure people receive the best possible support. Care staff
 support people with dignity, kindness and respect. They have a good understanding of people’s
 needs and fully support their individual routines.

 People feel safe and comfortable in their home. They have developed good relationships with care
 staff, who know how to manage risks to their safety and well-being. Managers lead with skill and
 passion. Call bells are answered in a timely way and care staff are responsive. They promote a
 positive culture in the home and a strong sense of teamwork amongst staff. Care staff are safely
 recruited and well trained and they receive a good level of support from managers to enable them
 to undertake their roles effectively. They are familiar with safeguarding and whistleblowing
 procedures and feel confident managers would deal with any concerns immediately. The RI
 supervises the management of the service closely and effective auditing systems help drive
 constant improvement.

 The environment mainly supports people to achieve a good standard of well-being. People enjoy a
 spacious, homely environment that has a good range of facilities to promote independence. They
 are accommodated in rooms that suit their needs and preferences. There is ongoing refurbishment
 work such as the conservatory renovation, new windows and new flooring in some parts of the
 home. The manager has identified potential environmental hazards and has taken steps to
 minimise risks to people.

       Care & Support                       Good



Personal plans provide a good level of detail about people’s care needs and preferences, including
their chosen language of communication. They are regularly reviewed with people and/or their
representative. Care records demonstrate people consistently receive the right level of care and
support. The manager encourages care staff to keep insightful records about how people have
spent their days. These show that care staff support people to follow their interests and routines.
The service follows Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards procedures to ensure restrictions are only
applied in their best interests.

People enjoy a range of nutritious homemade meals and can look forward to activities. Ample food
stocks are available to accommodate choices. People’s weight is monitored closely, and swift
action is taken if needed to prevent any further weight loss. People have plenty to eat and drink
throughout the day. The menu is displayed in the lounge/dining room. The menu is varied and
currently being reviewed so people can enjoy new food combinations. Each person will contribute
to its development and their likes and dislikes are considered. Activities are tailored to people’s
needs and interests. People recently enjoyed an afternoon ice cream party, baking, hand and head
massage, and arts and crafts.

The service actively promotes people’s health and well-being. Care staff ensure people receive
input from the relevant medical and specialist services in a timely manner. Accidents and incidents
are dealt with appropriately and routinely audited by the manager so any lessons can be learned.
The manager rigorously monitors medication systems. An electronic medication system
automatically tracks stock levels and provides alerts to ensure staff give medicines at the right time.
Medicines are stored securely and at appropriate temperatures. Records show people consistently
receive their prescribed medicines. The service ensures care staff are suitably trained and
competent before administering any medication.

       Environment                         Good



The home is benefitting from environmental upgrades, but these need to continue. The
conservatory is undergoing considerable renovation work which is due for completion in the next
month. We found some décor to be in need of repair or uplift, such as the carpet on the main
corridor in the upper floor is worn and has some stains. The external grounds are not tidy, safe, or
attractive. People are unable to access the outside area and sit in the grounds due to ongoing
building work. The RI has stated this will be addressed along with new guttering and painting the
external walls of the home once the conservatory renovation has been completed. People are
pleased with their individual rooms, which we found to be adequately furnished and decorated.
People told us they spend time in their rooms as they wish, and care staff respect this.

The service has suitable arrangements in place to ensure equipment and utilities are appropriately
serviced and inspected. People are safe from strangers entering the premises as care staff
checked our identity before allowing us into the home and ensured that we completed a visitors’
book. We saw fire safety equipment is serviced and regular fire drills and checks of emergency
lighting also take place. Personal emergency evacuation plans can be accessed quickly and easily.
Staff complete fire safety and health and safety training. There are systems in place for
management to audit the environment and request any repairs or upgrades as needed. Most
repairs are undertaken by a member of the company’s maintenance team. A maintenance book is
used to log any general repairs needed in the home, which the maintenance worker dates and
signs once complete.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service benefits from strong leadership and management. Staff morale is high, creating an
upbeat, positive atmosphere. The manager and deputy manager take pride in their roles and work
diligently to provide people with the best possible service. People look to managers for advice and
guidance and managers lead by example, instilling confidence in the staff team. People can access
the office when needed and the RI, manager and deputy manager are involved in service delivery
as well as managing the day to day running of the home. Managers carry out regular internal audits
to ensure the service is performing well, which the RI oversees. The RI ensures people’s voices are
heard during formal assessments of the service. These are carried out during three-monthly visits.
A Quality-of-Care report has been produced but requires improvements. The report includes limited
amounts of feedback from people who use the service and consultation with other relevant parties
on the quality-of-care and support provided. The service has the necessary resources and staffing
arrangements to operate safely and effectively.

There are robust systems in place for recruiting and training staff. The service is fully staffed, so
people receive good continuity of care from familiar staff. Staff undergo the required pre-
employment checks, including a criminal check via the Disclosure and Barring Service. Staff get to
know people and familiarise themselves with policies and procedures during their induction
programme. Staff value their training, and one staff member said, “We have so much training”. We
found training statistics to be consistently high. Staff feel comfortable speaking openly during staff
meetings and their formal, individual supervision every three months. Staff supervision meetings
include a review of feedback from colleagues and the people they support. They allow staff to have
meaningful discussions about their overall performance, personal well-being and learning and
development. Staff receive support which makes them feel valued and care about staff’s personal
well-being. The provider recently organised a BBQ for staff and has planned a day out for them to
show their appreciation of their hard work and dedication.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
