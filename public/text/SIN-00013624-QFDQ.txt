Summary:


 Llys Hafren Care Home is located within walking distance of Welshpool town centre. The service
 provides respite, residential and personal care for adults aged 65 and over of all genders, including
 frail elderly people and those living with a diagnosis of dementia.

 People experience positive well-being outcomes. They are treated with dignity and respect, have
 good relationships with staff and are supported to make choices about their daily lives.

 Care and support is person-centred and of good quality. People’s care and support plans are made
 and reviewed with them, or a representative, on a regular basis. Staff understand people well and
 respond to changes in their health and well-being. Committed care staff are safely recruited, well
 trained and caring.

The home environment is warm, clean and homely and supports people’s comfort and

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People are supported to achieve positive wellbeing outcomes, with choice and control in their daily
 lives. Care staff support people to maintain independence and pursue what matters to them. They
 are involved in decisions about their care and support, and their views are listened to and acted
 upon. People choose how they spend their time, when to see family and friends, and whether to
 participate in social activities, according to their preferences and aspirations. During inspection, we
 saw people choosing to spend time in the garden. People tell us they feel supported to achieve
 what matters most to them and have positive relationships with staff. One person commented, “The
 girls are good, some are exceptional. It is a good home.” and another person said “I can’t fault
 them. It’s the staff who make it good here. These ladies look after me well”. People’s
 representatives we spoke with spoke positively about the service, telling us “I have peace of mind
 knowing all is ok and staff care”. A visiting professional commented, “staff are quick to respond or
 tell us about people’s health needs”. They share information easily”. We found evidence the service
 promotes the Welsh Language Active Offer, signage and information leaflets were seen at the
 service. This supports people to receive services in their preferred language and recognise the
 importance of Welsh in maintaining wellbeing and identity.

 The service has effective arrangements in place to support people’s health and well-being. Pre-
 admission assessments help ensure the service can meet people’s identified needs, with
 information gathered from individuals, their representatives and relevant professionals to inform
 care and support planning. We saw staff work proactively with healthcare professionals, making
 timely referrals and sharing relevant information promptly to help achieve positive outcomes for
 people. A visiting professional told us staff are proactive in seeking health advice and making
 appropriate referrals when concerns arise. We also observed people being supported to attend GP
 appointments, demonstrating the service promotes access to healthcare and responds effectively
 to people’s changing health needs.

 People are treated with respect and staff respond promptly and appropriately to their requests.
 People we spoke with told us they feel comfortable with the staff who support them and know who
 to speak to if they have any concerns. Our review of personal plans, risk assessments, incident
 records and other relevant documentation show there are systems in place to identify, manage and
 regularly review risks to people's well-being. We found staff have access to safeguarding policies
 and procedures and demonstrates a clear understanding of their responsibilities to protect people
 from harm and promote their safety. The evidence gathered during the inspection is consistent with
 the positive feedback received from people and supports our finding people feel safe and protected
 within the service.

People are supported to maintain a healthy lifestyle through access to a balanced and nutritious
diet, with meals designed to promote positive health outcomes and meet individual needs and
preferences. We saw a dignified meal experience, people were offered choice, with alternatives
available and feedback about food was positive.

People engage in activities which reflect their individual preferences and choices. No organised
activities were taking place on the day of the inspection because of warm weather; however,
people told us they were looking forward to watching the World Cup later in the day. We found the
provider recognises the importance of supporting people to engage in meaningful activities which
promote their well-being, independence and social inclusion. Discussions with management
highlighted plans to further develop the range of activities available to people. Records show
people are consulted about the types of activities they would like to participate in, and the service is
seeking external support to further enhance activity provision. These planned improvements aim to
enhance people's quality of life, reduce the risk of social isolation and provide greater opportunities
for people to maintain relationships, develop skills and pursue interests which are important to
them. We also saw some people choosing to spend time in quieter areas of the service, and staff
respected these individual preferences. This approach supports people to exercise choice and
control over how they spend their time and ensures their personal wishes are respected.

       Care & Support                       Good





Care staff support people to communicate their choices and take part in decisions about their care.
The approach is person-centred, promotes rights, well-being and personal outcomes. Staff
understand people's communication needs and use appropriate approaches to ensure people
express their wishes, feelings and preferences. We observed staff offering choices one at a time
and allowing people sufficient time to respond, enabling them to participate in decisions about their
daily lives. Where appropriate, family members, advocates and others who know the person well
are involved to help inform decisions and ensure the person's voice is represented. Staff also
consider people's previous wishes, feelings, life history and known behaviours, particularly where
communication or capacity has changed. Any restrictions are identified and supported by
appropriate best-interest decision-making documentation. Discussions with staff demonstrates a
good understanding of consent and the importance of ensuring it is meaningful and informed.
Records show decisions are based on each person's individual circumstances, preferences and
assessed needs, reflecting a personalised rather than standardised approach to care and support.

Care and support plans contain up-to-date information and are written in a respectful, dignified and
person-centred manner, often using first-person language. Records reviewed and discussions with
staff demonstrate a clear focus on achieving positive outcomes for people. Care plans provide
consistent guidance for staff and reflect an individualised approach to support. We also found
through review of records incidents are appropriately recorded and reported to relevant
professionals in a timely manner.

People receive good-quality care and support from staff who are safely recruited, appropriately
inducted and receive ongoing training to meet people's needs. Individual risk assessments are
clear and provide practical guidance for managing identified risks and promoting people's safety
and well-being. Physical, mental, emotional and social well-being is supported effectively, and staff
know people well, enabling them to respond appropriately to changing needs and circumstances.
People told us, and our observations confirmed, the commitment and dedication of the staff team is
a significant strength of the service.

Provider routinely seeks feedback through meetings and questionnaires with records
demonstrating this informs service improvements. Staff we spoke with consistently spoke positively
about the care provided, describing a supportive team culture, strong commitment to people's well-
being and positive leadership from the manager, who is described as approachable and responsive
with an open-door policy. Safeguarding arrangements are in place; concerns are acted upon
promptly with appropriate referrals made to relevant agencies.

       Environment                         Good





The home environment is calm, safe and secure. Appropriate identification checks are in place, and
a visitor signing-in book is used to ensure only authorised individuals enter the service. The care
home is clean, warm and welcoming, supporting people’s comfort and wellbeing. Bedrooms are
personalised and equipped to meet individual needs, including specialist beds and moving and
handling equipment, which is safely stored when not in use. Communal areas are well used to
promote social interaction, independence and meaningful activities, with opportunities for private
use where required. People are supported to make good use of the environment, including outdoor
spaces for activities such as gardening, reflecting personal interests and backgrounds.


Domestic staff maintain good hygiene standards throughout the day, with appropriate infection
prevention and control supplies readily available. The environment supports people's well-being
through the effective use of space to promote engagement and reduce anxiety. We identified some
maintenance issues, including parts of the access road, flooring and garden areas. The provider
assured us a maintenance plan is in place to address them. At the time, these issues did not
significantly affect people’s comfort or access to care, but timely completion of the planned work
will help maintain a safe and pleasant environment.


People are protected from the risk of infection through effective infection prevention and control
practices. We found the environment and equipment is clean, well maintained and promotes
people's safety and well-being. Food hygiene standards are good, and the service has achieved a
Food Hygiene rating of 5, reflecting high standards of food safety.


Staff demonstrate good knowledge of safe practices, including appropriate use of personal
protective equipment (PPE), with systems in place to monitor supplies and minimise risk. The
service provider ensures the premises comply with relevant legislation and guidance. Health and
safety systems are in place, including fire risk assessments, secure storage of hazardous
substances and water safety checks such as legionella monitoring. Gas and electrical testing is
completed within required timescales. Fire safety is managed, with fire drills and clear Personal
Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) to support safe evacuation in an emergency. We saw the
provider has arranged a dedicated day for staff to undertake full fire drill exercises. This
demonstrates a proactive approach to fire safety and ensuring staff are prepared to respond
effectively in the event of an emergency.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The manager is visible and approachable, they promote a positive, person-centred culture. Since
taking up post, the manager has established themselves within the role and, alongside the
leadership team, has identified key areas for improvement, including increasing the frequency of
staff supervision, completing spot checks, improving training compliance, ensure staff obtain
appropriate consent from people prior to use of their information and or comments and further
strengthening person-centred care plans. Daily meetings have been introduced so care staff can
share information promptly and respond to people’s changing needs. Staff consistently described
the manager as supportive and approachable, with an open-door policy which encourages
communication. They told us there is clear direction within the service, and as a result, improved
stability and continuity of leadership is having a positive impact. The manager is not yet registered
with Social Care Wales, and the provider must ensure registration is completed in a timely manner
to meet regulatory requirements. The RI and senior management team continue to provide
oversight and support to the service.


Governance and quality assurance processes demonstrate a clear focus on continuous
improvement. We found effective governance systems are in place to monitor standards, drive
service development and ensure care and support remained aligned with people's needs, wishes
and preferences. The RI maintains effective oversight through regular three-monthly visits, which
incorporated feedback from people and staff. Quality of care reports are completed in a timely
manner and provide effective monitoring of service delivery. Policies and procedures are up to
date, with some under review, and are consistently applied in practice. These documents reflect
relevant legislation, guidance and best practice. Leaders ensure policies and procedures are
accessible and embedded through supervision, training and team meetings, promoting
consistency, accountability and good standards of care.


The provider is committed to maintaining a skilled and well-supported workforce. All care staff are
registered with Social Care Wales and hold, or are working towards, the required qualifications.
Recruitment and vetting processes are effective, including the timely renewal of Disclosure and
Barring Service (DBS) checks. We found evidence of appropriate induction and ongoing staff
development. Staff demonstrate a good understanding of the complaints process and told us they
feel confident raising concerns. Staff also describe feeling well supported in their roles and feeling
valued. The provider holds annual “Star” Awards, recognising and celebrating staff achievements
and contributions. Team morale is positive supporting continuity and good quality of care for people
living at Llys Hafren.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
