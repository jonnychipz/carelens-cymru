Summary
People are happy with with the support they receive at Woodside Grange. People are
supported by responsive and skilled staff who know them well. Care staff provide positive
reassurance and interaction regularly. People are supported to make choices about their
daily lives wherever possible. Personal plans are person-centred, detailed, reflect people’s
needs, reviewed and changed accordingly. Activities are on offer with staff facilitating them
and external entertainers coming to the service periodically.


Staff feel well supported by Management and are provided with training to meet people’s
needs. There are suitable governance arrangements in place with the Responsible
Individual (RI) visiting the home regularly to oversee management of the home and gather
the opinions of people, relatives and staff to help to improve and develop the service.
People’s views are reflected in six monthly quality of care review reports. The environment
is well maintained, and the service is operating in line with the statement of purpose.

--- PAGE 4 ---
Well-being
People have control over their day to day lives where possible, where people do not due to
capacity; relatives or advocates are consulted. They feel listened to and contribute to
decisions that affect their life. Personal plans are written together with the person and cater
for people’s preferences. People say they like living at the service and say, “Staff go above
and beyond.” People have choices around food and activities on offer and care staff listen
to people’s wishes. Call bells are answered in a timely way. Bedrooms are well-maintained,
homely and personalised to people’s own taste. Care records give care staff the instruction
required to support people accurately in a person-centred way. Reviews are carried out in
line with regulations.


Staff know residents well enough to notice when people are starting to experience poor
mental or physical health. People have visitors coming to the service regularly and have
good relationships with other people they live with and care staff. There are activities on
offer in the service, with noticeboards on display telling people what activities are
happening and when. Activities are facilitated by staff and arrangements are made for
external entertainers to visit. A trip to Blackpool to see the illuminations had taken place the
week before we visited, an entertainer visited to sing and play games with people on the
day we visited, and fireworks were arranged in the evening to celebrate bonfire night.
People are supported to practice their faith with arrangements made for religious
representatives to come to the service if people wish. The service is working towards the
Welsh language ‘Active Offer’ with Welsh speaking staff matched to Welsh speaking people
and celebrations of Welsh culture taking place.


People are protected from abuse and neglect. Care staff receive training in safeguarding
and policies and procedures are in place and followed. People are supported to maintain
and improve their health and wellbeing through access to specialist care and advice when
they need it. Referrals are made in a timely manner to specialist services, ensuring people
receive the right care and support, as early as possible. Care staff and management are
proactive and work collaboratively with support agencies.


The lay out of the home supports people to achieve a good standard of well-being. People
are encouraged to be independent and strategies for reducing the risk to people while they
move around the home are sufficient. The manager has identified potential hazards and
has taken steps to minimise risks to people.

Care and Support
People can feel confident the service provider has an accurate and up to date plan for how
their care and support needs should be met. Pre-assessments completed by management
take place before people move to the service and consideration is given to whether people
will be well matched to those already living in the home. Pre-assessments gather
information around people’s history and how they came to live at the service.


People and relatives are encouraged to co-produce their personal plans and have choice
over everyday decisions, such as meals, clothes they wish to wear and times they get up in
the morning. Personal plans are personalised, up to date, accurate and regularly reviewed.
They contain person-centred outcomes, likes, dislikes and preferences, with clear
consideration around how people’s emotional support needs can be met as well as physical
support needs. Robust risk assessments are in place and regularly reviewed. People
receive care in line with their personal plans and risk assessments and care staff are kept
informed of important updates using thorough daily handovers.


Relatives said the service has given their loved one “a new lease of life” and “the attention
and devoted care they receive is second to none”. Relationships between care staff and
people are positive. Care staff are responsive to the needs of people living at the service,
identifying when people may be experiencing a dip in mental health and finding ways to
boost their mood, such as supporting them to get out of the service for a walk. Mealtimes
are sociable, people have choice of what to eat and can have more if they wish. Dietary
choices and specialist dietary requirements are well known by kitchen staff, who make
bespoke recipes for people if they do not like certain ingredients. Staff complete manual
handling in accordance with manual handling recommendations.


People have access to specialist advice and support from health and social care
professionals. Care plans and risk assessments are promptly updated to reflect
professional advice and care staff access appropriate and specialist training depending on
the needs of people living at the home.


Medication storage and administration practices in the service are good and keep people
safe. Medication is administered by care staff who are trained to do so. Competency to
administer medication is assessed annually and weekly medication audits are carried out
by the designated medication lead.

 Environment
People live in an environment suitable to their needs. The service provider invests in the
decoration and maintenance of the home to ensure it meets people’s needs. Bedrooms and
communal areas are well maintained and there are plans in place for all bedrooms to be
refurbished. The décor in the home is of high quality. There are communal dining and
lounge spaces in the home, people can choose to spend their time in busier more sociable
spaces or to go to smaller quieter lounges if they wish. People’s rooms are clean, tidy and
personalised to their own taste with belongings. Moving and handling equipment is stored
accessibly but out of the way to prevent trips and falls. People say they like their rooms and
find them homely.

Gardens are very well maintained, there is access to an enclosed beach themed garden at
the side of the property which is used well in the warmer months. People access the main
building through a securely locked and coded door and visitors are required to sign in and
provide identification on arrival. Cleaning staff were seen around the building throughout
our visit and all areas were clean and tidy. The service provider has infection prevention
and control policies, with good measures in place to keep people safe.


People can be confident the service provider identifies and mitigates risks to health and
safety; regular health and safety audits are completed with actions dealt with swiftly by
maintenance staff. This is monitored by management and the RI. The home has the highest
food rating attainable. Routine health and safety checks for fire safety, water safety and
equipment are completed and records show the required maintenance, safety and servicing
checks for gas and electrical systems are up to date.

Leadership and Management
People can feel confident the service provider has systems for governance and oversight of
the service in place. The RI is at the service for at least three days in the week. They
complete a quarterly RI visit report which documents inspection of the property and
discussions with staff, residents and relatives. We have discussed the need for the reports
to include evidence of reviewing of a selection of records and have been assured this will
be included in future reports. Regular management audits are completed and there is
continual oversight of quality of personal plans and care records. A quality of care report is
conducted every six months and outcomes of surveys are analysed, we have discussed the
need to clearly document actions taken in response to feedback and the service have
assured us this will be done going forward. People say they can speak to the manager
about changes to their care and action is taken. The provider has submitted an annual
return as required by Regulation.


People can be satisfied they will be supported by a service which provides appropriate
numbers of staff who are suitably fit. Care staff have the knowledge, competency, skills and
qualifications to provide the levels of care and support required to enable people to achieve
their personal outcomes. The manager has suitable numbers of staff on duty each shift to
support people’s needs. New staff undergo thorough vetting checks before starting work in
the service and receive an induction specific to their role. Management ensure staff receive
the support that they need through regular one to one supervision meetings. Care staff say,
“It’s really good working here, we all like to make the residents happy, they come first”.
Care staff state they feel well supported by the manager and have access to the training
required to meet people’s needs, telling us “The office door is always open, if we have a
problem, we can talk to management about it”. Training is provided to staff through a
combination of online and face to face courses and training records are reviewed and
updated to make sure they accurately reflect training compliance. Care staff have either
registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator, or are in the process of doing
so.

People can be confident the service provider has an oversight of financial arrangements
and investment in the service so it is financially sustainable, supports people to be safe and
achieve their personal outcomes.
