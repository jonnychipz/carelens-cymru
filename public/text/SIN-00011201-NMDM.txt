        Well-being                           Good

       Environment                         Good



        Leadership & Management               Good





Summary:

 People who have made Belmont Court their home receive a good standard of care.

 Wellbeing is rated as good overall because there are high levels of satisfaction in the service. Once
 the programme of activities starts, people’s wellbeing will enhance further.

 Care and support is rated as good because care workers and other members of the team are
 motivated and experienced. Relatives have high levels of confidence in the service and feel
 reassured people are well cared for.

 The environment is rated as good because the service is clean, well maintained and the property
 has some character.

 Leadership and management is rated as good because the provider is visible in the service and is
 generally very well regarded by the team. Governance arrangements are robust.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People live healthily and receive the care and support they need from a team of experienced and
 motivated workers. The atmosphere in the service is relaxed and friendly with some good
 humoured banter between people, workers and visitors.

 There are few opportunities for people to engage in activities but the provider gave assurances that
 this will be improving in the coming weeks when a care worker is released from their current role to
 do activities both individual and in groups.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. Most staff have completed training in
 Safeguarding and are confident about reporting any concerns they have. They are also confident
 the manager would take the actions needed to make sure people are appropriately safeguarded.
 The provider is to consider making sure all staff complete this training. A clear policy provides clear
 guidance for staff.

 People live in accommodation that is suitable for their needs. The service is well maintained, light
 and airy. There is space for people to spend time with others or in their rooms if they prefer.
 Bedrooms are personalised and there is some outside space for people to enjoy when the weather
 permits.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the quality of care and support they need. People are generally satisfied with the
care they are offered, with one saying “I am very well looked after” and another said “I’m happy
here. Nothing’s too much trouble (for the staff)”. Care is not rushed with care workers saying most
of their colleagues understand the importance of person-centred care. Some consider additional
staff are needed, but others feel they have enough staff to meet people’s needs.


Personal plans are very comprehensive and contain a lot of helpful information including a personal
and family history. There are support plans and risk assessments for a range of areas including
oral care, nutrition, mood and medication. Care workers complete the records contemporaneously
and the nurse in charge is alerted if an action is not completed. Care workers find the records
helpful and together with the time they spend with people mean they feel they know them well.


People’s physical health needs are safely met. There is a registered nurse on duty at all times and
a district nurse visits daily. Care workers know how to recognise signs of skin pressure damage
and they have the equipment they need to reduce the risk of people whose mobility is severely
impaired from developing pressure sores. Repositioning is carried out regularly and in accordance
with personal plans. The provider has agreed to make sure relatives and other representatives are
involved in decisions made about aspects of people’s care. Records show people’s weight and
other physical observations are recorded at least monthly.


People experience care and support that is dignified and respectful. We observed some friendly
and relaxed interactions which show a rapport has been built. However, the options for people to
engage in meaningful activities is limited. Comments from people include “No activities” and “I do
get bored”. Some people are happy with this and enjoy spending time doing crosswords, reading
and watching TV. Care records did not provide evidence of meaningful activities and engagement
but the provider feels this is due, in part, to a lack of accurately recording such activities. During the
inspection we did not observe any meaningful activities taking place and care workers confirmed
they do not have the opportunities to spend time engaging with people other than when carrying
out personal care. The manager confirmed plans for a care worker to be rostered to offer
meaningful activities both individually and in groups.


There is some understanding of the importance of good nutrition and most meals are made using
fresh ingredients, although there is some reliance on processed foods. Food is available outside of
mealtimes and there is a menu for people to choose from. Food cupboards are well stocked.
People are encouraged to use the dining room which is a way to enhance people’s dining
experience. People’s views about the food varies with some describing it as “very good” while

another said “It could be better. It’s sometimes a bit cold”. Care workers understand the importance
of people remaining hydrated and drinks are offered throughout the day, with people offered
assistance as needed. Relatives appreciate having the opportunity to share meal times with their
loved one and told us how they value having a meal at the service.


People’s medication is safely managed. It is stored in a locked room and in locked cupboards. A
medicines reconciliation showed there are the correct number of controlled drugs. A nurse is
always on duty. Records show medication is administered as prescribed and there is little use of
PRN (or as required) medication. The Responsible Individual has agreed to review the training for
support workers who may be required to witness the administration of medication which is classed
as controlled drugs to ensure they are competent to do this. There was a lack of clarity about the
use of covert medication and whilst this is included in the Medication Policy, some individual
records had not always been updated when changes were made to people’s prescriptions.

       Environment                         Good



The provider makes sure people live in an environment which meets their needs. Accommodation
is provided over two levels and there is a lift meaning people can move safely between floors.

Parts of the service has retained some original and attractive original features, including a wide oak
staircase, some large windows and high ceilings. There are two main lounge areas as well as
dining room and a further room for people to spend time either with others or their visitors. The
property is well maintained and standards of decoration throughout are good, although some work
is needed in parts of the staff area because of damp. There is an attractive bar area in one of the
lounges but this is used for storage. This area was previously used for snacks and drinks and could
be a welcome addition for people. The maintenance team take a lot of pride in their work and have
good knowledge of their priorities.

Most bedrooms are light and airy with many having pleasant views. People have personalised them
with photographs, soft furnishings and ornaments. Some people have brought in their own furniture
to help personalise their rooms further. Many bedrooms have ensuite facilities and there are
additional bathrooms and toilets throughout the service.

The areas just outside the building are accessible for people and there are some raised beds
meaning people can contribute to the gardens. During the inspection, the maintenance team were
planting some vegetables and flowers for people to enjoy watching grow. There are some pods
which can be used for people to meet with visitors or to just spend time outside.
Internet access throughout the service is patchy, meaning some people do not have access to it.
Whilst care workers may write their notes contemporaneously, they may not be uploaded to the
electronic system until they are back in an area where there is a signal.

Standards of cleanliness throughout are good, with several people describing it as “spotless”.
There are no malodours and the housekeeping team clearly take a lot of pride in their work,
recognising their important contribution to people’s overall care and support.

The kitchen has been awarded the maximum score of five by the Food Standards Agency. The
kitchen appears clean and staff have the equipment they need, although one of the freezers would
benefit from defrosting. Food cupboards are well stocked.

The maintenance team have good processes in place for making sure equipment and services are
in good working order. This includes regular checks and cleaning of beds and wheelchairs; water
safety checks including legionella and electrical safety checks. They have good knowledge of the
service and work flexibly to make sure standards are maintained.

Confidentiality is maintained. People are safe from unauthorised visitors entering the building, as all
visitors have to ring the front doorbell before gaining entry and are asked to record their visits in a
visitor’s book when entering and leaving.
Care records are held electronically, and staff personnel records are securely stored in the

       Leadership & Management               Good



The provider has good oversight of the service. The Responsible Individual (RI) visits the service
regularly and also works some shifts. They describe having a close working relationship with the
manager. Care workers find the manager and RI to be responsive and are able to discuss any
ideas or concerns they have with them. Reports written are comprehensive and reflective and used
to consider how improvements can be made.


Care workers are appointed following a robust recruitment process. Staff files are easy to navigate
and contain the information needed, including photographic identification, references and security
checks. Care workers are appropriately registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator
and nurses are registered with the Nursing and Midwifery Council. Care workers are supervised
and they have the opportunity to discuss their work and get constructive feedback.

There is an emphasis on training. Care workers have training in a range of areas including first aid,
dementia care and Moving & Handling. Whilst most training is up to date there are some gaps and
some training has been booked. Care workers consider they have the training they need to safely
and effectively carry out their duties.


Care workers feel valued and supported. They are able to raise any ideas or concerns and are
confident of getting a helpful and timely response.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
