Summary:

 College Fields Nursing home is based in Barry and can accommodate 68 people with a range of
 nursing and personal care needs. This inspection was unannounced. There is a Responsible
 Individual (RI) for the service and a manager who is registered with Social Care Wales (SCW) the
 workforce regulator.
 People experience excellent care and support from a kind and friendly staff team. Staffing levels
 are sufficient to meet individuals’ needs, with arrangements in place to cover any staffing shortfalls.
 Wellbeing outcomes for people are good, supported by a caring staff team and the provider’s
 ongoing commitment to improving the quality of the service.
 Leadership and management arrangements are good and continue to strengthen and promote a
 culture of openness and transparency within the service. The manager is visible, approachable,
 and actively involved in the day-to-day operation of the home, providing clear leadership and
 demonstrating a commitment to improving outcomes for people.
 People live in a good, clean and well-maintained environment that is well decorated. Some areas of
 the home have been identified as requiring improvement, and improvement works remain ongoing.
 While these works do not impact the people living in the home, their management and associated
 safety considerations continue to be monitored.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Well-being is good. People feel safe, and generally receive high-quality, person-centred care and
 support that is tailored to their individual needs. Their preferences, choices, and aspirations are
 respected, and staff consistently provide care in a warm, compassionate, and supportive manner.
 People are encouraged to maintain important relationships and welcome visitors into the home.
 Visitors told us they are always made to feel welcome and are often invited to join their relatives
 and friends for lunch at mealtimes. Systems are in place throughout the home to promote good
 practice. Some improvements are required to strengthen quality assurance and audit processes
 regarding the environment. The manager demonstrates good oversight of incidents, accidents,
 complaints, and safeguarding matters.

 Care and support is excellent. Outcomes for people using the service are positive and achieved to
 a high standard. Individuals are supported to work towards their personal goals and desired
 outcomes. Feedback from people using the service is extremely positive, with many describing
 good relationships with staff and expressing satisfaction with the support they receive at College
 Fields. Comments included, “Staff are extremely kind and they can’t do enough for us, it’s homely
 here” and “the food is really good, I can choose each day what I want, there are lots of continuing
 improvements.” Staff also spoke positively about their experience of working at the service and
 highlighted the support they receive from management during some changes of management
 structure. Comments included, “I really enjoy my job and always feel so supported, staff morale is
 good, I wouldn’t work anywhere else.”

 People live in a good environment, supported by systems that ensure equipment and facilities are
 regularly serviced and with ongoing refurbishments and maintenance throughout. Routine safety
 checks are undertaken; however, there are opportunities to further strengthen these processes,
 and this remains an area for improvement. Fire safety is appropriately prioritised within the service
 in response to enforcement action from South Wales Fire Authority, the provider has taken interim
 steps to mitigate risks and improve safety at this time. The service also ensures that Deprivation of
 Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) are in place for individuals who lack the capacity to make decisions
 about their care and support.

 People are protected from abuse and harm through effective safeguarding arrangements. Robust
 recruitment processes ensure staff are suitable for their roles, and staff promote person-centred
 care by respecting individual preferences and supporting choice and control. Individuals are
 encouraged to follow their preferred routines and access a range of meaningful activities. The RI
 provides regular oversight and quality assurance, identifying and addressing areas for
 improvement.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



Care and support is excellent. People told us they felt safe and secure living in the home. We also
observed this through people’s body language, behaviour and interactions, including for those who
were unable to verbally express their views. Documentation we looked at confirmed that staff had
completed safeguarding training, and a comprehensive safeguarding policy was in place, clearly
outlining staff responsibilities for protecting adults from harm, abuse and neglect. We observed
people appearing relaxed, comfortable and at ease in the presence of care staff. People told us,
and we observed, that staff are warm, kind and respectful, demonstrating an excellent
understanding of individuals’ needs and the most effective ways to support them. Care staff
responded sensitively and appropriately when people experienced difficulties with communication
or decision-making. We saw staff providing reassurance, encouragement and person-centred
support, while anticipating and responding to people’s needs, particularly for those who were
unable to communicate these themselves.

People were positive about the support they receive and we observed individuals being
consistently encouraged to make choices in line with their preferences, including decisions about
meals, drinks and snacks. The dining experience was of a high standard, with meals attractively
presented and tailored to meet individual needs. The chef told us menus are adapted to reflect
residents’ likes, dislikes and dietary requirements and had excellent knowledge of each person’s
choices. They also confirmed that residents can request alternative meals at any time and that,
where a meal is not eaten, suitable alternatives are offered promptly. Fresh meat, fish and
vegetables are delivered regularly, helping to ensure the provision of nutritious and varied meals.
The service has achieved a rating of five (very good) from the Food Standards Agency.

People have access to a range of services that support their ongoing health, wellbeing, and quality
of life. Care records evidenced timely referrals to appropriate healthcare professionals, including
GPs, dentists, and opticians. Personal plans contained the necessary information and accurately
reflected the assessed needs, preferences, and wishes of individuals living in the home. Although
we identified that both electronic and paper documentation being used at this time. We were told
plans were underway to transition to a fully electronic system when all refurbishment works are
complete at the home.

We observed care workers providing attentive, person-centred support, consistently anticipating
and responding to individuals’ needs. Personal plans were regularly reviewed and updated to
ensure they remained current and reflective of changing circumstances. Where individuals
experienced difficulties with decision-making, the service demonstrated good practice by making
appropriate applications to the relevant authority and ensuring that the person’s views and wishes
were considered as part of the decision-making process.

Medication management arrangements within the home were robust, with clear procedures in place
for the safe storage, administration, and recording of medicines. These systems were well
organised, consistently followed, and supported the safe and effective management of medication.

       Environment                         Good



People are supported in a clean, welcoming and homely environment. Bedrooms are personalised
to reflect individual preferences and include personal belongings, helping people feel comfortable
and at home. The service offers several smaller communal areas, along with a large dining room
where we observed people socialising and enjoying activities together. During our visit, we
observed the home to be clean, fresh and overall, well maintained throughout. However, we
identified some areas required improvement. Visitors were warmly welcomed throughout the visits
and people told us they can visit at any time during the day. The front gardens are well kept,
attractive and easily accessible. People told us they enjoy spending time outdoors, particularly
during the warmer weather, and we saw several people making use of this garden area and
enjoying the recent life-sized animals around the garden. People spoke enthusiastically about the
recent activities and party, describing it as an enjoyable occasion for everyone involved.
Throughout our observations, people appeared safe, happy and well supported, and were able to
spend their time in ways that reflected their preferences and interests.

There are mostly effective systems in place for monitoring and maintaining both the environment
and equipment. Maintenance staff ensures that all facilities and equipment are regularly inspected,
serviced, and tested. External contractors are engaged to undertake specialist checks, including
gas electrical safety and lift safety. Repairs are generally addressed promptly, and a planned
redecoration programme supports a good standard of presentation within the home. We discussed
several issues identified during the visit and the manager told us the matters would be addressed
immediately. At this time phased refurbishments remain ongoing to further improve the
environment; the provider is reminded that works will be kept as minimally disruptive as possible for
the people living at the home. Although an enforcement notice remains in place from the South
Wales Fire Authority, all appropriate action has been taken to ensure people living, working and
visiting the home are safe.

Effective systems are in place to promote public safety and minimise the risk of cross-infection.
Staff have access to appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) and demonstrate a clear
understanding of its correct use and disposal. All staff receive training in infection prevention and
control and recognise its importance in safeguarding people. Robust oversight arrangements are
established, ensuring staff consistently follow current infection control guidance. People are further
protected through effective security measures, including keypad entry systems and controlled
access for visitors. A visitors’ book is maintained in line with fire safety procedures, and identity
checks are undertaken for all visitors prior to entry.

Overall, the service provides comfortable and appropriate accommodation for people living in,
working in, and visiting the home. Management demonstrates a strong commitment to ongoing
development and continuous improvement, ensuring positive outcomes for those who reside there.
People experience a very strong sense of belonging, supported by the provider’s dedication to

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management is good. People are supported by care staff who are appropriately
trained and recruited safely. Care staff are extremely happy working at the service and told us they
feel valued and supported by both the manager and RI. We looked at a selection of staff
recruitment documentation and found they contained all the required information including
identification and full employment history. We saw evidence that pre-employment checks including
DBS certificates and references are sought prior to employment commencing to ensure that all staff
are suitably qualified and fit to work with vulnerable people. Individuals using the service and their
relatives consistently praised the quality of care, with one stating “I can’t fault it, the care staff are
amazing, I am very happy here.”

The provider demonstrates a strong commitment to staff development across all roles. Care staff
undertake a thorough induction programme, including opportunities to shadow experienced
colleagues, and their competence is assessed before they work independently. Training is tailored
to the requirements of individual roles and responsibilities. One staff member told us, “We are an
established team here with really good staff morale, everyone works together ensuring people’s
needs are met.” Regular supervision sessions are held between staff and management, and
several care staff described extremely very well supported in their roles. Staff turnover remains low,
which many staff attribute to the positive and supportive working environment. A number of care
staff told us they have worked at the home for many years, and several told us, “This is the only job
for me, I love my job, always have and wouldn’t consider working anywhere else.”

People benefit from strong leadership and effective management within the service. College Fields
is a registered service with effective governance arrangements in place. The RI maintains an
active and visible presence most days within the service, engaging positively with individuals and
staff and promoting an open and approachable culture. A comprehensive range of policies and
procedures supports the day-to-day operation of the service and provides staff with clear guidance
regarding their roles and responsibilities. The manager demonstrates a sound understanding of
their safeguarding responsibilities and legal duties, ensuring timely referrals are made to the local
authority when concerns arise. Individuals are provided with detailed information about the service,
including guidance on how to raise concerns or make complaints should they be dissatisfied. The
home’s Statement of Purpose (SoP) is regularly reviewed and updated as necessary.

During our visit, we spoke with management and staff who described the RI’s commitment to
promoting staff wellbeing and morale through a range of initiatives, including a dedicated Staff
Appreciation Week. Additional measures have been introduced to recognise and support staff,
such as themed evenings, raffles, and any ongoing support when needed. These initiatives
contribute to a positive working environment and ensure that individuals receive care from a
motivated, valued, and experienced workforce. While recognising that further improvements are still
needed, the management team acknowledges that the compliments and positive feedback

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
