Summary:

 Well-being is rated good because people reported feeling safe, happy and well supported.
 Relatives consistently described people as settled, respected and treated with dignity. Meaningful
 activities, family involvement and person-centred support promoted positive wellbeing outcomes.
 Welsh language provision was developing but not consistently evidenced through an active offer
 approach.

 Care and Support is rated good as people received personalised care that generally met their
 assessed needs. Care planning, safeguarding and medication management arrangements were
 effective, supported by positive feedback from relatives and professionals. Minor opportunities were
 identified to strengthen person-centred information and some care records.

 Environment is also rated good because people lived in a generally clean, welcoming and well-
 maintained home with appropriate facilities, equipment and safety systems. Relatives and
 professionals spoke positively about the environment. However, the prolonged lift outage created
 risks relating to mobility, dignity and emergency evacuation, and Long Term Care Team (LTCT)
 findings highlighted environmental and equipment concerns requiring ongoing monitoring.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience positive wellbeing outcomes and are supported to live safely, make choices and
 maintain important relationships. Residents told us they were happy living at the home, felt safe
 and received support in the way they wanted. Relatives consistently described people as settled,
 well cared for and treated with dignity, with comments including “very settled,” “looks cared for,”
 and “100% very happy”.

 People were involved in decisions about their care, daily routines and activities. Care records
 demonstrated ongoing monitoring of health needs, risks and outcomes, with access to specialist
 advice and support when required. Relatives told us they were kept informed of changes in their
 family member’s wellbeing and felt confident in the care provided.

 Safeguarding arrangements were in place and staff had access to policies, training and reporting
 procedures. Residents reported feeling safe and there was evidence that concerns could be raised
 and responded to appropriately. Relatives expressed confidence that people were protected and
 supported.

 People were encouraged to maintain relationships with family and friends and participate in
 meaningful activities. An activities coordinator was employed and a varied programme of activities,
 celebrations and community engagement opportunities was available. Relatives highlighted the
 welcoming atmosphere, strong family involvement and positive social opportunities within the
 home. Observation and activity records demonstrated that people were supported to participate in
 activities that reflected their interests and preferences.

 People lived in accommodation that generally met their needs and promoted comfort, safety and
 accessibility. Relatives and visiting professionals described the home as clean, welcoming and
 homely. People had access to communal areas, outdoor space and equipment that supported their
 wellbeing and independence.

 Some concerns were identified regarding the impact of the prolonged lift outage, which affected
 access around the home and temporarily altered accommodation arrangements for one resident.
 Welsh language provision was developing, with some bilingual staff and signage available;
 however, evidence did not fully demonstrate that Welsh language needs were consistently
 identified, reviewed and proactively supported. Overall, people achieve positive wellbeing
 outcomes through caring relationships, meaningful engagement, personalised support and
 generally safe living arrangements.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive personalised care and support that promotes their wellbeing, dignity and individual
outcomes. Residents told us they felt safe and supported, whilst relatives consistently described
care as compassionate and responsive, commenting that staff “treat her like family” and “I wouldn’t
want her anywhere else.” provide excellent communication and support people to maintain their
wellbeing.

Care records demonstrated comprehensive assessments, care planning, risk assessments and
regular reviews. People’s health needs were monitored appropriately, including nutrition, hydration,
skin integrity and mobility, with referrals made to health professionals when required. Activities
were tailored to individual interests and preferences, helping people remain engaged and maintain
important relationships. Visiting professionals described care as person-centred and observed
positive partnership working with families and external agencies. Some opportunities for
improvement were identified. One care file lacked sufficient personal history and person-centred
information, and a small number of inconsistencies were noted in pressure care documentation,
requiring review to strengthen assurance that care records accurately reflect people's needs.

Safeguarding arrangements were in place, with staff having access to safeguarding policies,
training and reporting procedures. Records demonstrated safeguarding concerns were
appropriately recorded, monitored and reviewed. People and their representatives were provided
with information about how to raise concerns, and staff understood their responsibilities for
protecting people from harm.

Medication systems were well established. Policies, audits, storage arrangements and
administration records provided assurance that medicines were managed safely. Monthly audits
demonstrated good oversight and evidence of improvement where minor recording issues had
previously been identified. No significant medication concerns were identified during the inspection.

Appropriate infection prevention and control arrangements were in place, including staff training,
PPE availability, cleaning systems and monitoring processes. Relatives consistently described the
home as clean and well maintained. However, previous LTCT monitoring identified issues relating
to cleanliness, equipment hygiene and storage arrangements. The provider had acted to address
these concerns, although continued oversight is required to ensure improvements are sustained.

Overall, people benefit from caring staff who know them well and provide support that is responsive
to their needs. Care planning, safeguarding and medication management arrangements support
positive outcomes for people, although some record-keeping and quality assurance processes
would benefit from further strengthening.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a safe, comfortable and well-maintained environment that supports their wellbeing
and independence. Relatives consistently described the home as “spotless,” “clean,” “comfortable”
and supportive of their family members’ wellbeing, while a visiting professional described the home
as clean, welcoming and homely.

People have access to suitable accommodation, communal areas, outdoor spaces and equipment
that support their assessed needs. Bedrooms were personalised, clean and appropriately
equipped. The home had effective maintenance arrangements, servicing records, fire safety
systems and specialist equipment available to promote safety and wellbeing. Evidence also
showed ongoing investment in the environment, including refurbishment works, redecorating and
improvements to outdoor areas.

However, a prolonged lift outage had affected residents living on the upper floor and required one
resident to be temporarily accommodated elsewhere in the home. This presented potential risks
relating to dignity, privacy, mobility, access to activities and emergency evacuation. Some
additional environmental concerns were identified through Long Term Care Team (LTCT)
monitoring, including equipment storage, damaged furnishings and cleanliness issues. The
provider had responded with an improvement plan and remedial actions.

The service had health and safety systems in place, including risk assessments, fire safety
arrangements, maintenance monitoring, contingency planning and updated PEEPs. Management
responded to the lift failure through risk assessments, communication with families and liaison with
relevant agencies. Maintenance records and safety certificates provided assurance that key safety
systems were monitored.

Inspection evidence also identified risks requiring ongoing oversight. These included the continued
impact of the lift outage, bed rail safety concerns, infection prevention issues, equipment storage
arrangements, environmental cleanliness concerns and an electrical safety issue requiring further
assessment. While the provider had taken action and developed improvement plans, the findings
indicate that environmental monitoring and quality assurance processes require strengthening to
ensure emerging risks are identified and addressed promptly.

Overall, people benefit from a pleasant and well-maintained environment, with positive feedback
from relatives and professionals. Further improvement is required to strengthen environmental
oversight and provide assurance that safety and maintenance issues are consistently identified and
resolved at the earliest opportunity.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from stable and visible leadership arrangements. The Registered Manager and
Responsible Individual (RI) provide oversight through statutory visits to the service, Quality of Care
reviews, audits, feedback mechanisms and regular supervision of staff. Governance systems
support person-centred care, continuity of service and positive outcomes for people.

Leadership was viewed positively by staff, relatives and professionals. Staff described
management as supportive, approachable and communicative, with strong teamwork and a
positive workplace culture. Relatives consistently reported management were responsive and
maintained open communication. Visiting professionals described the service as well organised,
collaborative and committed to people’s wellbeing, dignity and rights. These arrangements help
people receive care from a stable and supportive workforce.

The service has established quality assurance systems including audits, stakeholder feedback,
complaints monitoring, quality reviews and Responsible Individual oversight. Quality of Care
reports demonstrated increasing focus on measurable outcomes, safeguarding, staff development
and service improvement. Evidence showed the provider acted on concerns identified by the Long
Term Care Team (LTCT) visit through an improvement plan, enhanced monitoring and
strengthened managerial oversight.

However, the LTCT findings identified environmental, equipment and infection prevention issues
that had not been fully identified through routine quality assurance processes. While the provider
had a quality assurance folder and action plan in place, audit systems, action tracking and
governance monitoring require further strengthening to provide greater assurance that emerging
risks are identified promptly and consistently. The Statement of Purpose would also benefit from
updating to strengthen governance and regulatory assurance.

Staffing was stable, with consistent nursing cover, low turnover, regular supervision and appraisal
arrangements. Staff reported feeling valued and supported and all respondents said they would
recommend the service as a place to work. Training compliance was generally good at 91%, with
evidence of specialist and mandatory training. However, some staff had incomplete or expired
training, indicating further management oversight is required to ensure competency requirements
remain up to date and people continue to receive safe care.

Overall, leadership and management arrangements support positive outcomes for people and
demonstrate a commitment to continuous improvement. Further strengthening of audit
effectiveness, governance oversight and training compliance would improve regulatory assurance
and support sustained service improvement.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
