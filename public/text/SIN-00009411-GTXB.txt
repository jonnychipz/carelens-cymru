Summary:

 Beechwood Court is a care home service providing residential care, support and specialist
 education for adults with learning disabilities, autism, mental health needs, acquired brain injuries
 and epilepsy. The service is comprised of six homes and delivers personalised education,
 independent living skills and work-related learning opportunities alongside residential care,
 supporting people to develop independence, participate in their communities and achieve their
 personal outcomes through a person-centred approach.

 People achieve excellent well-being outcomes because they are supported to make choices,
 maintain relationships, access healthcare, participate in education and engage in meaningful
 community activities. Strong advocacy arrangements, excellent health support, positive family
 involvement and a wide range of social opportunities enable people to develop independence,
 achieve personal goals and enjoy fulfilling lives.

People receive excellent care and support that is personalised, responsive and based on detailed
assessments of their needs and aspirations. Strong multidisciplinary working, effective
communication support, robust behaviour support planning and safe medication management
ensure people receive consistent support that promotes independence, safety and positive
outcomes across all aspects of their lives.

The environment is good and largely supports people’s independence, well-being and daily
routines. Personalised bedrooms, welcoming communal spaces and extensive outdoor facilities
contribute to positive outcomes and enhance people’s quality of life. However, improvements are
required to ensure maintenance issues in some areas are addressed in a timely manner and are
consistently maintained to a good standard. This will help ensure people continue to experience
safe, comfortable and well-maintained environments that fully support their well-being outcomes.
The provider has recognised these areas for improvement and has developed action plans to
address them.

People benefit from excellent leadership and management arrangements that promote a positive
culture, effective governance and continuous improvement. Leaders are visible, knowledgeable
and committed to high-quality care. Care staff feel valued and supported, families express high
levels of confidence in the service, and robust quality assurance systems help drive positive
outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience exceptionally positive well-being outcomes because they are supported by a
 highly committed care staff team who place their rights, aspirations, health and happiness at the
 centre of daily practice. People have strong opportunities to exercise choice and control over their
 lives and are actively involved in decisions about their futures.

 People achieve excellent outcomes in relation to their physical health, emotional well-being and
 independence. Robust healthcare arrangements ensure people receive timely access to GPs,
 dentists, opticians, podiatry services and specialist clinicians. A dedicated health and well-being
 coordinator supports people to attend appointments and access specialist interventions. We found
 care staff are strong advocates for people’s health and well-being. They work effectively with
 healthcare professionals to promote positive outcomes and provide sensitive, compassionate
 support to people when they experience changes in their health or circumstances. One family
 member stated, ‘Beechwood college have gone above and beyond for our son and ourselves’,
 whilst another reported, ‘Our son’s physical and mental well-being is optimal thanks to the very
 good care.’

 People are supported to develop meaningful relationships and participate fully in community life.
 People access a wide range of educational, recreational and employment opportunities, including
 work experience placements, volunteering, community activities, holidays and cultural visits. We
 saw evidence of activities including bowling, museums, beach visits, Cardiff Bay, St Fagans, dog
 therapy and beauty sessions. Innovative work experience opportunities, such as supporting stock
 rotation and customer service within the onsite shop, help people develop valuable skills for when
 they finish college. Feedback from people was overwhelmingly positive. One person stated, ‘I have
 fun at Beechwood’ and ‘I love my college, I have lost weight, I am happy.’ During inspection we
 observed warm, respectful interactions between people and care staff, with people appearing
 relaxed, engaged and confident.

 People feel safe and benefit from strong safeguarding arrangements. ‘Student voice’ is embedded
 through reviews, forums, key worker sessions and daily practice, ensuring people are listened to
 and can influence decisions about their lives. Families consistently praised the support provided,
 with one relative describing the care as ‘above and beyond what you expect’ and another stating,
 “Basically everything!” when asked what the service does well. These exceptional outcomes
 demonstrate a service that consistently promotes and enhances 's well-being and supports people
 to achieve their full potential through person-centred, compassionate and highly effective care and
 support.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People receive exceptionally high-quality, personalised care and support which is designed around
their strengths, aspirations, preferences and individual needs. Comprehensive assessments are
undertaken before admission to ensure the service is suitable and that people receive the right
support from the outset. Information gathered from families, professionals and multidisciplinary
teams informs detailed support arrangements which are regularly reviewed to ensure they remain
responsive to changing circumstances and outcomes.

Care records demonstrate an excellent understanding of people as individuals. Personal plans are
person-centred, outcome-focused and provide clear guidance to care staff on how to support
people consistently. Plans reflect people's routines, preferences, communication methods, goals
and aspirations, helping ensure care is delivered in a way that promotes dignity, choice and
independence. We saw strong evidence that people, their families and professionals contribute to
care planning and review processes, ensuring support remains meaningful and relevant.

The service benefits from highly effective multidisciplinary working arrangements. Residential,
education, behavioural support, therapy and clinical teams work closely together to review progress
and respond promptly to changes in need. Six-weekly multidisciplinary reviews provide valuable
opportunities to evaluate support arrangements, identify emerging trends and agree actions to
improve outcomes. This collaborative approach helps ensure people receive seamless support and
benefit from consistent practice across all areas of the service.

Communication support is a particular strength. People are provided with personalised
communication passports, social stories, visual tools and specialist speech and language therapy
support where required. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of individual communication
needs and adapt their approach accordingly. This helps people express their wishes, make choices
and participate in decisions which affect their lives. We saw excellent examples of personalised
resources being used to reduce anxiety, improve understanding and promote engagement.

Positive Behaviour Support arrangements are comprehensive and informed by specialist clinical
input. Detailed plans provide care staff with clear guidance regarding proactive, preventative and
responsive strategies. These plans are regularly reviewed and include personalised information
about triggers, communication styles and effective interventions. Risk assessments are thorough
and promote positive risk-taking whilst ensuring people's safety and well-being. Care staff have
access to clear, detailed guidance which supports them to respond consistently and confidently to
complex situations.

Medication management arrangements were found to be robust. Medication administration records
were accurate, audits were completed regularly and protocols were in place to support safe
administration, monitoring and review. Records demonstrated clear oversight and evidence of

       Environment                         Good



People live in environments that generally support their well-being, independence and day-to-day
needs. Accommodation is spread across a range of houses and self-contained flats, providing
opportunities for people to develop independence and prepare for the future after college.
Bedrooms are personalised and reflect people's individual interests, hobbies and preferences
through the use of photographs, personal possessions, sensory items and decorations. We saw
examples of bedrooms personalised to meet specific needs, helping people feel comfortable and
secure within their home environments.

The service benefits from extensive outdoor facilities which provide meaningful opportunities for
recreation, exercise and social engagement. People have access to gardens, accessible pathways,
swings, football goals, trampolines, sensory spaces and other outdoor areas. These spaces are
generally well maintained and encourage people to spend time outdoors, develop social skills and
participate in activities which support their well-being. The grounds also include facilities that
enable people to engage in work experience opportunities and spaces which promote both student
and staff well-being.

Communal areas are generally comfortable, welcoming and suitable for their intended purpose.
During the inspection we observed people relaxing in living rooms, listening to music, spending
time with their peers and accessing sensory facilities. Several homes have benefited from
refurbishment works, including modernised bathroom facilities and well-maintained communal
spaces. The self-contained flats provide a particularly positive environment for developing
independent living skills, with suitable kitchen and living facilities that enable people to practise
everyday tasks and prepare for the next stages of their lives.

The provider has systems in place to monitor the condition of the environment and identify required
maintenance works. Environmental audits, maintenance logs and refurbishment plans provide
oversight of the premises and demonstrate a commitment to ongoing investment. We found
evidence of planned improvement works and agreements for significant refurbishment projects,
including replacement fire doors and repairs to areas affected by water damage. The provider had
responded positively to concerns raised by external agencies and had developed action plans to
address identified issues.

However, improvements are required to ensure all areas of the environment consistently meet the
standard expected. During our inspection of one of the homes, we identified maintenance issues
including water damage, mould around a bath, damaged flooring, peeling paintwork, damaged
furnishings and some bedrooms without appropriate window coverings. While many of these issues
had been reported and plans were in place to address them, they had not always been resolved

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from a service that is led by a strong, experienced and visible leadership team which
is committed to delivering high-quality, person-centred care and support. The Responsible
Individual (RI) and managers demonstrate a detailed understanding of the people using the service
and maintain a clear focus on achieving positive outcomes. Throughout the inspection, we found
evidence of a positive culture where people, families and care staff are valued, listened to and
encouraged to contribute to the ongoing development of the service.

Governance arrangements are comprehensive and provide effective oversight of the quality and
safety of care. Regular audits, quality and safety meetings, multidisciplinary reviews, provider
assessments and RI visits are used to monitor performance and identify areas for development.
Managers respond appropriately to incidents, complaints and concerns through detailed
investigations, reflective practice and action planning. The service demonstrates a willingness to
learn and make improvements, promoting accountability and continuous development across all
areas of practice.

Workforce development is a significant strength. Care staff benefit from structured induction
programmes, competency frameworks, supervision, appraisals and access to a broad range of
training opportunities. We found strong compliance with mandatory training requirements and
evidence of ongoing professional development opportunities for care staff at all levels. Innovative
arrangements are in place to support internationally recruited care staff, helping them to settle into
both their roles and life within Wales. Care staff described the organisation as supportive and
committed to their development.

Care staff consistently spoke positively about the leadership and support available to them. One
care staff member stated, “I would recommend working at BWC (Beechwood Court) because of the
supportive and approachable management team, the dedication and teamwork demonstrated by
staff, and the opportunities available for personal and professional development.” Another told us,
“Overall, I am proud to be part of the service. There is a strong commitment to providing high-
quality, person-centred support, and staff work hard to ensure individuals are safe, happy, and
supported to achieve their goals.” During discussions, care staff described managers as
approachable, visible and responsive, and reported feeling valued and supported within their roles.

The service promotes a positive workplace culture which supports care staff well-being and
retention. Care staff described strong peer support, meaningful supervision arrangements and
opportunities for career progression. We saw additional initiatives to support staff welfare, including
a staff food bank, recognition awards and dedicated spaces for prayer and reflection. Team
meetings are regular, detailed and demonstrate meaningful engagement, with care staff

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
