Summary:

 The Manor House is based in St Hilary and can accommodate 30 people with a range of nursing
 and personal care needs. This inspection was unannounced. There is a Responsible Individual (RI)
 for the service and a manager who is registered with Social Care Wales (SCW).

 People receive exceptional care and support from a skilled, experienced, and committed staff team.
 Effective staffing arrangements ensure people’s needs are consistently met, resulting in excellent
 outcomes for their wellbeing.

 Leadership and management are admirable and promote a culture of inclusivity, respect and
 transparency. Staff are highly valued and benefit from effective training, ongoing professional
 development and clear guidance to support them in their roles. Managers provide first-rate visible
 leadership and are actively involved in the day-to-day running of the service. Robust governance
 arrangements effectively monitor quality, drive continuous improvement and ensure care is
 delivered in line with people’s wishes and preferences. Feedback from people’s families,
 representatives and staff demonstrate excellent levels of satisfaction, with the service described as

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People are safe and receive excellent, person-centred care and support reflecting their individual
 needs and preferences. Staff treat people with dignity and respect and demonstrate a caring,
 friendly and responsive approach. Care records are clear, comprehensive and support the
 consistent delivery of care. People are encouraged and supported to maintain meaningful
 relationships and visitors are welcomed to the home. Visitors spoke positively about their
 experiences, telling us they are always made to feel welcome and are regularly invited to share
 meals with their relatives or friends. Effective systems are in place to promote good practice and
 support positive outcomes for people. The management team maintains robust oversight of
 incidents, accidents, complaints and safeguarding matters. The service has a statement of purpose
 that accurately reflects the care and support provided.
 Outcomes for people using the service are consistently achieved and are of an excellent standard.
 People are supported to achieve and exceed their personal outcomes, promoting wellbeing and
 independence. Feedback from people was highly positive, with individuals describing strong,
 supportive relationships with staff. Comments included, “Staff are very kind us here” and “The food
 is absolutely excellent; I feel very blessed.” Care staff spoke positively about their roles and
 demonstrated a strong commitment to providing high-quality care and support. They described
 feeling well supported by the management team and valued the positive team culture within the
 service. Comments included, “I love my job here, I wouldn’t consider working anywhere else.” and
 “We are a very good team; we all work well together.”
 People are at the heart of the service, and effective measures are in place to ensure excellent
 standards of care and support. Where possible, people are enabled to maintain choice and control
 over their daily lives, following their preferred routines with encouragement and support from care
 staff. People have access to a wide range of meaningful group and individual activities, which are
 available daily to promote well-being and engagement. People are safeguarded by effective
 systems and processes that promote their safety and wellbeing. During the inspection visit, we
 observed a welcoming and homely environment, with positive interactions contributing to a strong
 sense of belonging. People appeared relaxed and comfortable in the presence of care staff, with
 their body language and expressions indicating they felt safe and secure. Individuals told us they
 were happy living at the service and felt well supported. Comments included, “I couldn’t ask for
 more” (resident) and “Staff are amazing and very kind here, it really is faultless” (relative).

 People live in a safe environment where robust arrangements are in place to ensure equipment
 and facilities are regularly serviced and maintained. Comprehensive safety checks are undertaken,
 and fire safety is given a high priority. The service ensures that appropriate Deprivation of Liberty
 Safeguards (DoLS) authorisations are in place for individuals who lack the capacity to make
 decisions about their care and support. These measures help to promote positive outcomes for
 people and support their safety and wellbeing.
 The leadership and management culture is highly effectual, ensuring people receive consistently
 high-quality, person-centred care and support that meets their individual needs and promotes

       Care & Support                           Excellent



Care and support is excellent and consistently delivered by skilled, experienced staff who
demonstrate professionalism and competence in meeting people’s needs. Staff maintain accurate
and up-to-date records clearly reflecting individuals’ current needs and the high standard of care
provided. Personal plans reviewed evidenced comprehensive and robust documentation. Nurses
and care staff told us that any changes in a person’s needs, condition, or concerns are reported
promptly to the manager, ensuring care plans are reviewed and updated in a timely manner to
maintain high standards of care. Care staff spoke positively about their roles and described feeling
valued and respected within the organisation. One staff member told us, “I love my job here, we
make a real difference, I have never worked anywhere with such good standards.” and “We are a
really excellent team.”

We observed care staff engaging with people in a friendly, respectful, and person-centred manner.
People’s choices and preferences were consistently promoted, including decisions relating to
meals, drinks, and snacks. The dining experience was of an exceptional standard, with meals
observed to be nutritious, beautifully presented, and tailored to individual needs. The chef
confirmed menus are adapted to reflect people’s likes, dislikes, dietary requirements, and personal
requests. We were told that if a meal is returned uneaten, alternative options are always offered
and encouraged to ensure people receive food they enjoy. Fresh meat, fish, vegetables, and locally
sourced produce are delivered regularly, supporting the provision of high-quality meals. The service
has achieved a food hygiene rating of five (very good), demonstrating effective food safety and
hygiene practices.

The service has systems in place for medication management. People receive their medication as
prescribed. We looked at the medication administration records (MARs) and saw they are
appropriately completed. Fridge and room temperatures are recorded daily by staff, and we saw
the medication room to be clean and extremely well organised. The service promotes good
hygiene practices and manages infection risks effectively. We observed staff using appropriate
personal protective equipment (PPE) when required.

A safeguarding policy is in place to guide staff in protecting adults at risk from abuse, harm and
neglect. Records confirmed staff have completed up-to-date safeguarding training. Where people
are unable to advocate for themselves, family members or appointed representatives are involved
in care reviews and decision-making processes, helping to ensure their views and best interests
are represented.

       Environment                              Excellent



People experience a true sense of belonging within the home and benefit from a warm, welcoming
environment. A variety of small communal areas provide opportunities to socialise with others,
spend time quietly, or meet with family and friends. Bedrooms are personalised with people’s own
belongings and chosen items, promoting a homely and familiar atmosphere. Several dining areas
and well-maintained gardens further enhance the high quality of the environment. We observed
people making full use of the recently introduced outdoor seating areas during the warmer weather.
People were seen enjoying time outdoors throughout the day with drinks and snacks provided. The
area is further enhanced by an outdoor call bell system, enabling people to access support when
needed while promoting independence. The seating areas provide a welcoming environment that
encourages social interaction, conversation and laughter among people living in and visiting the
service.

People are cared for in an extremely clean and homely environment. People are protected from
environmental health and safety risks. The home offers first-rate accommodation for people living
at the home and the provider has shown a commitment to developing and improving it for their
people’s benefit. People can be confident that there are effective arrangements at the home that
will protect public safety and minimise cross infection. There is oversight to ensure staff follow the
correct infection guidance. We found call bell checks are carried out and call bells accessible
throughout all areas of the home.

Access and egress throughout the home is suitable for individuals with mobility needs. The
entrance is secure, and staff requested identification and a signature in the visitor book before
allowing entry. This demonstrates that the service monitors visitors appropriately to ensure only
authorised individuals are permitted access.

People can be assured they live in a safe and well-maintained environment. During our tour of the
home, we found hazards had been reduced as far as practically possible and hazardous
substances stored securely. We observed domestic staff carrying out cleaning duties and reviewed
a range of health and safety records, which demonstrated effective oversight of environmental
safety. Confidential information was stored securely in locked areas. Regular fire safety checks are
completed, individual personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) are in place to support safe
evacuation, and a current fire risk assessment is maintained. Staff receive appropriate fire safety
and first aid training. All required fire safety works have been completed. The home, including its
attractive gardens, provides a high-quality environment which is safe, secure, and exceptionally
well maintained. People told us they value the home and enjoy spending time both within the
interior and in the pleasant gardens, which offer stunning views and opportunities for relaxation and
enjoyment.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management are excellent, providing people with confidence that the service is
well governed and effectively overseen. The Statement of Purpose has been reviewed and clearly
outlines the service’s values and philosophy of care, placing a strong emphasis on choice, dignity
and safety. Care records we examined demonstrated that these principles are consistently
embedded in practice and reflected in the support people receive.

People can be assured that leadership and management provide effective oversight of the service
and closely monitor the quality of care delivered. The service provider demonstrates a strong
commitment to continuous improvement, supported by well-established systems and processes
which promote the safe and efficient operation of the home. Comprehensive clinical and non-
clinical audits are undertaken across key areas, including falls management, nutrition, medication,
and skin integrity. The Responsible Individual is present at the home daily and maintains regular
contact with people, relatives, and staff to obtain feedback on the quality of the service. We
reviewed evidence of monitoring arrangements, including the most recent Quality of Care Review
report dated January 2026, which was completed to a high standard and demonstrated robust
quality assurance practices. These arrangements support the delivery of high-quality care and
positive outcomes for people.

People can be confident that staff are fully competent in their roles, supported by robust recruitment
procedures and comprehensive pre-employment checks to ensure they are suitable to work with
vulnerable adults. Staff receive formal supervision every three months, alongside regular clinical
observations of practice, providing opportunities to reflect on performance, discuss development
needs and raise any concerns. We were informed that staff turnover is exceptionally low, with many
employees having worked at the service for several years. This promotes continuity, consistency
and positive relationships for people receiving care and support. At the time of the inspection, there
was one staff vacancy. Staff told us they were keen to take the time needed to recruit the right
person who would share the home's values and contribute to maintaining the excellent standards of
care provided. The RI, manager and care staff described a range of initiatives to ensure staff feel
valued and supported, including access to wellbeing support and encouragement to undertake
further professional qualifications, particularly in relation to palliative and end-of-life care. The
manager told us a member of staff has recently completed a MSc in Palliative Care and another
undertaking a MSc in Clinical Geriatrics. This investment in staff development benefits people
through the provision of excellent care delivered by a knowledgeable, confident and committed
workforce. Innovative leadership, effective governance and a positive organisational culture
contribute to consistently positive outcomes for people.

People benefit from safe, person-centred care delivered by trained and competent staff. Staff
complete mandatory training, including safeguarding and dementia care, and receive a thorough
induction. Policies and procedures support good practice, and the service makes safeguarding

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
