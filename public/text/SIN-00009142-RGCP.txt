Summary:

 East Park Care Centre is a pleasant and spacious 21 bed residential home for adults. It is situated
 in the village of Jeffreyston in Pembrokeshire.

 At this inspection we found peoples well-being is good, because both the care and support people
 receive and the environment they live in support them to achieve good well-being outcomes. There
 is a real sense of community and friendship within the service, with people feeling happy,
 comfortable and at home.

 Care and Support is good. People are supported to maintain and improve their physical, mental
 health, and emotional wellbeing by caring and compassionate care staff. Care staff know people
 well and interactions are respectful and personalised to individual needs.

 The environment is good and people benefit from a warm, welcoming, comfortable, and homely
 environment which promotes their well-being.

 Leadership and management of the service is good because the positive actions of the
 Responsible Individual (RI), manager and care staff team ensure people achieve good outcomes.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People experience good outcomes because they have choice and control over their day. They
 decide when to get up, dress and they can move freely around the home as they wish. Care staff
 know people well and treat them with dignity and respect. People told us “I’m happy here” and
 “Nothing is too much trouble” reflecting positive relationships within the home. Family members told
 us, “They treat people as individuals” and “It’s their home”.

 People enjoy a range of nutritious, home-cooked meals, and told us they have choice, one person
 told us, “The food is very good” and another “They get to know what you like”. A four weekly menu
 is in place to offer a variety of meals and people are offered an alternative if they do not like what is
 available. Many people receive support from family members and friends to personalise their rooms
 with photographs and meaningful belongings, promoting identity and emotional well-being.

 People are supported to maintain their physical, emotional, and social well-being. They have
 opportunities to develop and sustain positive relationships with those they live with and the wider
 community. Activities are varied and meaningful, supporting people to reach their potential. These
 include visiting local beaches, arts and crafts, singers, musicians and board games. These are
 activities people told us they enjoy and look forward to.

 Leaders and care staff demonstrate a commitment to enhancing people’s lives, wellbeing and self-
 worth. Care staff know people well and treat them with dignity and respect. Family members told us
 their loved ones are valued, and care staff feel appreciated. We saw warm and positive interactions
 between care staff and people, including attentive listening and reassurance. People described
 feeling safe and happy in their home, one person told us “It’s home from home”. Family members
 echoed this, one said, “There is a family feel, they genuinely care, they know mum well”.

 People are supported to maintain and sustain relationships with family, friends and those important
 to them. Family members and friends are integral to the services and encouraged to visit freely.
 Family members told us they feel welcomed and involved, sharing comments such as “We are
 welcome anytime” and “I can visit whenever I want”. This approach promotes emotional wellbeing
 and positive relationships.

 Measures are in place to keep people as safe as possible and protected from harm and abuse. All
 care staff have received training in safeguarding procedures and know how to raise any concerns
 they may have.

       Care & Support                       Good



People are supported by care staff who are kind, reliable, compassionate and know them well.
Warm, reassuring and respectful interactions were seen throughout the service, and people told us
they feel listened to and supported. Family members described positive communication with the
team and expressed confidence in the care provided.

People are supported to maintain meaningful relationships with family and friends, which
contributes positively to their emotional well-being. People and their family members feel they
receive the care they need and speak highly about the care staff. People told us, “I can talk to all of
the staff”, “Care is excellent”, and “I’m very happy here”. Family members told us, “Staff
communication is great”, “Communication is really important and its exceptional there”, “Nothing is
too much trouble” “They are friendly and approachable”, and another “I find them fantastic,
absolutely amazing”, demonstrating the welcoming, friendly and inclusive atmosphere.

The service has assessment arrangements in place to ensure it can safely and appropriately meet
people’s needs. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of people’s routines, preferences,
and communication needs, and documentation provides useful guidance to support consistent
practice. While personal plans and daily notes reflected the service's individual and strengths-
based approach. However, opportunities were identified to strengthen recording to ensure
information was consistently reviewed and updated to reflect people's current needs, experiences,
aspirations and risks. More robust oversight of care planning documentation and daily notes would
provide greater assurance that support remains responsive to changing needs.

People’s health needs are identified and responded to in a timely way, with evidence of good
communication between care staff, families, and external professionals.

Medication is stored securely. We found procedures can be strengthened further by managers
completing audits more frequently to identify any discrepancies in records in a timely manner.


Systems are in place to protect people from harm. Care staff understand their safeguarding
responsibilities and felt confident reporting any concerns to managers and were assured they
would respond promptly. Care staff ensure effective infection prevention and control measures are
implemented to reduce the risk of infection and illness amongst individuals. Appropriate personal
protective equipment (PPE) was available and used correctly by staff to support effective infection
prevention and control practices.

Overall, people receive effective, person-centred care, and the provider is actively strengthening
care-planning processes to further support positive outcomes.

       Environment                         Good



People live in accommodation that meets their needs and benefits them emotionally and physically.
They experience a warm, comfortable, and homely environment which promotes their well-being.
The building and its contents are well maintained and in a good state of repair and decor. Domestic
staff are employed to ensure the environment is kept clean and hygienic.

The setting is on one level and communal areas are spacious, allowing easy access for people
around the home. There is also an outside patio area which is easily accessible from the main
lounge area and the garden is well maintained providing additional space that supports people’s
well-being.

People benefit from clean, uncluttered, and safe surroundings. Corridors were free from hazards,
and all bedrooms were clean and tidy. A family member told us, “The home is always clean, it’s a
nice environment”.

Communal areas are welcoming and well-decorated. The dining area is bright, airy, and colourful,
supporting social interaction at mealtimes. People access both communal and private spaces
where they can spend time alone, socialise or meet visitors. Care staff respect people’s choices
about how and where they spend their day. There is sufficient space for people to move freely,
which supports independence and allows activities and recreation to take place.

Bedrooms are personalised according to people’s preferences, with photographs, soft furnishings
and personal items that help maintain identity and promote a person-centred approach. One
person told us, “I’m quite happy with my room” and a family member said “Their room is lovely”.

The home provides accessible bathroom and toilet facilities that promote privacy and dignity. Wet
rooms enhance independence for people who can manage aspects of their personal care. There is
ample, well-maintained equipment to meet people’s needs. There are good arrangements in place
to minimise risks to people’s health and safety and a range of maintenance checks and audits are
undertaken. Records show equipment is routinely serviced by an external agency. The kitchen was
clean and organised, with appropriate checks in place. The service has achieved a food hygiene
rating of 5 (Very Good), demonstrating strong infection control and food safety practices. Laundry
arrangements support good hygiene and reduce risks of cross-contamination. Substances
hazardous to health (COSSH) are safely stored and only accessible to authorised staff.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service is led by a highly visible, approachable and values-driven management team, including
the Responsible Individual (RI) and registered manager. They promote a clear vision centred on
respect, dignity and person-centred care, and this is fully embedded across the service.

Leadership and management at the service demonstrate a strong commitment to providing safe,
consistent, and person-centred care. This creates an environment in which people and care staff
feel well supported. There is a positive culture throughout the service, and leaders are visible,
passionate, approachable, and engaged in day-to-day operations. This presence helps ensure
communication and supports the effective day-to-day running of the service and managers
maintain regular oversight of practice.

Quality assurance systems are in place and support service delivery; however, opportunities were
identified to strengthen the recording, evaluation and monitoring of audits and governance activity.
More consistent documentation of quality assurance processes would provide clearer evidence of
oversight, learning and continuous improvement. While aspects of governance and oversight could
be strengthened, there was no evidence these issues had negatively affected people's care,
support or outcomes. We observed people who were happy, engaged and living fulfilling lives.

People benefit from support delivered by a stable, experienced and knowledgeable workforce,
creating continuity and emotional security for people. Care staff are supported through regular
supervision and access to training which provides them with the skills to carry out their role. Staff
retention is positive, and feedback was consistently favourable regarding management support,
workplace culture and opportunities for development. Care staff described feeling valued,
respected and listened to, with strong working relationships evident throughout the service. People
and family members also provided positive feedback, and we found care staff were knowledgeable
about people's needs, preferences and personal outcomes. Care staff demonstrated the skills,
knowledge and commitment needed to support people effectively, and this positive workforce
culture supports the continuity, consistency and quality of care and support people receive.

Recruitment and vetting processes are effective and support the safe employment of care staff. We
looked at several key policies and found them to mostly hold relevant up to date information. Some
amendments are required to ensure all policies are up to date and current. This is being addressed.

Overall, leadership and management are effective and supportive, contributing to positive
outcomes for people, with a confident, well-supported workforce. The provider’s ongoing work to
strengthen internal systems and documentation will support continued development of the service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
