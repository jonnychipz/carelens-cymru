Summary:

 Overall people experience good wellbeing outcomes and enjoy living in Heathfields Residential
 Home where they feel safe, respected and valued. People told us they feel safe, are treated with
 dignity and respect, and are confident in the current manager’s ability to lead the service effectively.
 Relatives provided positive feedback about the quality of care, the approachable nature of staff and
 the communication maintained by the service. We observed warm and positive interactions
 throughout the inspection and found the atmosphere to be welcoming and person centred. While
 people benefit from positive relationships and support, opportunities remain to further enhance
 well-being  through  increased  access  to  meaningful  activities,  occupation and  community
 engagement to ensure all people can consistently enjoy fulfilling and enriching experiences.

 People receive good care and support from a committed staff team who know them well and
 understand their individual needs and preferences. A stable core staff team promotes continuity of
 care and enables positive relationships to develop. Care staff work collaboratively with health and
 social care professionals to support people’s health needs and ensure access to appropriate
 healthcare services when required. Regular multidisciplinary meetings help to monitor people's
 well-being and respond to changing needs.

People live in a comfortable and homely environment that supports their safety, comfort and well-
being. We found the home to be welcoming, with communal areas that provide opportunities for
social interaction and engagement. People have personalised  their bedrooms to  reflect  their
preferences and individuality, helping to create familiar and meaningful living spaces.

The manager has developed a good understanding of the service since taking up post and has
identified areas for further development to strengthen outcomes for people. The provider must
ensure the service is delivered consistently in line with its Statement of Purpose so people can be
confident they receive the care, support and opportunities described within this document.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People achieve positive well-being outcomes because they receive support from care staff who
 treat them with kindness, dignity and respect. People told us they feel safe living at the service and
 said care staff respond to requests for assistance in a timely manner. Relatives consistently
 described staff as caring, patient and knowledgeable about people's needs and preferences.
 People describe the care staff as “Very helpful”. “Pretty good” and “I can have a laugh with them,”
 Relatives told us, “Staff are amazing and know her sense of humour”, “I think they are excellent”
 and “It’s a nice atmosphere”.

 People maintain important relationships with family and friends. Visitors are welcomed without
 unnecessary restrictions and relatives told us they can visit at times that suit them. We saw positive
 interactions between people and care staff throughout the inspection and found care staff knew
 people well. The service promotes people's rights and offers opportunities for feedback through
 resident meetings. Records showed people discuss matters affecting daily life, including meals and
 service provision. People know who to approach if they have concerns and spoke positively about
 the new manager.

 People receive support to maintain  their health. The service maintains regular contact with
 healthcare professionals and participates in monthly multidisciplinary meetings to help prevent
 unnecessary  hospital admissions. Professionals described the service as approachable and
 responsive.

 People benefit from a comfortable home environment; however, opportunities to participate in
 meaningful activities require strengthening. Several people, relatives and care staff told us there
 are  limited organised  activities and  little community engagement. While occasional quizzes,
 entertainers and themed events take place, we saw limited evidence of individualised activity
 programmes or support for people to access their local community. Some people told us they
 would value greater social stimulation and more opportunities to maintain connections outside the
 home.

 People receive meals and refreshments throughout the day. However, meal choice is limited as the
 service generally offers one main meal option each day. Some people told us they enjoy the food
 provided, while others said menus can become repetitive over time and would benefit from greater
 variety and choice. This does not always support people to exercise meaningful choice and control
 in relation to their dining experience.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive care and support from a stable and experienced care staff team who know them
well. Many care staff have worked at the service for a significant period, which helps develop
positive relationships and promotes continuity of care. People told us they are happy with the care
they receive and spoke positively about the kindness and approachability of care staff. Relatives
also described care staff as caring and committed to supporting people’s well-being. We found care
staff knew people's needs, preferences and routines well and responded to them in a respectful
and compassionate manner.

The service uses an electronic care management system to record and monitor care and support.
Personal plans contain information about people's assessed needs. While plans generally provide
care staff with sufficient guidance to deliver day-to-day care, they could be developed further to
better reflect people's personal outcomes, life histories and individual aspirations. The manager
has recognised opportunities to further improve person-centred care planning. Personal plans are
reviewed routinely; however, records do not consistently demonstrate people's involvement or the
involvement of their representatives in review processes. Some care records contained limited
information about people's personal histories, aspirations, interests and preferences. The manager
has already started work to develop more detailed "This is Me" information to ensure staff have a
richer understanding of people as individuals.

Medication systems support the safe administration of medicines; however, practice requires
strengthening. We identified gaps in some staff medication administration signatures and found
records relating to 'as required' medicines did not consistently provide sufficient detail regarding
decision-making and outcomes. These issues reduce assurance  that medicines are always
managed in line with best practice and require improvement. The manager had already begun
strengthening audit processes and addressing these matters.

Infection prevention and control arrangements were appropriate and supported people to live in a
safe environment. We found a good supply of personal protective equipment (PPE) available to
staff, and observations showed staff followed infection control practices. Hazardous substances
were stored securely, with appropriate COSHH arrangements in place to reduce risks to people
and staff.

People  are  protected  from  harm,  abuse  and  neglect  because  appropriate  safeguarding
arrangements are in place. Care staff have completed safeguarding training, understand their
responsibilities and are able to clearly describe the action they would take  if they had concerns
about a person's safety or well-being. The service maintains a detailed safeguarding policy and
procedure, which were regularly reviewed to ensure they reflected current guidance and practice.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a safe environment. The front entrance was secure and remained locked to help
protect people from unauthorised access. Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are in
place and provide care staff with clear guidance to support people safely in the event of an
emergency.

We found recent issues relating to the passenger lift had been resolved. Records and discussions
with management confirmed appropriate action was taken to repair the  lift and ensure people
continued to access all areas of the home safely. The provider demonstrated a proactive approach
to contingency planning and informed us that a stair lift is currently being installed. This additional
equipment will help minimise disruption and maintain accessibility should any future issues arise
with the main lift.

The home  is  clean, welcoming and  provide  people  with  sufficient space  to spend  time
independently or  socially. Bedrooms  reflected people's  individual preferences and contained
personal belongings that helped create a homely atmosphere. Communal areas were comfortable
and supported people to engage with others or receive visitors. Although the home is generally
comfortable and suitable for people's needs, some areas would benefit from redecoration to further
enhance the environment. The manager had already identified these areas and informed us that a
structured improvement plan is being developed to prioritise and complete the required work.

The recently appointed manager has been unable to access  all historical records relating to
required environmental and health and safety checks completed before their appointment. They
acknowledged this has limited their ability to fully evaluate previous monitoring arrangements. To
address this, the manager has introduced a new suite of audits and oversight systems designed to
provide assurance  that required checks are completed consistently and  effectively. Records
evidenced these systems are being embedded and are beginning to provide clearer management
oversight of the service.

The manager has also identified opportunities to strengthen cleaning arrangements. Whilst we
found the home to be clean at the time of inspection, a more structured cleaning schedule would
support greater consistency, provide clearer accountability and further promote effective infection
prevention and control practices. The manager is developing these arrangements as part of the
service improvement programme.

       Leadership & Management   Requires Improvement



The service benefits from committed leadership and a management team that is focused on
improvement. A new deputy manager has been recruited. People, relatives, staff and professionals
spoke positively overall about the registered manager, describing her as approachable, visible and
responsive. The manager has introduced a range of improvements since commencing in post and
has demonstrated a clear commitment to service development.

The provider has recruitment processes in place that help ensure staff are suitable for their roles.
We reviewed staff recruitment records and found required pre-employment checks had been
completed. Staff told us they had received induction training and ongoing learning opportunities
relevant to their roles.

Staff feedback about staffing levels and morale was mixed. While people generally told us their
needs are met, several care staff reported pressures during busy periods and concerns about
staffing arrangements. The manager has recognised these concerns and now needs to review
staffing systems, including how staffing levels are assessed and monitored.

The provider has some systems in place to monitor the service, but these are not yet sufficiently
robust to ensure the service is consistently delivered in line with its Statement of Purpose. We
found some aspects of service delivery did not reflect the commitments and arrangements
described within this document. The Statement of Purpose must accurately describe how the
service operates and people must receive the service as set out within it. Outcomes for people
require improvement because the service is not consistently delivered in accordance with the
Statement of Purpose across all themes. We expect the provider to take action to address this
matter and ensure service delivery aligns with regulatory requirements.

The responsible Individual (RI) undertakes the required quarterly visits and biannual quality of care
reports but needs to strengthen the oversight and support provided to the manager. They must
maintain effective monitoring of the service and demonstrate robust assurance regarding the
quality and safety of care provided. They must also ensure the manager receives appropriate
support and guidance, to effectively fulfil their role and drive continuous improvement within the
service. This is an area for improvement, and the RI must take action to address.

The new manager has begun strengthening governance systems; however, quality assurance
arrangements remain under development. They do not have access to some historical records and
have had to establish several monitoring systems from the beginning. While the manager has
started to complete audits and tracking systems, these processes are not yet fully embedded
across all areas of service delivery.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
