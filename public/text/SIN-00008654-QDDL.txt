Summary:

 Plasgwyn Nursing Home provides a good standard of care in line with legislation People living in
 the home experience positive outcomes across well-being, care and support, the environment, and
 leadership and management.

 People are supported to maintain their well-being through respectful relationships, opportunities for
 meaningful activity, strong connections with family, friends, and the local community. Feedback
 from people and their families is consistently positive, reflecting kindness, dignity and compassion
 in care delivery.

 Care and support are well planned, person-centered and responsive to individual needs. Staff
 understand people’s preferences, enable good risk management and provide safe, consistent care.
 Medication systems and safeguarding arrangements are effective.

 The environment is clean, safe and well maintained, supporting people’s comfort and
 independence. Ongoing improvements and regular safety checks ensure the

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People at Plasgwyn Nursing Home experience good well-being outcomes and are supported to live
 meaningful lives. We observed people are relaxed and comfortable, with staff interacting in a warm,
 respectful, and person-centered manner. Staff consistently offer choices and communicate in ways
 that meet people’s needs, including in Welsh, which supports identity and inclusion. One person
 told us, “Dwi’n hapus yma” meaning they are happy in the home, and another said, “Mae’r bwyd yn
 dda”,           meaning             the             food                 is            good.

 People have opportunities to take part in activities which reflect their interests. Care staff ensure
 people remain engaged through walks, music and crafts. Entertainers often  visit which people
 appreciate and enjoy. There are links with community groups and schools which enable people to
 remain a part of the local community. Seasonal events, such as Easter celebrations, further
 promote                                       social                                        inclusion.

 People maintain meaningful   relationships. We observed visitors  throughout  the   inspection,
 including a family member  visiting with a pet dog. This supports emotional well-being and
 connections with loved ones. Families spoke highly of the service, stating “He could not have had
 better care… simply wonderful” and “Could not be more helpful, all staff encountered were kind.”

 Staff promote dignity and respect, particularly at end of life, where compassionate practices are
 embedded. Feedback  included  “Dignity  right  to  the  very end” and  “Exceptional care and
 compassion.” People are supported to be safe and have their rights respected, care staff are aware
 of   local   safeguarding   procedures   to   protect   people   from   neglect   and   harm.

 People can personalise their rooms and make choices about daily routines, food and activities.
 Improvements  to menu  choice have been  implemented,  people  reported  having  choices
 at mealtimes, this was reflected in printed menus and diary notes. People spoken with said they are
 happy       with      the      food     and      choices       available       to      them.

       Care & Support                       Good




People receive good quality care and support tailored to their individual needs. Personal plans are
detailed, regularly reviewed, and reflect people’s preferences, needs, and risks. A good pen picture
of  the  person   is  included  with   their  preferred  routines.  Plans  include  clear  goals,
risk assessments and  guidance   for   care   staff,   supporting   consistent   care   delivery.

Care records are comprehensive and person-centered. Information about people’s life histories,
preferences, and communication needs are clearly recorded. Care plans include monitoring of
nutrition and hydration, falls risk management and mental capacity assessments where required.
Risk assessments are included in people’s personal plans and are regularly reviewed to mitigate
risks               and               keep                people                   safe.

Where people lack capacity, appropriate Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) are in place to
protect their rights. Advocacy services are available to ensure people’s voices are heard. Steps are
taken to protect people’s rights where appropriate. The service operates in a transparent manner,
communicating   well   with   local   authorities  and  Care   Inspectorate  Wales   (CIW).

Healthcare needs are well managed, with regular reviews and timely referrals to health care
professionals  such  as   dietitians  when required.  People   at   risk   of  weight  loss  are
closely monitored and           supported            with               fortified              diets.

Medication  is managed  safely  through an  electronic  system,  with  robust  processes  for
administration, recording, and auditing. Controlled drugs are handled appropriately with checks in
place                                                                               to minimise errors.

People benefit from continuity of care, as staffing levels are stable, and key workers are allocated
to people.Retention of staff is good and people receive care from care staff who are familiar to
them.Care staff use electronic devices to record care in real time, supporting accurate and up-to-
date                                                                                     records.

Feedback from families reflects high standards, including “The person’s personal hygiene was dealt
with   to   perfection”  and  “Means  a   great  deal  they  are  so   well  looked   after.”

.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a clean, safe, and comfortable environment that supports their well-being and
independence. The home is well maintained, with evidence of ongoing investment, including
redecorated lounges and refreshed communal areas.

Bedrooms are personalised, clean and well equipped, with call bells, appropriate furniture and
personal items reflecting individual preferences. Communal areas are welcoming and decorated in
a seasonal manner, contributing to a homely atmosphere.

Health and safety systems are robust. Regular checks of fire alarms, emergency lighting and
equipment are carried out, and personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) are in place to
ensure people can be evacuated safely if needed. Certification for gas safety, legionella and
insurance is current as are all certificates for utility checks and safety.

The kitchen is well managed, with a food hygiene rating of 5, demonstrating high standards of
cleanliness and food safety. People have access to nutritious meals and snacks, with improved
processes for gathering feedback on menu choices.

Equipment such as hoists are regularly serviced, and maintenance systems ensure issues are
addressed promptly. Corridors are clear and accessible, ensuring people can walk safely without
encountering obstacles.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Plasgwyn Nursing Home benefits from effective leadership and governance. The manager is well
supported, and able to access resources needed to meet people’s needs. Systems are in place
to monitor quality, including audits, supervision, and regular reports. These findings are discussed
amongst senior staff and are communicated to care staff to enable good practice and continually
improving standards of care.

The responsible individual maintains oversight through regular visits to the home and produces
reports in line with legislation, which include feedback from people, families, and staff. This
supports continuous improvement and accountability.

Care staff are well trained and supported. Training records show staff receive ongoing
development, and competency assessments are completed regularly. Staff spoke positively about
the service, with one stating “Mae’n dda yma, mae pobl yn cael gofal da ac yn hapus “meaning the
service is good, people have good care and are happy, and another reporting “everything is going
well.”

Care staff levels are stable, supporting continuity of care. Recruitment practices are safe,
with appropriate checks in place. Long-standing staff contribute to a consistent and experienced
workforce.

Policies and procedures are in place to guide practice, including safeguarding, complaints, and
data protection. These are regularly reviewed to ensure they remain relevant. Safeguarding
concerns are managed appropriately, demonstrating adherence to procedures.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
