Summary:

 Snowdon Care Home provides residential care and support for up to 27 individuals in the village of
 Llanberis, North Wales. People have access to a bustling community village with good amenities
 and public transport connections to busier neighbouring towns.

 People experience good wellbeing outcomes because of the significant changes implemented
 since the last inspection. People are encouraged to have a voice and participate in developing their
 care and support.

 Care and support are good at Snowdon Care Home. We found improved outcomes for people
 whereby they are supported to access the services and support needed for good physical and
 mental well-being.

 The environment at the service is developing to support people’s needs and provide pleasant
 accommodation. Whilst improvements are clear to see, further improvements are needed to ensure
 all areas of the service are in good condition.

 The leadership and management of the service is good. Recruitment to key leadership roles has
 provided structure and direction. Improved oversight has resulted in targeted action to address
 areas requiring development. The provider is yet to appoint a new Responsible Individual for the
 service.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People have choice and control over most aspects of their day-to-day life. People have the freedom
 to choose when they want to get up and go to bed, what they want to do with their time and where
 they want to do this. Many people at the service can access the community independently and do
 so with appropriate support and risk assessment in place. The new manager has taken action to
 make sure people have a voice and can contribute their views about what happens at the service.
 People are asked about what activities they would like to see, what meals they would like cooked
 and ideas for decorating and enhancing the communal areas. We saw ideas put forward by people
 have already begun to be implemented which is having a positive impact on the atmosphere and
 environment. The provider has supported these ideas by making resources available.

 People access the support they need to meet their physical, mental, and emotional needs. Specific
 actions for people which were outstanding at the last inspection have been actioned, this includes
 obtaining specific equipment and ensuring targeted risk assessments are in place. The manager,
 along with senior staff have reviewed all individuals at the service to ensure any additional support
 from external health professionals is in place or has been requested. The manager has a good
 understanding of what services are available to support people’s specific needs. One person told
 us they were so happy they were in the process of getting a new, better wheelchair. Health care
 professionals provided positive feedback about the changes at the service.

 We observed a pleasant and sociable lunch time experience. The completion of the conservatory
 area and new floors throughout have encouraged people to use these communal areas and we
 saw people sitting together for lunch, talking, and enjoying their meal. Care staff told us they have
 time in the afternoons to spend with people chatting or doing an activity. Time has been taken to
 ask people what they enjoy doing and the manager has begun to implement different activities to
 suit these preferences. Family and friends are welcomed by the service as we saw people
 receiving visitors on the day of inspection. Positive feedback has been provided to us by visiting
 family member. One person commented on the positive feel to the home, another said “there have
 been huge improvements recently.”

       Care & Support                       Good



People are  in receipt of care and support which meets  their  identified needs and personal
outcomes. This  is because the manager has  fully reviewed  all care records to ensure the
information recorded is accurate, relevant and captures people’s views and wishes. A resident of
the day system has been introduced which has facilitated regular review of each person and
ensures any actions or changes are implemented in a timely way. People or their representative
are actively involved in the assessment process and the creation of their care records. This is
evident from the person-centred nature of the information recorded. At this inspection we found
care records to be complete and reflective of the care and support people need. Risk assessments
have been completed to inform care staff of potential risk and how to manage these with dignity
and respect.

The safety of people living at Snowdon has improved since the last inspection, with risks posed by
areas of non-compliance now rectified. Progress has been made to completion levels of key
training subjects,  specifically safeguarding. Assessment, care planning, and  risk assessment
ensure the service can meet people’s needs. Improvements have been made to the oversight and
management of legal authorisations where people experience restrictions in their best interest. The
manager has a good understanding of these processes and the importance of having accurate and
up to date records. The provider has demonstrated good practice  in reporting safeguarding
concerns and engaging with the relevant professionals to appropriately manage potential risks.

Significant improvements have been made to the safe management of medicines at the service.
People receive the medication they need, when they need  it, as directed by the prescriber. The
provider has taken immediate action to rectify issues identified at the last inspection in relation to
medicines management. The manager has retained incredibly good evidence to show the positive
impact new processes have had on medication processes. Increased oversight of this area by the
manager and senior  staff means care  staff are well supported and ensures best practice  is
consistently carried out.

       Environment               Requires Improvement



People continue to benefit from the ongoing renovations being carried out at the service. At the last
inspection work had been completed on the sun lounge to a high standard. New windows provide
improved insulation as well as better visibility of the outdoor space. New flooring has been installed
across the ground floor, and the provider has purchased new armchairs, tables, and dining chairs.
These investments have significantly improved the appearance of these areas and people are
making more use of communal space. A shelter has been erected outside to provide cover for
people who smoke and there are plans in place to further develop the outdoor areas in the spring.
People told us they are looking forward to helping with the outdoor work. Work is still required to
address the outstanding renovations to bathrooms. The provider acknowledges the importance of
people having access to well-equipped and pleasant areas for personal care and had given
assurances this is a priority. This is an area for improvement, and we expect the provider to take
action.

The accommodation mostly supports people to achieve their personal outcomes. People have
access to equipment which supports their physical needs and promotes independence. The
manager is proactive in accessing external support to make sure people have the equipment they
need. Equipment at the service is checked on a regular basis to ensure it is in good working order
and safe for use. The provider invests in equipment and furniture which supports people’s
wellbeing. People have clean and well-equipped bedrooms which are personalised to their
personal tastes and reflect what is important to them. People have access to call bells so they can
call for assistance when needed. High importance is placed on checking the safety and cleanliness
of people’s rooms daily. The housekeeping team work hard to make sure people live in a clean
environment which supports infection prevention and control practice.

       Leadership & Management               Good



People can be assured the provider has taken notable action to address the issues identified at the
last inspection which were impacting on the overall delivery of the service. An experienced and
confident manager has been appointed at the service which has created a strong leadership team,
the impact of which is visible across the service. The manager took immediate action to fully audit
all areas of the service, identify what action was needed and make necessary changes. A clear
evidence trail has been retained to show the progress made. Staff at the service are very positive
about the new management and can see the positive impact the recent changes have had on the
service. The provider has made progress in their oversight of the service in the absence of a
responsible individual (RI) and has supported the manager as required. The provider is still
required to appoint a suitable person to the role of RI and this remains an area for improvement
which we will review at the next inspection. The changes at the service have been communicated
with people and their representatives through a new newsletter and invite people to a relative and
residents meeting.

People have access to important documents about where they live. The statement of purpose and
guide to the service have been updated to be an accurate reflection of the service and to provide
information about the complaints process and who people can speak to if they have a concern,
including access to advocacy services. The provider has ensured they have access to their CIW
account and submits notifications as required so the regulator is aware of key events at the service.

We found previous improvements to the supervision of staff at the service have continued to be
sustained with the manager continuing to provide one to one supervision to staff on a regular basis.
Staff meetings take place and detailed minutes of these meetings are recorded to evidence what
was discussed and what actions were set. Progress has been made for training with improved
completion rates. This continues to be a priority for the manager. Action has been taken to ensure
staff complete training in areas which are specific to meet the needs of the people they are
supporting.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.
