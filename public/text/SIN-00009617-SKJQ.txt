Summary:

 Ystrad Fechan  is a Local Authority respite service  for adults with a learning  disability and
 associated needs, located centrally in Treorchy.  It  is co-located on a site with another Local
 Authority service, being based in the smaller bungalow building.


 Well-being is good because people are supported to make choices, maintain independence and
 achieve outcomes which are important to them. Strong person-centred planning, meaningful
 activities and positive risk-taking help people enjoy their respite experiences. Care and support is
 good because people receive personalised care from skilled care staff who understand their needs
 and  preferences.  Good  safeguarding,  medication  management  and  infection  prevention
 arrangements help keep people safe and well. The environment is good because people stay in a
 comfortable, welcoming and well-maintained setting which promotes independence and meets a
 wide range of needs. Leadership and management is good because leaders provide effective
 oversight and continue to drive improvement. Strong governance, quality assurance and learning
 arrangements support positive outcomes  for people, while a well-trained  staff team delivers
 consistent care.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 Well-being is good because people are supported to achieve positive outcomes through person-
 centred planning, meaningful choice and an environment which promotes independence and well-
 being. Care staff provide support centred on individual strengths, preferences and outcomes. Pre-
 stay assessments and reviews involve people and their families in planning support, helping people
 maintain control over daily routines and activities. Care staff promote positive risk-taking and
 support people  to access  activities,  maintain community  links and develop independence.
 Communication needs, cultural preferences and health needs are well understood and reflected in
 personal plans. Accessible information, advocacy guidance and support in Welsh are available.
 Effective partnership working with health professionals helps ensure health needs are met, with
 care staff understanding how to respond to people’s support needs.


 The service has good safeguarding arrangements. Care staff receive safeguarding training and
 understand their responsibilities. The service identifies and assesses risks before respite stays
 begin and work effectively with health and social care professionals to help keep people safe.
 Information about safeguarding, complaints,  rights, and advocacy  is  available  in accessible
 formats. We found safeguarding concerns are managed appropriately and in line with procedures.
 The service learns very well from incidents and uses  this learning to improve practice, the
 environment and staff training, helping to strengthen outcomes for people.


 People are valued as individuals and receive support which reflects their interests, strengths and
 preferences. Equality, diversity and inclusion are promoted through personalised support, and
 respite experiences focus on maintaining routines and achieving personal goals. Interests and
 hobbies are incorporated into support planning, and people are encouraged to remain socially
 active and engaged with  their communities. Financial arrangements are well managed, with
 accurate records and effective systems in place to safeguard people’s finances and property.


 People stay in an environment which supports their well-being outcomes. The service offers a
 home-from-home experience  during  respite  stays. Bedrooms can be personalised and  the
 environment  is  flexible  in responding  to  individual preferences,  cultural needs and complex
 physical, behavioural and sensory requirements. Appropriate equipment and adaptations support
 independence and mobility, while consultation with people and professionals informs environmental
 improvements. The premises are well maintained, and people benefit from accessible outdoor
 areas and good access to community facilities.

       Care & Support                       Good



Care and support is good because people receive personalised care which meets their needs,
promotes positive outcomes and is delivered by care staff who understand how to support them
safely. People receive care and support based on person-centred plans which focus on their
strengths, preferences and personal outcomes. Families are actively involved in planning support
before and during respite stays, helping to ensure continuity and consistency. Transition and
admission arrangements are a significant strength, being tailored to each person and introduced at
a pace which suits them. Care  files include detailed information from health and social care
professionals,  enabling  care  staff  to meet  people's  needs  safely and  consistently.  Risk
assessments support  positive outcomes while helping people maintain independence.  Daily
records provide meaningful information about people's experiences during their stay, and care staff
have clear guidance about preferences, routines, activities and health needs.


People are protected through safeguarding and risk management arrangements. Leaders maintain
oversight of incidents and use information to review practice and drive improvements. Safeguarding
concerns are managed in line with procedures and referrals are made to local safeguarding teams
when required. Risk management arrangements balance safety with people's rights, choice and
well-being. The service promotes least restrictive practice and uses positive behaviour support
approaches to help people achieve positive outcomes. Families and professionals are involved in
decision-making where appropriate, and any restrictions are documented and understood by care
staff.


People receive their medicines safely because systems are in place for the storage, administration
and monitoring of medication. Medication records are accurate, and medicines are stored securely.
Controlled  drugs  are managed  appropriately.  Appropriate  communication  with  healthcare
professionals supports safe medication management, and medicines are reviewed before respite
stays commence. Clear communication processes ensure care staff are aware of any changes,
while regular audits help identify and address issues promptly.


People benefit from a clean environment which supports their health and well-being. The service
maintains thorough standards of hygiene throughout, and care  staff follow detailed cleaning
schedules. Effective infection prevention and control arrangements reduce the risk of infection and
help keep people safe. Cleaning equipment is used appropriately, and suitable arrangements are in
place for laundry and clinical waste. Care staff have access to sufficient supplies to maintain good
infection  control  practices. Leaders understand outbreak management procedures and have
suitable contingency arrangements. Regular audits provide oversight and help ensure standards
remain consistently high.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is good because people stay in a safe, comfortable and well-maintained setting
which supports their individual needs, independence and enjoyment during respite stays. The
service  is  welcoming, homely and  consistent  with  the  Statement  of  Purpose.  Extensive
redecoration has enhanced the appearance  of the service and creates a home-from-home
experience for people. Accommodation is suitable for people with a wide range of mobility, physical
and behavioural support needs. Specialist equipment, including profiling beds, tracking hoists and
specialist seating, helps people remain comfortable and as independent as possible during their
stay. Creative and personalised signage supports orientation within the service and helps people
feel  familiar and take ownership of  their surroundings. Bedrooms are spacious and can be
personalised to reflect people's preferences and needs. Rooms are equipped with items which
promote comfort and well-being, including sensory resources. Communal areas are comfortable,
well-presented and designed  to encourage  social  interaction and  participation  in everyday
activities. People have opportunities to spend time with others, take part in activities and develop
daily living skills such as meal preparation.


The outdoor environment is extremely positive. People have access to outdoor facilities which
promote physical activity, relaxation and enjoyment. Leaders continue to invest in the outdoor
space and have plans to further enhance facilities, including the development of a summer house
and a ground-level trampoline. These improvements aim to increase opportunities for recreation
and meaningful engagement during respite stays. This shows a clear commitment to continuous
improvement. Alongside the improvements already completed, plans are in place to further develop
other outdoor areas. This ongoing investment helps ensure the environment continues to meet
people's changing needs and supports positive outcomes.


People benefit from an environment which is clean, well lit, comfortable and maintained to a very
good standard. Bathroom facilities are safe, accessible and meet a range of individual needs. The
service  is secure, with health and safety more generally well-managed. Fire safety checks,
maintenance records and health and safety documentation are current and demonstrate effective
oversight of the premises.

       Leadership & Management               Good



Leadership and management is good because leaders maintain effective oversight of the service,
support positive outcomes for people and seek to drive continuous improvement. The manager
maintains oversight of quality, safety and performance. We found strong values-led leadership, with
a clear focus on supporting positive outcomes for people and ongoing improvement of the service.
Monitoring systems, regular audits and reflective practice help leaders identify trends, respond to
issues and improve the quality of support provided. Leaders maintain oversight of incidents,
safeguarding matters and  service performance, and use  this  information  to inform  service
development and practice improvement. We saw evidence lessons are learned from events and
changes are implemented to improve outcomes for people. The Responsible Individual (RI) visits to
the service, and reviews of the quality of care, provide an additional level of oversight and meet
regulatory requirements. Leaders work well with external professionals and demonstrate good
planning and consultation when developing the service. Although the service is operating within a
challenging staffing context and organisational change, the quality of the service has generally
been maintained.


People benefit from support provided by a skilled, well-trained and passionate staff team. Care staff
receive comprehensive  training which  reflects people's  individual needs,  including  specialist
training in areas such as epilepsy and positive behaviour management. Staff registrations with
Social  Care  Wales  are  maintained,  supervision  arrangements  are  appropriate  and  staff
development is actively encouraged. The induction programme prior to new care staff starting is
thorough and helps prepare for their role. Staff files contain the required regulatory information.
Care staff told us,  "It’s lovely, really, really lovely. The manager is really approachable.  If I'm
unsure, I can talk to them, and we find a solution together…absolutely brilliant" and "I find it really
supportive here. I enjoy working with staff and individuals and find staff really supportive." These
comments reflect aspects of the positive culture within the service. We found leaders have a strong
commitment to supporting and developing care staff. We are aware of staffing issues impacting on
the service. We discussed these with the RI and how they can impact both respite bookings and
staff well-being. We are assured the RI, who is new in post, is aware of these challenges and is
taking steps to address them.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
