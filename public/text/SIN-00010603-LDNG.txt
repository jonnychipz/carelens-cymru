       Care & Support                       Good



       Environment                         Good



        Leadership & Management               Good





Summary:

 People who have made Yr Ysgol their home receive a good standard of care. Most people are
 happy with the care and support they get and have formed positive relationships with the staff
 team. Relatives have high levels of confidence in the service.

 Wellbeing is rated as good because the motivated staff team strive to give people opportunities to
 do things that matter to them.

 Care and support is rated as good because the team is led by an experienced manager and deputy
 who are well regarded by the rest of the team. Personal plans help inform and direct care and
 support.

 The environment is rated as good because standards of cleanliness are high and the service is well
 maintained with plenty of outside space as well as several small lounges and dining rooms.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are treated with dignity and respect. Care workers speak fondly about the people they
 support, describing the relationships as “like a big family”. Positive risk taking is shown in some
 practices, with people retaining some independence by spending time away from the service as
 well as preparing their own meals.

 There are opportunities for people to do things that matter to them and that they enjoy. During the
 inspection some people were going out into the local area and others were engaged in activities
 with care workers. Care workers value the time they have with people and say care and support is
 never rushed, but dependent on individual needs. The activities worker and occupational therapy
 staff work closely with the rest of the team. They respect the contribution of all members of the staff
 team and describe colleagues as “just amazing”.

 People are safe and protected from abuse and neglect. This is because there are good security
 measures to prevent unauthorised access to the service. Staff know their responsibilities in relation
 to safeguarding and they have completed the relevant training. People are treated with dignity and
 respect because workers understand they are working in the individual’s home and the trust and
 responsibility placed on them. The recruitment processes are robust and safe meaning people
 appointed have the right skills, knowledge and aptitude for the job. Staff have a period of induction
 before working independently. Relatives have high levels of confidence in the service.

 The environment contributes to people’s well-being. Bedrooms are comfortable and personalised
 and communal areas are as homely as possible. There is plenty of space meaning people can
 spend time either alone or with others as they choose.

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive the care and support they need. Personal plans are reviewed regularly and care
workers find them helpful. They contain a brief personal profile as well as comprehensive support
plans and risk assessments for a range of areas including nutrition, mobility, personal care and
mood. Daily records set out what care and support is offered and activity records show how the
person engaged. The staff team have time to read personal plans and consider they get the
information they need to meet people’s needs. The manager is going to give more attention to
make sure activities and engagement are better recorded and evaluated.

There is an understanding of the importance of good nutrition. Most meals are made using fresh
ingredients and there is little reliance on processed food. The catering team are satisfied with the
quality of ingredients they work with, and they have the equipment they need. Special events are
celebrated and special diets catered for. A Speech and Language therapist is available for advice
and support. People and staff are satisfied with the meals with one person describing the food as
“alright” and a care worker said it’s “absolutely amazing”. Some people, as part of their personal
support plan, prepare some of their own meals with support from staff.

Physical health needs are met because the provider has a physiotherapist and occupational
therapist as part of the team. People attend hospital appointments as needed and a registered
nurse is always on duty. Moving and handling equipment is available, as is pressure relieving
equipment for people who have been assessed as needing this.

People are safe and protected from harm and abuse. Visitors are required to sign into a visitor’s
book meaning staff know who is in the service at all times. All staff spoken with know their
responsibilities to report any safeguarding concerns and there is an up-to-date safeguarding policy
for staff to refer to as well as regular safeguarding training. Referrals are made to the adult
protection team as needed. Whilst care workers have had no reason to raise any safeguarding
concerns, they are confident they know how to and are also confident the manager would deal with
any concerns correctly.

People’s medication is generally safely managed. A check of controlled drugs showed there are the
correct number of tablets available and accurate records are maintained. Regular checks are made
of medication records and any gaps or omissions are identified. The provider takes action to
address any medication issues to ensure people’s safety but greater rigor is needed to make sure
this is done in a timely manner as we saw some omissions had been identified but not acted upon.

The risk of infection is minimised because of good hygiene practices. Housekeeping staff are
satisfied with the quality of products they use and understand their important role in people’s overall
care and support and feel part of the team. Standards of cleanliness throughout the service are
good.

       Environment                         Good




People live in an environment that meets their needs. The service retains the original stonework outside and
inside has been updated to a good standard. Hallways are free of any clutter or trip hazards and communal
spaces are homely. There are several lounges and dining areas where people can spend time with others.
Bedrooms are personalised to the individual’s choice of décor with items of furniture, photographs and soft
furnishings.
There are additional bathrooms, some of which contain assisted baths for people whose mobility is reduced.

Standards of cleanliness throughout are good and the housekeeping team are satisfied with the quality of
products they have. They understand their important role in people’s overall care and value feeling part of
the team.

There is a large garden which is divided up into different areas. A polytunnel is used to grow vegetables and
there are other vegetables being grown in the beds. There are seating areas as well as a small astroturf
area which is used for football and other physical activities.

The kitchen has been awarded the maximum score of five, which means standards of cleanliness and
hygiene were assessed as being very good.

There are some effective security measures with visitors being required to sign in using a visitors’ book, so
staff know who is in the service at all times. The gardens are secure and safe from any unauthorised
access.

There are effective processes in place to make sure fire safety checks are carried out and
equipment and services are appropriately maintained.

       Leadership & Management               Good



There are some effective and robust governance arrangements in place to monitor quality. The
responsible individual (RI) visits the service regularly and is accessible at other times. They have
good oversight of the service and is supported by an experienced and well-regarded manager. The
quality assurance reports written are detailed, reflective and focus on quality and service
improvements.

Some quality audits are completed, and these show a generally high level of compliance as well as
action points to monitor improvements.

Staff are appointed following a safe recruitment process. Information is stored electronically and
there are the required references and security checks. All staff have a period of induction before
they work independently. There is some use of agency workers but the manager tries to make sure
they are regular staff who know the service. Care workers are mostly positive about the training
offered and records show that whilst this is largely up to date, there are some gaps. They can ask
for additional training if they feel this is needed. Care workers are appropriately supervised. They
consider they get balanced feedback on their work to help them with their professional
development and find the manager, deputy and the nurses to be receptive to any ideas or concerns
they have.

The staff work well together and describe having a very strong team. Care workers are appointed
with a range of skills and experiences and these help enhance people’s quality of care and support.
We saw some friendly and supportive interactions between members of the team which shows a
level of trust and respect.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
