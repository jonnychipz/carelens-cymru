Summary:

 Pen-Y-Graig offers a family home for people with physical and mental disabilities. They are cared
 for by a family and people feel they are part of the family. People have access to transport and
 local amenities.

 Well-being for people is good. They attend day services if they choose, the people attending enjoy
 these services. People are supported to be as independent as possible. People live a full life,
 helping with the animals on the small holding and attending local events.

 Care and support are good. People access health care in a timely way as the family know them
 well and can anticipate their needs. People receive their medications as prescribed. There has
 been a bereavement in the family, people received support in a sensitive way and were able to
 attend the funeral.

 The environment is good. The home is a busy family home and small holding. People assist with
 chores. The home is subject to ongoing maintenance; grants have been received to upgrade

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good





 People have control over their day-to-day life. Care is planned with each individual and they set
 personal objectives. Care is person centred; people have a voice and can choose their daily
 activities. Preferred daily and nightly routines are recorded, some people prefer to go to bed early
 and rise early whilst others prefer to retire a little later. The family can communicate with each
 person on their individual level and support them to access the information needed for their care.
 People are part of the family and are treated with dignity and respect. People’s first language
 preferences are recorded. Members of the family can understand Welsh and could give an Active
 Offer of Welsh if required. People are supported to remain in contact with their biological families,
 and we saw visits and family events recorded in their plans. People can access an advocate if
 needed, to ensure their rights.


 People are supported to maintain their mental health and emotional well-being. People are active
 around their home and in day care services. People can do what makes them happy, they have
 collections and hobbies, they enjoy tending to the animals and honeybees and growing vegetables.
 People can access health care advice in a timely way as the family know them well and can
 anticipate their needs.


 People feel safe in their home. They have risk assessments in their plans to mitigate common
 health risks and to maintain their health and safety. The family have safeguarding training and are
 aware of who to contact locally should there be any incidents or issues with people’s care. Policies
 are in place to guide the family towards safe care.


 People can do what matters to them. People attending day services consider it their regular work
 and enjoy gardening and sorting through textiles. People are supported to be independent and help
 with chores around the home and small holding. People remain a part of the local community and
 attend events such as The Anglesey Agricultural Show.


 People are happy in their home. They showed us their bedrooms with pride. They keep their rooms
 tidy and have places to keep hobby materials and things of importance to them. They have
 televisions and photos of people who are important to them. People choose the décor for their
 bedrooms.

       Care & Support                       Good





People are consulted about their care. People are included in their personal plans and can choose
care objectives. Daily notes are kept measuring progress and achievement of objectives. People’s
preferred routines are recorded, health needs, aid required with activities of daily living and
methods to comfort and de-escalate anxiety behaviours. People access medical advice, dental
care and eye tests. People are assisted to attend health appointments with support providing
comfort and reassurance. Health appointment outcomes are carefully recorded in plans. The plans
are person centred and contain a good pen picture of the person, their history, and people of
importance to them.

People are supported to be as independent as possible. They can wash and dress themselves with
prompting in most cases and are enabled to maintain their hygiene and mouthcare. People assist
with laundry and house-hold tasks where possible.

People are supported individually to meet their needs. The family know them well and can make
rapid assessments of their needs and know if they are unwell. Each person communicates
differently and the family have a good grasp of their communication needs; conversations flow
naturally at home.

People are safe in their home. The family work closely with local authorities and are open in their
dealings. Family members are working towards Social Care Wales (the governing body),
qualifications to give measurable care of a high standard.

People receive their prescribed medications. The family ensure prescriptions are ordered in time to
ensure good stocks. Medications are kept safely. People are prompted to take their medications as
directed by the prescriber to ensure therapeutic effect.

People receive good nutrition and hydration. They shop with the family and can choose foods they
like. The family encourages people to have a healthy diet with an occasional treat. People enjoy
growing and eating their own produce and enjoy potting honey from the bee hives.

People receive sensitive care. The family had a recent bereavement which was unexpected.
People were encouraged to talk about it and ask questions. They were able to attend the funeral,
extra staff attended to support people and assist them to a quieter area if they became
overwhelmed. People said they had been sad but were coping well.

       Environment                         Good





People live in a home which supports their personal outcomes. Their home is situated in a rural,
quiet area which people like. People can access transport, some have mobility vehicles, and can
go out as they wish. People are assisted to maintain their vehicles and meet roadworthy legal
requirements. There is parking to the front of the home. People can access the local amenities and
shops and are happy living in the location.
People access equipment needed for their care such as hoists and electric wheelchairs. These are
maintained and kept in good condition and are serviced as required. The family have good relations
with community health providers and access assessment and equipment for people as needed.

People are supported with positive risk management. People are encouraged to be independent
but safe. Those tending to the honeybees wear protective suits. People in wheelchairs can access
ramps to move safely around their home. Environmental risk assessments are in place to mitigate
common risks.

Fire checks are maintained. There are escape routes and wider opening windows on the ground
floor for people to leave in the event of a fire. People have personalised Personal Emergency
Evacuation Plans (PEEPS) for emergency services to aid them effectively in the event of a fire or
emergency. These are kept by the main entrance to the property so that emergency services can
obtain them easily.

Utilities such as water and electricity are checked by qualified persons. The boiler is regularly
serviced for safety. People can access bathrooms and showers. The home is kept warm and
comfortable in winter. There is a patio area to the rear of the conservatory that people use in
summer and enjoy having BBQs. There is sufficient seating outside and umbrellas for shade.

The house is a busy family home. Family and people work together to keep it clean and tidy.
Animals are well cared for, and people assist in their care. Several pets live in the home which
people enjoy. Grants have been applied for, and in some cases received, to upgrade people’s
rooms and facilities. There are plans to further upgrade bedrooms and facilities and to maintain the
patio area. Maintenance plans are being considered to address areas of the home which are a little
tired and in need of improvement.

       Leadership & Management               Good





The family have good oversight of the care given to people. People can access information about
the service they receive in a format which is appropriate for them and reviewed to ensure it remains
relevant. The Responsible Individual (RI) lives in the home and cares for people. The RI supports
the manager and family offering advice and supervision. Reports regarding the quality-of-care
people receive are kept in handwritten form The RI knows people well and is aware of any current
health or well-being issues that need to be addressed. The RI supports the manager and gives
advice and direction. The manager can access training needed for their role and accesses support
from local authorities as required.


All members of the family involved in people’s care have enhanced DBS checks in place. They
receive informal and formal supervision to ensure their well-being and to enable good care
practices. We observed people receiving effective, friendly care and being treated as part of the
family with dignity and respect. Family members working as care support workers said they love
looking after people and feel they have good care. They can access training to help them in their
roles to ensure people are cared for by knowledgeable individuals who are updated in their
practice.


The family ensure the home is financed and people receive fresh foods and snacks. Grants are
obtained to maintain the home to provide people with dignity in their environment. Some people
receive funded care, and the service works well with the local authorities who are pleased with the
care provided.


Policies are in place and accessible to those providing care. They give support and direction for
them in their work and inform them of local protocols and contact numbers should people have
concerns about their care. People are supported to raise any concerns and worries and are
listened to, the family are proactive in addressing any issues to ensure a continually improving
service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
