Summary:

 59 Beechley Road is a residential home with individual apartments for up to seven people with
 complex needs. It is located close to the town of Wrexham with transport links to access the local
 and wider community.

 Wellbeing is excellent because people are empowered to have control over their lives. They are
 supported extensively to understand, communicate and make choices. People have made
 significant improvements in their physical, mental and emotional wellbeing. They achieve their
 outcomes which are celebrated. People benefit from positive and trusting relationships with others
 and are proactively protected from harm.

 Care and support is excellent because people experience high-quality care and support from staff.
 Comprehensive person-centred plans are tailored to their individual needs. People, relatives,
 independent advocates, staff and relevant professionals are involved in plans, reviews and their

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People have strong control over their lives. Tailored information empowers them to communicate,
 make choices and participate fully in daily life. People’s communication needs are assessed and
 person-centred communication tools and aids are used and there is access to a speech and
 language therapist. Social stories help people with transitions, changes in routines, expectations
 and new experiences. The service actively promotes the Active Offer of Welsh with bilingual
 information, documentation and signage available where appropriate. The manager told us,
 “Communication is viewed as everyone's responsibility and sits at the heart of our care and support
 model”.

 People live healthier lives with significant improvements in their physical, mental and emotional
 wellbeing. The manager shared examples of work around desensitisation and social stories to
 assist and prepare people for upcoming health appointments. Consultations and visits take place
 with doctors and opticians carry out home visits. The use of ‘My dental passport’ promotes good
 oral health with access to dentists where required. Monthly reviews show examples of healthier
 diets, reductions in weight, increased mobility and positive behaviours and discussions around any
 health concerns. People build on their strengths and develop their skills and independence. Staff
 said, “Residents well-being, dignity and individual needs are always prioritised, staff go above and
 beyond to ensure they receive the best care possible”. Relatives told us, “My son has settled in well
 and always seems happy” and “The management and staff are always friendly and happy to help”.

 People benefit from positive and trusting relationships. They maintain existing friendships, families
 come to visit, people enjoy overnight stays with them and regular contact is maintained. Staff said,
 “We go above and beyond to meet individual needs, and it really feels like a caring family
 environment”. Professionals told us, “During my time visiting I noticed that staff were attentive,
 friendly and really cared for the residents at Beechley Road”.

 People are consistently achieving outcomes and these are celebrated. A ‘wall of fame’ shows
 photographs of people’s achievements. They enjoy venturing out to new places, using public
 transport, and we saw scrap books capturing adventures. Staff also try new activities for the first
 time alongside people, sharing in their enjoyment and accomplishments. Professional’s commented
 “My experience of working with the staff team has been positive. They are professional, thoughtful,
 and focused on achieving positive outcomes for the individuals they support, particularly those with
 complex needs”.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



Highly detailed and extensive person-centred plans ensure people’s complex and specific needs
are consistently met. Transition planning is comprehensive, using specific assessment tools to
gather important information with input from family members and professionals. Monthly resident’s
updates are also shared with families and professionals. Person-centred reviews are interactive,
involve and include people where possible with their families, independent advocates and others
involved in their care and support. Staff said, “The fact that the management involve support staff in
reviews, meetings and future plans for the residents work really well as they notice that as support
staff, their opinion is highly valued”. Relatives commented “The communication from the staff is
amazing, I get updated regularly on what my relative has been up to and get monthly reports. They
also inform me of how he has got on at appointments”.

People receive high-quality care and support from a service tailored around their every need. Plans
and other documentation are strengths based and clearly record how staff can best support them.
The manager shared examples of highly positive outcomes for people following reductions in
medication resulting in enhanced quality of life. Another example given was when staff spent time
with a person during their stay in hospital to help them feel settled and safe. The manager and staff
are supported by a clinical skills team and multidisciplinary meetings are held in people’s best
interests. Professionals commented “Their approach is person-centred, and they work
collaboratively with multidisciplinary professionals, ensuring that best interest decisions are
followed appropriately and in line with agreed procedures”. Staff told us, “The management and
staff are consistently pushing for high care standards”. Relatives said “Beechley has made such a
positive difference to my son’s life, he is able to access the community safely every day”. They also
commented “The staff and management always go that extra mile to ensure my son is looked after
and cared for well”.

Robust systems protect people from harm. Staff receive specific training to respond in a positive
and proactive manner using a consistent approach. The manager is passionate about positive
behaviour support and is very proud of the plans in place. Staff said, “The use of Positive
Behaviour Support (PBS) helps staff understand behaviours and respond appropriately rather than
simply reacting to them”. The manager delivers workshops to help family members understand and
use the same strategies at home. This gives them a greater understanding and confidence and
promotes a highly consistent approach to supporting their relative.

The service promotes good hygiene and effectively manages infection risks through clear policies,
regular audits and staff training. Infection prevention and control champions support good practice,
while the manager regularly attends forums to maintain their own knowledge and understanding.
Staff have access to personal protective equipment (PPE). A staff member involved in audits
identified a need to raise staff awareness of the infection control grab bag and delivered a
presentation at a team meeting.

       Environment                         Good



People live in accommodation which meets their individual needs. There are separate apartments
and each has a lounge, bedroom and en-suite. Apartments are personalised to varying degrees in
line with peoples’ preferences and reflect any specialist interests and items of importance. Shared
communal areas are available for activities and socialising including lounges, kitchen and laundry.
There is a small quieter lounge for families and visitors to use. The dining room has a smaller
kitchen area created within it. The manager told us people chose the dining room layout
themselves. The laundry room has pictorial strips and visuals to assist people to be more
independent in tending to their own clothing. An activity room is filled with lots of different items
which interest people. Emphasis is placed on creating a low arousal environment to promote calm
with the use of visual information to aid orientation. Adjustments are made to accommodate
peoples changing needs, for example reduced mobility.

The manager acknowledges there is a small, enclosed garden space. They told us about initial
assessments which consider if this is the right environment for each person. The manager makes
flexible use of the limited garden space, adapting it to people’s preferences. For example, a
trampoline and basketball hoop has been added to support people’s chosen activities. A person is
taking an active role in personalising and maintaining their own part of a small courtyard area by
choosing and buying items to enhance the space. The manager ensures people have access to
alternative outdoor space. This includes the wider community, local amenities, parks, leisure
facilities, countryside walks, social opportunities and activities in line with people’s personal
preferences and goals.

The environment is kept safe, secure and well maintained with continuous monitoring and
maintenance work. The RI visits to the service include looking at the premises and addressing any
emerging issues. Plans are in place to monitor work completed and due to be done. There is
careful consideration and consultation around any planned works to ensure people experience the
least disruption possible. Health and safety and fire audits and checks are carried out to identify
and improve any areas. Personal emergency evacuation plans are in place to support people to
leave their home safely. They record any extra support required as well as items to offer to provide
comfort and shelter to people. Grab bags to use in emergency situations contain visual images of
safe locations to support people’s communication needs as well as umbrellas and foil blankets. A
piece of work is being completed to support people who have difficulties with the sound of the fire
alarm going off. Staff are issued with an employee safety handbook. Staff commented “The service
has done very well in providing a Conducive Environment for both residents and members of staff”.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



People benefit from a highly motivated and dedicated staff team who work well together. The
manager told us a values-based recruitment and selection process is used. Recruitment checks
include disclosure and barring service (DBS) checks and staff are registered with Social Care
Wales (the workforce regulator).

Staff feel very well supported, valued and respected. Changes have been made to the way
supervisions are carried out to encourage staff to talk more. Team meetings take place away from
the home and are highly creative. These include fun, practical activities with competitions,
challenges, prizes and presentations by staff. Staff commented “The company is really doing well
for staff. Listening to our concerns and letting our voices be heard”. Staff wellbeing is supported
with access to an employee assistance programme, with incentives and other benefits available to
them. There is a monthly recognition scheme for staff going above and beyond in their role or
demonstrating excellent practice and they are rewarded with a voucher of their choice.

Staff have opportunities for learning, development and promotion. They complete training relevant
to their roles and responsibilities and the people they support. Staff said, “The constant training
given to staff members plays a major role in making sure staff know how to take good care of the
residents”. Staff commented “There is career progression and there is no discrimination”. Additional
support is provided to team leaders. Staff said, “The people who lead on every shift are all
exceptional leaders”. Professionals commented “I was very impressed; the staff were outstanding”.

Governance arrangements ensure a high-quality service is provided for all. The RI is very
complimentary about the manager and is proud of the service. The manager feels very much
supported, listened to and heard and has been sponsored to complete a degree to further their
studies. Staff told us the manager is “exceptional”, “The management team at 59 Beechley Road is
nothing short of excellent” and “The way the home is run is excellent”. Extensive and robust
systems include monthly quality assurance and improvement reports, RI three-monthly visits and
six-monthly quality of care reviews. The manager provided examples of shared practice and
lessons learnt to further improve the service.

A positive and compassionate culture is strongly promoted. The organisations values are clear and
revisited with the staff team. Staff said, “There is a positive and supportive culture throughout the
service, making it a lovely place to work”. Staff also commented on an “amazing experience” and
an “exceptional service”. Staff told us “The management team are fantastic, approachable and
always willing to listen and provide support. The directors are equally committed and accessible
with a willingness to help whenever needed”.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
