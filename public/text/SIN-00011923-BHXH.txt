Summary:

 People experience excellent outcomes and are supported to engage in recreational activities of
 their choice, while having access to community health and social care services. Care staff have a
 strong understanding of people’s individual needs and are provided with clear guidance and
 strategies to provide personalised care and support. Robust pre-admission assessments ensure
 admissions are suitable and consider compatibility between individuals, with the information used
 effectively to develop provider assessments, personal plans, and risk assessments. Significant
 improvements have been made since the last inspection, demonstrating the service provider’s
 commitment to continuous improvement.

 The service provides an excellent environment that is clean, spacious, well-maintained, and
 personalised to reflect people’s choices. Effective maintenance arrangements and regular health
 and safety checks help ensure people live in a safe and comfortable home.

 Leadership and management arrangements are good. Safe recruitment practices are in place,
 newly appointed care staff receive a structured induction, and care staff speak positively about the
 support and guidance provided by the manager. Care staff benefit from regular supervision and
 access to relevant training opportunities. The Responsible Individual (RI) maintains good oversight

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being outcomes because they are listened to, valued and
 supported to have meaningful control over their day-to-day lives. A well-established keyworker
 system and personalised communication approaches, including verbal, unaided and aided
 communication methods, help ensure people can express their views, wishes and preferences. We
 observed consistently warm, respectful, and compassionate interactions between care staff and
 people. Care staff actively promote choice, independence, and inclusion, supporting people to
 make informed decisions about their routines, activities, and future aspirations. People can access
 advice, support and advocacy when making decisions or raising concerns, ensuring their rights and
 voices are upheld.

 People benefit from strong emotional, physical, and social well-being support. They have timely
 access to health and social care services and receive prompt medical attention when required.
 Care staff understand people's individual needs and ensure their physical health, emotional well-
 being, emotional health, and behavioural support needs are met through person-centred
 approaches. Feedback by people and their representatives and observations of interactions
 demonstrated the positive impact of support provided. As a result, people make progress in
 developing confidence, independence, and life skills.

 There is a strong emphasis on helping people achieve positive outcomes and live fulfilling lives.
 People are encouraged to pursue their interests, participate in recreational and community
 activities, develop healthy lifestyles, and increase their independence. Opportunities to attend
 college, work placements and contribute to small daily household tasks support people build
 confidence, gain new experiences, and maximise their potential.

 People's safety and well-being are further promoted through robust safeguarding arrangements.
 The service provider has appropriate safeguarding policies and procedures, for example, they
 undertake enhanced recruitment checks and ensure care staff receive safeguarding training. Care
 staff demonstrate a good understanding of safeguarding and whistleblowing procedures and are
 confident people are safe and protected from harm.

 People live in an excellent environment that enhances their well-being, comfort, and independence.
 The home is clean, spacious, well maintained and personalised to reflect individual preferences
 and interests. People have access to equipment, resources and facilities that support the
 development of everyday living and independent skills. Effective health and safety arrangements
 and timely health and safety maintenance ensure people enjoy a safe, comfortable, and homely
 environment.

       Care & Support                           Excellent




People receive highly personalised care and support that is centred on their individual needs,
preferences, and outcomes. Comprehensive provider assessments, personal plans, and risk
assessments are informed by detailed referral information and robust pre-admission processes,
ensuring placements are suitable and compatible with others living at the service. Significant
improvements have been made to the provider assessment process, strengthening the service's
ability to plan and provide effective, person-centred care. Personal plans provide clear guidance for
care staff and focus on helping people achieve their goals, maximise their independence and
experience positive outcomes.

People are supported to express their views and make choices about their care and daily lives.
Care staff understand the communication needs of individuals and use a range of person-centred
approaches, including Picture Exchange Communication System (PECS), Makaton, and emotion
cards, to ensure people communicate their wishes and preferences. These approaches help
people to participate in decisions that affect them and enable care staff to provide responsive and
consistent support. Independent advocacy services are available when required, further promoting
people's rights and ensuring their voices are heard.

People benefit from timely access to healthcare services and receive appropriate support to
promote their physical, emotional, and emotional well-being. Care staff have a good understanding
of people's health and behavioural support needs and work closely with health professionals to
help people achieve positive outcomes. Improvements made to medication management systems
since the last inspection demonstrate the service provider's commitment to continuous
improvement and safer care practices.

Robust safeguarding arrangements help ensure people receive safe care and support. Care staff
complete safeguarding training, understand their responsibilities and are confident in recognising
and reporting concerns. Appropriate referrals are made when required, risk assessments are
regularly reviewed, and Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) notifications are submitted to
Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) appropriately. Significant improvements have also been made in
the timely submission of notifiable events to CIW, demonstrating strengthened governance and
oversight.

The service promotes people's health and well-being through effective infection prevention and
control measures. Care staff have access to relevant policies, training, personal protective
equipment and cleaning resources, helping to maintain a safe and hygienic environment for people
living at the home.

       Environment                              Excellent



People live in an exceptional environment that is thoughtfully designed to promote their well-being,
independence, and quality of life. The home is located within easy access of local shops,
recreational facilities, and healthcare services, enabling people to remain active members of their
community. The home is clean, spacious, comfortable, and well maintained. Communal areas
provide welcoming spaces where people can socialise, enjoy activities together or spend time
relaxing in quieter surroundings. The kitchen and utility facilities encourage people to develop and
maintain valuable independent living skills, whilst the home's food hygiene rating 4 provides
additional assurance regarding standards of cleanliness and food safety.

People's bedrooms are personalised to reflect their individual interests, preferences, and needs,
helping them feel secure, valued and at home. Bedrooms are suitably furnished and contain
personal possessions that are important to people, supporting a sense of belonging and emotional
well-being.

Significant investment has been made in the outdoor environment, creating an innovative and
engaging space that offers a wide range of recreational, sensory, and social opportunities. The
front and rear gardens are attractive, well maintained and make excellent use of the available
space. The rear garden has developed into a vibrant extension of the home, featuring themed
areas designed around the interests and preferences of people. Enhancements include a sensory
water wall, bubble machine and relaxing sensory spaces that provide opportunities for stimulation,
relaxation, and emotional well-being.

The outdoor areas actively promote social inclusion and meaningful engagement. A bar area,
regular themed evenings and celebrations of events provide opportunities for people to enjoy
shared experiences with family and care staff. Families are encouraged to visit informally and
without appointments, enabling them to participate in activities and maintain important relationships
in a natural and welcoming environment.

The environment reflects the service provider's commitment to continuously improving people's
lived experiences. Care staff describe the service provider as highly responsive to suggestions and
supportive of investment in both the indoor and outdoor environment. Health and safety
arrangements are robust, with regular checks, prompt maintenance, and effective oversight of
electrical and fire safety systems. This ensures people benefit from an environment that is not only
safe and well maintained but also enriching, stimulating and genuinely tailored to support positive
outcomes and an excellent quality of life.

       Leadership & Management               Good



The service provider has established governance arrangements that support the effective operation
of the home. Since the last inspection, the service provider has addressed matters arising from an
action plan implemented through the escalating concerns process, demonstrating a commitment to
improvement. A new Responsible Individual is now in post and has developed a positive and
constructive working relationship with the manager. Regular communication takes place between
the RI and manager to discuss operational matters, areas for development and future objectives.
Improvements in governance systems have strengthened oversight and accountability, resulting in
more robust auditing, monitoring, and reporting processes.

Appropriate arrangements monitor the quality of care provided. The RI undertakes regular visits to
the service and completes three-monthly monitoring reports alongside six-monthly quality of care
reviews. The quality assurance processes provide oversight of the service and support ongoing
improvements. Recent developments have strengthened the effectiveness of auditing and
monitoring arrangements, providing greater assurance the service is identifying and responding to
issues in a timely manner.

There are enough suitably qualified and experienced care staff to meet people's needs. Care
staffing arrangements are stable, with the service operating close to full capacity and not having to
rely on agency care staff. Care rotas demonstrate people receive support in line with their
assessed needs and commissioning arrangements. Safe recruitment practices are followed,
including enhanced checks and verification of employment references. Newly appointed care staff
complete a structured induction programme, including the All-Wales Induction Framework for
Health and Social Care where required.

Care staff are supported to develop their skills and knowledge through regular supervision and
access to a broad range of training opportunities. Records evidence supervision is generally
completed within required timescales and care staff confirmed they receive appropriate guidance
and support from management. Care staff spoke positively about the manager, describing them as
‘very good’ and ‘very supportive,’ which contributes to competent care staff morale and a positive
working culture. Online and face-to-face training opportunities ensure care staff are equipped to
meet the complex and individual needs of people living at the service.

Leadership and management arrangements are effective and provide a stable foundation for the
service. Improvements since the last inspection, particularly in governance, auditing and RI
oversight, demonstrate a clear commitment to continuous improvement. Care staff feel valued and
supported, and there is a strong emphasis on maintaining a skilled workforce. Whilst further
opportunities remain to evidence how quality assurance processes consistently drive service
development and improvement; leaders have demonstrated good oversight and are promoting

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
