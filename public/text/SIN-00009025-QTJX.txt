Summary:


 Celynbrook Care Home is a small service situated in the Vale of Glamorgan, with local amenities
 and attractions within easy reach. People are content, settled and receive a high standard of care
 and support from dedicated and compassionate care staff. Well-being is excellent because care
 staff know them extremely well, the provider prioritises well-being, affording people to live
 meaningful and valued lives. Care and support is excellent. People access medical professionals
 and receive effective support to maintain their physical and mental health. The service is highly
 responsive when there are changes in people’s health with admirable outcomes for people with
 complex support needs. The environment is good. People live in a nurturing atmosphere where all
 care staff and management present a united approach to delivering the best outcomes for people.

 Leadership and management is excellent because the organisational structure is stable, reliable
 and highly effective in delivering a high-quality service. Robust governance procedures provide
 highly effective oversight of the service which supports continuous improvement, staff development
 and excellent outcomes for people. Service leaders are held in high regard by stakeholders, and
 they work well with visiting professionals and people’s representatives to support the very best
 outcomes for people using the service.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent





 All people are treated with dignity and respect and achieve excellent well-being outcomes.
 Whenever possible, people play an active part towards their daily routines. Meaning the support
 they receive ensures they maintain independence as much as possible during day-to-day tasks and
 decision making. We saw care staff interacting with people in ways which enables their
 participation, whilst acknowledging their individuality. There is an outstanding and clear
 commitment from the provider and all care staff to ensure people are listened to. We saw excellent
 examples where the service strongly advocates on behalf of people to ensure they achieve the best
 possible health outcomes. The service meets people’s communication needs and care staff are
 kind, attentive and professional. Care staff told us how strongly they feel about people, and how the
 service “Feels like family.” The service does not currently provide the Welsh active offer of
 delivering a service in Welsh. But people enjoy celebrating cultural events together, which the
 service arranges. People enjoy multiple opportunities to socialise and celebrate key events
 together.



 The service offers people choice in their daily activities, which follows their personal plans, hobbies,
 interests and aspirations. People take part in social events they enjoy depending on personal
 choice. The provider arranges a weekly ‘Friendly Club’ social group for people to get together with
 their peers. People new to the service are experiencing positive outcomes and are welcome to take
 part in the club. They are growing in confidence, exploring new opportunities and developing new
 connections because of the aspirational approach of the service. We saw people engaging in
 meaningful activities during inspection which match their hobbies, interests and preferred routines.
 For people who have lived at Celynbrook for a long time, the service has worked exceptionally well
 with them to look at new hobbies and interests, which has made a remarkable impact on their
 mental well-being.



 People receive highly effective support to cultivate safe and healthy relationships. People have
 frequent opportunities to meet with those important to them, and care staff hold exceptional
 knowledge about people’s personal networks. Representatives are highly complementary about
 how inclusive and thoughtful the service is; therefore, they feel a valued part of people’s lives. One
 representative told us they have peace of mind because of the “Superior quality of care,” and
 people are “Nourished” by the service. Where people require additional safeguards in place,
 appropriate authorisations are applied for and embedded in everyday practice at the service.

       Care & Support                           Excellent





People experience excellent care and support outcomes because they receive highly
individualised, responsive and consistently well-coordinated care. The care staff team have an
extensive understanding of people living at Celynbrook, who are treated with the utmost respect.
The service thoroughly assesses people before they receive a service to ensure their needs can be
met. We found comprehensive personal planning and risk management arrangements which the
service reviews regularly. The service involves people and their representatives in their reviews,
which are highly detailed, person centred and outcome focused. They fully inform care staff of
people’s needs and preferences on how they want to be cared for and what is important to them.
We observed care staff to be highly knowledgeable about people and interactions were warm,
compassionate and care staff were genuinely kind.


The service maintains detailed healthcare records, comprehensive professional contact logs, and
appointment outcome records. This demonstrates people receive timely, appropriate support.
Records strongly evidence prompt action following concerns about a person’s physical or mental
well-being. Prompt referrals are made to health professionals, and the service works well with
people’s care team such as dietetics, occupational therapy, and clinical specialists, resulting in
improved outcomes for people’s physical and mental well-being. The service responds quickly and
effectively when people’s needs change. Care staff are vigilant and management maintain
excellent oversight of people’s daily care and support outcomes.


People’s medication is safely managed and administered by trained and competent care staff. Daily
medication records are complete. People access other medical professionals to review medication.
We observed people to look well because they receive exceptional daily support to maintain their
hygiene and personal grooming standards. People access home cooked meals with fresh produce,
and they have easy access to drinks and snacks. The service meets the needs of people with
additional support to maintain a good nutritional in-take in-line with clinical advice. Care staff
receive specialist training to provide enhanced support to those with complex dietary needs.


People are protected as much as possible from the risk of infection because the premises and
equipment are kept clean and hygienic. Care staff complete relevant training and understand their
responsibilities; we observed infection control practice to be good throughout the home.

       Environment                         Good





People experience positive outcomes because they live in a safe, clean, comfortable and highly
personalised environment where health and safety arrangements and environmental oversight
supports their well-being. The provider has established robust systems to minimise infection risks,
maintain the premises and equipment, and monitor safety, enabling people to achieve their
outcomes in a homely and welcoming environment.


The home was observed to be clean and well maintained throughout. We found a structured and
consistent approach to maintaining environmental standards include comprehensive cleaning
schedules, infection prevention monitoring, food safety checks, temperature monitoring and
healthcare-related infection control. Care staff receive appropriate health and safety, and food
hygiene training, with excellent compliance levels. Sufficient Personal Protective Equipment and
cleaning supplies were readily available. The responsible individual (RI) and management audits
provide effective environmental oversight, ensuring standards are maintained and improvements
identified where necessary.


People live in accommodation which promotes dignity, independence and quality of life. Bedrooms
are highly personalised and reflect individual interests, preferences and identities. Communal areas
are comfortable, welcoming and well used, while recent investment in the kitchen, medication
room, furnishings and garden areas demonstrates the provider's commitment to improving the
environment. People have access to suitable indoor and outdoor spaces, they can choose where
they spend their time, and benefit from a relaxed atmosphere where family and visitors are
welcomed. There is a large rear garden which is an accessible patio where people socialise. For
some people who enjoy spending time outdoors, this is enhancing their mental well-being. We
observed them in the garden with care staff enjoying the space in the pleasant weather


We found comprehensive records relating to maintenance and servicing of utilities and facilities. All
were completed and maintained. Weekly audits are mostly effective, but records relating to the fire
door checks require further detailing to ensure their accuracy. The RI highlighted fire door safety
during one of their visits and action is being taken to ensure the service operates in-line with fire
safety legislation. People's records, medication and hazardous substances were securely stored,
and security arrangements balanced safety with people's rights and privacy.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent





The organisational structure is clear. Leadership and management arrangements are highly
effective. People, care staff and visitors have high levels of confidence in the service because there
is a strong positive culture which is supportive, inclusive and respectful. The RI and the manager
are extremely knowledgeable about the needs and preferences of people. This means care staff
receive successful guidance and support which positively impacts on people’s well-being. Auditing
systems work well to monitor the quality of the service. The RI completes formal visits to the
service to speak with people and care staff. These visits go beyond compliance monitoring and
focus on evaluating the impact of care on people's lives. Management maintain robust oversight of
people's health, welfare and care outcomes. The RI completes a quality-of-care review which
informs the provider the service is working effectively to meet people’s needs. Quality assurance
activities work well in identifying areas the service wishes to develop. The service is extremely
responsive to people’s developing health needs, because systems, procedures and management
are successful, meaning early interventions are highly effective.


The provider actively seeks feedback from people using the service, relatives, professionals and
care staff. Quality reviews include analysis of responses and actions taken as a result. Feedback
consistently indicates high levels of satisfaction with the quality of care, leadership and staff team.
There is clear evidence the provider values stakeholder views and uses them to inform service
development. The provider monitors concerns, complaints, compliments and safeguarding matters.
We found improved records relating to accidents and incidents since the last inspection where the
service has implemented changes and learnt from feedback.


People achieve excellent outcomes because they are supported by care staff who are well trained,
effectively supervised and highly valued by leaders. Timely supervision provides care staff the
opportunity to have reflective discussions around practice, well-being, performance, professional
development and training needs. Care staff hold relevant qualifications in health and social care.
Disclosure and barring certificates are in date, and eligible care staff are registered with Social
Care Wales, the workforce regulator. Care staff receive support to access external training
opportunities, and management demonstrate a positive approach to workforce well-being and
retention. Care staff told us, “Management are friendly and approachable.” Regular meetings take
place to keep care staff up to date with service development and audit findings. Expectations are
reinforced and service improvements discussed openly with care staff. This supports a culture of
accountability and continuous improvement.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
