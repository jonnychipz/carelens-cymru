Summary:
 Since the last inspection, the service has appointed a new Responsible Individual (RI) and
 undergone a change in directorship. The new leadership brings experience, investment, and a
 strong commitment to improving outcomes, while the registered manager has remained in post,
 providing continuity and stability for people, relatives, and the staff team at Lakeside.
 People experience positive well-being outcomes because they are supported to make choices,
 maintain relationships, stay safe and take part in meaningful activities. People feel valued, listened
 to and supported by kind staff and approachable leaders.
 Care and Support is good because people receive personalised support from familiar staff who
 know their needs, preferences and routines well. Effective healthcare involvement, safe medicines
 management and positive mealtime experiences support people’s health and well-being. Stronger
 oversight of personal care planning is needed to ensure all aspects of care are consistently
 delivered, recorded and reviewed.
 The environment is good because people live in a comfortable, clean and well-maintained home
 which promotes dignity, independence and quality of life. Ongoing investment and planned
 improvements will further enhance people’s experiences.
 Leadership and Management is effective because people benefit from visible, responsive
 leadership and a stable, well-supported workforce. Governance arrangements support consistent,
 person-centred care, and leaders continue to strengthen quality assurance and service
 development to improve outcomes.

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People are supported to have choice and control over their daily lives and are actively involved in
 decisions about their care and support. They are encouraged to express their views, wishes and
 preferences and contribute to reviews of their care. People choose how they spend their time,
 including opportunities to socialise with others or enjoy the privacy of their own rooms. Relatives
 spoke positively about their involvement and told us they are kept informed about their family
 member's care and support. One relative commented, “Communication is excellent.” Feedback is
 actively encouraged through surveys and discussions, and the provider demonstrates a
 commitment to listening and responding through a "You said, we did" approach. These
 arrangements help ensure people's views influence the development of the service and care
 remains focused on what matters to them.

 People's health, safety and well-being are promoted through effective oversight and partnership
 working with healthcare professionals. Risks are assessed and managed in a way which supports
 people to remain safe whilst maintaining as much independence as possible. People have timely
 access to healthcare services and receive appropriate support when changes in their health or
 well-being are identified. As a result, people experience continuity of care and support to help them
 achieve positive outcomes.

 People told us they feel safe living at the service and have confidence in the care and support
 provided. They said they would be comfortable raising concerns and were confident these would be
 addressed appropriately. Care staff demonstrate a good understanding of safeguarding
 responsibilities and describe leaders as visible, approachable and supportive. One member of staff
 told us, “They are highly visible, approachable and quick to respond to concerns or take action
 when required.” This contributes to an open and positive culture where people feel protected,
 valued and supported.

 People maintain important relationships with family and friends and benefit from a welcoming
 environment for relatives and visitors. We observed a warm and friendly atmosphere throughout
 the service. People benefit from a comfortable environment with a range of communal and quiet
 spaces that reflect their individual preferences. One person told us, “I love spending time in the
 courtyard because of the colourful flowers and the beautiful weather.” Continued investment in the
 environment will further support people's comfort and quality of life.

 Most people enjoy a varied programme of activities and meaningful opportunities for social
 interaction, community involvement and recreation. Dedicated activity staff support people to
 participate in group activities, events, outings and intergenerational experiences, helping them
 maintain interests and relationships. We observed people enjoying activities and engaging
 positively with care staff. Recent improvements have enhanced opportunities for engagement and

People’s language, communication and cultural needs are recognised and supported. The service

       Care & Support                       Good



People receive care and support from a staff team who know them well and understand their
individual needs, preferences and what matters most to them. Information about the service is
available to help people and their families make informed decisions. Comprehensive assessments
are completed before admission to ensure the service can meet people’s needs and support them
to achieve their personal outcomes.

People spoke very positively about the care and support they receive and consistently described
nurses and care staff as kind, caring and respectful. Comments included, “The care here is great,
and I have no complaints” and “I know all the nurses and staff, they are lovely.” We observed
warm, caring and respectful interactions throughout the inspection, with staff responding promptly
to people’s needs. Many nurses and care staff have worked at the service for several years,
helping to provide continuity of care and build trusted relationships. Staff demonstrated a good
understanding of people’s life histories, preferences and routines, helping people feel comfortable,
valued and secure.

People and relatives generally rate the care and support provided as good. We found people
largely receive care that meets their assessed needs and promotes positive outcomes. They are
involved in developing and reviewing their personal plans, helping to ensure care reflects their
preferences, choices and individual circumstances. However, some care records did not always
contain sufficient detail about people's preferences, emotional well-being and aspects of personal
care. We identified inconsistencies between some records and people's presentation, particularly in
relation to oral care, nail care, hair care and bathing. This reduced assurance that all aspects of
care were consistently delivered and recorded in line with assessed needs and preferences. The
provider had already identified these issues following the introduction of a new electronic care
system and had begun strengthening oversight and quality assurance arrangements. While further
improvements are required, we did not identify evidence of harm or a significant impact on people's
well-being and will review progress at the next inspection

People's health and well-being are promoted through effective partnership working with healthcare
professionals. Referrals are made promptly when specialist support is required, and nursing staff
maintain appropriate oversight of people's health needs. We saw evidence of effective wound care
management and monitoring of health conditions. Medicines are managed safely by trained and
competent nurses and care staff, helping people receive appropriate care and treatment.

People enjoy positive dining experiences that support their nutritional needs and social well-being.
Meals are appetising, varied and tailored to individual preferences and dietary requirements. One
person told us, “If I don't like something, I send it back and they always give me an alternative”,
while another said, “The food is lovely, varied and always warm.” We observed calm and sociable
mealtimes where people were offered choice and received the support they needed. Nutritional
needs are regularly monitored, and specialist advice is sought when required, helping people
maintain their health and well-being.

       Environment                         Good



People live in a welcoming and comfortable environment that promotes their well-being and quality
of life. Communal areas are bright, warm and well maintained, providing attractive spaces where
people can relax, socialise and participate in activities. Comfortable seating throughout the service
encourages people to spend time outside their bedrooms and maintain relationships with others.
Adapted bathing and showering facilities support people's comfort, dignity, independence and
choice.

People benefit from well-maintained indoor and outdoor spaces. The courtyard garden provides
accessible seating, shaded areas, water future and raised planting beds, creating opportunities for
relaxation, social interaction and engagement with nature. A person told us, “I love spending time in
the courtyard, enjoying the lovely weather and looking at the colourful flowers.” Communal areas,
including lounges, the bar and hairdressing salon, are furnished and decorated to a good standard,
helping to create a pleasant and homely atmosphere. One member of staff told us, “We benefit
greatly from having a homely environment."

The provider continues to invest in the environment to improve people's experiences. An estates
manager and maintenance team support refurbishment, repairs and the overall presentation of the
home. Environmental audits and maintenance systems help ensure issues are identified and
addressed promptly, while servicing arrangements support the safe operation of equipment and
facilities. Staff spoke positively about the improvements and said the environment continues to
improve.

The provider demonstrates a commitment to improving the environment through an ongoing
refurbishment programme. Many areas of the home provide a comfortable and welcoming
environment that supports people's well-being and quality of life. Planned improvements to some
bedrooms, en-suite facilities and bathrooms, the replacement of worn carpets, increased
opportunities for personalisation, and enhancements to sensory experiences will further strengthen
people's day-to-day experiences. The provider has also identified aspects of security, fire safety
and environmental maintenance where further progress is needed and has begun taking action to
address these areas. Continued investment in the environment will help ensure people benefit from
surroundings that support their comfort, well-being and individual need.

People are protected by arrangements that promote their safety and reduce the risk of harm. The
provider continues to make progress in addressing identified fire safety matters and has provided
assurance that planned improvements are being progressed. Personal Emergency Evacuation
Plans identify the support people may require in an emergency, and staff receive appropriate fire
safety training. Planned evacuation exercises will provide further assurance that people can be
supported safely and effectively if required.

People are protected from the risk of infection through effective infection prevention and control
arrangements. The environment was clean and well maintained during our visit, and people spoke

       Leadership & Management               Good



People benefit from a service led by an experienced and committed leadership team focused on
continuous improvement and positive outcomes. Since the last inspection, leadership
arrangements have been strengthened through the appointment of a new director, RI and
additional senior leaders, increasing oversight and support across the service. The long-standing
registered manager continues to provide stability and continuity. Together, these arrangements
promote a positive culture and help ensure the service remains responsive to people's needs.

People's views and experiences help shape the development of the service. The Responsible
Individual maintains a visible presence through regular engagement with people, relatives and staff,
and feedback is actively used to inform improvements. Since the last inspection, the provider has
strengthened governance and quality assurance arrangements through investment in digital
systems, enhanced clinical oversight and improved auditing, resulting in positive changes to some
aspects of care delivery. While these arrangements provide effective oversight and support
continuous improvement, further development of audit analysis, evaluation and wider sampling
would provide greater assurance that trends and areas for improvement are consistently identified
and addressed. Nurses and care staff described leaders as “highly visible, approachable and quick
to respond to concerns or take action when required” and having “a clear vision for improvement.”
These arrangements promote accountability and help drive positive outcomes for people.

People are supported by nurses and care staff who are safely recruited and provided with
opportunities to develop the knowledge and skills needed for their roles. Structured induction,
supervision and training arrangements support the delivery of effective and responsive care. A
blended approach to learning, including face-to-face training, online learning, shadowing and
mentoring, helps develop care staff competence and confidence. Further development of condition-
specific training would strengthen care staff knowledge and their ability to respond to people's
changing health needs. Care staff are registered with Social Care Wales, the workforce regulator,
but just under half of staff hold a relevant professional qualification.

Regular supervision provides opportunities for reflection, support and professional growth. The
provider has recently introduced initiatives to recognise and value staff contributions, helping to
promote staff well-being, retention and a positive workplace culture.

People benefit from support provided by a stable and experienced staff team who know them well
and understand their preferences, routines and individual needs. Long-serving nurses and care
staff help build trusting relationships and provide continuity of care. New staff are supported
through shadowing and guidance from experienced colleagues, and leaders consider staff skills
and experience when planning rotas. People's health and care needs are reviewed regularly, with

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
