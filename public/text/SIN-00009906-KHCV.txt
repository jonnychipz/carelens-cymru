Summary:

 Plas Lorna is a home that provides a warm and nurturing environment for the adults living there.
 People receive support from a care staff team who know them well and understand their needs.
 Personalised support helps people to remain as independent as possible and achieve positive
 outcomes in a safe and supportive home.

 Well-being is good. The home offers high level of care, supported by a team who know each
 person well and treat them with dignity. Care staff work in a way that encourages independence,
 choice and respect.

 Care and support provided is good. Care staff provide person-centred care that is tailored to
 people's individual needs and preferences. People are supported to access healthcare services,
 maintain relationships with family and friends, and participate in a range of meaningful activities
 that reflect their interests. Care plans provide clear guidance to ensure care is delivered

Findings:

        Well-being                           Good



 People living in the home benefit from a supportive and reassuring environment and have positive,
 respectful relationships with care staff. Care staff know people well and understand their individual
 needs, preferences and communication styles. We saw people are comfortable interacting with
 care staff and seek support when needed. A consistent care staff team supports each person,
 helping to build trusting relationships and promote positive outcomes. The home continues to work
 towards providing the Welsh Language Active Offer, enabling people to receive care and
 communicate in their preferred language.


 People are supported to make choices about their daily lives and are encouraged to take part in
 activities, hobbies and routines that are meaningful to them. Care staff regularly engage with
 people to understand their wishes and preferences and ensure these are reflected in the support
 provided. People are supported to communicate in ways that meet their individual needs and
 abilities. Care staff use personalised communication approaches, including visual aids, schedules
 and countdown tools, to help people understand daily routines, prepare for upcoming events and
 make informed choices.


 People are supported to maintain relationships with family and friends and remain connected to
 their local community. The home maintains open and positive communication with families through
 a messaging app, which provides regular updates and photographs of activities and events, helping
 families remain informed and involved in loved one's lives. Feedback from a relative was positive.
 They describe the home as "lovely" and that care staff support people's well-being whilst
 maintaining open and regular communication with them.


 Each person has a personalised activity plan based on their individual interests, preferences and
 goals. Activity records show people are encouraged and supported to participate in a range of
 opportunities, including attending local social groups, going to the gym and accessing community
 facilities. We saw evidence of people being encouraged to maintain and develop daily living skills;
 for example, one person proudly showed us their bedroom and, with support from care staff, made
 a cup of tea.


 People are supported to maintain their health and well-being through access to healthcare services
 and routine appointments. Care  staff arrange appointments, provide support to attend when
 required and ensure relevant information is accurately recorded within care records. Pre-admission

       Care & Support                       Good



Care and support provision is planned with input from families and relevant professionals, including
the home's Behaviour Analyst. This helps to ensure people's assessed needs, preferences, goals
and risks are reflected in their care plans. Some people are unable to fully take part in formal care
planning. In these cases, care staff gather information from families, professionals and their own
knowledge of the individual. This helps care staff understand what is important to the person,
including their likes, dislikes, routines and preferred ways of communicating. This helps ensure
care and support are tailored to each person's needs and preferences.


Information within people's pen portraits, admission documents, personal plans, positive behaviour
support plans and risk assessments is consistent and aligned, providing a clear and
comprehensive overview of each person's needs, preferences and support requirements. This
triangulation of documentation ensures care and support is well informed, person-centred and
tailored to the individual. Daily records are completed for each person, capturing information
relating to meals, daily routines and activities undertaken throughout the day. This level of
recording supports effective continuity of care, ensuring care staff on the next shift have an
accurate and up-to-date understanding of each person's recent experiences and needs. The home
follows the Active Support Model, supporting people to be actively involved in decisions about their
lives and encouraging them to develop and maintain independence in line with their abilities and
preferences.


The home is actively recruiting to support a stable and consistent care staff team and minimise the
use of agency care staff. Care staff currently working in the home have established positive
relationships with people and demonstrate a good understanding of their individual needs and
preferences. During the inspection, we observed the manager and care staff interacting with people
in a warm, caring and respectful manner. Care staff spent meaningful time with people and
provided support with daily tasks and routines in a person-centred way.


The home provides effective oversight of medication management, ensuring medicines are stored
securely and managed in line with required procedures. Medication Administration Records
showed that appropriate checks are completed and records maintained accurately, providing
assurance that medicines are managed safely.

Care staff undertake training that reflects the diverse needs of people living in the home, ensuring
they are equipped with the knowledge and skills required to deliver safe, effective and person-
centred support. Training records are well maintained and demonstrate that mandatory training is
current. Care and support are planned with input from families and relevant professionals,
including the home's Behaviour Analyst. This helps to ensure people's assessed needs,

       Environment                         Good




The home provides a range of accommodation options to meet people's individual needs and
preferences. There are six bedrooms within the main house, where people have access to
communal living and dining areas that support social interaction and shared activities. The home
also has five self-contained flats. At the time of the inspection, four flats were occupied and one flat
was vacant. The manager explained the flats are primarily used by people who find shared living
arrangements more challenging and benefit from having their own private living space. The six
bedrooms within the main house all have en-suite facilities. Each of the self-contained flats has its
own bathroom, which supports people's privacy, dignity and independence. People's bedrooms and
self-contained flats are personalised to reflect their preferences, interests and personalities. The
manager told us people are involved in decisions about the decoration and furnishing of their rooms
wherever possible. When people require support to make these decisions, care staff work with
family members to ensure personal preferences and individual needs are considered from the point
of admission.

People live in an environment that promotes their well-being and supports their individual needs.
The home is clean, tidy and generally well maintained. We found communal areas and people's
accommodation provide comfortable and suitable spaces for living, socialising and relaxing. The
manager acknowledged that some communal areas would benefit from redecoration and
refreshing. Plans are in place to modernise the dining room and create a more homely, welcoming
and sociable environment for people.


People live in an environment that is suitably equipped, maintained and designed to support their
well-being. Facilities and equipment are appropriate to meet people's individual needs. The home
maintains a maintenance log to record repairs and outstanding issues which an in-house
maintenance team responds to, helping to ensure the environment and equipment remain safe and
in good working order.


Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs) are in place for people and are specific to their
individual accommodation. Safety and maintenance records are current and complete. Records
relating to servicing, testing and routine safety checks are up to date, monitoring the environment
effectively. The home is working with the Fire Service to implement improvements and plans are in
place to further enhance fire safety within the home.

       Leadership & Management               Good




The home's Statement of Purpose is kept under regular review, and a Home Guide is provided to
people when they move into the home. The provider also has an information brochure which
outlines the care, support and facilities available. Together, these documents clearly explain the
home's aims and the support provided. The manager is relatively new to the home and care staff
spoke positively about their leadership. Care staff described the manager as approachable,
supportive and proactive. This helps promote a positive working culture and supports the delivery
of person-centred care. The Responsible Individual (RI) maintains oversight of the home through
regular visits and monitoring activities. The provider also completes a six-monthly quality of care
review, which evaluates the quality of the home and identifies areas for further improvement.


We reviewed the training matrix and found it was up to date, with a small number of refresher
courses already booked for the coming weeks. Records showed all care staff are registered with
Social Care Wales and have completed mandatory training within required timescales. Training
records clearly document completion and renewal dates, enabling the provider to monitor care staff
compliance and training needs effectively.


Supervision and appraisal arrangements are well managed, with completed sessions recorded and
plans in place for upcoming meetings. The home uses an electronic system to manage care staff
records, and supervision and appraisal templates support reflective discussions about practice,
performance and professional development. Team meeting minutes show that care staff have
opportunities to share information, discuss people's needs and contribute to home developments.
Weekly Senior Leadership Team meetings provide oversight and support to the home, enabling
leaders to monitor performance, address emerging issues and drive continuous improvement.


Feedback from family members is positive. Relatives told us they value the care and support
provided and appreciate the commitment shown by management and care staff in meeting people's
individual needs. This demonstrates the home works effectively with families and promotes positive
outcomes for people living at the home.


Policies and procedures are well maintained and up to date. Documents include clear review dates
and version control information, enabling the home to monitor and update guidance regularly to
reflect current practice and legislative requirements. Incident records are completed appropriately
and provide clear information about events, actions taken and outcomes. Reports are shared with
the Behaviour Analyst for review, enabling behavioural support plans and risk management
strategies to be updated where required and people's changing needs continue to be met

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
