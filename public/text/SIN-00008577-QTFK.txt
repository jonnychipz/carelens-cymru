Summary:

 Belvedere House is a welcoming care home located Tenby. The service provides a comfortable
 and homely environment, with several communal areas and opportunities for residents to
 personalise their living spaces. Its location enables access to local amenities and community
 facilities, supporting residents to maintain links with the local area. The service is registered to
 provide residential care for up to 19 people.

 People’s wellbeing is excellent. Individuals are safe, settled and happy, with consistently positive
 feedback from relatives, staff and professionals. A range of meaningful activities are available,
 including opportunities for community engagement and social interaction. Staff interactions are
 kind, respectful and promote a person-centred approach to care delivery.

 Care and support are excellent. Staff have developed strong, positive relationships with people and
 demonstrate a good understanding of their needs, preferences and histories. The service works

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being at Belvedere House. They are supported to have as much
 control as possible over their lives and to understand their rights and entitlements as far as they are
 able. Personal plans are detailed and provide clear information about people’s routines, likes and
 dislikes, enabling staff to deliver consistent, person-centred care. This supports people to make
 everyday choices, such as selecting clothing, choosing food and drink, and deciding how to spend
 their time. People are also supported to take part in wider community life, including exercising their
 civic rights, which maintains inclusion and a sense of belonging.

 People are consistently positive about their experiences of living at the service. One relative told us
 they feel “blessed and lucky” their family member lives at the service, and a person said they
 “wouldn’t be anywhere else.” People feel confident their views are valued and acted upon, and they
 are encouraged to express their preferences and raise any concerns. Information about how to
 raise concerns is accessible, and there is a positive culture where individuals feel listened to.

 People have strong, positive relationships with staff, who treat them with kindness, warmth and
 respect. Staff know people very well, including their life histories and preferences, and use this
 knowledge to promote meaningful engagement and maintain dignity. This is reflected in daily
 interactions and the personalised support people receive.

 People are supported to maintain meaningful relationships with family and friends. Visiting
 arrangements are flexible, and some people use technology to stay in contact where needed. A
 range of meaningful activities is available, including regular trips into the local community, coffee
 mornings and social outings. Staff use tools such as the “Daily Sparkle” newspaper to encourage
 conversation and engagement, supporting people’s social and emotional well-being. The service
 subscribes to the newspaper service, which includes topics such as historical events, past
 memories and themes relevant to people’s lives, helping to prompt discussion and reminiscence.

 People’s health and safety are promoted effectively. Staff understand safeguarding procedures and
 take appropriate action to protect people from harm. Risks are well managed, enabling people to
 remain as independent as possible while maintaining their safety. The environment also supports
 people’s well-being, with dementia-friendly approaches, clear signage and personalised spaces
 that help individuals feel comfortable and at home.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



With few exceptions, people experience high-quality, person-centred care and support at Belvedere
House. Outcomes for people are exceptional because care staff know them extremely well. Staff
have developed positive and meaningful relationships with people and demonstrate a strong
understanding of their individual needs, preferences and life histories. A visiting professional told
us, “they (staff) know every individual… they know their history,” highlighting how this knowledge
informs care delivery. In addition, Also, further continuity of care, supported by a stable staff team,
enables staff to recognise changes in a person’s presentation and respond promptly.

The provider ensures a person’s needs can be met, alongside those of people already living at the
service, before offering them a place. Pre-admission assessments are comprehensive and involve
engagement with the person, their family and relevant professionals to gain a clear understanding
of needs, preferences and personal outcomes. People are supported to become familiar with the
service through opportunities such as respite stays, which helps to support smooth transitions and
positive experiences when moving in.

Care planning is detailed, person-centred and clearly reflects what matters to people. Personal
plans include clear information about routines, likes, dislikes and how people wish to be supported,
enabling staff to provide consistent and responsive care. Plans are regularly reviewed, and people
and their relatives are involved in decision-making, ensuring care remains relevant and promotes
positive outcomes.

People are supported to make choices about their daily lives and maintain their independence in
line with their abilities. Staff use their knowledge of individuals to provide flexible, responsive care
and support people to engage in routines, activities and community opportunities that are
meaningful to them.

The service provider has strong, established links with health, mental health and social care
services, resulting in people experiencing excellent outcomes. People benefit from coordinated
access to healthcare professionals such as GPs, pharmacists, opticians and dentists. This
collaborative approach ensures emerging health needs are identified and addressed promptly and
provides strong assurance that people’s health needs are effectively monitored and managed. One
professional told us communication is “very proactive and quick to act,” which supports timely
intervention and coordinated care.

People are protected by high standards of hygiene practices which comply with legislation.
Cleaning regimes are robust, and we observed good standards of hygiene throughout the service
which has a Food Standards Agency hygiene rating of 5, demonstrating excellent hygiene
practices. Mandatory infection control training is in place for staff, and sufficient infection control
supplies are readily available, supporting safe and consistent practice.

       Environment                         Good



The environment is good. It is welcoming, comfortable, clean and well-maintained, providing a
pleasant setting for people. Feedback from relatives and professionals is positive. One relative
described the home as “so homely,” and both relatives and professionals told us it is “always
clean,” which reflects our observations during the inspection. This contributes to a calm and
reassuring atmosphere for people living at the service.

People have access to suitable communal areas as well as private spaces. Bedrooms are
personalised to reflect individual tastes, with personal keepsakes and family photographs evident,
reinforcing people’s sense of ownership and identity. Communal areas are used effectively to
support social interaction and activities, including coffee mornings, celebrations and quizzes,
helping to promote a sense of community within the home. People may spend time in shared areas
or in private, depending on their preferences. The environment includes safe outdoor areas,
supporting people access to fresh air and social engagement.

The premises are suitably equipped with aids to promote independence and support daily living.
People are able to move around the home safely and access facilities with ease. Personal
emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) are in place and provide clear guidance to support people to
remain safe in the event of an emergency.

People benefit from a positive dining experience. There is a good choice of meals available, and
food is of good quality, with locally sourced ingredients used where possible. People spoke
positively about the meals provided, with one person describing the food as “delicious.” Mealtimes
are relaxed and provide opportunities for social interaction.

Dementia-friendly approaches are evident and support people’s orientation and general well-being.
This includes clear signage, orientation aids and a familiar environment which supports people
living with dementia to navigate the home and maintain independence.

Arrangements are in place to maintain the safety and quality of the environment. Environmental
audits and regular checks are completed to monitor standards, and ongoing maintenance plans
ensure the premises remain safe and well-maintained. Systems are in place to identify, record and
address any issues in a timely manner. Overall, the environment meets people’s needs and
supports their well-being outcomes.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management are excellent. People have high levels of confidence in the service
provider because leaders promote a strong, positive culture and ensure there are well-established
systems in place to support the safe and effective running of the service. Leaders are visible and
approachable, acting as positive role models and setting clear expectations for staff practice and
conduct. The manager is experienced and provides clear leadership, supported by a stable and
committed staff team. Staff described the service as “a warm and caring environment” where they
“work well together,” reflecting strong teamwork and a positive culture.

There are effective governance and oversight arrangements in place. Audits, quality assurance
processes and regular reviews are completed to monitor the service and drive improvement. These
systems provide assurance that high standards of care are maintained. The Responsible Individual
(RI) is based in the service, has strong oversight and is actively involved in monitoring quality,
performance and compliance. Feedback from people, relatives and professionals is used to inform
service development and improvements.

People and their relatives are encouraged to share their views about the service. There is a culture
of openness, and people feel confident their feedback will be listened to and acted upon. One
relative told us management have been “very supportive and professional and always
communicate,” which reflects the positive experiences of families and the responsive approach
taken by leaders. Arrangements are in place to manage concerns and complaints appropriately,
and information about how to raise concerns is accessible. The provider demonstrates a strong
commitment to sharing information with relevant agencies, supporting transparency and
accountability.

Staff recruitment processes are robust and ensure suitable staff are employed. Staff receive
appropriate training, supervision and ongoing development, ensuring they have the skills and
knowledge to meet people’s needs effectively. The stable staff team supports continuity of care and
enables staff to build strong, trusting relationships with people.

There is clear evidence of forward planning to support sustainability and ongoing development.
Leadership arrangements are effective in maintaining high-quality care and demonstrate a
commitment to continuous improvement, ensuring positive outcomes for people living at the
service.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
