Summary:

 Care Inspectorate Wales inspected Oakhill Care Home and rated the service Excellent in all four
 areas. The home consistently exceeds expected standards and achieves positive outcomes for
 people living at the service.

 People experience outstanding wellbeing and are supported to live meaningful, fulfilling lives. They
 are treated with dignity, warmth and respect, and are fully involved in decisions about their care
 and daily routines. Staff use positive, well-managed risk-taking to promote independence. People
 feel safe, listened to and valued, and are supported to maintain relationships and participate in
 community life. The homely, personalised environment helps people feel settled, secure and at
 home.

 Care is highly personalised, consistent and responsive to individual needs. Staff know people
 extremely well and actively support social inclusion and meaningful activity. Health needs and
 medication are managed safely and effectively. The home is exceptionally clean, safe and
 welcoming, with accessible indoor and outdoor spaces. Strong leadership drives a positive culture.
 Robust governance and proactive quality assurance support continuous improvement, staff
 development and exceptional outcomes for people.

Findings:

        Well-being                                Excellent



 People experience excellent well-being outcomes, with care and support consistently exceeding
 regulatory expectations. The service demonstrates a strong, embedded culture of person‑centred
 practice, enabling people to live safe, meaningful lives with choice, dignity and positive risk‑taking.

 People are supported to live healthily and safely; with clear evidence they have control over
 decisions which affect their daily lives. Care is individualised and based on people’s preferences,
 abilities and aspirations. Positive risk management enables people to maintain independence,
 including involvement in financial decision‑making where appropriate. Health needs are proactively
 managed through timely access to primary care and specialist services, alongside effective
 monitoring of nutrition, hydration, mobility and skin integrity. People feel safe, respected and
 empowered. One person told us, “Staff help me the way I want,” and confirmed they felt safe living
 at the service.

 Robust safeguarding arrangements are in place and well understood by staff. Clear procedures,
 regular training and a strong safeguarding culture ensure people are protected from abuse and
 neglect. People are supported to raise concerns in ways that meet their communication needs, and
 staff demonstrate confidence in recognising and responding to safeguarding issues. People feel
 protected and reassured. Families consistently described the care as “caring and compassionate,”
 and noted staff treat people “like family.”

 The service strongly promotes social inclusion and meaningful relationships. People are supported
 to maintain contact with family and friends and to develop relationships within the home and the
 wider community. Individualised activity planning, regular outings, therapy dog visits and
 resident‑led initiatives support social engagement and emotional well‑being. Technology is used
 effectively to help people stay connected. People are settled, valued and socially engaged.
 Families described people as “happy, well supported and settled.”

 People live in a safe, homely and well‑maintained environment that supports their individual
 well‑being outcomes. Bedrooms and communal areas are personalised, clean and comfortable,
 and people are involved in decisions about adaptations and improvements. Outdoor spaces are
 accessible and encourage independence and enjoyment. People feel comfortable, dignified and at
 home. Families praised the “homely atmosphere” and quality of the environment. Feedback from
 people and families is consistently positive, with individuals feeling safe, listened to and supported
 to live fulfilling lives.

       Care & Support                           Excellent



People experience exceptional care and support that consistently achieves and enhances their
personal outcomes. Care is clearly person-centred, well planned and delivered in partnership with
people and, where appropriate, those important to them. Care planning documentation is
comprehensive, accurate and regularly reviewed, and reflects people’s wishes, aspirations,
specialist needs, communication requirements and assessed risks. Case tracking confirmed people
experience strong continuity of care, supported by effective multi-disciplinary working and timely
referrals to health and specialist services. As a result, people are supported to live meaningful,
fulfilled lives with choice and control over their day-to-day experiences.

People are exceptionally well protected from harm and abuse. Safeguarding arrangements are
robust and embedded into everyday practice, with staff demonstrating strong awareness of their
responsibilities. The service responds promptly and appropriately to any concerns, with clear
evidence of learning and improvement where incidents have occurred. This reflects a positive
safeguarding culture and effective management oversight. People told inspectors they feel safe
and reassured living at the service, and families expressed high levels of confidence in how risks
are managed.

People’s medication is managed to a very high standard, with effective systems in place to ensure
safe ordering, storage, administration and review. Staff are suitably trained and competent, and
records demonstrate medicines are administered safely and in line with prescribed instructions.
Regular auditing and oversight provide strong assurance that practice remains safe and
person-centred, supporting positive health outcomes.

The service demonstrates excellent infection prevention and control practice. Staff consistently
adhere to Public Health Wales guidance, with appropriate use of personal protective equipment
(PPE), effective cleaning regimes and well-organised laundry and waste management systems.
The environment is clean, well maintained and supports people’s health and wellbeing.

People spoke positively about the care they receive, telling inspectors they feel listened to,
respected and supported in ways that matter to them. One person said, “I like living here… staff
help me the way I want.” Families and relatives described the care as “excellent,” noting that staff
are caring and compassionate and people appear settled, happy and well supported.

Overall, the service consistently exceeds expectations, delivering compassionate, personalised
care that results in highly positive outcomes for people.

       Environment                              Excellent



The service provides an outstanding environment which goes beyond meeting people’s basic
needs, actively promoting wellbeing, independence, choice and quality of life. The home is bright,
spacious and thoughtfully designed, with wide corridors and accessible walkways which fully
support people’s mobility and independence. Communal areas enable people to choose how and
where they spend their time, whether socialising or enjoying quieter spaces, supporting emotional
wellbeing and autonomy.

Bedrooms are highly personalised, immaculately maintained and aligned with personal plans.
People are supported to make decisions about their personal space, décor and furnishings,
creating a strong sense of ownership, dignity and belonging. Equipment and specialist aids are not
only appropriate and well maintained but are proactively reviewed and adapted, enabling people to
do more for themselves and maintain independence.

Outdoor space is a particular strength. People have access to a secure, attractive and stimulating
garden featuring seating, planting and opportunities for meaningful activity. People are actively
involved in the upkeep and use of the garden; for example, a resident growing vegetables, which
promotes purpose, confidence and enjoyment. These opportunities contribute directly to people’s
positive mood, engagement and overall wellbeing.

People consistently report positive experiences of living at the home. One person told inspectors, “I
like living here,” and confirmed they feel “safe” and well supported. Feedback described the service
as welcoming, comfortable and homely, with people valuing both the environment and the social
opportunities it supports.

The provider demonstrates exceptionally strong governance and oversight of health and safety.
Comprehensive risk assessments are routinely reviewed, and the environment is maintained to a
consistently high standard. Fire safety arrangements are robust and person-centred, with individual
personal emergency evacuation plans, regular drills and clear evacuation planning. Maintenance
systems are well-organised, forward-planned and responsive, ensuring all equipment and systems
remain safe and fit for purpose.

Cleanliness and infection prevention standards are exemplary, evidenced by a food hygiene rating
of 5 and consistently positive observations. Control of Substances Hazardous to Health (COSHH)
management and clinical waste disposal arrangements are secure and well managed, further
supporting people’s safety. Family members reflected these outcomes, describing the service as
“well managed, safe and homely,” and reporting their relatives are settled, happy and thriving.

       Leadership & Management                Excellent



Leadership and management arrangements at Oakhill are highly effective and make a significant
contribution to the positive outcomes experienced by people. Leaders demonstrate clear vision,
strong values and a sustained commitment to continuous improvement, ensuring people receive
high-quality, person-centred care in a stable and well-governed service.

The service benefits from exceptional governance, with robust oversight systems that are well
embedded and used proactively to drive improvement rather than simply maintain compliance. The
manager and Responsible Individual (RI) work in close partnership, with clearly defined roles and
responsibilities. Monitoring systems are comprehensive and provide a strong understanding of
risks, performance and people’s outcomes. As a result, people experience a service that is safe,
well organised and consistently responsive to their needs.

Quality assurance arrangements are a particular strength. Regular audits, RI statutory visits and
quality of care reviews are thorough and analytical, and findings are clearly translated into service
development. Feedback from people, families and staff is actively encouraged, valued and acted
upon, leading to tangible improvements that enhance people’s quality of life. People told us they
feel safe and well supported. Families consistently described the service as “very well managed”
with “strong leadership” and high standards of care.

There is strong oversight of financial arrangements, with clear evidence of planned investment in
the service. Improvements to the environment, facilities, staffing and digital systems demonstrate a
long-term commitment to meeting people’s needs and promoting dignity, comfort and safety. This
financial stability provides reassurance and supports the continuity of high-quality care.

Leaders place a strong emphasis on supporting and developing staff. Recruitment, vetting,
induction, supervision and appraisal arrangements exceed regulatory expectations and contribute
to a confident, skilled workforce. Training compliance is high, with effective oversight ensuring staff
maintain the skills required to meet people’s complex needs. Staff reported feeling valued and
proud to work at the service, benefiting people through continuity, positive relationships and
high-quality support.

Outstanding leadership and management create a positive culture where people are at the heart of
the service. People are settled, happy and confident in their care, and families expressed high
levels of trust. Leadership arrangements go beyond requirements and significantly enhance
people’s wellbeing outcomes.

Areas identified for improvement
Where we identify Areas for Improvement but we have not found outcomes for people to be at
immediate or significant risk, we discuss these with the provider. We expect the provider to take
action and we will follow this up at the next inspection.


Where we find outcomes for people require significant improvement and/or there is risk to
people’s well-being we identify areas for Priority Action. In these circumstances we issue a
Priority Action Notice(s) to the Provider, and they must take immediate steps to make
improvements. We will inspect again within six months to check improvements have been made
and outcomes for people have improved.


CIW has no areas for improvement identified following this inspection.


CIW has not issued any Priority action notices following this inspection.
